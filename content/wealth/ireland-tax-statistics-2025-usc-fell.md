The State collected **€137 billion** in taxes and social contributions in 2025. That was 8%, or €10 billion, more than in 2024, according to [Ireland's Tax Statistics 2025](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-itxs/irelandstaxstatistics2025/keyfindings/), which the CSO published on 23 November. The total has more than doubled in ten years, and it made up 95% of all government revenue.

The CSO estimated the population at about 5.46 million in April 2025. That works out at roughly **€25,100 for every person in the country**. Most of it, though, does not come from household pay packets.

## Where the money came from

| Tax, 2025 | Amount | Change on 2024 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income tax (excluding USC) | €31.3bn | +5.7% |
| Universal Social Charge (USC) | €5.6bn | **−1.8%** |
| Corporation tax | €32.9bn | +17.2% |
| VAT | over €23bn | +5.2% |
| PRSI | €18.3bn | +8.2% |
| Excise duties | €6.6bn | |
| Capital gains tax | €2.1bn | +25% |

Income tax and USC together raised almost **€37 billion**, a little over a quarter of all tax. Corporation tax was close behind at **24% of the total**, up from 8% in 1995. The CSO says corporation tax receipts have grown nearly five-fold since 2015. A small number of large multinationals pay much of it, which is why the Department of Finance and the Irish Fiscal Advisory Council keep warning against relying on it to fund day-to-day spending.

## Why USC fell while income tax rose

USC was the only major tax to bring in less in 2025 than in 2024: **€5.62 billion against €5.73 billion**. More people were working and wages were rising, so the fall came from a policy change. Budget 2025 cut the middle USC rate from **4% to 3%** and raised the ceiling of the 2% band from €25,760 to **€27,382**, according to [Revenue's USC rates table](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/usc/standard-rates-thresholds.aspx).

Here is what that meant in 2025 for a single PAYE worker earning **€50,000** with the standard credits:

- **Income tax:** 20% on the first €44,000 is €8,800, and 40% on the remaining €6,000 is €2,400. Taking away the €2,000 personal credit and the €2,000 employee credit leaves **€7,200**.
- **USC:** 0.5% on the first €12,012 is €60.06. 2% on the next €15,370 is €307.40. 3% on the remaining €22,618 is €678.54. That comes to **€1,046**.

Under 2024's USC rules, the same €50,000 would have produced a USC bill of about **€1,305**. That means the changes were worth roughly **€259 a year** to this earner, or about €5 a week. Across millions of workers, that is enough to push total USC receipts down even as pay rose.

Income tax and USC together came to **€8,246**, about 16.5% of gross pay, before PRSI.

## What it means for you

- **The national total says little about your own bill.** Your tax depends on your pay, credits and circumstances. Use the [income tax calculator](/income-tax-calculator/) and the [USC calculator](/usc-calculator/) to check the current year.
- **Make sure you are claiming every credit.** Many PAYE workers miss credits and reliefs such as the rent tax credit, medical expenses and remote working relief. Revenue generally allows claims for up to four previous years. Our guides to [tax credits](/wealth/tax-credits-ireland-explained/) and [unclaimed PAYE refunds](/news/paye-tax-refunds-2025-unclaimed-november-2026/) explain how to claim.
- **Keep corporation tax in mind when you hear budget promises.** Nearly a quarter of all tax now comes from one volatile source. That is a real risk to future tax cuts and spending increases. See what [Budget 2027](/wealth/budget-2027-what-to-watch/) changed for next year.
