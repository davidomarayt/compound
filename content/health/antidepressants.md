## At a glance

Antidepressants are a group of medicines used for depression and sometimes for anxiety, pain or other conditions. The group includes several families with different targets and side-effect profiles. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antidepressants/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Transporters, receptors and the time course of treatment

SSRIs reduce serotonin reuptake. SNRIs affect reuptake of serotonin and noradrenaline. Tricyclics often affect those transporters and several other receptors. Changes at transporter level can occur earlier than changes in mood and function. Repeated exposure and changes in nerve circuits are among the explanations for that delay. A diagnosis cannot be reduced to measuring a single neurotransmitter. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antidepressants/)

## What the evidence measures

Trial results depend on the condition studied. A depression symptom scale, return to usual activities, relapse after recovery and treatment discontinuation measure different things. Discuss which of those outcomes is most important at a follow-up appointment.

## Compare medicines by the question that matters

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Why it was prescribed | depression, anxiety or another indication |
| What to monitor | mood, sleep, function and side effects over time |


## What if a side effect appears before there is benefit?

Record when it started, its severity and which part of day-to-day life it affects. The timing can inform a discussion about managing the effect, changing treatment or continuing to the planned review.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Discuss dose changes or tapering with the prescriber. Seek urgent help if thoughts of self-harm emerge or you feel unable to stay safe. Bring other prescription, non-prescription and herbal products to the medication review. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antidepressants/)
