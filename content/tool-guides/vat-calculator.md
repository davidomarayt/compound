## What this calculator is for

This tool converts between net and VAT-inclusive prices at the selected Irish VAT rate. It is useful for invoices, quotations and quick price checks, but it does not determine which VAT rate legally applies to a product or service.

## How to read the result

If you start with a net amount, VAT is added to reach the gross amount. If you start with a VAT-inclusive amount, the VAT element must be backed out by dividing by 1 + the rate rather than simply subtracting the percentage from the gross price.

## Worked example

At the standard 23% rate, a €100 net price becomes €123 gross. If €123 is already VAT-inclusive, the VAT component is €23—not 23% of €123.

## Irish context

Revenue's current 2026 rates include the 23% standard rate, 13.5% reduced rate and 9% second reduced rate, together with other categories such as zero-rated supplies and the livestock rate. The correct rate depends on the supply.

## What can change the answer

The applicable VAT rate can depend on the exact goods/services, place of supply, customer status, exemptions and special schemes. Businesses also need to consider whether they are registered or required to register.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not assume every Irish sale is 23%. Do not calculate VAT included in a gross figure by multiplying the gross by 23%. And do not confuse VAT collected from customers with business revenue.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I remove 23% VAT from a gross price?
Divide the gross price by 1.23 to find the net amount.

### Is VAT 23% on everything?
No.

### Does this tell me whether I must register for VAT?
No.

### Can I reclaim the VAT shown?
Input VAT recovery depends on business circumstances and Revenue rules.

## Why removing VAT is not the reverse of adding it

A common arithmetic error is to add 23% to a net price correctly, then try to remove VAT from the gross price by subtracting 23%.

If €100 net becomes €123 gross, 23% of the gross figure is €28.29—not the €23 VAT originally added. To recover the net price, divide €123 by 1.23.

The same principle works for other VAT rates. That is why this calculator has separate net-to-gross and gross-to-net directions.

For businesses, the VAT shown on an invoice is also not automatically the business's tax cost. VAT-registered traders can, subject to the rules, collect output VAT and recover allowable input VAT. The economic burden and cash-flow timing depend on the business.

Registration thresholds, exempt activities, reverse-charge rules and cross-border supplies can all change the position. This calculator should therefore be used for arithmetic, not for deciding the legal VAT treatment of a transaction.

## Method and limitations

For net-to-gross, the calculator multiplies the net amount by 1 + VAT rate. For gross-to-net, it divides the gross by 1 + rate and treats the difference as VAT.
