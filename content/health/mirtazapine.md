## At a glance

Mirtazapine is an antidepressant with a receptor profile that differs from SSRIs. Its effects on sleep and appetite may be prominent during treatment. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/mirtazapine/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Receptor signalling, wakefulness and appetite

Mirtazapine blocks certain alpha-2 adrenergic receptors, altering noradrenaline and serotonin transmission. It also blocks selected serotonin receptors and histamine H1 receptors. The H1 action helps explain sedation. The benefit for depression is assessed over a longer timeframe than an early change in sleep. [DailyMed source](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=577c3ceb-316b-4c93-9b0c-0a4b1b4773e9)

## What the evidence measures

Record daytime alertness, appetite and mood separately. Sleep improvement can occur on a different timescale from broader improvement in daily functioning. A prescriber can help interpret those patterns during review.

## Keep two columns for your review

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Potential benefit | mood, sleep and participation in usual activities |
| Potential harm | daytime drowsiness, appetite and weight changes |


## Can increased appetite happen before mood changes?

Different receptor effects can occur on different timescales. Tracking appetite, sleep and mood separately helps make a better assessment at review.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Discuss other sedating medicines and alcohol. Seek urgent clinical advice if severe infection symptoms or significant mental health deterioration occur. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/mirtazapine/)

## Why sleep, appetite and mood need separate columns

Sedation can emerge from mirtazapine's action at histamine receptors. Appetite effects and the eventual change in depression symptoms can have different timelines. In the weeks before a review, note when you go to sleep and how you function the next morning. Keep a separate note of whether you can do the activities depression had interrupted. That makes it possible to discuss a change in sleep without assuming mood has improved in the same way. [HSE mirtazapine guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/mirtazapine/) · [Depression treatment guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222/chapter/Recommendations)

## Decide which changes would make you contact the prescriber

Ask what to do if sleepiness affects driving or work, appetite or weight changes are hard to manage, or symptoms worsen before your next appointment. Bring other medicines that can contribute to sedation. The care team can assess the whole list instead of attributing the result to one medicine. If treatment needs to change, ask for a planned adjustment and how to report symptoms during it. [HSE mirtazapine guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/mirtazapine/)

If you feel unable to keep yourself safe, seek urgent help. A fever with sore throat or mouth ulcers warrants prompt medical advice while taking mirtazapine. [HSE mirtazapine guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/mirtazapine/)
