**Mortgage protection insurance is a small monthly bill attached to an enormous financial commitment.** Get the right cover and a surviving partner may be able to remain in the family home without a mortgage to repay. Get the wrong structure, fail to disclose important information or let a policy lapse, and that protection may not work as expected.

Most people in Ireland need mortgage protection before drawing down a mortgage on their own home. But you **do not have to buy it from your bank**. You can compare policies from different insurers, provided the policy satisfies your lender's requirements.

This guide explains what you are legally required to have, how premiums work, which insurers to compare, and where people can accidentally overpay. It also covers an important change in Irish law due to begin on **15 October 2026** for eligible cancer survivors.

<h2 id="what-is-it">What is mortgage protection insurance?</h2>

Mortgage protection is a form of life assurance linked to a home loan. It is designed to pay a lump sum if an insured person dies during the cover term. Where the policy is assigned to the lender, the proceeds are used to meet the lender's claim first.

For example, suppose two people borrow **€320,000 over 30 years** and take out an appropriate decreasing-term mortgage protection policy. If one insured person dies when the outstanding loan is €245,000, the policy is intended to provide an amount sufficient to clear the mortgage, subject to the policy schedule and conditions.

The insured amount normally reduces on an agreed schedule. It does not necessarily equal the actual mortgage balance on every single day. That distinction matters if you change interest rates, extend the loan term, miss repayments or borrow more money.

**Mortgage protection does not pay your mortgage every month if you become ill, lose your job or take unpaid leave.** Our [Income Protection Insurance Ireland guide](/wealth/income-protection-insurance-ireland/) explains the different type of policy that may replace part of your earnings while you cannot work.

<h2 id="mandatory">Is mortgage protection mandatory in Ireland?</h2>

**For most borrowers taking a mortgage on their principal private residence, yes.** Section 126 of the Consumer Credit Act 1995 sets out the general requirement and exceptions. It is worth separating the legal requirement from what an individual bank may demand as a condition of lending.

### When might the legal requirement not apply?

Citizens Information identifies exceptions including situations where:

- You are **over 50** at the relevant time.
- The mortgage is not on your **principal private residence** (for example, certain investment-property loans).
- You already have enough suitable life assurance to repay the mortgage.
- You cannot obtain the required cover because of an illness, disability or other recognised reason within the statutory rules.

**An exception is not a guarantee that the lender will advance money without any life cover.** The lender can still assess the application and its security requirements. Ask the lender for its decision in writing, especially when health or age is the reason cover is unavailable.

A first-time buyer should arrange cover well before the proposed closing date. The [Buying a Home in Ireland 101 guide](/wealth/buying-a-home-in-ireland/) explains the full process, while our [mortgage protection and drawdown checklist](/wealth/mortgage-protection-drawdown-ireland/) covers the evidence your solicitor and lender may need.

<h2 id="cost">How much does mortgage protection cost in Ireland?</h2>

There is no single national price. Some insurers advertise entry-level premiums near **€10–€13 a month**, but those are *starting* prices subject to specific eligibility and cover assumptions, not promises that a typical family can obtain a large 30-year mortgage policy for that amount. For context, see the published product pages for [Royal London](https://www.royallondon.ie/our-products/mortgage-protection/), [Aviva](https://www.aviva.ie/protection/mortgage-protection-insurance/) and [Irish Life](https://www.irishlife.ie/life-insurance/mortgage-protection/).

Your premium typically depends on:

- **Age:** generally, new cover becomes dearer as applicants get older.
- **Amount borrowed:** €450,000 normally needs more cover than €180,000.
- **Term:** 35 years of protection usually costs more than 15 years of otherwise comparable cover.
- **Smoker status:** tobacco or nicotine-use definitions vary by insurer.
- **Health and family history:** underwriting may affect acceptance, price or exclusions.
- **Single, joint or dual life:** different payout structures can have different prices.
- **Add-ons:** serious illness, conversion options and additional benefits may increase the premium.

You may also see a **1% life assurance levy** included in quoted premiums. Compare the amount you will actually pay, including applicable levies, rather than a headline price before charges.

### Worked example: comparing two like-for-like offers

Imagine a couple needing €300,000 of decreasing-term cover for 30 years. Suppose two insurers offer the **same accepted coverage structure, equivalent benefits and the same start date**, but one final quote is €19 a month and the other is €27.

- Insurer A: €19 × 12 × 30 = **€6,840**.
- Insurer B: €27 × 12 × 30 = **€9,720**.
- Potential nominal difference: **€2,880** over the term.

This is a budgeting illustration, **not a current quote from either provider**. Real contracts can differ in exclusions, claims features, whether the premium is guaranteed and how the insured amount decreases.

<h2 id="types">Reducing-term versus level-term mortgage protection</h2>

### Decreasing-term cover: the usual mortgage choice

Most repayment-mortgage protection policies use **decreasing cover**. The insured amount falls over the policy's life according to a schedule intended to broadly reflect the repayment of capital.

Because the insurer is exposed to less potential payout as time passes, this normally costs less than keeping the original insured sum unchanged for the whole period.

The important catch: if your actual mortgage reduces more slowly than the assumed policy schedule — perhaps because you extend the term — the sum assured could become **insufficient to clear the loan**. Check the schedule with your lender or adviser if you change the mortgage.

### Level-term life cover: more expensive, potentially more protection

With **level-term** cover, a fixed insured sum generally stays in place during the agreed term. If you insure €300,000 and the remaining mortgage is only €150,000 at the time of an accepted death claim, proceeds may be sufficient to clear the debt with the residual available to the appropriate beneficiary or estate, depending on ownership, assignment and policy terms.

That extra protection normally means a higher premium. It can make sense for a household that also wants a family life-insurance benefit, but compare it with buying mortgage protection and separate [life or family protection cover](/wealth/how-to-make-a-will-ireland/).

<h2 id="dual-life">Joint-life versus dual-life mortgage protection</h2>

This is one of the most important differences for couples, and a common source of confusion.

| Structure | What generally happens after the first insured death? | Potential second claim? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Joint life, first death** | One benefit is paid and the joint policy ends. | **No**, because the policy has ended. |
| **Dual life** | Each person has separate cover under the plan; the other life can remain insured. | **Potentially yes**, subject to terms and cover remaining. |
| **Two single-life policies** | Each separate policy operates on its own terms. | **Potentially yes**, if both policies remain valid. |

Consider a couple with €260,000 of outstanding debt when one spouse dies. A suitable joint-life policy might pay €260,000 to discharge the lender's debt and then end. A dual-life arrangement could pay out for the first insured life while preserving separate cover on the survivor, depending on the policy.

That doesn't mean dual life automatically delivers a full second cash payout: the cover **decreases over time**, the second claim must meet policy conditions, and assignment/beneficiaries matter. Royal London specifically advertises dual-life mortgage protection at the same price as joint-life in relevant cases, so this is worth checking in a like-for-like comparison.

**Unmarried couples** should also ask about policy ownership, beneficiaries and inheritance-tax implications rather than assuming every structure produces the same result. A regulated adviser and solicitor can explain the consequences for your circumstances.

<h2 id="serious-illness">Should you add serious illness cover?</h2>

Ordinary mortgage protection pays following the death of an insured life. **Specified serious illness cover** is a separate optional benefit offered on some plans. It may pay if you are diagnosed with an illness meeting the policy's precise definition and severity criteria.

The phrase *serious illness* is broader in everyday conversation than in an insurance contract. Not every cancer diagnosis, heart condition, mental-health condition or period away from work qualifies. Check:

- The list of covered illnesses and severity thresholds.
- Whether a payment reduces or uses up the remaining life cover.
- Any survival period, waiting period, age limit or exclusions.
- Whether serious illness benefit is accelerated, standalone or additional.
- The total monthly premium with and without the option.

If your concern is **paying monthly bills while unable to work**, read [Income Protection Insurance in Ireland](/wealth/income-protection-insurance-ireland/). If your concern is **hospital access**, that is a different question addressed in our [Health Insurance Ireland guide](/wealth/health-insurance-ireland/). These policies do different jobs and are not interchangeable.

<h2 id="bank">Should you buy mortgage protection from your bank?</h2>

**You are free to shop around.** The CCPC explicitly states that a mortgage lender cannot insist you purchase mortgage protection from it, as long as an alternative policy meets the lender's conditions.

A bank may offer an individual policy or access to a **group scheme**. The latter can be convenient, but the lender owns the group policy and you may not be able to carry it to another lender when switching mortgages.

Compare the paperwork, not the salesperson's convenience claim. Ask:

1. Who actually underwrites and owns the policy?
2. Is the premium guaranteed or reviewable?
3. Is the policy transferable to a new mortgage lender?
4. Will it terminate automatically if the mortgage is switched or repaid?
5. What is the exact sum-assured reduction schedule?
6. What happens if one or both borrowers die?

A bank mortgage adviser may be able to arrange the cover quickly, but there is no reason to assume that makes it the lowest-cost option.

<h2 id="switch">Can you switch mortgage protection insurance and save money?</h2>

**Yes, in many circumstances.** You can compare your existing premium with the cost of new cover. But switching becomes more complicated as you age, your health changes or the existing policy contains features no longer easily available.

### If you are switching mortgage lender

An **individual mortgage protection policy** can often be reassigned to a new lender if the cover amount and remaining term still meet the new loan's requirements. A bank's group policy, by contrast, may end with that bank's mortgage.

Our [mortgage switching guide](/wealth/is-switching-your-mortgage-worth-it-ireland/) covers the mortgage-rate calculation, while this guide addresses the separate insurance decision.

### If you are switching insurance provider

Follow this sequence:

1. Ask your current provider for your policy schedule, insured amounts, end date and any additional benefits.
2. Obtain quotes for equivalent cover using your **current** age and medical circumstances.
3. Declare medical and lifestyle information accurately and complete any underwriting.
4. Get **written acceptance** from the new insurer, and lender approval/assignment arrangements where needed.
5. Confirm the new policy's effective date and that there will be **no gap**.
6. **Only then** cancel the original policy, if replacement is appropriate.

Do not cancel a valuable existing policy merely because a comparison site initially shows a lower price. The final quote after underwriting can be higher or cover can be declined.

### What if you top up, extend or overpay your mortgage?

- **Top-up:** existing cover may be insufficient; you may need to increase it or take a separate top-up policy.
- **Longer term:** the insurance may expire before the extended mortgage ends.
- **Overpayments:** the debt may reduce faster than insured cover; review but avoid automatic cancellation.
- **Paid off early:** you may be able to cancel, or potentially keep eligible individual cover for its original term depending on policy type.

Our [Mortgage Overpayment Calculator](/mortgage-overpayment-calculator/) lets you model the effect of paying extra towards the mortgage itself.

<h2 id="medical">Medical conditions, underwriting and cancer survivors in 2026</h2>

Most new policies involve health and lifestyle questions. Depending on your answers, the insurer may request GP information, a medical examination or additional evidence. A medical history does not automatically mean you cannot be insured, but it can affect price, exclusions and the time to acceptance.

**Answer every question accurately and fully.** Omissions or misstatements can cause disputes over claims. If you have an existing policy and a new health condition, preserving existing accepted cover can be particularly important.

### Right to Be Forgotten: new legal rules starting 15 October 2026

Ireland's **Insurance (Disregard of Certain Medical History and Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 2026** introduces statutory protections for eligible cancer survivors in mortgage protection underwriting. As at this article's review date, **9 October 2026, the key provisions are scheduled to commence on 15 October 2026 — they have not yet commenced**.

Under the announced statutory framework, insurers must disregard qualifying cancer-related history for a mortgage protection application where all the relevant conditions are met:

- The applicant has **completed active cancer treatment at least five years earlier**.
- The applicant has been in **complete remission for at least five years**.
- The application relates to a mortgage on the applicant's **principal private residence**.
- The mortgage protection sum assured is **no more than €650,000** under the threshold rules.

The new statutory framework supersedes reliance on the older voluntary approach for applications covered by the law. The **five-year** period is a major change from the standard **seven-year** period in the earlier industry code. It does not remove ordinary underwriting of unrelated medical factors, nor does it mean that every applicant automatically receives insurance.

If you are a cancer survivor applying around the commencement date, ask the prospective insurer or regulated broker **which rules apply to the timing of your application** and request a written explanation if you believe the criteria have not been followed.

Read the [Law Society Gazette's commencement explanation](https://production.lawsociety.ie/gazette/top-stories/2026/august/date-set-for-right-to-be-forgotten-law/) and the [Government's policy announcement](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-finance/press-releases/t%C3%A1naiste-and-minister-troy-provide-update-on-right-to-be-forgotten-legislation/).

### What if you cannot get cover?

Ask insurers or regulated brokers whether alternative underwriting, individual single-life policies, existing acceptable cover or the statutory exemption route could be relevant. Your lender must consider its own lending conditions. If you believe a financial provider has treated you unfairly, first use its complaint process and then consider the [Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman](https://www.fspo.ie/make-a-complaint/how-to-make-a-complaint-to-your-provider/financial-services-provider/).

<h2 id="claims">What happens when a mortgage protection claim is made?</h2>

An eligible claimant, personal representative or surviving borrower should contact the insurer or broker as soon as practicable. The insurer will usually ask for a completed claim form, proof of death and relevant policy/identity documents.

Where the policy is assigned to a lender, the claim is ordinarily directed towards the secured mortgage first. Any benefit beyond the loan depends on the form of cover and legal arrangements. The insurer checks whether the claim meets its policy conditions before paying.

It is sensible to keep the policy number, insurer contact details and the lender assignment documents somewhere your partner or executor can locate them. Our [How to Make a Will in Ireland guide](/wealth/how-to-make-a-will-ireland/) explains the wider estate-planning paperwork.

<h2 id="mistakes">Seven mortgage protection mistakes worth avoiding</h2>

1. **Buying from the lender without comparison.** Convenience can be useful, but there is no legal obligation to use the bank's chosen insurer.
2. **Comparing a joint-life price with a dual-life price.** These are not identical benefits.
3. **Taking the shortest cover term to reduce the premium.** Cover may expire before the mortgage does.
4. **Assuming every illness triggers a claim.** Ordinary mortgage protection covers death, not a general inability to work.
5. **Replacing an old policy before underwriting is complete.** You could lose valuable existing cover.
6. **Ignoring mortgage changes.** Top-ups, term extensions and interest-rate assumptions can create a gap between cover and debt.
7. **Leaving it until closing week.** Unexpected underwriting requests can delay drawdown.

<h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2>

### What is the cheapest mortgage protection insurance in Ireland?

There is no universally cheapest company. The least expensive final quote depends on the insured people, sum assured, term, underwriting and features. For a standard repayment mortgage, **decreasing-term life cover** will usually be cheaper than equivalent level-term cover, but obtain competing quotations.

### How much is mortgage protection on a €300,000 mortgage?

It depends heavily on age, smoker status, health, term and single/joint/dual life structure. A mortgage amount alone does not produce a reliable premium. Ask at least two providers or a multi-agency broker for identical €300,000 cover terms. Treat public 'from' prices as marketing entry points, not an estimate for your circumstances.

### Is mortgage protection compulsory for first-time buyers?

Generally yes if the mortgage is for your principal home, with statutory exceptions. Your lender will normally request evidence of acceptable cover before drawdown.

### Can I choose my own mortgage protection provider?

Yes. The CCPC states that lenders cannot force borrowers to purchase mortgage protection from the lender. The external policy must satisfy lender requirements.

### Is dual-life better than joint-life cover?

Dual life can provide broader independent protection for both borrowers, but may cost more depending on provider and policy. Evaluate the premium, payout rules and assignment. Some providers offer dual life at the same quoted cost as joint life under defined conditions.

### Can I switch mortgage protection while staying with the same bank?

Potentially yes. Arrange and have the replacement accepted before cancelling your existing policy, and confirm what the bank needs for assignment or evidence.

### What happens to mortgage protection if I switch banks?

An individual policy may be reassigned if the sum and term still fit the mortgage; a lender-owned group arrangement generally does not transfer. Confirm the arrangements before switching.

### Does mortgage protection pay out if I get cancer?

**Ordinary life-only mortgage protection does not pay merely because you receive a cancer diagnosis.** A separate specified serious illness benefit may respond to certain diagnosed conditions under precise policy definitions. The new cancer-survivor legislation concerns **access to mortgage protection underwriting**, not automatic illness claim payments.

### Can I get mortgage protection after cancer?

Depending on medical circumstances and the applicable underwriting rules, yes. New statutory protections for eligible cancer survivors are scheduled to take effect on **15 October 2026**, subject to criteria including two five-year periods, main-home use and a €650,000 threshold.

### Can mortgage protection be cancelled once my loan is repaid?

Often yes, although some individual policies can remain useful as life cover depending on their terms. Check what happens before cancelling.

### Is mortgage protection tax deductible in Ireland?

For most private homeowners, ordinary mortgage protection premiums are a personal expense rather than a standard income-tax deduction. Do not confuse them with tax relief potentially available for qualifying income-protection policies or with separate mortgage-interest rules.

### Can I use an existing life insurance policy instead?

Possibly, if the policy has adequate cover and is suitable for assignment to the lender, and if the lender accepts it. Check whether its sum insured, remaining term and ownership meet the relevant requirements.

## The bottom line

For most Irish homebuyers, a mortgage protection policy is necessary. But **the bank's suggested policy is not your only option**, and choosing between joint and dual life can matter as much as choosing the lowest premium.

Get like-for-like quotes, review the complete payout and reduction rules, disclose medical information accurately, and never let old cover lapse before replacement is securely in force.
