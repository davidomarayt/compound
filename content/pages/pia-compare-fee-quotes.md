A €5 monthly account fee is €60 a year. A 0.40% annual platform fee also costs €60 at a €15,000 balance. Either could be the cheaper offer for you, depending on how much you hold and how often you trade.

## A worked fee comparison

These are invented prices for comparison, with a constant balance for one year. Both examples assume a fund charge of 0.20% a year and zero dealing, advice and foreign-exchange costs. No provider has supplied these quotes. The figures show fees before investment account tax.

| Balance | A: €5/month plus fund cost | B: 0.40% plus fund cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| €5,000 | €70 | €30 |
| €15,000 | €90 | €90 |
| €50,000 | €160 | €300 |

At €50,000 the common fund charge is €100. Add €60 for A or €200 for B. At €5,000 the fixed charge alone consumes 1.2% of the account. That is why comparing percentages at just one balance can give a new investor the wrong impression.

## What changes if you buy monthly?

Suppose A also charges €2 per purchase and B includes purchases. Twelve trades add €24 to A. The break-even balance rises from €15,000 to **€21,000**: €84 divided by 0.004. One trade a year under the same terms would put it at €15,500. Use the dealing pattern you actually expect.

## Why fee transparency became a Finance Bill issue

An important development since the original fee example: [committee amendments to Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026, dated 3 November](https://data.oireachtas.ie/ie/oireachtas/bill/2026/98/dail/3/amendment/numberedList/eng/b9826d-dcnl.pdf#page=9), included proposed **amendment 25**. It would have required providers, before an individual investment account opened and then annually, to show all account and underlying-investment fees in euro and percentage terms, along with illustrations of their cumulative effect over 10 and 20 years. Proposed **amendment 32** separately addressed reporting annual costs in euro and percentage terms.

This is **not an enacted PIA-specific disclosure rule**. At the [5 November select-committee debate](https://voterconnect.ie/proceedings/select_committee_on_finance_public_expenditure_public_service_reform_and_digitalisation_and_taoiseach/2026-11-05/dbsect_2/part-2), the Minister said he did not propose to accept these amendments. The recorded division on amendment 25 was **4 in favour, 6 against**, so it was lost at that stage. Other amendments' final outcomes and the Finance Bill's final enacted wording were not independently established in this check. Existing investment disclosure obligations may still apply, depending on the provider and product; do not mistake the rejected PIA-specific proposal for a guarantee of a universal standard fee sheet.

For shoppers, the practical consequence is that **you still need comparable all-in figures** from each prospective provider. A bank saying it intends to offer an account is not a fee quote. As at the 16 November source check, the five providers in [Compound's pre-launch tracker](/pia/providers/) had no verified complete PIA-specific fee schedules.

## What a fee difference can cost over 20 years

Take a hypothetical **€10,000** invested once, with a constant **6% gross annual return**, no further contributions, and no currency, advice or trading charges. Compare an all-in annual investment/provider cost of **0.25%** with **1.00%**. Both are illustrative assumptions, **not actual PIA provider prices**.

| Years | 0.25% annual cost | 1.00% annual cost | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10 | €17,466 | €16,196 | €1,270 |
| 20 | €30,505 | €26,231 | €4,274 |

Method: at each year-end, multiply the prior value by **1.06**, then by **(1 − annual fee rate)**; repeat for 10 or 20 years. These are smooth annual illustrations, not forecasts. Both balances remain below €50,000 throughout, so the *proposed* State investment-account tax would be zero under this example. Actual PIA tax, if enacted, is calculated using average daily values and is **separate** from provider fees. Real fund charges, dealing frequency, market movements and timing can change the result.

At 20 years, that apparently small **0.75-percentage-point annual fee gap** makes the higher-cost outcome roughly **€4,274 lower** on identical assumed investments. The calculation isolates price; it does not establish that either provider offers the same investment range, advice or services.

## What to copy from a provider’s price list

Record the effective date, platform minimum, account fee, custody fee, fund or ETF ongoing charge for your exact share class, dealing fee, currency conversion spread, advice charge and any transfer or exit fee. Request the **euro amount and percentage** for the *same* illustrative holding and trading pattern, and ask whether a quoted all-in price already includes the fund charge before adding it. Check for minimum annual charges, tiered rates and any one-off costs. Where a price is missing, mark it **unknown**, not zero. Re-run the comparison at the balance you might have in five or ten years, not only the balance you have today.
