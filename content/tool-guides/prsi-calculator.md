## What this calculator is for

PRSI funds access to Ireland's social-insurance system. This calculator estimates employee PRSI under the 2026 Class A assumptions implemented in the tool.

## How to read the result

PRSI is not simply another Income Tax band. Liability depends on PRSI class, weekly earnings and credits/thresholds. The annual estimate is built from weekly-equivalent earnings.

## Worked example

Two employees with the same annual salary can have similar annual PRSI, but payroll treatment works through weekly or pay-period rules rather than a simple annual flat-rate multiplication.

## Irish context

Most private-sector employees are commonly in Class A, but other PRSI classes exist. Rates and thresholds can change, and employer PRSI is separate from the employee deduction shown here.

## What can change the answer

PRSI class, weekly earnings, employment type, multiple employments and changes in rates during the year can all affect the actual deduction.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not assume everyone is Class A. Do not confuse employee PRSI with the employer's separate PRSI cost. And do not use an annual percentage shortcut around weekly thresholds where the payroll rules are more detailed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does my employer also pay PRSI?
Usually yes, but that is a separate employer cost.

### Is PRSI the same as USC?
No.

### Why can weekly earnings matter?
Class A thresholds and credits operate on a pay-period basis.

### Does this decide my PRSI class?
No.

## Method and limitations

The tool converts annual salary to weekly earnings and applies the implemented 2026 Class A employee rates/credit structure across the relevant parts of the year. It does not cover every PRSI class or payroll edge case.
