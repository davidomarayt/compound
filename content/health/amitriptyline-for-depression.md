## At a glance

Amitriptyline is a tricyclic antidepressant. This guide focuses on depression. Its use for pain and migraine has a different treatment goal, so instructions and the way progress is assessed may differ. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/amitriptyline-for-depression/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### A tricyclic antidepressant and its different receptor effects

It inhibits transporters that normally move noradrenaline and serotonin back into nerve cells. It also blocks several other receptors, including muscarinic and histamine H1 receptors. Those additional actions help explain effects such as dry mouth, constipation and drowsiness. Early receptor effects and the slower changes involved in mood improvement happen on different timescales. [DailyMed source](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4132f91e-6f24-476a-9028-bab86ad0598e)

## What the evidence measures

At follow-up, include sleep, appetite, ability to do daily tasks and mood. Better sleep alone can reflect sedation. It helps to describe which symptoms changed and when rather than score the medicine on one evening.

## A before-and-after mood snapshot

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Before review | write down sleep, mood and daily functioning |
| At review | separate improvement from daytime sedation, dry mouth or constipation |


## Does getting sleepy mean that the depression treatment is working?

Sleepiness reflects one of the medicine’s receptor effects and may start early. Mood, motivation and function need their own assessment. Mention any impairment when driving or carrying out safety-sensitive work.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Give the prescriber the full list of medicines, including sleep aids and products that affect heart rhythm. Severe drowsiness, collapse or suspected overdose needs emergency assessment. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/amitriptyline-for-depression/)
