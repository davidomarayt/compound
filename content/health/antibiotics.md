## At a glance

Antibiotics treat particular bacterial infections. Choosing one depends on the likely organism, site of infection, local resistance and the person’s allergy and medical history. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antibiotics/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### How bacterial targets and resistance shape treatment

Some antibiotics block the enzymes that build bacterial cell walls. Others interfere with bacterial ribosomes, nucleic acid synthesis or metabolic pathways. Mutations and genes acquired from other bacteria can change a target, reduce the drug concentration inside the cell or allow an enzyme to inactivate a drug. Treatment therefore depends on which organism is present as well as what the drug can reach. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antibiotics/)

## What the evidence measures

Evidence for one infection and one regimen cannot simply be carried over to another. Cultures can refine an initial decision when appropriate. Improvement in symptoms, resolution of infection and side effects all matter, and unnecessary exposure can encourage selection of resistant bacteria.

## Why two infections may need different treatment

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Site | ask whether the antibiotic reaches the infected tissue |
| Organism | organism and susceptibility can change the preferred drug |


## What does a laboratory susceptibility report tell us?

It describes how the tested organism responded under standard laboratory conditions. Clinicians also consider dose, infection site, achievable exposure, allergies and whether the tested organism caused the illness.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Follow the specific prescription. Keep a record of any previous reaction and describe what happened rather than simply listing an allergy. Facial or throat swelling, breathing difficulty or collapse needs emergency help. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antibiotics/)
