## At a glance

Low-dose aspirin is an antiplatelet medicine. The balance of benefit and harm depends strongly on the reason it was prescribed, including whether someone has already had a vascular event. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/low-dose-aspirin/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Why aspirin changes clotting and bleeding together

Platelets use cyclo-oxygenase-1, also called COX-1, to make thromboxane A2. This signal helps recruit additional platelets. Aspirin irreversibly acetylates platelet COX-1. Platelets cannot replace the enzyme as readily as cells with a nucleus, so the effect persists in affected platelets as the body gradually makes new ones. [DailyMed source](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cc5db55a-a2b0-48f4-802b-9e5e94fd9ceb)

## What the evidence measures

For people with established vascular disease, evidence is often about preventing another event. For people without it, the starting risk differs and serious bleeding must be weighed against any reduction in vascular events. Look for absolute risks over a stated period when comparing studies.

In ASPREE, 19,114 generally healthy older adults without established cardiovascular disease were followed for a median of 4.7 years. The rates of cardiovascular events were 10.7 with aspirin and 11.3 with placebo per 1,000 person-years. The difference was not statistically clear. Major bleeding rates were 8.6 and 6.2 per 1,000 person-years respectively. A person-year measures time under observation, so these rates are not the same as five-year risks per 1,000 people. The trial addressed prevention of a first event in this group. [ASPREE trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6289056/)

## Two different prevention questions

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| After an event | what is my reason for ongoing antiplatelet treatment? |
| Before any event | how would my baseline risk and bleeding history affect the decision? |


## Why do prevention studies report bleeding and vascular events separately?

The medicine can change both outcomes. A smaller probability of a vascular event must be weighed against the probability and severity of bleeding in the same population and timeframe.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Mention other antiplatelet or anticoagulant medicines, and check before adding an anti-inflammatory painkiller. Vomiting blood or black, tarry stools needs urgent assessment. Check with the prescribing team before changing treatment. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/low-dose-aspirin/)
