## At a glance

Paxlovid combines nirmatrelvir and ritonavir for eligible people with COVID-19. Eligibility, timing and interaction checks depend on the current Irish pathway and the person’s medicines and medical history. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/paxlovid/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Blocking a viral protease and slowing drug breakdown

SARS-CoV-2 makes long protein chains that its main protease cuts into working proteins. Nirmatrelvir inhibits that protease. Ritonavir inhibits CYP3A and slows breakdown of nirmatrelvir, maintaining exposure. It also changes exposure to other drugs processed through that pathway, which is why the interaction check is central. [EMA source](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/paxlovid)

## What the evidence measures

Antiviral trials often measure hospitalisation or death in people at higher risk. Absolute benefit depends on baseline risk, population and when treatment starts. Results should be read in the context of the population studied.

## Have two lists ready for a treatment call

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Illness | symptom onset date and relevant medical history |
| Medicines | all regular and recently used prescriptions, products and supplements |


## Why do clinicians ask about recently stopped medicines?

Some medicines can continue affecting drug metabolism after the last dose. Provide names and dates so the team can make the interaction and eligibility assessment.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Ask a clinician or pharmacist to screen the whole medicine list and check kidney function. Do not stop co-medications or decide eligibility on the basis of a web page. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/paxlovid/)
