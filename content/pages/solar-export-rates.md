<section class="sol-article-grid"><article><h2>How solar export payments work</h2><p>Ireland's Clean Export Guarantee pays eligible microgenerators for metered kWh sent to the grid. Your installer submits ESB Networks connection notification (NC6 or NC7 as appropriate); the supplier then receives the relevant export data. The payment normally appears as a credit on your electricity bill or in a separate statement.</p><p>Use <a href="https://www.cru.ie/consumer-information/microgeneration/">CRU's microgeneration guide</a> for the official process.</p></article><article><h2>Compare more than the export figure</h2><p>A higher export tariff can be offset by a higher import rate or standing charge. Ask whether export remuneration requires you to buy electricity from the same supplier, whether rates are fixed, and when the contract or any introductory discount expires.</p><p>For storage decisions, use the <a href="/solar/batteries/">battery economics tool</a> to consider export payments you would forgo.</p></article></section>
<div class="sol-sourcebox"><h2>Get your own data</h2><p>ESB Networks' customer portal provides metered import/export information including half-hour interval data where available. Check your annual usage, solar yield assumptions and current supply contract before accepting a supplier offer. Where you have a smart time-of-use tariff, a single blended import rate is only an approximation.</p></div>
