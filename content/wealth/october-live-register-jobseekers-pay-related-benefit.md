Ireland recorded **5,077 more Jobseeker’s Pay-Related Benefit claims** in October than a year earlier. Over the same period, claims for the older Jobseeker’s Benefit fell by 4,846. The [CSO’s 6 November release](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-lr/liveregisteroctober2026/) explains why the two series have moved apart. A new scheme began on 31 March 2025 for people becoming fully unemployed, so the mix of claims is still changing.

Those extra claims cannot be read as 5,077 redundancies. The seasonally adjusted Live Register across schemes rose by 400 in the month to 172,800. For a worker who has just lost a job, the more useful figure for the household budget is how much of their former pay the scheme can replace.

## What happens on former gross pay of €700 a week?

Consider an eligible employee with five or more years of paid PRSI and average reckonable gross pay of €700 a week. Under the [payment rules](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2024/si/635/made/en/), the weekly caps start to matter as the claim continues.

| Claim period | Calculation | Weekly benefit before any personal tax |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Weeks 1–13 | 60% × €700 = €420, below the €450 cap | €420 |
| Weeks 14–26 | 55% × €700 = €385, capped at €375 | €375 |
| Weeks 27–39 | 50% × €700 = €350, capped at €300 | €300 |

By week 27 this person receives €120 less each week than at the start. An eligible worker with two years but fewer than five years of paid contributions has a different schedule. On the same €700 average, the 50% rate is capped at €300 a week for up to 26 weeks. The Department uses an earnings reference period, so a single final payslip can give the wrong estimate.

## Which payment should you apply for?

Pay-related benefit requires full unemployment and a qualifying PRSI record, including recent contributions. Someone whose hours have been cut may have a different route. Use the [MyWelfare eligibility check](https://services.mywelfare.ie/en/topics/out-of-work-payments/apply-for-jobseekers-payment/) or contact an Intreo centre. The Department pays the benefit without deducting tax, so the amount received can differ from the final after-tax position. [Department payment guidance](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/jobseekers-pay-related-benefit/).

If you are receiving a redundancy lump sum, keep it separate from weekly income when planning how long your savings will last. Compound’s [Irish redundancy guide](/wealth/redundancy-ireland-statutory-ex-gratia-tax/) covers termination payments.
