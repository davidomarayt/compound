## At a glance

This guide covers corticosteroid medicines used to control inflammation or replace hormones in specific conditions. Preparations include tablets, inhalers, injections, sprays and creams. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroids/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Anti-inflammatory receptors and why the route changes exposure

Corticosteroids bind intracellular receptors and alter gene regulation. Glucocorticoid effects can reduce inflammation. The degree of exposure in the rest of the body depends on route, dose, strength, duration and other medicines. Longer systemic exposure can suppress the body’s own cortisol production. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroids/)

## What the evidence measures

Evidence and monitoring are route- and disease-specific. A nasal symptom score, lung flare-up, skin response or improvement in a systemic inflammatory condition refers to a different outcome.

## Start with two pieces of information

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Target | the tissue or condition being treated |
| Exposure | list every steroid tablet, inhaler, injection, cream and spray |


## What should go on a complete steroid list?

Include creams, sprays, inhalers and injections as well as tablets. Add the timing, reason for use and whether the product is ongoing or finished.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Ask which stopping instructions apply and whether you need a steroid card. Check both prescribed and over-the-counter steroid products at a medicine review. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroids/)

## What changes between a tablet, an injection and a cream?

A tablet reaches much of the body through the circulation. An injection around a joint can target a particular area, although some exposure can reach the rest of the body. A cream reaches skin tissue, with absorption varying by area, strength and how it is applied. Inhalers and sprays depend on deposition at the target surface. These differences change the expected benefit and which adverse effects are worth watching for. [HSE steroid guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroids/)

## Ask how the total exposure is assessed

List ongoing and recently finished prescriptions across different clinics. Include when the last injection was given and how often you use each inhaler, spray or cream. Ask which treatments could contribute to adrenal suppression, whether you have a written plan for serious illness, and what instructions apply before surgery. [HSE steroid tablet guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-tablets/)
