The mortgage rate matters.

So do the rules attached to it.

A lower headline rate can be less attractive if it comes with:

- limited overpayments;
- expensive early repayment;
- a short introductory period;
- poor follow-on options;
- or a cashback offer that distracts from the total cost.

The right comparison is not just **rate versus rate**.

It is **total cost + flexibility + certainty**.

## Fixed-rate mortgages

A fixed rate sets the mortgage interest rate for a defined period.

That can give you:

- predictable repayments;
- protection from rate increases during the fixed period;
- easier household budgeting.

The trade-off is reduced flexibility.

The CCPC notes that breaking a fixed rate early can trigger an early repayment or breakage charge.

That can matter if you:

- sell;
- switch lender;
- make a large lump-sum repayment;
- or change rate before the fixed period ends.

## Variable-rate mortgages

A variable rate can change over time.

The lender sets the rate.

It does not have to move one-for-one with European Central Bank policy rates.

Potential advantages can include:

- greater overpayment flexibility;
- easier switching;
- no fixed-rate break cost in some cases.

Potential disadvantages:

- repayments can rise;
- future cost is less certain;
- and the lender's pricing can change independently.

## Fixed versus variable at a glance

Neither is automatically cheaper over the life of the mortgage.

You are choosing which risk you prefer to carry.

## What happens when a fixed rate ends?

Your mortgage does not end.

The fixed-rate period ends.

The CCPC says lenders must give at least **60 days' notice** before the end of a fixed period and set out available options.

If you do nothing, you may move to the lender's default follow-on rate.

That may not be the best available rate.

Put the fixed-rate end date in your calendar months in advance.

## Mortgage term

The term can matter as much as the rate.

A longer term:

- reduces the monthly repayment;
- but usually increases total interest.

A shorter term:

- increases the monthly repayment;
- but can materially reduce lifetime interest.

Example:

A €300,000 mortgage over 35 years can look much more affordable each month than the same mortgage over 25 years.

But you are borrowing the money for ten extra years.

Use the [Mortgage Calculator](/mortgage-calculator/) to compare the same loan at different terms.

## Do not choose a term only to maximise approval

A lender may approve a longer term because it lowers the tested monthly repayment.

That does not mean you should automatically keep that term for decades.

A sensible approach can be:

- choose a payment you can safely afford;
- preserve emergency cash;
- then use permitted overpayments later if appropriate.

But check fixed-rate overpayment limits first.

## Overpayment flexibility

Mortgage overpayments can reduce:

- principal;
- future interest;
- and possibly the mortgage term.

But fixed-rate products may limit how much you can repay without a charge.

The exact allowance is lender/product specific.

Before signing, ask:

> How much can I overpay each year without a break fee?

Then get the answer in the product terms.

Use the [Mortgage Overpayment Calculator](/mortgage-overpayment-calculator/) to see what regular or lump-sum overpayments could do.

## Break fees

A break fee is not a fixed penalty table in every case.

The CCPC explains that early repayment charges on fixed-rate mortgages relate to the lender's financial loss and can change with market conditions.

A fee could be:

- zero;
- modest;
- or significant.

If you may move, sell or receive a large lump sum during the fixed period, flexibility has real value.

## Cashback

Cashback can be useful when buying because cash is tight.

But it is not free money.

A mortgage offering €5,000 cashback can still cost more if the interest rate is higher for long enough.

The CCPC recommends comparing:

- total cost over the fixed period;
- and total cost over the full mortgage term.

Do not compare only:

**cashback today** versus **no cashback today**.

Compare:

**cashback - extra interest - fees = economic value.**

## Lower LTV can improve pricing

Mortgage rates are often priced in Loan-to-Value bands.

For example, a lender may have different rates at:

- 90% LTV;
- 80% LTV;
- 70% LTV;
- 60% LTV.

A larger deposit can sometimes move you into a lower-rate band.

But do not empty your emergency reserve solely to cross an LTV threshold without calculating the actual saving.

## Green mortgages

Some lenders offer lower rates for homes meeting specified BER criteria.

If you are buying an energy-efficient home or new build, check:

- qualifying BER;
- rate period;
- whether the discount applies only initially;
- what evidence is required.

A green label is useful only if the actual mortgage economics are competitive.

## APRC

The Annual Percentage Rate of Charge is designed to help compare the cost of credit.

It incorporates more than the headline interest rate.

But it still relies on assumptions about how long rates remain in place.

For a mortgage with a short fixed rate followed by a variable assumption, the APRC is not a prediction of future interest rates.

Use it as a comparison tool, not a forecast.

## Rate on the day of drawdown

The CCPC notes that the mortgage interest rate can be set on the day funds are drawn down.

That means the rate on your early approval may not be the rate that ultimately applies.

Ask your lender or broker:

- what rate is currently reserved, if any;
- what happens if rates fall before drawdown;
- what happens if rates rise;
- and when the final rate becomes binding.

## Stress the mortgage

Before choosing, model:

- today's rate;
- +1 percentage point;
- +2 percentage points.

This matters even if you fix now.

A 30-year mortgage will probably go through multiple rate environments.

Your home should remain affordable after the first fixed period.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a fixed mortgage safer?

It provides repayment certainty during the fixed period, but can reduce flexibility and may have early-repayment charges.

### Can I overpay a fixed mortgage?

Often yes within product-specific limits. Check the exact allowance and break-fee rules.

### Is mortgage cashback worth it?

Sometimes, but compare the full cost. A higher rate can outweigh upfront cashback.

### Should I take the longest mortgage term possible?

Not automatically. A longer term lowers the monthly payment but typically increases total interest.
