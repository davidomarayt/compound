## Start with the three bills that can decide the budget

Get the college’s fee statement, the accommodation offer and an estimate of travel for a normal teaching week. Put the fee statement and the accommodation agreement side by side. Mark the dates when payments are due. The cheapest option over the year can still need the most money before the first week of term. If you are comparing two colleges, use the same assumptions about meals, travel home and the number of months in each budget.

If you are living at home, add what you would contribute towards board and what you would actually spend getting to campus. Include the added travel for early lectures, evening labs and placements. If you are renting, use your own share of rent, utilities and transport. For digs, ask how many nights you have each week, whether meals and bills are included, and what happens during exams, reading week and placements. Count only the food and utilities you pay separately.

## How Irish college fees and SUSI affect the total

The Higher Education Authority lists a **€2,350 student contribution for eligible Free Fees Initiative students in 2026/27**. The institution assesses eligibility. The contribution can differ from the full amount shown on a college bill once separate charges or applicable tuition are included. Use the bill for your course and the year you are budgeting for. The opening figure in this calculator illustrates the published contribution. [HEA Free Fees Initiative](https://hea.ie/funding-governance-performance/funding/student-finance/course-fees/).

SUSI fee grants and maintenance grants do different jobs. SUSI pays the **fee grant to the college**. Use it to reduce the eligible fee you entered. If the fee shown on your bill already reflects the grant, do not deduct it again. SUSI says maintenance generally goes to the student’s bank account in **nine instalments**, subject to confirmation of the award and college registration and attendance. Count confirmed annual maintenance in the academic-year budget. Check the first payment date before relying on it for a deposit or other amount due before classes. [SUSI payment guidance](https://www.susi.ie/payments/).

Grant eligibility depends on the applicable rules. If an application is pending, run a budget using zero support and save it. Run a second scenario with a possible award if that helps you plan, then replace it with the confirmed amount once you have your letter. Use the official [undergraduate award tables](https://www.susi.ie/eligibility-criteria/income/full-time-undergraduate-income-thresholds-and-grant-award-rates/) for the right year and course type.

## Compare commuting, renting and digs

Start with the actual length of the accommodation contract. An academic-year licence might include different dates from a private rental. For a fixed total, record the full contracted rent and divide by the number of months you enter for an equivalent monthly amount. Use Advanced mode if you pay rent for a different number of months from your term living costs. If you will keep paying rent over part of the summer, include those months even if college has finished.

Ask what is included in rent. Some offers cover utilities and internet. Other offers leave heat, electricity, broadband or laundry as separate charges. If there is a long commute, check what happens on placement days and whether public transport is available early or late enough. Budget for the travel you will really make, including regular visits home if you move.

DCU’s 2026/27 fact sheet illustrates Dublin rent of **€800 to €1,200 per month** in its sample student budget. That is a dated planning example for Dublin, with separate lines for transport and utilities. Use the amount quoted for your room or digs. [DCU’s 2026/27 fact sheet](https://www.dcu.ie/sites/default/files/inline-files/dcu-fact-sheet-2026-2027_4.pdf).

## Worked example for nine months near college

Suppose rent is €800 a month, other living costs €300 and transport €100. Over nine months the recurring bill is **€10,800**. Add €2,350 in fees and €400 in setup costs to get **€13,550** before support.

A confirmed €1,000 fee grant reduces the fees to €1,350. If there is also €2,000 confirmed in maintenance support, the academic-year cost left to cover is **€10,550**. If €800 rent, an €800 refundable deposit and the net €1,350 fee are all due before term, the upfront figure in this example is **€3,350** including €400 in setup. This is a cash-timing illustration. The deposit is held separately from the academic-year spending figure and maintenance money is not assumed to have arrived.

Save the result for comparison. Then change accommodation to the board you would pay at home, adjust transport to reflect a full commute and enter the appropriate upfront payments. Compare the figures with the time and flexibility each arrangement gives you for attending class and placements.

## Before transferring a deposit

Confirm the address, who you are paying, whether the room is available and what the written agreement says about refunding the deposit. Ask how rent is paid, whose name utility accounts are in and what happens if you need to leave early. Check the relevant rules for the agreement you are signing. The RTB explains the rules for student-specific accommodation, including what can be requested in advance. For digs, get the house rules and payment arrangements in writing, particularly what happens during breaks. [RTB student-specific accommodation guidance](https://rtb.ie/renting/types-of-tenancies/student-specific-accommodation/).

Ask your college about deadlines for fees, hardship support and what to do if your grant is delayed. The HEA says the Student Assistance Fund can help eligible students with certain living costs, such as rent, books, food and essential travel, through their institution. Check with student services before treating any amount as available. [HEA Student Assistance Fund](https://hea.ie/policy/access-policy/student-assistance-fund/).

## Make the budget useful through the year

Write down which figures are confirmed and which could change. Re-run the budget if a room quote changes, a placement adds a commute, or you receive your award letter. If accommodation takes most of the budget, test the alternatives by changing the actual rent, number of months and travel cost together. To set money aside for the gap by a known date, use the [Savings Goal Calculator](/savings-goal-calculator/).

## Method and limitations

The annual budget adds fees, setup costs, accommodation over its entered months and food, bills and travel over the entered living-cost months. Fee support is capped at the bill, and confirmed cash support then reduces the remaining annual cost to a minimum of zero. The monthly result shows recurring costs in a month when both sets of bills are active. Advanced mode estimates cash needed before term as the refundable deposit, the entered rent and net fee amounts due, and setup costs. Rent and fees due upfront are capped at the relevant annual bills. Setup costs are assumed payable upfront. Grants, fees and private agreements vary, so use confirmed numbers.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does this check whether I qualify for SUSI?

Use SUSI to check eligibility and payment dates. The calculator uses the confirmed fee and cash support amounts you enter.

### Why is a refundable deposit outside the annual cost?

It is money you need to have available before moving in. The calculator treats it as refundable and counts it in the upfront figure.

### Can I include a 12-month lease or a summer placement?

Yes. Set the housing months to the period you must pay for and the other months to the period you want to budget for. For substantially different expenses during summer, calculate that period separately.
