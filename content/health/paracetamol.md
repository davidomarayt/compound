**Paracetamol relieves pain and lowers a high temperature.** Adults commonly use it for headaches, toothache, period pain and aches from a cold. Panadol is one brand. The active ingredient on the pack is what matters, especially if you take more than one medicine. [HSE paracetamol guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/paracetamol/)

This guide covers adults in Ireland. Follow the instructions for your own product and ask a pharmacist if anything is unclear. Sources checked 19 November 2026.

> **Think you took too much?** Get advice immediately, even if you feel well. Call your GP or the National Poisons Information Centre on **[01 809 2166](tel:+35318092166)** (8am–10pm daily). Outside those hours, contact a GP or go to an emergency department. Call **112 or 999** or go to an emergency department immediately for a deliberate overdose or if you have vomiting, tummy pain or yellowing of the skin or eyes after taking too much. Bring the packet or leaflet and any remaining medicine. [HSE overdose advice](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/paracetamol/)

## How much paracetamol can adults take?

For standard **500 mg tablets**, the usual adult dose is **one or two tablets**, leaving **at least four hours** between doses. The HSE’s usual adult limit is **four doses and eight 500 mg tablets in any 24 hours**. Follow the instructions for your exact product. If you weigh under 50 kg or have liver or kidney problems, check with your pharmacist or GP before using the standard adult dose. [HSE dosing and precautions](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/paracetamol/)

You can take ordinary paracetamol tablets with or without food. Liquids and hot-drink sachets can come in different strengths, so check the milligrams per dose on the label. For a child, use the [HSE’s separate guidance on children’s paracetamol](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/paracetamol-for-children/).

## Can you take it with cold and flu medicine?

Check the **active ingredients** on both packs. Some cold and flu sachets and combination painkillers already contain paracetamol. Taking a separate paracetamol tablet alongside one of these products can push you over the recommended dose. Ask a pharmacist if you cannot tell what your product contains. [HSE guidance on mixing medicines](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/paracetamol/)

If you take warfarin, ask your pharmacist or GP before using paracetamol regularly, as it can increase bleeding risk. Tell them if you regularly drink above the low-risk alcohol guidelines or have liver or kidney problems. The HSE advises against taking paracetamol and [ibuprofen](/health/medicines/ibuprofen/) at the same time unless your GP advises it. [HSE interactions and cautions](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/paracetamol/)

## What side effects should you watch for?

Side effects are very rare at the recommended dose. Call **112 or 999** if you develop signs of a severe allergic reaction, such as difficulty breathing or swelling of the lips, tongue or throat. Speak to your GP if pain or fever worsens or lasts more than a few days. [HSE side effects and advice](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/paracetamol/)

## Can you take paracetamol during pregnancy?

The HSE says paracetamol can be taken at recommended doses during pregnancy and breastfeeding. Use the lowest dose that helps for the shortest time. Ask a pharmacist or GP if symptoms persist or you need it more often. [HSE pregnancy and breastfeeding guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/paracetamol/)
