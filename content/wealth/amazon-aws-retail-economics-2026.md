Amazon combines businesses that earn money in different ways. A retail sale, a marketplace fee, an advertising placement and a cloud-computing bill should not receive identical assumptions about margins or capital requirements.

For investors, the challenge is to connect those activities to sustainable cash flow. This guide provides a reusable framework; the [Amazon company page](/stocks/amzn/) carries current reported results and the interactive stock chart.

## How does Amazon make money?

Amazon's 2025 annual report presents North America, International and Amazon Web Services as reporting segments. Its activities include direct retail, third-party seller services, subscriptions, advertising and cloud services. Advertising is not a separate reportable profit segment in that structure. [Source: Amazon annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm).

| Activity | How to think about the economics | What not to assume |
|---|---|---|
| First-party retail | Product margin after fulfilment and other costs | All sales dollars have high margins |
| Marketplace services | Fees associated with serving sellers | Marketplace merchandise value equals Amazon revenue |
| Advertising | Monetisation of shopper and advertiser demand | A standalone advertising profit figure exists |
| AWS | Consumption of computing and related services | Revenue growth requires little capital |
| Subscriptions | Repeat customer relationship and included benefits | Subscription revenue is entirely incremental profit |

## Why revenue mix matters so much

If Amazon sells a product itself, recognised revenue and associated product costs differ from a transaction where a third-party seller makes the sale and Amazon earns fees. A shift between these activities can change the relationship between customer spending and Amazon's reported revenue.

The practical implication is to avoid valuing the entire company using a single revenue multiple without examining the mix. Two dollars of revenue can have very different profit and reinvestment characteristics.

Similarly, fulfilment improvements should be assessed against service quality. A cost reduction that weakens the customer proposition may not be sustainable. Ask whether delivery density and infrastructure use are improving enough to support both customer demand and margins.

## AWS: focus on returns as well as growth

Cloud customers pay for resources and services that help them run workloads. From an investment perspective, the important question is whether growth creates attractive returns after building and maintaining the infrastructure.

Compare AWS revenue and operating income over consistent periods, then consider the group cash-flow statement and relevant investment disclosures. Segment operating income is not a standalone free-cash-flow figure.

A **hypothetical sensitivity** shows the importance of margin assumptions. At $100bn of revenue, a 25% operating margin produces $25bn operating profit; a 30% margin produces $30bn. That $5bn difference exists before tax and further investment. Neither assumption here is a forecast of Amazon's results.

## Advertising and the limits of disclosure

Advertising can strengthen the economics of a shopping platform, but investors should avoid inventing a precise profit figure when the company does not separately disclose one. Any estimate needs explicit assumptions about costs and allocation.

There is also a customer-experience trade-off. More monetisation is not automatically better if it makes discovery less useful for shoppers or reduces the attractiveness of the marketplace for sellers. A durable investment case requires the ecosystem to keep creating value for its participants.

When using a sum-of-the-parts valuation, do not value advertising separately and then also include the same profit in a retail segment valuation. Double counting can make an elaborate model less reliable than a simple one.

## Working capital can flatter or depress cash flow

The timing of customer receipts, inventory purchases and supplier payments affects operating cash flow. A growing business may receive cash before paying some associated costs. That timing benefit is useful, but it is not identical to a permanent improvement in operating margins.

Review inventory, payables and other working-capital movements across a full cycle. An unusually strong quarter should not be annualised without understanding seasonality and payment timing.

Also distinguish cash purchases of equipment from equipment obtained through financing arrangements. Read the company's free-cash-flow reconciliation and lease disclosures instead of relying on one headline measure.

## How to value Amazon without false precision

One approach values the group using sustainable future cash generation. Another values major activities separately. Both require discipline: include corporate costs, reconcile financing obligations and use consistent assumptions about capital expenditure.

Start with conservative assumptions for retail margins and cloud reinvestment. Then show what better execution would add. A model that needs exceptional margins in every activity simultaneously is making a more demanding bet than a broad claim that online shopping and cloud computing will grow.

Reported net income can include investment gains or losses. Distinguish those from operating performance before applying an earnings multiple. They may have economic value, but their recurrence and relationship to the core business need separate treatment.

## Risks that deserve an explicit scenario

Retail competition can pressure prices and delivery economics. Cloud competition can affect pricing or the pace of customer spending. Regulation can change marketplace practices. Infrastructure expansion can consume cash for longer than expected. A weakening consumer environment can affect demand and the efficiency of the fulfilment network.

For each risk, ask which model input changes: sales, margin, investment, working capital or the eventual valuation multiple. This is more useful than a generic list with no connection to expected returns.

## Amazon investor checklist

- Read segment revenue and operating income together.
- Separate first-party sales from seller-service economics.
- Treat advertising profitability estimates as estimates.
- Reconcile operating cash flow, cash capex and financing arrangements.
- Inspect working-capital changes and seasonality.
- Exclude non-operating gains from a recurring-profit estimate unless justified.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is AWS the same business as Amazon retail?

No. They have different customers and economics, although both belong to Amazon. Group results combine activities with different margins and investment requirements.

### Why can profit and free cash flow move differently?

Capital investment, working capital, non-cash items and financing arrangements can create differences. Read both statements and the reconciliation.

### Can I value Amazon by adding separate businesses together?

Yes, as an analytical method, but only with consistent costs, financing and no duplicated profit. Separate estimates do not become more certain merely because they are detailed.
