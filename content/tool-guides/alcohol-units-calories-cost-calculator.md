## Ireland uses a 10 g standard drink

In Ireland, **one standard drink contains 10 grams of pure alcohol**. That differs from the UK “unit”, which contains 8 grams.

Compound calculates grams of alcohol from the amount and strength of each drink using the formula in Ireland's Public Health (Alcohol) Act labelling guidance:

**volume in mL × ABV as a decimal × 0.789 = grams of alcohol**

The result is then divided by 10 to give Irish standard drinks.

This is more accurate than assuming “one glass equals one drink”. For example, the HSE notes that a pint of 4.5% beer/stout/cider is roughly **two Irish standard drinks**, while a 100 mL glass of 12.5% wine is roughly one.

## Current HSE weekly low-risk guidance

The HSE currently advises no more than **11 standard drinks per week for women** and **17 for men**, spread across the week, with at least 2–3 alcohol-free days. It also advises not drinking more than **6 standard drinks on any one occasion**.

The HSE explicitly states that **there is no level of alcohol use that is safe for health** and that drinking less reduces risk. It also notes that the evidence for its weekly low-risk guidelines is under review in 2026.

This calculator can compare your weekly total with the current guideline if you choose a comparison group. It cannot determine whether your drinking pattern is safe, because it does not know how drinks are distributed across occasions or your individual health circumstances.

## What the calorie number means

Ethanol provides approximately **7 kcal per gram**. The calculator therefore shows the energy supplied by the alcohol itself.

It deliberately does **not** label that number as the total calories in your drinks. Beer, cider, wine, liqueurs and mixers can contain additional carbohydrate, sugar or other energy. Brand-specific nutrition information is needed for a more complete calorie total.

## Irish alcohol labels and the grams figure

Ireland has legislated for comprehensive alcohol labels that will include the **grams of alcohol** and **energy value** in the product alongside health warnings. The relevant Section 12 labelling requirements are currently scheduled to commence on **3 September 2028**, following the 2025 amendment to the original commencement order.

When those labels are in force, the grams figure will map directly onto Ireland's standard-drink definition: divide grams of alcohol by 10. For example, 17 g of alcohol is 1.7 Irish standard drinks.

## The money calculation is deliberately simple

Enter what you actually spend in a normal week. The annual figure multiplies that by 52. The reduction scenario then applies the percentage you select to both the entered spend and calculated alcohol amount.

It is an illustration of what a sustained change would mean over a year, not a prediction that spending or drinking will remain identical every week.

## What this calculator does not do

It does not estimate blood alcohol concentration, tell you whether you can drive, diagnose alcohol dependence or recommend a personalised “safe” amount.

The HSE warns that even one standard drink can put some people over the legal driving limit. Do not use a drinks calculator to decide whether you are safe to drive.

The HSE also advises avoiding alcohol during pregnancy. If alcohol is causing problems in your life or you are concerned about your intake, use HSE alcohol supports or speak with a healthcare professional.

## Method and limitations

The calculator converts each entered drink into grams of pure alcohol using volume × ABV × 0.789, then divides the total by 10 to express the result in Irish standard drinks. Alcohol-derived calories are estimated at approximately 7 kcal per gram. The annual spending and reduction scenarios simply scale the weekly figures entered by the user.

It does not model drinking pattern across individual days, food intake, body size, medicines, pregnancy, liver function, tolerance, blood alcohol concentration or driving fitness. The HSE guideline comparison is population-level guidance rather than a personalised safety assessment.

## Worked example

A pint is 568 mL. At 4.5% ABV:

**568 × 0.045 × 0.789 ≈ 20.2 g alcohol**, or about **2.0 Irish standard drinks**.

A 175 mL glass of wine at 12.5% ABV contains:

**175 × 0.125 × 0.789 ≈ 17.3 g alcohol**, or about **1.7 Irish standard drinks**.

So two 4.5% pints plus three 175 mL glasses of 12.5% wine would be roughly **9.2 standard drinks** before counting anything else consumed that week.

The example shows why counting glasses or pints alone is unreliable: serving size and ABV both change the amount of alcohol.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is one pint one standard drink?

Usually not. A pint of ordinary-strength beer, stout or cider commonly contains around two Irish standard drinks, depending on ABV.

### Is the Irish standard drink the same as a UK unit?

No. Ireland uses 10 g of pure alcohol per standard drink; a UK unit is 8 g.

### Are the calorie results the total calories in my drink?

No. They represent energy from ethanol itself. Sugars, carbohydrates and mixers can add more.

### Does staying within the weekly guideline make alcohol risk-free?

No. The HSE states that there is no level of alcohol use that is safe for health; lower consumption reduces risk.

### Can I use the money saving as an exact forecast?

It is a simple scenario based on the weekly spend and reduction percentage you enter.
