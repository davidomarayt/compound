## October prices and your household budget

The CSO released October’s Consumer Price Index at 11am on 11 November 2026. Annual inflation reached 4.3%, up from 4.1% in September. Prices increased 0.7% during October. Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels rose 10.7% over the year, while transport rose 9.0%. These figures describe measured price changes across the national basket. Your spending pattern determines how closely your budget follows it. [CSO October CPI](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-cpi/consumerpriceindexoctober2026/)

### A €2,500 budget example

A basket costing €2,500 a month a year ago would cost €2,607.50 after a 4.3% increase. That is an extra €107.50 a month, or €1,290 over twelve months if the higher monthly cost persisted.

The arithmetic is €2,500 × 0.043 = €107.50. This illustration assumes the same purchases and an increase matching the national index. Use your bills to work out your own change.

### Housing can move the result substantially

The CSO’s accompanying housing analysis reports annual increases of 7% in rents and 10.8% in mortgage-interest costs. Private rents rose 5.5% and local-authority rents rose 12.7%. Across five years, rents increased 36% and mortgage-interest costs increased 98.3%. Rents and mortgage interest together contributed 0.76 percentage points to October’s annual inflation rate, calculated as 0.44 + 0.32. [CSO housing-cost analysis](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/is/is-frmic/focusonrentandmortgageinterestcostsoctober2026/)

For illustration, applying 5.5% to an earlier private rent of €1,500 gives €82.50 extra per month. It would take that illustrative payment to €1,582.50. The index measures observed prices across households. Your tenancy’s permitted rent and any review must be established from its own circumstances.

For a mortgage, compare the interest component on statements. An illustrative €400 monthly interest cost increasing by 10.8% becomes €443.20. Repayment statements also contain principal, so this example addresses only interest. It provides no forecast of your lender’s next rate decision.

### Three checks before changing your budget

- Compare this month’s housing and energy bills with the same month last year. Separate price changes from changes in usage.
- Use the rent or mortgage payment actually due when setting a monthly spending limit.
- Test alternative assumptions in the [inflation calculator](https://compound.ie/inflation-calculator/) and [electricity cost calculator](https://compound.ie/electricity-cost-calculator/). Label future rates as your assumptions.
