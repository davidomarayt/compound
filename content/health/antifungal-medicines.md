## At a glance

An antifungal treats infection caused by a susceptible fungus. Creams, local treatments, tablets and hospital infusions reach very different tissues, so the site and type of infection matter. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antifungal-medicines/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Fungal membranes, cell walls and treatment route

A fungal cell membrane relies on ergosterol. Azoles inhibit an enzyme involved in making it, while allylamines affect an earlier step in the pathway. Some hospital antifungals instead block components of the fungal cell wall. Selecting a target that differs from human cell biology is central to how these medicines are designed. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antifungal-medicines/)

## What the evidence measures

Visible skin improvement may begin before the planned course ends. Nail appearance can take much longer to change because a healthy nail has to grow out. For deep or recurrent infections, a sample and identification of the fungus may help guide treatment.

## Match the treatment to the site

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Superficial skin | local contact may be sufficient for a suitable diagnosis |
| Nail or deeper tissue | drug delivery and confirmation of the organism matter |


## Why might nails look abnormal after treatment?

The visible nail reflects older growth as well as newer growth. Ask what rate of change is realistic and when persistent symptoms should prompt another examination or sample.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Oral antifungals can interact with other medicines. If a rash is spreading or symptoms keep returning, seek an assessment and avoid assuming that every similar-looking change is fungal. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antifungal-medicines/)
