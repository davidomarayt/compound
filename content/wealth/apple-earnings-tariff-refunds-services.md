*Research date: 18 October 2026. Reported figures are historical; illustrative calculations are identified below.*

Apple's latest reported June quarter was strong. The more useful investor question is how much of that strength reflects repeatable business performance and how much came from a separately disclosed benefit.

That distinction avoids two mistakes: extrapolating the entire headline growth rate, or dismissing the underlying business because one favourable item helped the result. A careful earnings analysis should do neither.

## The reported result and the disclosed adjustment

Apple's [fiscal third-quarter release](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/07/apple-reports-third-quarter-results/) covers the period ended **27 June 2026**. Revenue was $109.4 billion, up 16% year on year. Diluted earnings per share were $2.02, up 29%, including a $0.11 benefit from tariff refunds. Reported gross margin was 50.1%, including approximately two percentage points from those refunds.

Subtracting the disclosed EPS benefit gives **$1.91**. Against prior-year EPS of $1.57, that implies growth of approximately **21.7%**, compared with approximately 28.7% using the reported figures. This is Compound's limited adjustment, not a complete normalised earnings measure and not an Apple forecast.

| June-quarter EPS comparison | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Reported year-on-year growth | $2.02 ÷ $1.57 − 1 | 28.7% |
| EPS after removing disclosed refund benefit | $2.02 − $0.11 | $1.91 |
| Growth on that limited adjustment | $1.91 ÷ $1.57 − 1 | 21.7% |

The underlying picture remains positive on this narrow test. It simply supports a lower growth rate than the headline figure.

## Services changes the profit mix

The [quarterly SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320193/000032019326000020/aapl-20260627.htm) reports Services revenue of $30.739 billion and Services gross profit of $23.245 billion. Total company revenue was $109.417 billion and total gross profit was $54.770 billion.

Those figures put Services at approximately **28.1% of revenue but 42.4% of gross profit**. The filing gives Services gross margin as 75.6%, compared with 40.1% for Products. These are gross-profit comparisons, not separately reported operating profits after allocating all company expenses.

This is why investors should not treat every additional dollar of Apple sales as economically identical. A change in the balance between hardware and services can matter even when total revenue growth looks similar.

## Why the distinction matters for valuation

Consider two hypothetical businesses with identical current earnings. One needs customers to purchase another expensive device before producing the next profit. The other earns recurring revenue from an existing relationship. Investors might rationally value the second stream differently—but only if customers stay, pricing remains acceptable and the costs of maintaining the service are fully recognised.

Apple combines both mechanisms. The research task is to understand how they reinforce each other without treating recurring revenue as risk-free. A valuable installed base still needs useful products and services to retain it. Higher margins also attract competition and regulatory scrutiny.

The refund adjustment matters for the same reason. If a valuation assumes the full reported growth rate repeats, an investor may be paying for a benefit that is not part of ordinary operating momentum. Removing one disclosed item does not solve the valuation question, but it makes the starting point more disciplined.

## The counterargument to excessive caution

Focusing entirely on an adjustment can obscure the scale of the business improvement. EPS still grows meaningfully in the limited calculation above. The appropriate conclusion is not that the quarter was weak; it is that investors should separate the sources of strength before projecting them forward.

[Reuters' reporting on the results](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/apple-revenue-profits-beat-expectations-iphone-mac-sales-2026-07-30/) also identified supply constraints as an issue. A constraint can mean demand exists that cannot immediately be fulfilled, but it can equally delay revenue and complicate product economics. It should not automatically be interpreted as a positive signal.

## What would strengthen the investment case?

Look for revenue growth accompanied by resilient margins after disclosed unusual benefits are separated out. Track Services' contribution to gross profit, rather than just its revenue growth. Examine whether rising operating expenses consume that incremental gross profit. Avoid using one quarter's favourable mix as a permanent assumption.

The central question is whether Apple can turn an established customer relationship into durable cash generation without relying on temporary financial benefits or an unusually favourable replacement cycle.
