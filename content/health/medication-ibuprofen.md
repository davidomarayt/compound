**Ibuprofen is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)** used to relieve pain, reduce inflammation and lower fever. It is the active ingredient in some Nurofen products, but different formulations can have different directions.

## What is ibuprofen used for?

Depending on the product, ibuprofen may be used for short-term relief of headaches, period pain, dental pain, muscle pain and fever. Check the indications on the leaflet for your specific medicine.

## How does it work?

Ibuprofen reduces the body's production of prostaglandins, chemicals involved in pain, inflammation and fever.

## Who should be careful?

Ibuprofen is not suitable for everyone. Ask a pharmacist or doctor before using it if you have a history of stomach ulcers or gastrointestinal bleeding, kidney disease, heart disease, certain types of asthma, or take blood thinners or other NSAIDs. NSAIDs carry important pregnancy precautions; do not assume they are safe during pregnancy.

## Side effects and warning signs

Possible side effects include indigestion, nausea and stomach discomfort. Stop taking it and seek urgent medical help for symptoms suggesting a serious allergic reaction, vomiting blood, black stools or severe breathing difficulty.

## What dose should I take?

**Follow the patient leaflet for the exact product and strength**, or advice from your pharmacist or prescriber. Dose limits and age restrictions vary. Do not combine ibuprofen with another NSAID unless a clinician directs you to.

## Is ibuprofen the same as paracetamol?

No. Paracetamol relieves pain and fever, while ibuprofen also has anti-inflammatory effects. Their risks and suitability differ. Read our [ibuprofen vs paracetamol comparison](/health/medications/ibuprofen-vs-paracetamol/).
