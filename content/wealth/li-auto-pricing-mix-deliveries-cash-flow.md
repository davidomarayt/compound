**Li Auto, the Chinese maker of family SUVs and electric minivans, had a much better third quarter than second. Margins recovered and it generated cash again. But the improvement came mostly from selling pricier cars, not more of them. Its own guidance now needs a sharp rise in monthly deliveries before the end of December.**

For the quarter to 30 September 2026, Li Auto reported **total revenue of RMB28.8 billion (US$4.3 billion), up 5.4%** from a year earlier and up 12.3% from the second quarter. It delivered **99,964 vehicles, up 7.2%**. It still made an **operating loss of RMB700 million**, but that was much smaller than the RMB2.3 billion loss in the second quarter.

Li Auto reports in renminbi and is listed in New York and Hong Kong. Dollar figures here use the company's own conversion rate of RMB6.7045 to the dollar.

## The margin recovery is about mix

Li Auto's **vehicle margin**, its gross margin on car sales alone, rose to **15.3% from 9.4%** in the second quarter. The company attributes that to "a different product mix", meaning a larger share of more expensive models. It launched the new Li MEGA minivan, priced at RMB509,800, and the Li i9 SUV, at RMB369,800, in September and October.

A simple measure shows the shift. Vehicle sales divided by deliveries came to about **RMB271,000 per car**, up from about RMB245,000 in the second quarter, Compound's calculation. That is still slightly below the RMB277,500 of a year earlier.

The comparison with a year ago is less flattering than it looks. Vehicle margin was **15.3% against 15.5%** a year earlier, and the year-earlier quarter carried estimated costs for a recall of the original Li MEGA. Li Auto says the lower margin this year was mainly due to product mix, partly offset by the absence of those recall costs.

## Volume is the bigger problem

Quarterly deliveries have been stuck at around 100,000 for most of the past year:

- **Q4 2024:** 158,696
- **Q4 2025:** 109,194
- **Q1 2026:** 95,142
- **Q2 2026:** 98,330
- **Q3 2026:** 99,964

Fourth-quarter 2025 deliveries were about **31% below** the same quarter of 2024, Compound's calculation. Losing that volume matters because a carmaker's factories, research and stores are largely fixed costs. Li Auto spent RMB5.5 billion on operating expenses in the third quarter, including RMB3.1 billion on research and development, against gross profit of RMB4.8 billion.

## The fourth-quarter guidance needs a fast ramp

Li Auto expects fourth-quarter **deliveries of 110,000 to 115,000** and **revenue of RMB31.7 billion to RMB33.1 billion**, growth of 10% to 15%.

Its own October update showed **29,025 deliveries**, including more than 3,000 of the new MEGA. To reach the guided range, it would need about **81,000 to 86,000 deliveries in November and December**, or roughly **40,500 to 43,000 a month**. That is **40% to 48% above October's pace**, Compound's calculation. The refreshed 2026 Li i6 only began deliveries in October, so it will contribute more in the last two months. Even so, it is a steep ramp.

The revenue guidance implies about **RMB288,000 of revenue per vehicle** at either end of the range, similar to the third quarter and roughly 9% above the fourth quarter of 2025, Compound's calculation. In other words, the guidance assumes the richer mix holds.

## Cash flow turned positive

Operating cash flow was **RMB3.2 billion**, and after RMB1.3 billion of capital spending, free cash flow was **RMB2.0 billion (US$293 million)**. A year earlier, free cash flow was negative RMB8.9 billion, and in the second quarter it was negative RMB1.3 billion. The company says the improvement on the second quarter came mainly from more cash received from customers at higher selling prices, and on a year earlier from lower payments for inventory.

The balance sheet is large. Li Auto reports a **cash position of RMB88.2 billion (US$13.2 billion)**, a broad measure that includes deposits and some long-term investments. Against that it owes **RMB40.7 billion to suppliers** in trade and notes payable and has about **RMB10.3 billion of borrowings**. That reliance on supplier credit means cash can fall quickly if volumes drop and payments to suppliers catch up.

Li Auto is also returning cash. Under a **US$1.0 billion buyback** announced in March, it says it has bought back about **US$694 million** of shares so far. The weighted average ADS count fell about 3% from a year earlier, Compound's calculation.

## What would confirm or weaken the thesis

The thesis strengthens if deliveries reach the guided 110,000 to 115,000 without heavy discounting, if vehicle margin stays near 15%, and if free cash flow stays positive into 2027 as European deliveries of the Li 6 begin in the Netherlands and Norway.

It weakens if November and December fall short of the roughly 40,000-a-month pace needed, if margins slip back as cheaper models make up more of the mix, or if buybacks continue while the company is still losing money at the operating level.

**Li Auto has shown it can earn a healthy margin on its newer, pricier models. What it has not shown is that it can get back to the volumes it reached in 2024. The next two months of delivery numbers will say a lot about which matters more.** This analysis makes no price target.
