The bidding stage is where a financial plan can become emotional.

The best protection is to decide the maximum price **before** someone else starts bidding against you.

A house can be right for you and still be wrong at the price.

## Start with actual sale prices

Asking prices are marketing.

Completed sale prices are evidence.

Before viewing seriously, compare:

- recent nearby sales;
- similar property types;
- floor area;
- condition;
- BER;
- site size;
- parking;
- extensions;
- school / transport access;
- and whether the comparison was new-build or second-hand.

The Property Price Register is a useful starting point for completed residential transactions.

Do not compare a fully renovated home with a property requiring €100,000 of work simply because both have three bedrooms.

## Build a property scorecard

A viewing can create false urgency.

A scorecard forces you to compare the home with your actual requirements.

<div class="home-checklist">
<div><strong>Location</strong><p>Commute, schools, transport, family and local services.</p></div>
<div><strong>Size</strong><p>Current needs plus likely changes over the next five to ten years.</p></div>
<div><strong>Condition</strong><p>Visible work, likely survey risk and renovation budget.</p></div>
<div><strong>Energy</strong><p>BER, heating system and likely retrofit needs.</p></div>
<div><strong>Site / outdoor space</strong><p>Orientation, drainage, access, boundaries and parking.</p></div>
<div><strong>Ongoing costs</strong><p>Management fees, commuting, heating and maintenance.</p></div>
</div>

Score before bidding, not after winning.

## Decide three prices

Use three numbers.

### 1. Attractive price

The price at which you would feel comfortable buying quickly.

### 2. Fair value to you

The price that reflects the property, local comparables and required work.

### 3. Absolute maximum

The price above which you will walk away.

That maximum must include the effect of:

- extra mortgage;
- extra deposit;
- stamp duty;
- support limits;
- renovation;
- and monthly repayment.

A €10,000 higher bid is not just €10,000.

If financed, it can mean years of interest as well.

## The lender does not set your bidding limit

AIP might say you can buy at €450,000.

Your personal maximum could be €410,000.

That is not leaving money “unused”.

It is deciding that the remaining borrowing capacity has more value as future flexibility than as a higher house price.

## How private-treaty bidding works

For many second-hand homes, offers are made through the estate agent.

The seller decides whether to accept.

The estate agent acts for the seller, not the buyer.

You can:

- ask the current bid;
- make an offer;
- ask whether other bidders are active;
- increase your offer;
- or walk away.

The fact that another bidder exists does not tell you what the property is worth to you.

## Online bidding

Online systems can make the process feel like an auction.

It still pays to slow down.

The CCPC recommends setting a maximum budget in advance and avoiding “auction fever”.

A useful rule:

> Never increase a bid simply because the next increment looks small.

Five increments of €2,000 are still €10,000.

## Best and final offers

An estate agent may ask bidders to submit a best-and-final offer by a deadline.

There is no obligation to bid above your maximum.

A best-and-final process can feel like you need to guess other buyers' numbers.

You do not.

Submit the highest price at which you would still be happy to own the property.

If someone else values it more, let them have it.

## Auction purchases are different

At a public auction, the process can become legally binding immediately.

Citizens Information advises having your solicitor inspect the contract and title documents before auction and arranging a survey and mortgage approval in advance.

The successful bidder typically signs and pays a deposit immediately.

That is very different from ordinary private treaty.

Do not enter an auction with private-treaty assumptions.

## What “sale agreed” means

In a normal private-treaty sale, sale agreed means the seller has accepted an offer and the transaction is moving into legal and financial due diligence.

It does **not** necessarily mean the purchase is legally binding.

The CCPC states that before contracts are signed, another offer can still be passed to the seller and the seller can change course.

That is why the next stage should move quickly and deliberately.

## Booking deposit

The estate agent commonly asks for a booking deposit after sale agreed.

The CCPC notes that the booking deposit is refundable until contracts are signed in a standard private-treaty transaction.

It shows commitment to proceed, but does not by itself make the contract binding.

Keep the receipt and ensure the solicitor receives the sales details promptly.

## What to send after sale agreed

The estate agent will normally need:

- your solicitor's details;
- confirmation of finance / AIP;
- buyer details;
- and booking deposit.

Then the sales advice / memorandum goes to both solicitors.

You should immediately arrange:

- survey / engineer;
- lender valuation;
- final mortgage process;
- and any specialist inspections.

## Do not skip due diligence because you “won”

Winning the bid changes nothing about the building.

You still need to investigate:

- structure;
- planning;
- extensions;
- boundaries;
- rights of way;
- title;
- services;
- management company;
- and lender valuation.

The sunk-cost feeling after weeks of bidding can tempt buyers to ignore problems.

That is exactly when discipline matters most.

## A bidding example

Asking price: €375,000

Your analysis:

- similar local sales: €380,000–€395,000;
- required immediate work: €15,000;
- comfortable mortgage budget: €390,000;
- absolute all-in property price maximum: €392,000.

Bidding reaches €394,000.

Walking away is not “losing for €2,000”.

It is following the decision you made before emotion took over.

## Questions to ask at viewings

Ask about:

- age of roof;
- heating system;
- windows;
- BER;
- extensions / conversions;
- flooding or drainage;
- septic tank where relevant;
- boundaries;
- management fees;
- planning;
- known defects;
- sale chain;
- probate where relevant;
- and what fixtures/fittings are included.

The agent's answers are useful context.

They are not a substitute for your solicitor or surveyor.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is sale agreed legally binding in Ireland?

In a normal private-treaty transaction, generally not until contracts are signed. Auction purchases are different.

### Is the booking deposit refundable?

The CCPC says the booking deposit in a private-treaty sale is refundable until contracts are signed.

### How do I know what to bid?

Use comparable sale prices, property condition, required work and your own maximum affordable purchase price rather than the lender's maximum alone.

### Can a seller accept another offer after going sale agreed?

Before contracts are signed, the seller can still change buyer. Estate agents must pass relevant offers to the seller.
