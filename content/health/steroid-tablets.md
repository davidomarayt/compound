## At a glance

Oral corticosteroids reach the body through the circulation. They may be prescribed as a short course or for longer treatment, with different monitoring and stopping plans. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-tablets/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Whole-body exposure and the adrenal response

After entering cells, glucocorticoids bind their receptors and reduce inflammation through gene regulation. Systemic exposure also feeds back to the brain and pituitary, reducing the signal that tells the adrenal glands to make cortisol. Depending on exposure, the body may need time to recover its usual cortisol production. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-tablets/)

## What the evidence measures

Monitor the reason treatment was started and safety effects that are relevant to duration. The cumulative history of repeated courses may matter even when each individual course is short.

## Bring a record of all courses

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Treatment history | dates, names and any current taper instructions |
| Safety plan | ask whether illness advice or a steroid card applies |


## Does finishing a course mean the same thing for everyone?

Stopping instructions depend on the medicine, treatment history and reason for prescribing. Follow the plan provided for your course and double-check if it is unclear.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Follow your own prescribed stopping and illness instructions. Seek medical advice about possible serious infection or severe symptoms after an unsupervised change. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-tablets/)
