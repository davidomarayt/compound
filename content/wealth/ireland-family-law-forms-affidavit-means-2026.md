**New Circuit Court family-law forms came into effect across Ireland on 23 November 2026. From today, the Courts Service says it can accept only the new forms. The change includes a revised Affidavit of Means used to disclose income, assets, debts, spending, liabilities and, where relevant, pension information.**

This matters to people starting or responding to divorce, judicial-separation, civil-partnership and other Circuit Court family-law proceedings. It does not change the court fees, and it does not force everyone to file online: hard-copy filing remains available.

Anyone with documents prepared before 23 November should check the current form number and version before filing. A document being substantively complete does not establish that an older form will now be accepted.

## The main form changes

| New form | Main use |
| --- | --- |
| Form 37E | Divorce, judicial separation and dissolution of civil partnership |
| Form 37F | Other family-law applications |
| Form 37Z | Defence and counterclaim responding to Form 37E |
| Form 37ZA | Defence and counterclaim responding to Form 37F |
| Form 37ZB | Appearance across Circuit Court family-law case types |
| Form 37A | Affidavit or Statement of Truth of Means |
| Form 37B | Updated Affidavit or Statement of Truth of Welfare |

The Courts Service describes Form 37B as a new look and format rather than a change to the information required. The precise form needed still depends on the application and the relief sought.

## What the Affidavit of Means covers

Under the current Circuit Court rules, an Affidavit of Means must set out details of a party's:

- income;
- assets;
- debts and other liabilities;
- expenditure;
- relevant financial circumstances of dependent family members; and
- pension scheme information where pension relief is sought.

This is financial disclosure for court proceedings, not a household budget estimate. Figures should be supported by the required documents, and a person should not round, omit or reclassify an item merely to make the form look simpler.

The rules also contain timelines for exchanging and vouching financial information. Which timeline applies can depend on the pleadings and case progression, so it is unsafe to infer a filing deadline from a general article.

## Online and paper filing remain available

The Courts Service FAQ confirms that an applicant can choose either online or hard-copy filing. A hard-copy applicant does not also need to submit the same application online.

The distinction matters for signatures:

- the Courts Portal uses a Statement of Truth generated through the online process; and
- paper affidavits must be sworn and signed in person before an independent practising solicitor or a Commissioner for Oaths.

The Courts Service notes that a fee may arise for swearing paper affidavits. That is separate from the court fee structure, which the FAQ says has not changed as part of this forms update.

## A practical financial checklist

Before completing Form 37A, assemble current evidence rather than trying to reconstruct figures from memory:

1. payslips, benefit statements and evidence of other income;
2. bank, savings and investment account statements;
3. mortgage, loan, credit-card and other debt statements;
4. pension statements and scheme information where relevant;
5. regular housing, childcare, education, transport, insurance and healthcare costs; and
6. documents relating to property or other significant assets and liabilities.

That list is a preparation aid, not a substitute for the form, the rules or legal advice. The official form may require additional information, and a solicitor can advise on what must be vouched or exhibited in a particular case.

## What to do today

- Download the current form directly from [Courts.ie](https://www.courts.ie/guides/new-circuit-court-family-law-forms), even if an older copy is already saved.
- Confirm whether the case should use Form 37E or Form 37F rather than choosing by the everyday label for the dispute.
- If filing online, follow the Courts Portal prompts; if filing on paper, check the current copy and swearing requirements.
- Do not assume the forms update changes the legal test for divorce, maintenance, property or pension relief.
- Seek independent legal advice where the financial or legal position is contested.

Compound's [Irish pensions guide](/wealth/pensions-in-ireland/) explains how workplace and personal pensions normally operate. That general guide does not value pension rights for family-law disclosure or explain pension-adjustment orders; those questions need case-specific legal and, where appropriate, actuarial advice.

The central change from 23 November is procedural but consequential: the financial facts still matter, while the form and filing route used to put those facts before the Circuit Court must now match the new nationwide system.
