## At a glance

Find the leaflet that matches the name, strength and form on the pack. Similar-looking products can have different strengths, release mechanisms, ingredients or instructions. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/how-to-find-your-patient-information-leaflet-online/)

## The science in your product information

### Read the exact Irish leaflet and locate the pharmacology

The leaflet is written for patients. The Summary of Product Characteristics is the technical document. Section 5.1 generally describes pharmacodynamics, meaning what the medicine does to the body. Section 5.2 covers pharmacokinetics, including how the body absorbs, distributes and clears it. Warnings and interactions are in other sections. [HPRA source](https://www.hpra.ie/find-a-medicine/for-human-use/using-the-medicines-list/using-product-information)

## What the evidence measures

Use the regulator’s product record and revision information to assess whether the leaflet matches your pack. The physical leaflet is useful for product identification but online records can have a newer approved version. Confirm discrepancies with a pharmacist.

## Copy these details before searching

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Pack | active ingredient, strength and dosage form |
| Record | exact product name, marketing authorisation number and revision date |


## What if a leaflet search returns several results with the same brand?

Compare the strength, dosage form and authorisation number. An immediate-release product and an extended-release product can have different leaflets even if the brand and ingredient match.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Do not use a leaflet for another strength, modified-release form or jurisdiction to override instructions from your pharmacist. Ask them to retrieve the relevant Irish document if you cannot identify it. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/how-to-find-your-patient-information-leaflet-online/)
