A flat monthly subscription used to be a simple promise: pay the fee, use the product within some loosely defined fair-use limit. OpenAI's latest changes to ChatGPT move further away from that model. The subscription is becoming a prepaid compute budget, measured in tokens, with credits sold on top when the budget runs out.

OpenAI's [help centre](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001625-understanding-chatgpt-and-codex-usage-limits) now says that from December, the usage allowance on Plus, Pro and Business plans "will be shared across ChatGPT and Codex" as the agentic features of Work are folded into the main ChatGPT experience. A small group of users may see the change before then. [TechCrunch reported on 25 November](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/openai-says-heavy-chatgpt-users-may-hit-limits-sooner-as-it-merges-chat-and-work/) that a small group of Pro subscribers already has the combined experience, and that an OpenAI spokesperson said a small fraction of the heaviest users may reach their limits sooner.

The same week, OpenAI corrected what it called a launch billing mistake on its newest models. Taken together, the two changes show where the economics of consumer AI are heading, and why heavy users should start treating their plan like a metered account.

## What OpenAI has actually said

The help centre article is short, and its key points are specific:

- Usage across ChatGPT and Codex will be measured in tokens, the units of text a model reads and writes.
- How much of the allowance a request uses depends on the model and the task. More advanced models and complex tasks "generally use more".
- Instant chat will not draw from the shared allowance.
- OpenAI expects most users "won't need to wait for their usage to reset more often than they do now".
- Users who reach the limit can wait for a reset, upgrade their plan or buy credits for eligible features.

What OpenAI has not published is the size of the shared pool on any plan, or whether it equals the previous separate allowances added together. That matters: without a stated budget, a subscriber cannot check the "most users" claim in advance, only after the fact in Settings → Usage.

## The billing fix is the part with a number

On 26 November, OpenAI's Thibault Sottiaux said on X that a billing mistake at the launch of GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol had "incorrectly discounted" usage of both models for most users, as [reported by Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/ChatGPT-users-hit-limits-up-to-40-sooner-after-OpenAI-billing-fix.1460643.0.html). Compared with the previous week, users would reach their limits 40% faster on GPT-6.2 Astra and 25% faster on Sol. OpenAI said it would not charge for past usage, that other models were unaffected, and that every Go, Plus, Pro and Business account would get one banked reset.

"Reach your limit 40% faster" can be read two ways, and the difference is not trivial:

| Reading | Astra work per allowance | Sol work per allowance |
|---|---|---|
| Same work now uses 1.4x (Astra) or 1.25x (Sol) as much allowance | about 71% of before (1 / 1.4) | 80% of before (1 / 1.25) |
| Time to reach the limit falls by 40% or 25% | 60% of before | 75% of before |

*Compound arithmetic based on the percentages as reported. OpenAI has not published a formula.*

Either way, a heavy Astra user gets materially less work from the same subscription than they did a week earlier. The banked reset softens that once. According to [OpenAI's reset rules](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001498-how-banked-codex-resets-work), it can be redeemed only when at least one covered allowance has less than 80% remaining; on Plus it restores the five-hour and weekly allowances, and on Pro it restores the weekly allowance. It does not permanently raise any limit.

## How wide the range already is

OpenAI's [Codex pricing page](https://developers.openai.com/codex/pricing) gives a sense of how much a token budget can vary with model choice. It estimates local messages per five-hour period for Plus and Standard Business as:

- GPT-6.2 Astra: 6 to 50
- GPT-6.2 Sol: 17 to 180
- GPT-6 Luna: 350 to 3,000

OpenAI stresses that these are estimates rather than fixed limits, that cloud tasks can use more, and that weekly limits may also apply. The page does not say whether the figures reflect the corrected GPT-6.2 billing.

The spread inside a single model is the important point. A Plus subscriber paying $20 a month might get six Astra messages in a five-hour window, or 50, depending on context length, reasoning, tools and caching. Once chat, Work and Codex draw from the same pool, a long agentic Work task and a coding session compete for that same budget.

Speed is priced in the same currency. On the [Work and Codex help page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001275-chatgpt-work-and-codex), Ultrafast on GPT-6.2 models for Pro $500 uses the included allowance at six times the Standard rate. In plain terms, an allowance that would cover 60 standard tasks covers about 10 at Ultrafast. Purchased-credit usage for those models is charged at a promotional 3x Standard rate until 28 February 2027.

## Why this suits OpenAI

OpenAI is privately held and does not publish audited results, so the revenue effect of these changes is not disclosed. The commercial logic is still visible in the product rules.

**One budget is easier to price than five.** Agentic tasks use far more compute than ordinary chats. A single token-based allowance lets OpenAI charge for that cost without inventing a separate limit for every feature.

**Credits turn heavy use into extra revenue.** OpenAI's [credits page](https://help.openai.com/articles/12642688) says included usage is consumed first and credits after that. It also says a saved instruction such as "ask me before using credits" does not add a billing approval step: if a user keeps working in Work or Codex after the allowance runs out, available credits can be used automatically. Automatic reload, where offered, buys more credits when the balance falls below a chosen minimum.

**Price changes can happen without changing the price.** The headline subscription fees did not move this week. What moved was the conversion rate between a subscription and actual work. For a business, that is a quieter lever than a list-price increase, and harder for customers to compare.

The risk is trust. Two changes to how far a subscription goes, landing in the same week, make it difficult for a customer to tell which one caused a shortfall. Rivals with clearer limits could benefit if heavy users decide that predictability is worth more than a slightly better model.

## What a subscriber should do

For individual and small-business users, including Irish subscribers, the practical steps are simple:

1. Check Settings → Usage after the December change, and compare a normal week against a heavy one before deciding whether to upgrade.
2. Use Instant or a lighter model for routine work. Under the help centre rules, Instant chat does not draw from the shared allowance.
3. Decide in advance whether credits should ever be bought automatically, and set reload limits accordingly.
4. Save the banked reset for a week when a deadline genuinely depends on it.

## The investor reading

This is not a story about OpenAI raising prices. It is about the company moving its consumer and small-business products onto the same metered logic as its API, where usage, model choice and speed all show up in what customers pay. That aligns revenue more closely with compute cost, which matters for a company whose expenses scale with every token served.

The evidence to watch is whether OpenAI publishes the size of each plan's shared pool, how often heavy users report hitting limits after December, and whether credit purchases become a visible part of its disclosed revenue mix.
