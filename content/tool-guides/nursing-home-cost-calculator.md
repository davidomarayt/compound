## Start with the quote for the room you are considering

Ask the nursing home for a written weekly fee for the actual room and level of care you are considering. Ask for the private rate if you will be paying before Fair Deal starts. If the home takes part in Fair Deal, ask for the approved care rate and a separate list of charges that would appear on your own bill. A published rate is a useful benchmark while you shortlist homes. It can differ from the price the home quotes you for private care.

FairPath’s July 2026 snapshot of the HSE list put the median published private or voluntary rate at **€1,282 a week**. That figure describes the rates in the dated list. Private quotes and charges for individual homes can change, so check the list date and speak to the home before budgeting. Published HSE public-unit costs and agreed private or voluntary rates are prepared on different bases. A comparison between them tells you little about how much an individual resident pays. [Browse FairPath’s searchable price list](https://fairpath.ie/nursing-home-prices-ireland).

## Work out what you pay with Fair Deal

Under the Nursing Homes Support Scheme, commonly called Fair Deal, the HSE assesses your care needs and finances. If the HSE approves support, you pay your assessed contribution towards eligible long-term care and the HSE covers the balance of the approved care cost. The resident’s contribution comes from the assessment. A home charging a higher approved rate does not automatically increase that contribution. If the assessment is at or above the approved cost, the estimated HSE payment is zero.

If you have the HSE letter, enter your **weekly contribution** in the calculator. If you only have a monthly amount, check the letter or confirm the weekly figure with the Nursing Homes Support Office. If you are still waiting for an assessment, you can [estimate your contribution on FairPath](https://fairpath.ie/fair-deal-calculator) and use that amount to explore a scenario here. Untick **Include a Fair Deal scenario** if you want a budget based on private fees alone.

The HSE typically assesses 80% of assessable income and 7.5% a year of assessable assets for a single applicant, with the first €36,000 of assets disregarded. For one member of a couple, the headline rates are 40% of combined assessable income and 3.75% of combined assessable assets annually, with a combined €72,000 asset disregard. Deductions and individual circumstances affect the final figure. [See the HSE’s financial assessment and examples](https://www2.hse.ie/services/schemes-allowances/fair-deal-scheme/financial-assessment/).

## Budget for weeks before approval

Fair Deal funding starts from approval and the HSE says it cannot be backdated. If the person enters care earlier and has to pay the home privately, those weeks can make a large difference to the first-year budget. Use Advanced mode to enter how many weeks of private payment you want to model. This is your planning assumption. Ask your local Nursing Homes Support Office for the status of the application and confirm the start date before relying on funding.

## Worked example with six weeks of private care

Suppose the care quote is **€1,300 a week**, with **€50 in weekly extras**. A €450 assessed Fair Deal contribution brings the resident’s bill to **€500 a week** once approved, assuming the approved care rate is €1,300. Six private-pay weeks at €1,350 and 46 funded weeks at €500 give a **€31,100 first-year cost**, before any one-off costs. Twelve equal budget allocations would be **€2,591.67 per month**. The family would still need enough cash to cover the more expensive weeks when they fall due.

## Ask for a written list of extra charges

The HSE says Fair Deal excludes short-term care such as respite and can leave extra charges for services such as hairdressing, therapies and activities. Ask the home which extras would apply to your resident, whether they are optional, whether they are already included in the quote and how often they are billed. Request a copy of the contract and the home’s fee schedule. Use the written figures to compare homes on the same basis.

Enter regular charges as a weekly amount. If monthly extras are billed every calendar month, multiply by 12 and divide by 52 to estimate the weekly equivalent. Use the one-off field for costs that add to the first year, excluding amounts the home will repay or credit against a fee already counted. If a refundable deposit is required, it still affects the cash you need at move-in. Keep that sum aside separately.

## The three-year cap and the nursing home loan

The HSE generally stops counting the principal home after three years in care. Certain farms and businesses can also qualify, subject to conditions. Other assessable assets can still affect the contribution. If the HSE gives you a revised contribution after the cap, run a separate scenario with that new amount. The first-year model uses one contribution across the funded weeks you enter.

The optional nursing home loan can defer payment of the property-related part of the resident’s contribution in certain circumstances. It is generally repayable and the repayment can be adjusted for inflation. The calculator counts the contribution you enter as an amount you pay. For a loan case, ask the HSE to confirm how much would be paid now, how much would be deferred and the terms of repayment before using the estimate for cash-flow planning. [Read the HSE’s nursing home loan guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/services/schemes-allowances/fair-deal-scheme/nursing-home-loan/).

## Look beyond the weekly price

For each home on the shortlist, ask about the availability of a room, the fees at the level of care required, how family visits work and what happens if a resident’s needs change. Review the latest published inspection information and use it to ask specific questions when you visit.

## Method and limitations

The private weekly bill adds the private care fee and weekly extras. Where the model shows a positive HSE payment, the funded weekly bill adds your assessed care contribution and the same extras. Estimated HSE support is the difference between the approved care rate and the assessed contribution, with a minimum of zero. If the estimate shows no HSE support, the first-year model uses the private quote throughout. When you leave the approved care rate blank by entering €0, the private quote is used as a proxy.

The first year combines the weeks paying privately, the remaining funded weeks and any one-off costs. The monthly amount divides that 52-week total by 12. Fees and contributions are held constant over the periods entered. Tax relief, loan repayments and fee increases sit outside the calculation. All amounts are in euro and the tool covers the Republic of Ireland.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I budget without a Fair Deal contribution?

Yes. Untick the Fair Deal scenario to use your private quote and extras for all 52 weeks. You can compare it later with a funding scenario once you have an assessment or want to try an illustrative figure.

### Does the HSE pay for all of the extras?

Fair Deal applies to eligible long-term residential care. Ask the home and check its contract for services charged separately to you.

### Does this calculate short-term respite costs?

The Fair Deal comparison is for long-term care. For respite, request the separate price and ask the provider what support is available for that type of stay.
