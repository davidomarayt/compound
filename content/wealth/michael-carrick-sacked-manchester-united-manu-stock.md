*Manchester United news and stock analysis · 12 November 2026 · Reporting checked on publication. This story concerns an expected dismissal, **not an officially confirmed departure** at the time of checking.*

**Is Michael Carrick sacked by Manchester United? Reports on 12 November indicate that United are preparing to replace him, with Mauricio Pochettino reportedly lined up. But the club had not issued an official confirmation of Carrick's dismissal when this article was checked.** That distinction matters for football supporters searching for the latest news and investors assessing Manchester United's listed shares, **NYSE: MANU**.

After United's disastrous opening to the 2026/27 Premier League season left the club in the relegation zone, pressure on Carrick has intensified. [Sky Sports reported](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/13610412/michael-carrick-future-man-utd-head-coach-position-increasingly-in-doubt-but-no-final-decision-made) that his future was under review, while [subsequent reporting](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/13610949/mauricio-pochettino-man-utd-strike-agreement-with-michael-carricks-potential-successor) described an agreement in principle with Pochettino **if Carrick is removed**.

For shareholders, the central question is broader than who manages the next match. **How much could another managerial departure cost Manchester United, and could poor results threaten the revenue growth investors are expecting?**

## Has Michael Carrick been sacked by Manchester United?

As of the reporting checked for this article on **12 November 2026**, Carrick's departure was widely expected, but an official dismissal announcement had not been verified. [The London Standard's live coverage](https://www.standard.co.uk/sport/football/carrick-sack-news-live-man-utd-latest-pochettino-monaco-b1304053.html) continued to describe delays and Carrick attending the training ground despite reports that the club had decided to make a change.

A social-media post or graphic saying "sacked" should not be mistaken for a formal company announcement. The club's own disclosure remains important because Manchester United is a **publicly traded company**, not merely a football club.

On 22 May 2026, [Manchester United officially announced](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1549107/000110465926065415/tm2615462d1_ex99-1.htm) that Carrick had signed a new contract running until **2028**. Should the club terminate that agreement early, it may face contractual compensation or settlement costs. Neither the contract's payment terms nor any Carrick termination figure has been publicly established here.

## Why is Manchester United considering sacking Carrick?

Carrick's position is under pressure because of results. Manchester United entered the November international break in the **Premier League relegation zone**, following an eight-match league winless run and their worst opening to a season in decades. [AFP reported the review and negotiations](https://www.afp.com/en/man-utd-open-talks-pochettino-over-replacing-carrick-reports).

The irony is that Carrick had initially stabilised the club: United finished **third in 2025/26**, securing Champions League football for the following season. The early 2026/27 slump has therefore become both a sporting crisis and a threat to the next phase of the club's financial recovery.

The board may judge that changing manager is necessary to safeguard future revenues. But it is not yet possible to say that an appointment would deliver better results.

## How much could the Michael Carrick sacking cost Manchester United?

The precise compensation payable to Carrick, if dismissed, is unknown. A useful starting point is Manchester United's history of exceptional expenses.

| Financial year | Reported exceptional expenses | What the club said they included |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| FY2025 | **£36.6m** | Restructuring and costs relating to Erik ten Hag and other football-staff departures |
| FY2026 | **£8.2m** | Costs relating to Ruben Amorim's departure, restructuring and pension-related contributions |

*Source: [Manchester United's FY2026 Form 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1549107/000110465926110384/manu-20260630x20f.htm). These are combined exceptional expenses, **not** manager-only severance amounts.*

A further possible expense is recruiting Carrick's replacement. [Sky Sports reported](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/13610949/mauricio-pochettino-man-utd-strike-agreement-with-michael-carricks-potential-successor) that Pochettino's contract with the United States national team contains a release clause of **about £7 million**. That figure remains reported, not an independently disclosed Manchester United payment.

For comparison, £7 million is equivalent to approximately **1.0% of United's FY2026 revenue**. It would be a material one-off outlay but not, by itself, a threat to a business generating hundreds of millions of pounds of annual revenue.

Carrick's potential settlement, any departing coaching staff payments, the replacement's salary and associated recruitment costs would all need to be considered separately. Adding an invented severance figure to the reported release clause would give investors a false sense of precision.

## Manchester United's latest finances: record revenue, but a £43m loss

According to the club's [FY2026 results, filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1549107/000110465926109806/tm2625945d1_ex99-1.htm), Manchester United is a very large business, but not yet consistently profitable.

| Manchester United financial metric | FY2026 |
| --- | ---: |
| Total revenue | **£677.6m** |
| Commercial revenue | £317.3m |
| Broadcasting revenue | £206.8m |
| Matchday revenue | £153.5m |
| Adjusted EBITDA | £216.4m |
| Operating profit | £22.6m |
| Net loss | **£43.0m** |
| Net finance costs | £69.6m |

United achieved record annual revenue and a positive operating profit. However, the accounts also show **£46.9 million of profit on player disposals**. The club would have posted an operating loss of approximately **£24.3 million before those gains**, based on the reported figures. This is a simple adjustment, not a substitute for the club's audited operating profit.

That matters because investors should not confuse player-sale proceeds, adjusted EBITDA and sustainable cash generation. The club's [annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1549107/000110465926110384/manu-20260630x20f.htm) also reported **£69.6 million in net finance costs**, partly affected by non-cash foreign-exchange movements.

A managerial change will not, by itself, solve United's underlying financial structure.

## The biggest $MANU risk may be missing the Champions League again

United's latest guidance for the year ending June 2027 is:

- **Revenue: £740m–£760m**
- **Adjusted EBITDA: £205m–£225m**

The revenue midpoint of **£750 million** would be approximately **10.7% higher** than FY2026's £677.6 million. Champions League participation in 2026/27 is an important part of that season's financial picture.

However, qualification for **2027/28** is a separate question. If United's Premier League form does not recover, future European revenue could be at risk. Such a setback could affect broadcasting receipts, matchday opportunities and some performance-linked commercial economics.

It would be misleading to subtract this season's entire Champions League revenue from FY2027 guidance simply because United are currently struggling domestically. **The more direct risk is to future qualification and subsequent financial years.**

Relegation is a far more severe downside scenario, but it is not an outcome that can be assumed from the table in November. The potential loss of Premier League broadcasting distributions would be substantially more consequential than a single managerial settlement.

## Will Michael Carrick's sacking affect Manchester United stock (MANU)?

**There is no reliable basis here to attribute a particular MANU share-price movement to reports about Carrick.** The market response will depend on what investors already expected, whether the club formally announces a change, the financial terms disclosed and whether subsequent performances improve.

There are three plausible channels through which the situation could affect valuation:

1. **One-off costs:** compensation and recruitment could reduce reported profit or cash in the period when recognised.
2. **Future revenue expectations:** an improved league position could protect European qualification and broadcasting opportunities; continued poor performance could weaken them.
3. **Confidence in governance:** investors may reassess whether the club can maintain a coherent sporting strategy while controlling costs.

The reported £7 million release clause is small relative to total revenue. But the long-term value of $MANU is much more sensitive to whether management can build a competitive team **without repeated restructuring and sustained losses**.

Football clubs also differ from conventional listed businesses. Trophies, sporting prestige, brand value, transfer-market decisions and shareholder-control arrangements can influence expectations alongside financial performance. A headline about a manager should never be treated as a stand-alone buy or sell signal.

## Who could replace Michael Carrick at Man Utd?

**Mauricio Pochettino** is the leading reported candidate. [Sky Sports stated on 11 November](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/13610949/mauricio-pochettino-man-utd-strike-agreement-with-michael-carricks-potential-successor) that United had reached an agreement in principle with the USA head coach to take over **if Carrick is sacked**.

At the time this report was checked, this was **not the same thing as an official appointment**. Any eventual move would need confirmation from the relevant parties, particularly regarding the contractual release from his US national-team role.

For shareholders, an appointment matters less as a news event than for its potential to stabilise results and avoid another expensive cycle of managerial turnover.
