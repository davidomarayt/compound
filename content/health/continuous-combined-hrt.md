## At a glance

Continuous combined HRT combines systemic oestrogen with a progestogen taken continuously. It is generally considered for people who still have a uterus and are at an appropriate stage after menopause. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/continuous-combined-hrt/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Oestrogen and continuous protection of the womb lining

Oestrogen binds its receptor and changes gene expression in responsive tissues. In the uterus, systemic oestrogen stimulates the endometrium. The continuously given progestogen counteracts that stimulation and changes how the lining develops. This explains why the progestogen component matters even when it is the oestrogen that mainly targets symptoms. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/continuous-combined-hrt/)

## What the evidence measures

Track the original symptom, tolerability and any bleeding pattern. Benefits and harms depend on the formulation, route and personal history, so population averages are a starting point for a shared decision.

## Make a two-line treatment record

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Symptom to review | hot flushes, night sweats or sleep disruption |
| Bleeding history | record timing and whether the pattern changed |


## Why is a symptom diary useful?

Timing can reveal whether bleeding or side effects began after a formulation, schedule or adherence change. Record the detail and share it with the clinician instead of inferring the cause.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Report unexpected bleeding according to the care plan, particularly new or persistent bleeding. Ask the prescriber to review any change in the regimen so both components remain appropriate. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/continuous-combined-hrt/)
