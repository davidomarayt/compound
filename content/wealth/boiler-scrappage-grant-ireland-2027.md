<p><em>News analysis · 23 September 2026. The new €2,000 boiler scrappage payment is a proposal and still requires Cabinet approval. Final eligibility rules have not yet been published.</em></p>

Irish households with oil or kerosene boilers could be offered a new **€2,000 scrappage grant** to help them switch to a heat pump.

Minister for Climate, Energy and the Environment Darragh O'Brien said he plans to bring the proposal to Cabinet next week. RTÉ reports that the new payment would be available **in addition to existing heat-pump grants of up to €12,500**.

That creates an eye-catching headline figure:

> **Potential support of up to €14,500 for a qualifying house — if the proposed €2,000 grant is approved and can be fully combined with the existing maximum heat-pump package.**

The important word is **potential**.

The €2,000 scheme has not yet been approved by Cabinet, and the detailed rules on eligibility, qualifying heating systems and how the payment will interact with existing SEAI grants have not yet been published.

## What is being proposed?

The proposal is aimed at households that currently rely on **oil or kerosene boilers**.

According to RTÉ, the Minister intends to seek Cabinet approval for a grant of **€2,000 to scrap those boilers**, alongside the existing supports for replacing fossil-fuel heating with a heat pump.

The Journal separately reports that the scheme is expected to begin in **January 2027**, although the final implementation date will depend on Cabinet approval and the published scheme rules.

For many rural households, this matters because oil remains a common home-heating fuel where there is no connection to the natural-gas network.

## How much is already available for a heat pump?

The current SEAI Heat Pump System Grant can provide **up to €12,500** for eligible houses.

That maximum is not one single payment. It is a bundle made up of:

| Current support | Maximum amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Heat Pump System Grant | €6,500 |
| Central Heating Upgrade Grant | €2,000 |
| Renewable Heat Bonus | €4,000 |
| **Maximum combined amount for qualifying houses** | **€12,500** |

The **€4,000 Renewable Heat Bonus** is already specifically linked to replacing an existing fossil-fuel or electric-storage heating system with a heat pump.

SEAI says the bonus can apply when a homeowner replaces an **oil boiler, gas boiler, solid-fuel heating system or electric storage heating system**.

The central-heating element of up to €2,000 is available where works such as new radiators or underfloor heating are required.

## So could the total really reach €14,500?

Based on the Minister's statement reported by RTÉ, the proposed €2,000 scrappage grant would sit **on top of** the current maximum heat-pump support.

That would produce:

**€12,500 existing maximum + €2,000 proposed scrappage grant = €14,500**

But homeowners should not treat €14,500 as an automatic entitlement.

There are three reasons.

First, the new €2,000 measure is still a proposal.

Second, the current €12,500 figure is itself a **maximum** and depends on the property, heating system and works required.

Third, the detailed interaction between the new scrappage payment and the existing €4,000 Renewable Heat Bonus has not yet been published.

Until the final scheme documentation appears, the safest interpretation is that **€14,500 is the potential maximum described by combining the announced proposal with the existing maximum support**, rather than a guaranteed payment for every household.

## Do apartments get the same amount?

No.

Under the current SEAI scheme, the maximum grant for several water-based heat-pump systems is:

- **€12,500** for detached, semi-detached, end-of-terrace and mid-terrace houses;
- **€9,500** for apartments.

Air-to-air heat pumps currently have a lower maximum grant of **€7,500** across dwelling types.

The proposed boiler scrappage scheme could introduce its own property or technology rules, but those details are not yet available.

## Who qualifies for the existing heat-pump grant?

The current SEAI scheme is open to a broad range of property owners, including owner-occupiers, landlords, companies, registered charities, holiday-home owners and Approved Housing Bodies.

For the existing individual heat-pump grant, the home must have an **MPRN** and must have been **built and occupied before 2021**.

There are also technical requirements.

A heat pump works most efficiently in a home that does not lose heat too quickly. For homes built before 2007, SEAI may require a technical assessment unless an existing BER assessment shows that the home meets the required Heat Loss Indicator threshold.

SEAI currently offers a **€200 grant towards the technical assessment**.

These are the rules for the existing heat-pump scheme. They should not be assumed to be the final eligibility rules for the proposed €2,000 scrappage grant.

## Why insulation can matter before installing a heat pump

Replacing an oil boiler with a heat pump is not always a straight equipment swap.

Heat pumps generally work best when the home has sufficiently low heat loss, which can mean that insulation, windows, doors or heating distribution need attention first.

That is why the grant package includes support for central-heating upgrades such as radiators or underfloor heating where required.

For homeowners considering a switch, the practical question is not simply:

> "How big is the grant?"

It is:

> **"What will the whole project cost after the grant, and is my house ready for a heat pump?"**

A household that needs substantial fabric upgrades may still face a significant net cost even with a large headline grant.

## A simple example

Suppose an eligible house requires:

- a qualifying heat-pump installation;
- radiator or other central-heating upgrades;
- removal of an existing oil boiler.

If the household qualified for every part of the current €12,500 package, and if the proposed €2,000 scrappage grant were approved and fully stackable, the theoretical support could be:

| Support | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Heat Pump System Grant | €6,500 |
| Central Heating Upgrade Grant | €2,000 |
| Renewable Heat Bonus | €4,000 |
| Proposed boiler scrappage grant | €2,000 |
| **Potential total** | **€14,500** |

That example illustrates the maximum announced structure. It is **not** a quote for a particular home and does not include the cost of any insulation or other retrofit work that might be needed.

## What has not been confirmed yet?

Several important questions remain unanswered until Cabinet considers the proposal and the scheme rules are published.

These include:

- the exact launch date;
- whether every oil and kerosene boiler will qualify;
- whether there will be property-age or occupancy conditions beyond the existing heat-pump rules;
- whether the €2,000 will be paid directly to homeowners or deducted by a contractor;
- whether there will be a fixed funding allocation or application cap;
- whether every applicant who qualifies for the current Renewable Heat Bonus will also qualify for the new payment;
- what evidence will be required to prove that the old boiler has been scrapped.

Those details will determine how valuable the measure is in practice.

## What should homeowners do now?

There is no reason to apply for the proposed €2,000 payment yet because the scheme has not opened.

Anyone already considering a heat pump can, however, check the **current SEAI eligibility rules**, examine their BER and heat-loss position, and get a realistic view of the full installation cost before making a decision.

SEAI also warns that for its existing individual grants, homeowners must apply for and accept a grant offer **before works begin**.

That timing point matters. Starting work first can make an otherwise eligible project ineligible for grant payment.

## The key point

The proposal could materially increase the upfront support available to some households moving away from oil or kerosene heating.

But the scheme is **not yet final**.

The current confirmed position is that SEAI already offers up to **€12,500** for qualifying house heat-pump systems. The Minister is now proposing an additional **€2,000 boiler scrappage grant**, which — if approved and fully stackable — could lift the potential maximum support to **€14,500**.

The next important milestone is Cabinet consideration and publication of the final eligibility and application rules.
