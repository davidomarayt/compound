**Molina’s latest quarter is a test of whether medical-cost pressure is stabilising. Holding guidance is encouraging, but it is not the same as restoring healthy profitability.**

The insurer released its third-quarter results after the US market closed on 21 October 2026. For investors, the decisive issue is the relationship between the premiums collected and the cost of providing members’ healthcare.

## Molina earnings at a glance

| Metric | Q3 2026 | Q3 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Premium revenue | $10.457bn | $10.841bn |
| Total revenue | $11.082bn | $11.477bn |
| GAAP net income | $38m | $79m |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $0.73 | $1.51 |
| Adjusted diluted EPS, non-GAAP | $0.89 | $1.84 |
| Medical care ratio | 92.9% | 92.6% |

*Source: Molina Healthcare’s SEC 8-K, Exhibit 99.1; quarter ended 30 September 2026. US dollars.*

## Why a small ratio change can matter so much

A medical care ratio measures medical costs relative to premium revenue. It is not a net profit margin: administration, financing costs and other items still matter.

In a thin-margin insurance business, modest changes in claims costs can have an outsized effect on earnings. Investors therefore need to assess whether pricing adequately compensates for the risk assumed, rather than treating membership or premium growth as automatically positive.

A larger book of business can destroy value if the additional premiums do not cover the associated costs. Conversely, walking away from unattractive business can improve future economics while reducing near-term revenue. The size and quality of an insurer are different questions.

## Management sees signs of stabilisation

Molina attributed lower premiums to reduced Medicaid and Marketplace membership, partly offset by rate updates. Management described medical-cost trends as apparently plateauing at elevated levels and pointed to stronger 2027 rate updates.

That is a management assessment, not proof that margins have turned. A plateau in costs can still leave profitability inadequate if premiums have not caught up. Rate changes, utilisation and the mix of members need to be evaluated together.

The evidence investors should seek is repeatable improvement across comparable periods, supported by transparent explanations of claims experience. Optimism about future pricing is useful only when its timing and expected economic effect can be tested.

## Unchanged guidance still includes difficult business

Full-year guidance remains approximately $42bn in premium revenue and at least $5.25 adjusted EPS. It includes per-share losses of $1.50 from Florida Medicaid implementation and $1.00 from traditional MAPD, which Molina plans to exit for 2027.

The distinction between recurring underwriting weakness and implementation costs matters. Temporary costs may fade; underpriced risk can continue to erode earnings until contracts, membership or reimbursement change. Investors should not apply the same valuation treatment to both without evidence.

An exit can remove an earnings drag, but it is not equivalent to organic growth. The remaining business still needs to demonstrate attractive economics on its own terms.

## The next test is the explanation behind the numbers

Molina’s call is scheduled for 22 October at 8am Eastern, or 1pm in Ireland. This article assesses the release and does not anticipate answers from that call.

The useful questions concern the durability of cost stabilisation, the timing of rate relief and the path from those developments to cash earnings. An unchanged forecast gives investors a reference point; subsequent performance must validate it.
