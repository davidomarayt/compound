## Start with the fee you will actually be billed

Ask the crèche, preschool or after-school service for its written fee sheet or parent statement for the programme year your child will attend. Use the price for the exact age group, days, sessions and hours you would book. If a sibling discount applies to that place, use the discounted fee. If your child will attend the Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) programme, use the provider’s fee after ECCE and before the National Childcare Scheme (NCS) subsidy.

Some providers quote per week, some every four weeks and some per calendar month. A four-week quote divided by four gives the weekly fee. For a fixed calendar-month quote, multiply by the number of payments you will make in the year and divide by the number of weeks included in those payments. If the provider already quoted the amount after NCS, ask for the pre-NCS fee or enter the net quote with a €0 hourly subsidy to avoid subtracting it twice.

## How the National Childcare Scheme changes the bill

The NCS helps eligible families using participating childcare providers. As of 19 November 2026, the universal rate is **€2.14 per hour**, up to 45 hours per week where eligible hours are agreed and registered. An income-assessed award may be higher and the approved hours can differ. Use the hourly rate from your award and the hours your provider confirms it can register. The [official NCS page](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-children-disability-and-equality/campaigns/national-childcare-scheme/) links to the subsidy calculator if you are still checking eligibility.

The rate and hours are two separate figures. If your award is €2.14 an hour but your provider confirms only 25 eligible hours, enter 25 NCS hours even if the booking is longer. Check what happens during closures, holidays or a change in attendance. A week can be billable under your agreement even if NCS does not cover it. Advanced mode lets you budget for that gap.

The Department’s Budget 2027 parent FAQ says that from **September 2027** the universal rate will rise to **€2.50 per hour** for children up to and including Senior Infants. The universal rate for children from First Class up to age 15 will remain **€2.14**. The calculator starts with the current rate. For a future scenario, enter the applicable rate and your provider’s fee for that period.

## Worked example for a 45-hour crèche place

The Department’s 2026 Core Funding example uses **€280 per week** before NCS for a typical 45-hour place. With 45 funded hours at €2.14, the subsidy is **€96.30** and the family pays **€183.70 per funded week**. At that same cost for all 52 weeks, the annual spend is **€9,552.40**, or **€796.03 a month** when spread across the year. A bill for exactly four weeks would be **€734.80**. The Department uses a four-week month in its example, so both monthly figures can be correct depending on what you are measuring.

The **€280** is a cap for the relevant hours band at participating Core Funding providers in the 2026/27 programme year. It can serve as a benchmark while you wait for a quote. Ask each provider what it charges, whether it participates in NCS and Core Funding, and which extras or deposits would change the bill.

## Compare two quotes on the same basis

Enter the first provider’s quote and select **Save for comparison**. Then enter the second provider’s fee, booked hours, NCS hours and billed weeks. Do the same if you are comparing three days and five days at one crèche. The annual result is especially helpful when one quote covers a school term and the other covers the full year.

Before accepting a place, ask what happens when your child moves to a different age band, when a sibling discount ends, or when your subsidy award expires. Confirm if meals are included, if a deposit is credited against fees and which closure weeks you will pay for. If your childcare pattern changes during the year, calculate those blocks separately and add them. For example, run school-term weeks on one set of rates and holiday care on another. When you have a childcare budget, the [Childcare vs Returning to Work Calculator](/childcare-return-to-work-calculator/) can help you compare it with take-home pay and work costs.

## Method and limitations

For each child the weekly subsidy is the hourly NCS award multiplied by the lowest of booked hours, registered NCS hours and 45. The result is capped at the weekly fee it offsets. The annual cost adds each child’s weekly fee multiplied by billed weeks, subtracts their weekly subsidy multiplied by subsidised weeks, and adds any annual extras. Subsidised weeks are capped at billed weeks. The average monthly cost divides the annual total by 12.

The combined weekly result represents a week when all entered places are active and applicable subsidies are paid. If siblings use different schedules or awards, a real week may differ. Advanced mode supports up to three children with separate inputs. Calculations run in the browser. Use the provider’s confirmed hours and fee and your official award. The tool estimates the bill from those inputs and leaves eligibility and approved payments to the scheme and provider.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use this alongside the free ECCE preschool scheme?

Yes. Enter the quote with the ECCE fee deduction already applied and use the NCS hours the provider confirms for any separately eligible care. Keeping both numbers aligned with the provider’s statement avoids counting the same hours twice.

### What do I enter if I have no NCS award?

Enter €0 for the hourly award and zero subsidised hours to budget for the full fee. If you expect a later NCS start, run a second scenario or use Advanced mode to enter fewer subsidised weeks than billed weeks.

### Why does the monthly estimate differ from my invoice?

The result is an average across 12 calendar months. A four-week invoice covers four weeks, with 13 such periods in a 52-week year. Providers can also have different rules for charges over holiday or closure weeks.

### Can I compare term-time and holiday care?

Yes. If the fee or award differs between the two, calculate the annual contribution of each by using only the weeks when it applies, then add the results. Use the same approach for a mid-year fee or subsidy change.
