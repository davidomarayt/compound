**Both medicines can ease pain and lower a fever. Ibuprofen is also an anti-inflammatory medicine.** The choice for an adult depends on the symptoms, medical history and other medicines they take.

| What to compare | Ibuprofen | Paracetamol |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uses | Relieves pain, lowers fever and can reduce inflammation. | Relieves pain and lowers fever. |
| Main precautions to discuss | A history of stomach ulcers, a reaction to an NSAID, kidney or heart problems, pregnancy, or medicines such as blood thinners. | Liver or kidney problems, low body weight, regular use with warfarin, or another product containing paracetamol. |
| Active ingredients to check | Ibuprofen and other NSAIDs in painkillers, including combination products. | Paracetamol in painkillers, cold and flu remedies, and some prescription medicines. |

## What should I check before choosing?

A pharmacist can check whether a medicine suits your symptoms and medical history. Bring the names or packs of any medicines you already take, including cold and flu remedies. The [ibuprofen guide](/health/medicines/ibuprofen/) and [paracetamol guide](/health/medicines/paracetamol/) cover the separate precautions in more detail.

## Can adults take ibuprofen and paracetamol together?

The [HSE advises adults to take separate ibuprofen and paracetamol products at the same time only if their GP advises it](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/paracetamol/). The HPRA separately authorises [a pharmacy-only product containing both ingredients](https://www.hpra.ie/find-a-medicine/for-human-use/authorised-medicines/details/41271). That product has its own patient leaflet and directions. Ask a pharmacist or GP about suitability and check the active ingredients on every pack so you can spot overlap.
