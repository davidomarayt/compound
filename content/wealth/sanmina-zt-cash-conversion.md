**Sanmina’s acquisition of ZT Systems made fiscal 2026 look like a different company. Revenue rose 79%, margins expanded and free cash flow reached $541 million. The harder question is how much of that cash reflects repeatable operations rather than acquisition funding and favourable working-capital timing.**

For the year ended 3 October 2026, the electronics manufacturer reported **$14.53 billion of revenue**, **$681.5 million of GAAP operating income** and **$944.1 million of operating cash flow**. Sanmina defines free cash flow as operating cash less net purchases of property and equipment; $944.1 million less $403.3 million produced **$540.8 million**.

Compound does not currently maintain a dedicated Sanmina stock profile, so this analysis does not invent one or reproduce a routine quarterly table.

## The acquisition explains much of the new scale

Sanmina bought ZT Systems’ data-centre infrastructure manufacturing business from AMD in October 2025. The final accounting puts consideration net of acquired cash at approximately **$1.38 billion**, including cash, shares and the initial fair value of contingent consideration.

The 10-K says ZT contributed **$5.0 billion of revenue** from acquisition through year-end. That is about **34% of Sanmina’s $14.53 billion total**. Subtracting that disclosed contribution leaves roughly $9.53 billion, 17% above the prior year’s $8.13 billion. That is a simple residual, not a formal organic-growth measure: purchase accounting, timing and integration mean it cannot isolate every like-for-like movement. Sanmina separately says Integrated Manufacturing Solutions grew organically outside the acquisition.

The acquired mix improved economics as well as scale. Integrated Manufacturing Solutions gross margin rose to **9.6% from 7.7%**, which the filing attributes to higher-margin AI compute infrastructure, rack-scale programmes and product mix. The result absorbed a $49 million non-cash inventory fair-value charge.

This is the strategic promise of ZT: Sanmina moves further into complex racks and AI infrastructure rather than merely assembling a larger volume of lower-margin electronics. The risk is concentration. Ten customers represented about 63% of sales, and two customers each accounted for at least 10% of gross receivables. The AMD relationship and hyperscaler demand are important advantages, but they also make programme decisions by a small group of buyers unusually consequential.

## Cash flow benefited from working-capital movements

Operating cash flow exceeded net income before non-controlling interests by more than $540 million. Non-cash items explain part of the bridge, including depreciation, stock compensation, deferred tax, acquisition accounting and a **$150 million increase in the fair value of contingent consideration**.

Working capital also mattered. Accounts receivable fell by $347 million and accounts payable rose by $539 million, together providing $886 million of cash. Against that, inventory consumed $501 million, contract assets $195 million, and prepaid and other assets $310 million.

None of those movements is automatically low quality. Sanmina says the receivable reduction reflected customer mix and payment terms, while higher payables and inventory were consistent with greater business volume. They do show why one year’s operating cash should not simply be capitalised as a permanent run rate.

The cash-cycle indicators reinforce the caution. Cash-cycle days increased to **59 from 57**. Inventory days were broadly flat at 95, while customer-inventory-advance days fell to 26 from 40. Customer deposits still help finance components, but less of the inventory cycle was offset by advances at year-end than a year earlier.

## A healthy cash balance sits beside acquisition obligations

Sanmina ended the year with **$1.68 billion of cash** and approximately **$2.09 billion of current and long-term debt**. Subtracting cash gives simple net debt of about **$412 million**, before considering restricted cash or other obligations.

The ZT earn-out remains important. Its initial fair value was $111 million, then increased by $150 million to **$261 million**. Actual undiscounted payments can range from zero to $450 million, depending on revenue and gross-profit targets across three performance periods. A higher liability can signal stronger expected operating performance, but it is still a potential cash claim and the auditor identified its valuation as a critical audit matter.

Capital allocation adds another test. Sanmina repurchased **$393 million of shares**, equal to roughly **73% of fiscal-year free cash flow**. It also repaid $388 million of borrowings after raising debt to finance ZT. Buybacks can improve per-share results, but using most free cash flow for repurchases while integration and earn-out obligations remain raises the standard for judging the price paid and the durability of cash generation.

## Guidance assumes the step-up continues

Management guides to fiscal 2027 revenue of **$17.5 billion–$19.5 billion** and non-GAAP operating margin of 7.0%–7.5%. The $18.5 billion midpoint implies growth of about 27% from fiscal 2026. It also expects non-GAAP diluted EPS of $15.50–$17.50.

Those figures are guidance, and Sanmina says it cannot reconcile the forward non-GAAP measures to GAAP without unreasonable effort. The quality test is whether GAAP operating profit and cash conversion keep pace without another unusually favourable receivables-and-payables bridge.

The thesis would strengthen if ZT sustains higher margins, the customer base broadens, cash-cycle days improve and debt falls after earn-out payments and buybacks. It would weaken if inventory keeps absorbing cash, customer advances retreat further, or acquisition adjustments become a recurring reason for a wide GAAP/non-GAAP gap.

**Sanmina has shown that ZT can transform revenue and product mix. Fiscal 2027 needs to show that the combined business can transform those gains into repeatable cash without leaning on working-capital timing.** This analysis makes no price target.
