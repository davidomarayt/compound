Microsoft expects Xbox content and services revenue to grow in the mid to high single digits year over year in the December quarter, even as Xbox hardware revenue falls. A player who already owns a console can buy another game or renew a subscription without buying another machine. The investment question is how much spending Microsoft can keep once the holiday releases have had their first burst of sales. [Microsoft’s earnings call](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2027/earnings-fy-2027-q1)

## Where the September shortfall came from

Xbox brought in **$5.379 billion** in the three months ended 30 September 2026, Microsoft’s first quarter of fiscal 2027. A year earlier it earned $5.508 billion in revenue on the comparable, recast basis. That leaves a $129 million gap. Hardware revenue fell 14% as Microsoft sold fewer consoles. Content and services revenue also fell, by 2% in reported dollars and 1% at constant currency. [September 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526436133/msft-20260930.htm) · [Quarterly results](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2027-q1/press-release-webcast)

The percentages alone cannot tell investors how many of those missing dollars came from hardware. A 14% decline on a small base can cost less than a 2% decline on a large one. Microsoft gives the two growth rates rounded to whole percentages and reports their combined revenue. Using those rounded rates to reverse-engineer precise console sales or subscription revenue would give a misleading impression of accuracy.

Microsoft does identify which kinds of spending weakened. Revenue from third-party content and Game Pass fell, while games from Microsoft’s own studios partly offset those declines. The distinction matters for forecasting. A release from one studio can lift sales in its launch quarter. Subscription revenue depends on how many customers pay, what they pay and for how long. The filing gives the direction of Game Pass revenue, but leaves the respective contributions from subscriber numbers, pricing and plan mix open. [Microsoft’s discussion of Xbox results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526436133/msft-20260930.htm)

## What a holiday rebound would establish

On its October earnings call, Microsoft tied its December-quarter content forecast to the timing of releases from its own studios and outside publishers. Management expected the December quarter to deliver the strongest growth of the fiscal year for content and services, followed by slower growth in the March and June quarters. Those are management expectations. The September figures are reported results. [Microsoft’s second-quarter outlook](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2027/earnings-fy-2027-q1)

That outlook gives shareholders two tests. The first is whether spending on games and services grows enough in dollars to outweigh another decline in hardware. An increase in total Xbox revenue alongside lower hardware sales would answer that question for the quarter. Meeting the content growth forecast alone would leave the answer dependent on the size of the hardware decline.

The second test comes after the holidays. If growth concentrates in a handful of releases and Game Pass revenue continues to fall, the December rate would be an aggressive assumption for later quarters. If third-party spending and subscriptions both improve as launch activity eases, investors would have stronger grounds to expect continuing demand from existing players. Microsoft’s explanation of the mix will matter as much as the headline growth rate.

## How much Xbox moves Microsoft

Xbox represented **5.8% of Microsoft revenue** in the September quarter, down from 7.1% a year earlier. That change reflects two moving parts. Xbox revenue fell $129 million while company revenue rose from $77.673 billion to $92.279 billion. Holding everything else fixed, flat Xbox sales would have added about **0.17 percentage points** to Microsoft’s year-over-year revenue growth, calculated by dividing the $129 million shortfall by the prior-year company revenue base. [Microsoft’s product revenue table](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2027-q1/press-release-webcast)

For a shareholder, that puts the holiday forecast in proportion. Sales can establish whether players are spending enough to offset fewer console purchases. Valuing that spending also requires knowing the profit Microsoft keeps. It reports operating income for the broader Devices and Consumer segment, which includes search advertising and Windows alongside Xbox. Applying the segment margin to a dollar of Xbox revenue would assume those businesses share the same economics. [Microsoft’s segment disclosures](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526436133/msft-20260930.htm)
