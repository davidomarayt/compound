**Revolut has launched joint investment accounts in Ireland, giving two eligible customers the ability to buy, sell and monitor investments through a shared portfolio.** Announced on **Monday, 16 November 2026**, the feature is intended for people managing a financial goal together. It sits alongside — rather than merging — their personal Revolut investment accounts.

For Irish households, this could make shared investing easier to organise. But it comes with a consequential detail: **both account holders have authority to place trades, and Revolut's terms generally treat the investments and cash as equally owned even if one person contributed more.**

Here is how the launch works, what it could cost and what to consider before moving household investments into one account.

## What has Revolut launched in Ireland?

According to [RTÉ's 16 November announcement](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1116/1599360-revolut-joint-investment/), Revolut now lets two eligible Irish customers trade and track the performance of a joint investment portfolio.

Revolut says it has **more than 3.4 million users in Ireland**, with approximately **375,000 actively investing** through the platform. Those are company-reported user counts, not a forecast of how many joint accounts will open.

The product adds a new type of account, not a joint view of two existing personal portfolios. An individual investment account remains distinct from investments held jointly. That distinction matters for record-keeping, ownership and control.

Revolut's head of wealth and trading in the EEA, Rolandas Juteika, framed the launch as a way to remove the spreadsheet and account-juggling involved in managing shared investments. The practical question for couples is whether the convenience is worth the extra responsibility that comes with a shared legal account.

## Who can open a Revolut joint investment account?

The eligibility rules are more specific than simply having two Revolut users. According to [Revolut Securities Europe UAB's Irish terms](https://www.revolut.com/en-IE/legal/investment-services-terms/), **both people must**:

- Already hold a **Revolut joint current account together**.
- Have their own active **personal Revolut current accounts and sole investment accounts**, without relevant restrictions.
- Be **resident in the same European Economic Area country**, based on the address recorded for their personal investment accounts.
- Accept the investment agreement and relevant product-specific terms.
- Continue to meet these conditions while the account is open.

You can have **only one Revolut joint investment account at a time** and cannot open the account for business purposes. Opening one does not mean you must close either person's individual investing account.

For a couple in Ireland that has used Revolut only for day-to-day card spending, the existing joint *banking* account and individual *investment* accounts are important prerequisites. Revolut's [joint current account rules](https://www.revolut.com/en-IE/legal/joint-accounts/) have their own age, residency, account and identity-check conditions.

## How does shared investing work? The most important ownership rule

**Each holder generally has an equal legal share of the cash and securities in the account, regardless of who originally contributed the money or executed a purchase**, subject to applicable law.

Suppose one partner contributes **€8,000** and the other contributes **€2,000**. The account may show **€10,000** invested together, but Revolut's standard joint-account terms do **not** preserve an 80/20 ownership split just because the payments were unequal. The terms describe equal ownership and equal entitlement to dividends and other distributions.

This is a crucial distinction between a **joint investment account** and two people keeping separately owned portfolios and sharing a spreadsheet.

Revolut does not record a separate beneficial share of each individual holding within its joint account interface. Questions about property ownership between two people, the law applying to their relationship, gifts, separation or inheritance can require independent legal or tax advice.

### Can one partner sell shares without asking the other?

**Generally, yes.** Under the investment terms, either account holder can instruct Revolut to buy or sell investments without obtaining the other's specific approval. Both have access to shared balances, positions, confirmations and trading information.

That flexibility can be useful when one person regularly manages the portfolio. It can also be a risk if the two investors disagree about risk tolerance, timing or strategy.

Revolut says that, where it becomes aware of a dispute or conflicting instructions, it **may freeze part or all of the account**, including trading and withdrawals, until the issue is resolved. Before starting, agree on the investment goal, the product types allowed and how major trades will be discussed.

## What can couples invest in?

The product's exact menu of shares, exchange-traded products and other financial instruments can change. Revolut states that **joint-account instruments may differ from those available in an individual investment account**.

An instrument must also be available and appropriate or suitable for **both** account holders, not merely for the more experienced partner. Check what the app actually offers your joint account before assuming that every stock or ETF from an individual account can be bought together.

## Revolut joint investment account fees: is investing together cheaper?

**Do not assume the new account makes investing free.** Revolut's [Irish securities fee schedule](https://www.revolut.com/en-IE/legal/investment-services-terms/) says commissions and applicable plan-based trading limits depend on the relevant joint account holder's **individual Revolut subscription plan**.

Revolut offers some plan-based commission-free trade allowances, but a free-trade allowance is not the same as an entirely cost-free investment. Depending on the investment, currency, account plan and transaction, other costs can matter.

| Cost to check | What it means for a household portfolio |
| --- | --- |
| Trade commission | The charge for buying or selling when an applicable free-trade allowance does not cover the order. |
| Foreign exchange | A currency conversion may affect the euro cost of buying non-euro assets. |
| Fund charges | ETFs and other funds can have ongoing costs inside the product, separate from the broker's commission. |
| Bid–ask spread | The price you pay to buy an instrument may exceed what another buyer would pay at that moment. |
| Account and plan charges | A paid Revolut subscription should not be justified solely by a theoretical saving on a small number of trades. |
| Irish tax and reporting | Tax liabilities and administrative effort can outweigh small differences in dealing charges. |

Check the **current fee schedule inside your app**. Revolut's online terms indicate that a new free-trade allowance table is due to take effect on **24 November 2026**, so rates and thresholds should not be copied from older reviews without a date.

## How are joint investment accounts taxed in Ireland?

**The account is not a tax-free wrapper.** Putting shares or ETFs into a joint account does not itself provide the tax relief available through a qualifying pension or another specifically tax-advantaged product.

For Irish-resident individuals, the result depends on **the asset held, who is legally and beneficially entitled to income and gains, and the relevant tax rules**.

### Individual shares and capital gains tax

For most chargeable gains on directly held shares, the standard Irish **capital gains tax (CGT) rate is 31% from 7 October 2026**, following the October 2026 change documented by [Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/ebrief/2026/no-1422026.aspx). Individuals generally have a **€1,270 annual CGT exemption**, subject to the rules and their other gains and losses. Always check whether an asset qualifies for normal CGT treatment.

A shared portfolio is **not** a single combined €2,540 annual exemption. CGT exemptions and reporting depend on the holders' own taxable positions. The legal ownership arrangements in Revolut's terms, the actual facts and the holders' circumstances need to be considered when allocating gains.

See our [Irish capital gains tax guide](/wealth/capital-gains-tax-ireland/) and [CGT calculator](/capital-gains-tax-calculator/).

### ETFs can have different rules

For many investment funds and ETFs covered by Ireland's investment-undertaking or equivalent offshore-fund regimes, the tax treatment differs from ordinary shares. Revenue's [2026 exit-tax guidance](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/ebrief/2026/no-0162026.aspx) provides for a **38% individual rate from 1 January 2026** in relevant cases, and an **eight-year deemed-disposal** rule may apply.

That is not a blanket rate for every product labelled “ETF” or exchange-traded instrument. Classification, residence and the particular fund matter. Joint ownership also does not remove deemed-disposal obligations where they apply.

Neither Revolut's account screen nor a monthly investing target should be treated as a substitute for Irish tax records. Read Compound's [investment tax guide for Ireland](/wealth/investment-tax-ireland/) before choosing a product.

### What records should both holders keep?

At a minimum, keep copies of trade confirmations, purchase and sale dates, euro values and currency conversions, expenses and fees, dividend/interest statements, year-end records and any relevant fund deemed-disposal calculations. Because both holders have access and may trade independently, agree **who maintains the records and who checks tax filing deadlines**.

Revolut's terms specifically warn that joint investing may have legal, tax and inheritance effects different from sole investing. Couples with unequal contributions, complicated tax residence, significant holdings or estate-planning concerns should obtain advice before assuming a 50/50 app view resolves every legal question.

## Is a joint account better than investing separately?

There is no universal answer. Here is the practical trade-off:

| Joint investment account | Two separate investment accounts |
| --- | --- |
| One portfolio for shared goals | Each person retains direct control of their own holdings |
| Both can see transactions and performance | Sharing updates is a separate task |
| Either person may independently trade | Neither person can trade in the other's sole account |
| Assets generally treated as equally held under account terms | Contributions and ownership can be easier to distinguish |
| Potentially simpler to organise one shared strategy | Different investment approaches and risk tolerances can be maintained |
| Account disputes may restrict both holders | One person's account dispute may not affect the other's account |

A joint account can make sense for a clearly shared, long-term goal where both investors understand the risks and genuinely want common ownership. Separate accounts may suit people who contribute unequal amounts, prefer different investment strategies or want to keep legal ownership distinct.

Crucially, **convenience does not improve the expected returns of the shares or funds themselves**. Returns will still depend on the investments purchased, costs, taxes and market performance. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do both people need Revolut investment accounts already?

**Yes.** Both need active sole investment accounts, active personal Revolut accounts and an existing Revolut joint current account with one another, as well as meeting the other eligibility requirements.

### Can one account holder trade without the other?

**Generally, yes.** Revolut's joint investment terms allow either holder to place instructions without the other's approval, although disputes, restrictions and legal obligations can alter this.

### Are shares owned 50/50 even if contributions are unequal?

**Generally, that is how Revolut's joint account terms describe ownership**, subject to applicable law and any rights between the individuals outside the account. Do not assume the app keeps an unequal contribution split.

### Is Revolut’s joint investment account the proposed 2027 PIA?

**No.** Revolut’s currently available joint brokerage product is distinct from the proposed sole-name Irish Personal Investment Account (PIA). Under the draft PIA rules, each eligible partner would open their own tax-framework account; see our [PIA guide for couples, including two contribution limits and separate €50,000 thresholds](/wealth/pia-for-couples-ireland/).

### Is the joint investment account tax-free in Ireland?

**No.** Normal Irish investment tax rules still apply. Direct shares and qualifying ETFs may follow different regimes.

### Can we hold every stock and ETF available on our personal accounts?

**Not necessarily.** The joint account's instrument selection may differ and must be appropriate or suitable for both holders.

## The Compound view

Revolut has solved a real usability problem: a couple can see and manage one household portfolio instead of manually reconciling two. But the decision is about **ownership and shared control**, not just a convenient interface.

Before opening the account, make sure both people understand that either can trade, that equal ownership may be presumed under the contract regardless of who funds the purchases, and that Irish tax administration remains their responsibility. Then compare all-in fees and the product selection with the alternatives.
