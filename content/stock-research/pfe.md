## Pfizer’s newer medicines are growing. Can they move the whole company?

**3 November 2026 · fiscal Q3 results.** Pfizer’s $16.6 billion of revenue was roughly flat year on year on an operational basis, which strips out currency effects. Excluding its COVID products Comirnaty and Paxlovid, revenue grew 7% on the same basis. Both figures can be true: gains elsewhere in the portfolio had to replace lost COVID sales before they could lift the whole company.

Pfizer’s designated group of launched and acquired products generated $3.8 billion and grew 32% operationally. That is about 23 cents of every dollar of company revenue. A smaller portfolio can grow quickly for some time before its gains are large enough to change consolidated growth. For investors assessing the return on acquisitions and research spending, the dollar contribution of this group is as important as its percentage growth.

Pfizer raised the midpoint of annual revenue guidance by $1.5 billion. Its new range is $62 billion to $64 billion. Management also projects adjusted diluted EPS of $2.95 to $3.05 for the year. The distinction between adjusted and reported profit matters here: in Q3 Pfizer reported 56 cents of diluted EPS against 87 cents on its adjusted basis.

The next test is whether sales from newer products keep growing in dollars while total operational revenue begins to rise. Pipeline milestones can improve those prospects, but current sales and guidance provide a firmer basis for judging how far the transition has progressed.
