## At a glance

HRT is an umbrella term for several ways of treating menopause symptoms. The choice depends on the symptoms, whether a uterus is present and individual health risks. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/hrt/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Systemic and local hormone effects during menopause

Oestrogen binds receptors that regulate genes in tissues including bone, the brain and the urogenital tract. Systemic preparations deliver hormone through circulation. Vaginal products are usually chosen for local symptoms. For someone with a uterus, systemic oestrogen generally needs appropriate progestogen protection of the endometrium. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/hrt/)

## What the evidence measures

Evidence for relief of hot flushes, bone outcomes and urogenital symptoms addresses different questions. Risks vary with route, formulation and medical history. Ask how any estimate applies to your starting risk and duration of use.

## Three starting questions

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Symptoms | are they local, systemic or both? |
| Regimen | is progestogen needed and what changes require reassessment? |


## What does systemic mean in a menopause consultation?

Systemic treatment delivers hormone through the bloodstream to multiple tissues. A local vaginal product is generally selected for symptoms in that area. Discuss which symptoms need treatment and which route meets that aim.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Review unexpected bleeding with the treating team. Mention previous clots, hormone-sensitive cancer or new diagnoses before a regimen is chosen or changed. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/hrt/)
