A HUNDRED YEARS, IN PERSPECTIVE

Different chapters.
Room to change.

Possible priorities, not milestones you have to meet.

0A LIFE IN CHAPTERS100

AGES 0–19

Foundations, with support.

Money
Focus on a child’s security and access to opportunities. Everyday confidence with money can develop gradually; childhood is not a financial optimisation project.
Health
Think about care, play, food, rest and a supportive environment, with the adults around a child helping to meet their needs.
Connection & purpose
Make space for belonging, friendships and curiosity. There is no need to have a future identity or career worked out.

AGES 20–39

Build a life that can adapt.

Money
Get to know your everyday costs and what support is available. Saving, learning or rebuilding may take different forms at different times.
Health
Look for ways to care for yourself that fit work, study, disability or caring responsibilities. Start from the life you actually have.
Connection & purpose
Keep room for friendship, interests and learning. Marriage, parenthood and home ownership are not required milestones.

AGES 40–59

Maintain, and leave room to reinvent.

Money
Revisit the assumptions in your plans when income, housing or responsibilities change. Consider whether another working chapter could suit you.
Health
Notice the activities you value and the support you need to keep doing them. Bring health concerns to an appropriate professional.
Connection & purpose
Consider which relationships and interests you want to carry forward. Giving care and receiving help can both belong in this chapter.

AGES 60–79

Redesign work and time.

Money
Explore how household costs and income would fit together under different working arrangements. Check actual pension rules separately.
Health
Think about capability, suitable activities and the help that makes daily life easier. Adaptation can be part of independence.
Connection & purpose
If work changes, consider where company, structure and enjoyment could come from. Volunteering, learning and rest are possibilities, not obligations.

AGES 80–100

Support, participation and autonomy.

Money
Consider whether everyday arrangements are understandable and manageable, and who you would trust to help if you wanted assistance.
Health
Put comfort, appropriate care and your own priorities at the centre. Needing support does not make a life less valuable.
Connection & purpose
Keep preferences and relationships visible. There can still be things to enjoy, learn, decide and contribute, in ways that suit you.
