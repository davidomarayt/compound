## What this calculator is for

This calculator compares an employee salary with contractor income after selected business costs and simplified Irish tax assumptions. The purpose is to expose the economics that a headline day rate can hide.

## How to read the result

Compare **net annual cash**, but also look at the assumptions behind billable days and costs. A contractor can quote a much higher gross annual figure while funding unpaid leave, downtime, pension, insurance, accounting and other costs personally.

## Worked example

A €500 day rate across 220 billable days is €110,000 gross turnover. But 220 billable days already assumes time away from billing, and business expenses plus personal tax reduce the amount available to the contractor.

## Irish context

Employee and self-employed tax treatment differs. Employees may receive benefits such as paid annual leave, employer pension contributions, sick pay and employment protections. Contractors can have deductible business costs, but genuine self-employment status depends on the actual working relationship, not just the contract label.

## What can change the answer

Billable days, day rate, allowable expenses, pension contributions, tax credits, VAT registration, insurance and downtime can move the comparison substantially. A one-month gap between contracts can erase a large part of the apparent premium.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not annualise a day rate using 365 or even 260 days. Do not ignore unpaid holidays and public holidays. Do not assume every expense is tax deductible. And do not choose contractor status solely for tax reasons if the legal reality is employment.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many billable days should I use?
Use a realistic figure after holidays, public holidays, administration, training and downtime.

### Is contractor income taxed at a lower rate?
Not automatically.

### Does the calculator decide employment status?
No.

### Does it include VAT?
Not as personal income; VAT collected for Revenue is not ordinary turnover profit.

## Method and limitations

The tool annualises the entered contractor rate and billable days, subtracts selected business costs and applies the simplified 2026 employee/self-employed tax functions implemented in the site. It is not a full tax return or legal employment-status test.
