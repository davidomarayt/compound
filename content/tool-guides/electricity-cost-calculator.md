## What this calculator is for

This calculator estimates the **incremental running cost of an appliance** from its power, usage pattern and electricity unit rate. It is useful for understanding what heaters, immersions, dehumidifiers, dryers, EV-related equipment and other loads can add to a bill.

## How to read the result

The calculator focuses on **usage charges**, not the household's fixed standing charge. Reducing appliance kWh lowers the variable part of the bill, while a standing charge generally remains whether this individual appliance is used or not. On time-of-use plans, when the appliance runs can matter as much as how many kWh it uses.

## Worked example

Using 4,200 kWh a year at €0.30/kWh gives €1,260 in energy charges before standing charges, VAT, levies and any time-of-use differences. Moving some usage to a cheaper night rate can reduce cost without reducing total kWh.

## Irish context

Irish bills can include unit charges, standing charges, VAT and the PSO levy. Smart-meter tariffs can vary by time of day, and from June 2026 dynamic tariffs can vary every half-hour. CRU advises customers to compare tariffs using their actual consumption pattern where possible.

## What can change the answer

Annual consumption, day/night split, smart tariff windows, supplier discounts, standing charge and price changes all matter. Electric heating, heat pumps and EV charging can materially change a household's load profile.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not compare suppliers using the unit rate alone. A lower unit rate can be offset by a higher standing charge or discount conditions. Do not assume the advertised rate applies all day on a smart tariff. And use actual kWh from bills rather than guessing where possible.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a kWh?
One kilowatt-hour is the energy used by a 1 kW appliance running for one hour.

### Does the calculator include standing charges?
Where entered, yes.

### Do smart tariffs always save money?
No. Savings depend on when you use electricity.

### Are dynamic tariffs the same as day/night tariffs?
No. Dynamic prices can change every half-hour.

## Use your own load profile where possible

Annual kWh is the starting point, but two households using the same 4,000 kWh can face different bills if one consumes heavily at expensive peak times and the other shifts flexible loads overnight.

Smart-meter data can reveal that pattern. Large flexible uses such as EV charging, immersion heating, dishwashers, laundry and batteries can sometimes be moved without reducing comfort.

When comparing tariffs, calculate at least three elements separately:

- the annual standing charge;
- the weighted cost of the kWh you expect to use in each time band;
- any conditions attached to discounts or introductory offers.

A tariff with a very cheap night window can be excellent for an EV household but poor for a home with most consumption during the evening peak.

Also remember that electricity prices can change during the contract period. This calculator is most useful as a snapshot using today's tariff and your recent annual consumption, then repeated when a fixed discount or contract period ends.

## Advanced mode: cycling loads and off-peak use

A nameplate wattage is a maximum or rated input, not always a continuous draw. Thermostatically controlled appliances can cycle on and off, while variable-speed equipment can spend long periods below rated power. Advanced mode therefore lets you apply a duty-cycle percentage to the hours switched on.

You can also allocate a percentage of this appliance's kWh to an off-peak rate. Compound calculates a weighted unit rate from the main and off-peak tariffs and applies it to the modelled energy use.

For a device that actually draws 2 kW continuously, one hour uses 2 kWh. If a 2 kW appliance is switched on for one hour but averages a 50% duty cycle, the model uses 1 kWh. A plug-in energy monitor or manufacturer telemetry is preferable to guessing where available.

## Standing charges are deliberately excluded

This tool answers “what does running this appliance add?” rather than “what is my full electricity bill?”. A household standing charge is normally payable regardless of whether one particular appliance runs, so allocating the whole fixed charge to that device would overstate its incremental cost.

For whole-tariff comparisons, evaluate standing charges, discounts, VAT, levies and your complete time-of-use consumption profile separately.

## Method and limitations

The tool converts watts to kW, applies the entered operating hours and days, and in Advanced mode applies the duty cycle and weighted main/off-peak unit rate. It does not reproduce every supplier discount, levy, VAT treatment, standing charge or half-hourly dynamic tariff.
