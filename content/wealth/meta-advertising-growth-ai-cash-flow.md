*Research date: 18 October 2026. Reported figures are historical; illustrative calculations are identified below.*

Meta's advertising performance and its infrastructure bill answer different investor questions. Better advertising can strengthen the operating business, while spending on the next generation of products absorbs most of the resulting cash.

The investment case therefore needs two tests: are advertisers receiving enough value to keep spending, and is Meta converting that demand into attractive returns after the cost of building and maintaining its technology?

## What the advertising figures actually show

In its [second-quarter 2026 release](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx), Meta reported a 14% increase in ad impressions and a 12% increase in average price per ad. The quarter ended 30 June. Group revenue was $60.801 billion, while advertising revenue was $59.363 billion—approximately **97.6% of the total**.

Higher impressions and higher average pricing can reinforce each other, but the percentages should not simply be added. Multiplying 1.14 by 1.12 gives 1.2768, or approximately **27.7% combined growth**. The company's reported advertising growth was 27%; the operational growth percentages are rounded, so this simplified calculation is not an exact reconciliation.

It also does not prove that AI caused all the improvement. Demand, placement mix, advertiser budgets and other factors can affect the observed outcome. Causation needs more evidence than two improving headline metrics.

## Cash conversion supplies the second test

Meta's [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026050705/meta-20260630.htm) reports quarterly operating cash flow of $31.862 billion, property-and-equipment purchases of $30.116 billion and finance-lease principal payments of $0.962 billion. On the definition used in the results release, free cash flow was therefore **$0.784 billion**.

| Q2 2026 cash-flow component | US dollars |
|---|---:|
| Operating cash flow | $31.862bn |
| Property-and-equipment purchases | −$30.116bn |
| Finance-lease principal payments | −$0.962bn |
| Free cash flow | $0.784bn |

That leaves approximately **2.5% of operating cash flow** after these investment payments. The calculation does not mean the other 97.5% was wasted. It shows how little immediate cash remained under this definition, which makes the returns on new capacity particularly consequential.

## A sensitivity that makes the spending question concrete

Suppose operating cash flow rose 10%, with the two investment-payment lines unchanged. The resulting cash remainder would be approximately **$3.97 billion**. If those investment payments also rose 10%, the remainder would instead be approximately **$0.86 billion**.

These are hypothetical arithmetic cases, not forecasts. They exclude changes in working capital, payment timing and financing. Their purpose is to show why stronger operating performance may produce only a modest improvement in cash available after investment if spending grows at the same pace.

Investors should therefore avoid celebrating revenue growth in isolation or treating the entire investment programme as a permanent expense that can never earn a return. Both shortcuts skip the central question.

## Separate current costs from future opportunities

[Associated Press reporting](https://apnews.com/article/bcbc62dde6d2cac724e3b3385fcabeab) highlighted legal charges and severance costs alongside the quarter's revenue growth and profit decline. Those items complicate comparisons, but labelling a cost unusual does not make it irrelevant to shareholders. The cash and economic consequences still belong in the assessment.

The bullish case is that better recommendations and advertising tools improve outcomes sufficiently for advertisers to spend more. That could create a profitable feedback loop: useful ads support budgets, budgets fund product improvements, and improved products retain users and advertisers.

The bearish case is that Meta must commit large sums before proving which new products will monetise. Stronger engagement is not the same as a commercially valuable new business, and higher average ad prices are only sustainable if advertisers remain satisfied with the return.

Regulatory and legal uncertainty adds another reason to avoid valuing every dollar of growth as equally dependable. An operating thesis should allow for costs that interrupt the expected path, rather than rely on a perfectly smooth forecast.

## What would count as better evidence?

Look for a durable combination of advertising demand, controlled costs and improving cash conversion. Examine impression growth and pricing together, and distinguish group revenue from advertising revenue. Compare investment cash payments on the same definition each quarter, including finance-lease principal where the company includes it.

Most importantly, ask what each new investment allows Meta to earn that it could not earn before. More computing capacity is an input. A profitable customer outcome is the result investors need.
