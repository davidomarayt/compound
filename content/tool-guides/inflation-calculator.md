## What this calculator is for

Inflation measures how the general price level changes over time. This calculator shows both how much a current cost could rise under a constant inflation assumption and how the purchasing power of a fixed euro amount can fall.

## How to read the result

The two outputs answer opposite questions. **Future cost** asks what today's amount might cost later. **Purchasing power** asks what a fixed future euro amount would be worth in today's money.

## Worked example

At 3% annual inflation, something costing €10,000 today would cost roughly €13,439 after 10 years if that rate persisted. Conversely, €10,000 received in 10 years would have purchasing power closer to €7,441 in today's terms.

## Irish context

Ireland's official consumer-price data are published by the CSO through the Consumer Price Index. Actual inflation changes month to month and year to year; a constant rate is a planning assumption, not a forecast.

## What can change the answer

Small changes in the assumed rate become significant over long horizons because inflation compounds. The relevant inflation rate can also differ from your personal spending pattern: housing, childcare, healthcare or education costs may move differently from the overall CPI.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not use one recent high-inflation year as a permanent long-term assumption without testing alternatives. Do not compare future nominal savings with today's prices. And do not assume your personal cost of living moves exactly with headline CPI.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is inflation always positive?
No. Prices can fall in some periods, although persistent deflation is uncommon in modern Ireland.

### Is CPI my personal inflation rate?
Not exactly. CPI is an index for a representative basket.

### Why does 3% matter so much over decades?
Because the price level compounds.

### Does this predict future inflation?
No.

## Method and limitations

The calculator compounds the entered amount at the chosen annual inflation rate for the selected years. Purchasing power uses the inverse of the same compounding factor.
