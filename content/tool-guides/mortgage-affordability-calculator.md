## What this calculator is for

This calculator separates two ideas that are often confused: **how much the regulatory LTI framework may permit** and **how much monthly repayment you choose to regard as affordable**.

## How to read the result

The payment-percentage field is your own modelling assumption, not an official Central Bank affordability rule. The calculator converts that monthly capacity into a mortgage amount and compares it with the LTI ceiling.

## Worked example

For an €80,000 first-time-buyer household, 4× income gives €320,000 before exceptions. If your chosen payment cap and test rate support only €285,000, the payment constraint is the tighter one. That is the useful signal.

## Irish context

Central Bank rules set macroprudential LTI/LTV limits, while lenders conduct their own affordability and credit assessment. Gross income is only the starting point; repayments come from net pay after taxes and other commitments.

## What can change the answer

Interest rate, mortgage term, existing debt and your chosen payment cap can all move the result materially. A longer term lowers the monthly repayment but usually increases total interest. A lender may also stress-test repayments at a higher rate than the current product rate.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not interpret 30% of gross income as an official Irish rule; it is simply a scenario default. Do not ignore childcare, dependants, irregular income or large non-debt expenses. And do not use a long term solely to make the repayment look affordable.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a bank offer less than this?
Yes.

### Is the default payment percentage a recommendation?
No.

### Does this include childcare?
Not directly.

### Does an LTI exception make a mortgage affordable?
Not necessarily. It changes the regulatory ceiling, not your household cash flow.

## Method and limitations

The tool applies the standard LTI multiple, calculates the mortgage supported by the entered monthly payment, rate and term, and uses the lower result. It does not reproduce a lender underwriting model.
