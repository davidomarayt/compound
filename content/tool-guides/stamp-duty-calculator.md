## What this calculator is for

This tool calculates standard residential Stamp Duty using Revenue's progressive property rates. It is intended for a straightforward residential purchase rather than a complex transfer.

## How to read the result

The key idea is that the rates are progressive. The highest rate that applies does **not** apply to the whole purchase price. The calculator shows both the duty and the effective rate across the full price.

## Worked example

On a €400,000 home, 1% gives €4,000. On a €1.2 million home, the first €1 million is charged at 1% (€10,000) and the next €200,000 at 2% (€4,000), giving €14,000 in total.

## Irish context

Revenue's current standard residential bands are 1% on the first €1 million, 2% on the portion from €1 million to €1.5 million, and 6% on the portion above €1.5 million. Special rules can apply to bulk acquisitions, mixed-use property, gifts, exchanges and other transactions.

## What can change the answer

The final liability can differ where the transaction is not an ordinary cash purchase of one residential property. Legal structure, reliefs, exemptions, market-value rules and the nature of the property can matter.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not treat Stamp Duty as part of the mortgage deposit. It is a separate upfront cost. Do not assume first-time-buyer status creates a general exemption. And do not use this simple calculator for commercial or mixed-use transactions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Stamp Duty included in the mortgage repayment?
No.

### Are first-time buyers exempt?
There is no general exemption purely because it is your first home.

### Does this cover commercial property?
No.

### Does it cover gifts or transfers between relatives?
No. Those can involve different valuation and tax rules.

## Who pays and when?

In a normal home purchase, the buyer's solicitor usually handles the Stamp Duty return and payment as part of the conveyancing process. From the buyer's perspective, that makes Stamp Duty a **completion cash requirement** rather than an ongoing annual cost.

The practical planning consequence is simple: keep the duty amount separate from the deposit. A buyer with exactly enough cash for a 10% deposit but nothing left for Stamp Duty, legal fees and other outlays is not actually fully funded for completion.

For high-value properties, progressive bands matter. The jump to the 2% or 6% marginal band does not mean the entire property suddenly becomes taxed at that higher rate.

If the transaction involves a gift, exchange, mixed residential/commercial use, multiple properties or a corporate purchaser, the legal instrument and special rules can become more important than the basic price. That is the point where the solicitor or tax adviser, not a general calculator, should determine the final liability.

## Method and limitations

The calculator applies 1% to the first €1 million, 2% to the next €500,000 and 6% to the balance above €1.5 million. It does not model reliefs, exemptions, bulk-acquisition surcharges or mixed-use apportionment.
