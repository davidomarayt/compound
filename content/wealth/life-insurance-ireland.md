**Life insurance is not primarily about insuring the person who dies. It is about making sure the people who depend on them can still pay their bills.** For a couple with a mortgage and young children, the question is not simply whether there is already mortgage protection. It is what the household would live on after losing one person's earnings—or the childcare and unpaid work provided by a stay-at-home parent.

In Ireland, the usual starting point is **term life insurance**: you choose an amount, choose a period (often until children are independent), pay premiums, and an eligible claim pays a lump sum if the insured dies during the term. Unlike whole-of-life insurance, standard term cover expires without paying out if you survive the term.

<div class="li26 li26-callout"><p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> There is no universally best or cheapest life insurer in Ireland. For a family seeking protection during the years their children depend on them, <strong>level-term cover</strong> is often a useful starting comparison; <strong>joint life</strong> ordinarily pays once on first death, while <strong>dual life</strong> can pay independently for each insured life. The amount of cover depends on your family's actual financial shortfall, not just a rule such as ten times salary. <a href="https://www.ccpc.ie/manage-your-money/day-to-day-finances/insurance/life-insurance">CCPC consumer guidance</a>.</p></div>

<h2 id="needs">How much life insurance do you need in Ireland?</h2>

Start with the **financial gap left behind**, not a generic salary multiple. Estimate the extra annual amount your family would need if you were no longer there. Then allow for how long it would be required, debts not covered by another policy, childcare or education costs, and assets or other benefits genuinely available to survivors.

A rule of thumb like "ten times your salary" may be better than no estimate, but can substantially overinsure or underinsure someone. The calculation below is transparent enough to change as your circumstances evolve.

### How long should your life cover last?

A useful first question is: **when would the need substantially reduce?** Parents might compare coverage until their youngest child finishes education or until the surviving partner can maintain the household independently. Other households may instead need cover to bridge to retirement or until a business or estate liability ends.

Term length is not automatically the same as your mortgage term. Mortgage protection addresses the secured loan; family cover addresses the rest of life after a death. ### What does life insurance cost at ages 25, 30, 40 and 50?

There is no credible single age table without holding **sum insured, term, medical status, smoker status and policy type** constant. Age 40 will generally cost more to insure than age 25 under otherwise comparable newly underwritten terms, but the actual euro difference cannot be established from age alone.

To obtain a meaningful comparison, ask at least three providers for the **same initial cover** (for example €250,000 or €500,000), the **same term**, the **same lives insured**, and premiums including all levies or charges. Compare prices only *after* any medical underwriting is complete. The tables above provide scale, not actuarial age-based pricing.

### Can life insurance premiums rise?

A guaranteed-premium term policy ordinarily holds the agreed price throughout the specified term, unless an optional indexation arrangement changes the benefit and premium. A reviewable or unit-linked whole-of-life contract can be repriced. Ask the insurer whether the quoted premium is guaranteed, reviewable, inflation-linked or potentially reduced by investment performance. [CCPC's whole-of-life explanation](https://www.ccpc.ie/manage-your-money/day-to-day-finances/insurance/whole-of-life-insurance) distinguishes these structures.

### Which life insurance is best for a young family?

For many families with dependent children, it is useful to **compare level-term family life cover first**, then ask whether they also need independent cover on each parent's life, inflation protection or illness benefits. That is a framework, not a personalised recommendation. The cheapest policy is not necessarily the one that provides the right payout at the right time.

For example, two parents choose what is described as **€300,000 cover**. On a joint first-death contract, the qualifying first death usually produces one €300,000 claim and the contract ends. With suitable dual-life cover, a qualifying death may pay €300,000 and the surviving life can remain insured for another claim under the contract. Dual life may cost more; it is not always better for every household. Read the [CCPC term life guide](https://www.ccpc.ie/manage-your-money/day-to-day-finances/insurance/life-insurance).

### What about serious illness and terminal illness?

Life-only cover does **not** generally pay merely because you receive a cancer diagnosis or become unable to work. A specified serious illness benefit may pay on diagnosis of a condition **meeting the policy's exact definition and severity rules**. An *accelerated* illness benefit can reduce or exhaust the death benefit; a genuinely additional benefit may have a different structure. Terminal-illness advance payments depend on the precise contract and prognosis criteria.

If the risk you want to protect is loss of pay through illness or injury, also read our [Income Protection Insurance Ireland guide](/wealth/income-protection-insurance-ireland/). The [CCPC guide to serious illness insurance](https://www.ccpc.ie/manage-your-money/day-to-day-finances/insurance/serious-illness-insurance) explains the differences.

<h2 id="who">Who needs life insurance—and who might need less?</h2>

- **Parents with young children:** income, accommodation, childcare and education costs can continue long after a parent's death.
- **A stay-at-home parent:** replacing unpaid childcare and home responsibilities can be expensive even when there was no lost salary.
- **A couple with one main earner:** consider whether the surviving partner could meet expenses on a single income.
- **A sole trader or company director:** surviving family members may need funds separate from business loans, succession funding or key-person insurance.
- **Someone with no dependants and ample assets:** extra family term cover may provide little value, although funeral and estate considerations remain.
- **Older households with independent children:** a temporary income-replacement need may have ended; estate planning is a different question.

### Does death-in-service mean you can skip life insurance?

Not necessarily. Some employers provide a multiple of salary through a group life or pension arrangement. But **the actual sum, beneficiary rights, eligibility and conditions matter**. Cover may be tied to continuing employment; it can disappear when you change jobs, become self-employed or retire.

Request written scheme details from HR or your pension provider. Ask whether payment is discretionary, whether a nomination is held, whether the benefit is on top of pension assets, and whether it remains payable during long-term leave. Include only the amount likely to be available to your family when using the calculator above.

### Should both parents be insured?

A household can face a major financial disruption whether the parent who dies was employed full time or managed childcare and the home. A comparison of single policies, joint-first-death and dual cover is often more informative than assuming one partner needs all the insurance. Discuss the care burden and surviving household income for each parent's death separately.

<h2 id="tax">Life insurance and inheritance tax in Ireland</h2>

**Ordinary term life insurance is not an investment return.** A death payment is generally paid as a life assurance benefit rather than taxable employment income. However, the eventual recipient can have **Capital Acquisitions Tax (CAT)** implications depending on legal ownership, beneficiary arrangements, their relationship to the deceased, earlier gifts and inheritances, and any reliefs.

For a **spouse or civil partner**, an inheritance from the other spouse or civil partner is exempt from CAT. The same blanket exemption does **not** automatically apply to an unmarried cohabiting partner. Some qualifying cohabitant court-order reliefs exist, but they are not equivalent to being married. Check [Revenue's spouse exemption](https://www.revenue.ie/en/gains-gifts-and-inheritance/cat-exemptions/transfers-between-spouses-civil-partners/index.aspx) and [CAT thresholds and aggregation rules](https://www.revenue.ie/en/gains-gifts-and-inheritance/cat-thresholds-rates-and-aggregation-rules/index.aspx).

### Section 72 life insurance: insurance specifically for inheritance tax

A properly structured **Section 72 qualifying policy** can enable insurance proceeds to be used to settle eligible inheritance tax without creating a separate CAT bill **on the portion used for that tax**. It is not a general rule that every life insurance payment is CAT-free. Revenue specifies policy-approval, premium-payment, purpose and timing requirements, and excess proceeds not used for the qualifying liability may remain subject to CAT.

For example, an estate containing a valuable family asset may create an inheritance tax bill without enough liquid cash for beneficiaries to pay it. A correctly structured Section 72 policy may help provide liquidity rather than requiring a hurried sale. It requires specialist financial and tax advice: see [Revenue's sections 72–73 manual](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm/capital-acquisitions-tax/cat-manual/part-09-exemptions/sections-72-73-relief-in-respect-of-certain-policies-of-insurance.pdf) and our [Inheritance Tax Ireland guide](/wealth/inheritance-tax-ireland/).

### Can life insurance premiums qualify for income-tax relief?

For most individuals, **ordinary personal term-life premiums are not a routine income-tax deduction**. Certain approved pension-term-assurance arrangements may qualify for income-tax relief subject to Revenue pension rules; they are not interchangeable with ordinary family insurance. Whole-of-life policies with an investment element can also raise separate exit-tax issues when an investment gain is realised—do not treat them as identical to pure term life cover. [Revenue's life assurance exit-tax guidance](https://www.revenue.ie/en/companies-and-charities/financial-services/life-assurance/exit-tax.aspx) explains this distinction.

### Seven mistakes that are easy to avoid

1. **Counting mortgage protection as freely spendable cash.** It is generally intended for the lender's secured loan.
2. **Buying a salary multiple without calculating costs.** Families have different survivor incomes, savings, dependants and care needs.
3. **Ignoring the unpaid work of a parent.** Childcare, transport and household support have real replacement costs.
4. **Comparing joint and dual policies as though they were identical.** Payout structures differ.
5. **Assuming a quote is final before underwriting.** Medical and lifestyle information can materially affect the accepted price.
6. **Assuming insurance lasts after changing jobs.** Employer death-in-service benefits may end.
7. **Cancelling existing cover before replacement is in force.** Older cover can be more valuable than a cheaper-looking unaccepted quote.

### The process from application to payout

First decide what financial problem needs solving. Request comparable cover quotations and complete all medical, smoker, occupation and family-history questions accurately. The insurer may seek a GP report or examination. Before paying, review who is insured, who owns the contract, who receives money, the date cover actually begins and how premiums can change.

After a death, the personal representative, named recipient or surviving insured should contact the insurer or broker, obtain claim forms and provide the required documents, commonly a death certificate and policy details. Timing, assignment and documentation will depend on the policy. Keep the number and contact details with the family's estate paperwork. <h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions about life insurance in Ireland</h2>

### Is life insurance worth it if I already have mortgage protection?

Potentially. Mortgage protection generally targets the outstanding home loan; family cover is intended to support other expenses after the mortgage has been addressed. The overlap depends on policy assignment, payout type and the household's resources.

### What is the cheapest life insurance in Ireland?

There is no one cheapest company for every person. Several providers advertise low minimum premiums, but those figures do not tell you the cost of €250,000 or €500,000 cover for your particular age, term and health. Request like-for-like accepted quotes.

### Can I get €500,000 of life insurance in Ireland?

You can seek a quote for that sum, but affordability, acceptance, medical underwriting, financial justification and insurer limits can apply. Do not assume an advertised minimum premium applies to €500,000 of cover.

### What is the difference between life insurance and life assurance?

The words are sometimes used interchangeably. In product comparisons, *term insurance* usually refers to fixed-duration protection, while *whole-of-life assurance* ordinarily means cover designed to remain in force for life subject to the contract.

### Does life insurance pay out for cancer?

Not automatically on diagnosis. Life-only cover pays when the insured dies during the policy term. Specified serious illness policies or terminal-illness benefits may provide payments only if policy conditions are met.

### Does life insurance cover suicide in Ireland?

Policy exclusions and waiting periods vary. Some contracts restrict claims during an initial period. Do not assume identical treatment across insurers; inspect the wording before purchase.

### Can you get life insurance with a medical condition?

You can apply. An insurer may accept standard terms, add a premium loading, restrict benefits, postpone a decision or decline cover. A broker may be able to seek different underwriting assessments from multiple insurers. Complete all questions accurately.

### Is life insurance taxable in Ireland?

The death payout is generally not an ordinary income-tax gain. Recipients can still have inheritance-tax implications, depending on beneficiary, ownership, prior gifts and reliefs. Spouses and civil partners are CAT-exempt on transfers between them; Section 72 has specific qualifying conditions.

### Does a death-in-service pension benefit replace life cover?

It may reduce the amount of additional cover needed, but verify actual scheme terms, nominated recipients and whether it continues when you leave your employer.

### Should life cover run to age 65 or 70?

Base the term on the period dependants need financial support, not an arbitrary birthday. Consider children, partner income, debts and retirement assets.

### Is a joint policy or two single policies better?

Compare joint-first-death against dual life and two separate policies. The number and timing of payouts, survivor's cover, underwriting and final cost all matter.

### Can I cancel or switch life insurance?

Ordinary term cover can often be cancelled, but it usually has no cash value. Obtain and activate suitable replacement cover before stopping an existing policy; later age or changes in health may increase premiums or make acceptance difficult.

## The bottom line

For Irish families, **the amount of cover matters more than the cheapest advertised monthly premium**. Work out the likely surviving household shortfall, subtract benefits and assets that can genuinely be used, and obtain comparable underwritten quotes for an appropriate term and policy structure.
