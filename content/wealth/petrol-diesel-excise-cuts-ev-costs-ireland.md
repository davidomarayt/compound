<p><em>News analysis · 24 September 2026. The extension is under consideration and has not yet been finalised.</em></p>

The Government is considering keeping Ireland's temporary **petrol and diesel excise-duty reductions** in place until **late February or early March 2027**, potentially delaying fuel-price increases that were due to begin from November.

[RTÉ reports](https://www.rte.ie/news/politics/2026/0923/1592651-ireland-politics/) that Tánaiste and Minister for Finance Simon Harris and Minister for Public Expenditure Jack Chambers have indicated that the reductions could remain in place into spring.

For motorists, the immediate question is simple: **how much does that change the cost of running a petrol or diesel car — and how does it compare with an EV?**

## How much are the current fuel-tax reductions worth?

The current measures are equivalent to about:

- **27 cent per litre on petrol**
- **32 cent per litre on diesel**

relative to the full-duty rates, according to RTÉ.

On a 50-litre fill, that is roughly **€13.50 for petrol** or **€16 for diesel** compared with prices if the reductions were fully removed.

The existing plan had already deferred the restoration of excise increases, with the remaining reductions due to be phased out from November through the end of February. The Government is now considering pushing that process out further.

## What does this mean for EV running costs?

Lower petrol and diesel prices narrow the **energy-cost gap** between combustion cars and EVs, but they do not tell you which vehicle is cheaper to run overall.

For an EV, the result depends heavily on:

- annual kilometres driven;
- the car's real-world kWh/100 km;
- charging losses;
- your home electricity tariff; and
- how much charging happens at home versus public chargers.

A driver charging mainly at home on a competitive night rate can have a very different annual electricity cost from someone relying heavily on rapid public charging.

The same applies to petrol and diesel: annual fuel cost depends on your vehicle's actual litres per 100 km and the pump price you pay.
