**Netflix is becoming more profitable, but its latest earnings make the growth question harder to ignore. Advertising is taking longer to deliver than expected, and the strongest quarterly margin comes with an expense-timing caveat.**

Netflix reported **$12.87 billion of revenue and $3.66 billion of net income** in its latest results. Profitability improved, but the investment story is less straightforward than the earnings growth suggests: revenue growth slowed, management reduced its advertising outlook, and the strongest quarterly margin benefited from the timing of expenses.

The figures come from Netflix’s [shareholder letter furnished as Exhibit 99.1 to its 20 October SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000290/ex991_q326.htm). They are reported results and management forecasts, not analyst estimates. See our [Netflix stock and earnings page](/stocks/nflx/) for the interactive share-price chart and continuously updated financial tables.

## What did Netflix earn in Q3 2026?

Revenue reached **$12.874 billion**, up **11.8% year on year** and **2.5% from Q2**. Operating income rose to **$4.459 billion**, while diluted earnings per share reached **$0.87**, compared with $0.80 in Q2 and $0.59 a year earlier. Net income increased approximately **43.9% year on year**.

Subscription revenue was the main growth driver, supported by membership increases and pricing. Advertising also contributed. Netflix said customer churn improved year on year globally and in the US, and performed better than its forecast. That is encouraging evidence on retention, but it does not establish that viewing growth has accelerated everywhere.

*Source update, 28 October 2026:* The subsequent [Q3 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000291/nflx-20260930.htm) quantifies those contributions: subscription revenue increased by **$1.057 billion** year on year, while advertising revenue increased by **$256 million**. These are increases from the prior-year quarter, not the total revenue of either business. Advertising can therefore contribute to reported growth even while management reduces its forecast for the full year.

The operating trend is clearer than the net-income trend. Revenue and operating profit increased in each quarter, even as year-on-year revenue growth slowed. Q3 operating income grew about **6.3% from Q2**, faster than revenue.

**Q1 profit is not a clean recurring baseline.** Netflix’s [Q1 SEC 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000138/nflx-20260331.htm) records a **$2.8 billion termination fee** from the abandoned Warner Bros. Discovery transaction in interest and other income. That payment also contributed to operating cash flow. Comparing Q3 with Q1 without this qualification would obscure the underlying business performance.

## Why the 34.6% operating margin needs context

Netflix’s Q3 operating margin rose from 28.2% a year earlier to **34.6%**. However, management attributed the outperformance against its forecast primarily to expense timing, alongside slightly higher revenue.

That distinction matters. Expenses shifting between quarters can make one period unusually strong without changing full-year profitability to the same extent. Investors should therefore assess the quarter alongside Netflix’s **31.5% full-year operating-margin forecast**, rather than assuming the Q3 margin is its new annual run rate.

## Advertising: a $300 million reduction in the annual forecast

Netflix now expects approximately **$2.7 billion of advertising revenue in 2026**, down from about $3 billion. That is a **10% reduction** from the earlier target, which management attributes to a slower-than-expected rollout of certain advertising-product capabilities.

This is a reduction in forecast revenue, not a disclosed 10% fall in advertising sales from last year. Netflix also says subscription revenue growth has exceeded its expectations. Together, those movements leave its **$51.1 billion full-year revenue forecast** just 0.2% below the midpoint of the previous range.

The analytical question is whether the advertising delays mainly shift revenue into later periods or point to execution problems that persist. The filing does not settle that question. Future product delivery and advertising revenue will be more useful evidence than assuming either a rapid recovery or permanent damage.

## Q4 guidance: more revenue, a lower quarterly margin

For the quarter ending **31 December 2026**, Netflix forecasts:

- **Revenue of $13.442 billion**, up 11.5% year on year.
- **Operating income of $3.500 billion** and an operating margin of **26.0%**.
- **Net income of $2.787 billion** and diluted EPS of **$0.67**.

These are forecasts, not results. Management says headcount reductions will reduce the Q4 operating margin by approximately **1.2 percentage points**, and the full-year margin by about 0.3 percentage points.

The forecast Q4 margin is below Q3’s 34.6%, but above the 24.5% recorded in Q4 2025. Both comparisons matter: a sequential decline does not automatically mean year-on-year deterioration, and restructuring costs do not explain the whole quarter-to-quarter movement.

## Cash flow and buybacks: look beyond EPS

Netflix generated **$3.162 billion of operating cash flow** in Q3. After **$279 million of property and equipment purchases**, its reported non-GAAP free cash flow was **$2.884 billion**, up approximately 8.4% from a year earlier.

For the first nine months, free cash flow totalled **$9.503 billion**. That total includes the benefit of the Q1 termination payment. It should not all be treated as recurring cash generation from subscriptions and advertising.

Netflix cut its full-year free-cash-flow forecast from approximately **$12.5 billion to $12.0 billion**, primarily because it expects a real-estate transaction to close in Q4. The advertising and cash-flow forecast reductions therefore have different stated causes.

The company spent **$4.8 billion repurchasing 63.9 million shares** during Q3, exceeding that quarter’s free cash flow. It ended the quarter with approximately $8.2 billion of cash and equivalents and $15.3 billion of gross debt. Netflix says the roughly $1 billion increase in gross debt from Q2 prefunds a debt maturity in Q4.

Buybacks reduce the share count and can support EPS, but their value depends on the price paid and the cash needed elsewhere. They should be assessed alongside content investment and liquidity, rather than treated as an automatic shareholder benefit.

## What matters next for NFLX investors?

The positive case rests on rising operating profit, resilient retention and a large subscription business that continues to grow. The challenges are slower revenue growth, advertising execution and the need to turn content investment into sustained engagement.

Netflix acknowledges investor concern about viewing-hours growth and says it wants to accelerate it. Its Q3 India performance offers one concrete example of progress: management reports **24% growth in viewing hours** and approximately **18% revenue growth on a currency-neutral basis**. Those are India-specific figures, not global growth rates.

The next results should be judged on advertising delivery, retention after price changes, margins after timing effects and restructuring, and cash generation excluding unusual receipts. For comparisons across streaming and digital advertising, see [Disney](/stocks/dis/), [Amazon](/stocks/amzn/) and [Alphabet](/stocks/googl/).

Netflix’s Q3 shows that profit can grow faster than revenue. Whether that translates into an attractive return for investors depends on the valuation paid and how durable those profits prove to be.
