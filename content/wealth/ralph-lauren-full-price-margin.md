**Ralph Lauren’s reported gross margin jumped 620 basis points, but a tariff refund flattered the comparison. Unlike some refund-driven quarters, the adjusted result still shows a meaningful improvement.**

For the 13 weeks ended 26 September 2026, revenue rose 9% to **$2.19 billion**. Reported gross margin reached 74.2%, helped by tariff refunds. After the company’s adjustments, gross margin was **70.5%, up 250 basis points**, driven by higher average unit retail, geographic mix and lower product costs.

The investment question is whether that adjusted improvement reflects durable brand elevation—or favourable mix and timing that become harder to repeat when holiday growth slows.

For Ralph Lauren’s maintained financial record, see the [Ralph Lauren stock profile](/stocks/rl/). This article focuses on the quality of pricing, mix and margin rather than the refund headline.

## Pricing is contributing without stopping demand

Global direct-to-consumer comparable-store sales increased 10%, while average unit retail—or AUR—rose 9%. AUR is not a pure list-price measure: product, geography and channel mix can change it. Even so, higher realised selling value alongside positive comparable sales is stronger evidence than price growth paired with falling volume.

Management describes the result as full-price demand and reduced reliance on off-price distribution. That is a management interpretation, but the adjusted margin gives it some financial support. Adjusted gross margin expanded even after removing the tariff-refund effect.

The key limitation is that operating expenses also grew. Adjusted operating expense was up 11%, and its share of revenue increased to 54.8% from 53.9%. Marketing and long-term investment can support future growth, but they mean not every gross-margin point reaches operating profit.

Adjusted operating margin still rose 160 basis points to 15.7%. That is the more balanced measure of the quarter’s leverage.

## Asia changes the mix as well as the growth rate

Asia revenue increased **28% to $571 million**, with comparable sales up 24%. China revenue increased more than 35%, according to the results release. Asia operating margin rose 240 basis points to 27.4%.

North America remained the largest segment, with revenue up 6% to $886 million and operating margin up 160 basis points to 21.0%. Europe revenue rose 1% as reported to $696 million; its operating margin was flat at 29.4%.

Faster Asia growth can lift the group’s margin if full-price retail and product mix are favourable. It also increases exposure to regional demand, currency and distribution expansion. Investors should separate same-store demand from growth created by opening locations: the company added 22 owned and partner stores during the quarter.

## The tariff refund still needs to be isolated

The Form 10-Q records an **$82.3 million benefit** in cost of goods sold from refunds of tariffs previously paid under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. Ralph Lauren also recorded $33.2 million of related charitable donations within restructuring and other charges.

Those items help explain why reported gross margin expanded by 620 basis points while adjusted gross margin rose by 250. The adjusted result is not GAAP and depends on management’s exclusions, but it prevents the refund from being mistaken for recurring product economics.

This distinction also affects comparisons with peers. Two companies can report similar gross-margin expansion while one has genuine pricing and sourcing gains and the other mainly recognises a refund.

## Capital returns are supported by a net-cash position

Ralph Lauren ended the quarter with **$1.82 billion of cash and short-term investments** and $1.24 billion of total debt. Inventory was $1.15 billion, down 9% year on year despite the holiday build.

The company repurchased $250 million of shares in the quarter and $500 million in the first half, completing its planned fiscal-year repurchases. Six-month operating cash flow was $172.3 million and capital expenditure $114.2 million, so current-period cash generation alone did not fund the entire buyback. Existing liquidity did much of the work.

That can be sensible when the balance sheet is strong, but the per-share outcome still depends on repurchase price and future cash generation. A fortress balance sheet should not be confused with an unlimited one.

## Holiday guidance is the next test

Management raised full-year constant-currency revenue growth guidance to 6%–7% on a comparable 52-week basis and expects 80–100 basis points of constant-currency operating-margin expansion. Fiscal 2027 has a 53rd week, expected to add about one point to reported annual growth.

For the third quarter, however, management expects only 3%–4% constant-currency revenue growth and 50–70 basis points of operating-margin expansion. Wholesale timing and lower off-price sales are expected to reduce growth, while holiday marketing absorbs some gross-margin benefit.

That slower period is useful. If full-price demand remains healthy and adjusted margin still expands, the brand-elevation thesis becomes more credible. If comparable sales fade when price and marketing meet a tougher consumer, the second-quarter mix will look less durable.

**Ralph Lauren’s reported margin contains a one-off refund, but the adjusted figures show that pricing, mix and regional growth are doing real work.** The next test is whether that work continues when revenue growth normalises and holiday spending rises.
