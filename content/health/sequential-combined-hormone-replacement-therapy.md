## At a glance

Sequential combined HRT uses oestrogen and adds a progestogen for part of each treatment cycle. It can be used for selected people with a uterus who are still having periods or are relatively close to their last period. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/sequential-combined-hormone-replacement-therapy/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Cyclical progestogen and what bleeding can mean

Oestrogen acts on receptors in several tissues, including the uterine lining. The progestogen phase changes how the lining develops and protects it against sustained stimulation. A scheduled bleed can occur when the progestogen phase ends. Product schedules and instructions differ. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/sequential-combined-hormone-replacement-therapy/)

## What the evidence measures

Review symptom relief across the whole cycle, alongside side effects and bleeding timing. A new pattern or unexpected bleeding can have multiple causes and should be interpreted in context.

## A calendar you can take to review

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Mark | oestrogen and progestogen days from the prescribed plan |
| Record | bleeding, symptoms and any missed treatment days |


## What information helps when a bleed is unexpected?

Record the timing relative to the prescribed progestogen days, any missed applications and how heavy or persistent the bleeding is. Share the record with the care team.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Ask about bleeding outside the expected pattern and protection of the womb lining. HRT schedules and contraception are separate topics to discuss with the clinician. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/sequential-combined-hormone-replacement-therapy/)
