An Irish dairy household could sell the same volume of milk as last year and still have substantially less cash available for bills. In [preliminary estimates published on 6 November](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-apipe/agriculturalpriceindices-preliminaryestimates2026/), the CSO puts the average 2026 price farmers receive for milk **18.1% below 2025**. At the same time, it expects fertiliser prices to rise 18.0% and farm energy prices to rise 17.9%.

## What the two price changes look like on one farm

Suppose a farm sold €50,000 of milk last year. If it sells the same quantity and its price follows the national estimate, the cheque falls to **€40,950** (€50,000 × 0.819). Now take a €5,000 fertiliser purchase. Buying the same quantity at a price 18% higher costs **€5,900**.

Together those two lines leave the farm **€9,950 worse off** before changes in energy, feed, other receipts or tax. The milk price accounts for €9,050 of that difference and fertiliser for €900. These are illustrative amounts. A milk statement gives a family its own realised price, including the effect of composition and bonuses. Comparing litres delivered as well as the cheque helps separate a price change from a change in output.

## Why a 15.8% fall in the terms of trade matters

Across Irish agriculture, the CSO expects output prices to fall 10.3% while input prices rise 6.5%. Its terms-of-trade measure divides the output price index by the input price index. On the CSO’s estimate that ratio falls **15.8%**. In practical terms, the sale of a fixed basket of produce buys fewer farm inputs than it did a year ago.

Farm profit also depends on quantities, the mix of enterprises and other income. The [CSO methodology](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-apipe/agriculturalpriceindices-preliminaryestimates2026/backgroundnotes/) combines observed monthly prices with estimates for the remainder of the year. These are provisional full-year figures for farming in Ireland. A supermarket shopper cannot apply the 18.1% farm-gate estimate to the price of a litre on the shelf.

For a household drawing money from the business, start with the next milk receipts and the dates the farm’s input bills fall due. Then separate the amount available for household essentials.
