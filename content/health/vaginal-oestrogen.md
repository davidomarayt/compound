## At a glance

Vaginal oestrogen is used for selected genital or urinary symptoms associated with lower oestrogen after menopause. Products can include creams, tablets, pessaries or rings. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/vaginal-oestrogen/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Local tissue changes in the vagina and urinary tract

Oestrogen receptors in tissues around the vagina respond to local treatment. Changes in the epithelium, moisture and local environment can help with symptoms such as dryness and discomfort. Systemic exposure depends on the product and regimen and is generally lower with low-dose local treatment than with systemic HRT. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/vaginal-oestrogen/)

## What the evidence measures

Follow the particular local symptoms the treatment was chosen for. Persistent pain, bleeding or recurrent urinary symptoms may need assessment for another cause.

## Describe the symptoms you want reviewed

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Local symptoms | dryness, irritation or discomfort |
| Other symptoms | record urinary symptoms or any unexpected bleeding |


## What if urinary symptoms continue?

Several problems can cause similar urinary symptoms. Keep a short symptom record and discuss the need for assessment, especially if there is pain, fever or bleeding.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Ask about unexplained bleeding and discuss a history of hormone-sensitive cancer or current endocrine treatment with the relevant care team. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/vaginal-oestrogen/)
