**Banks and other lenders approved 5,265 mortgages worth €1.704 billion in October, according to the [Banking & Payments Federation Ireland (BPFI)](https://bpfi.ie/publications/bpfi-mortgage-approvals-october-2026/), which published the figures on Friday 27 November. That is 10.1% more approvals than a year earlier. Over the first ten months of 2026, 49,213 mortgages were approved, the highest January-to-October total since the series began in 2011.**

The headline is about volume. For households, two numbers inside the report matter more: how much first-time buyers are borrowing, and how fast mortgage switching is growing.

## October in one table

The figures below are from the [BPFI October report](https://bpfi.ie/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Mortgage-Approvals-Report-October-2026.pdf). The average loan is our own calculation: total value divided by number of approvals.

| Type of approval | Number | Value | Change in number vs Oct 2025 | Average loan (our calculation) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First-time buyers | 3,077 | €1,015m | +7.2% | about €329,900 |
| Movers | 992 | €374m | +11.0% | about €377,000 |
| Residential investment | 72 | €14m | −15.3% | about €194,000 |
| Switching / remortgage | 800 | €251m | +28.6% | about €313,800 |
| Top-up | 324 | €50m | +3.5% | about €154,300 |
| **Total** | **5,265** | **€1,704m** | **+10.1%** | about €323,600 |

Approvals fell 2.4% from September in number and 2.0% in value. That month-to-month dip is small. The year-on-year rise is the clearer signal.

## First-time buyers: about €330,000 per loan

First-time buyers took 3,077 approvals in October, 58.4% of the total. Their approvals were worth just over €1 billion.

- **October 2026:** €1,015m ÷ 3,077 = about **€329,900** per approval.
- **October 2025:** €936m ÷ 2,869 = about **€326,200**.
- **January to October 2026:** BPFI says first-time buyers got 29,314 approvals worth "over €9.7 billion". That works out at roughly **€331,000** each.

So the typical first-time buyer loan has grown by only about 1% in a year in October's figures. The bigger change is the number of buyers. BPFI chief executive Brian Hayes said first-time buyer approvals in the first ten months were the highest for that period since the series began.

**What a €330,000 loan needs.** Under the [Central Bank's mortgage rules](https://www.centralbank.ie/financial-system/financial-stability/macro-prudential-policy/mortgage-measures), first-time buyers can usually borrow up to four times gross income and up to 90% of the price. A €330,000 loan therefore points to:

- gross household income of at least **€82,500** (€330,000 ÷ 4), unless the lender grants an exception; and
- a price of about **€366,700** with a 10% deposit of about **€36,700**.

That income figure lines up closely with the CSO's median first-time buyer income of €82,600, which we covered in [Ireland's typical first-time buyer](/news/first-time-buyers-ireland-income-age-help-to-buy-2024/). Test your own numbers with the [Mortgage Borrowing Calculator](/mortgage-borrowing-calculator/) and the [House Deposit Calculator](/house-deposit-calculator/).

The average hides a wide spread. A Dublin buyer may need far more than €330,000, while a buyer elsewhere may need much less. Our [September house price report](/news/irish-house-prices-september-2026/) shows how prices differ by region.

## Switching: up 28.6% on a year ago

The fastest-growing line in the report is switching. Lenders approved **800** switching or remortgage loans in October, up 28.6% on October 2025 and 4.0% on September. The value rose 24.6% to €251 million, an average of about €313,800.

Switching does not show up in house prices, but it can matter a lot to an existing borrower's monthly budget. Here is an illustration. The rates are examples only, not quotes from any lender:

- **Loan:** €300,000 with 25 years left.
- **At 4.2%:** about **€1,617** a month.
- **At 3.5%:** about **€1,502** a month.
- **Difference:** about **€115 a month**, or about **€1,380 a year**. Over five years that is close to **€6,900**, before any legal or valuation costs or cashback.

Switching is not free and not always worth it. Fixed-rate break fees, legal costs and the time left on your loan all matter. Our [switching guide](/wealth/is-switching-your-mortgage-worth-it-ireland/) has a full €250,000 example, and the [Mortgage Switch Calculator](/mortgage-switch-calculator/) lets you try your own rate and balance.

## What it means for you

- **Buying your first home:** more first-time buyers have approval than at any point in the series for these ten months. Expect competition for homes in the price range most of them are chasing. Get your approval in principle and deposit evidence ready before you bid. Check whether you can use [Help to Buy or the First Home Scheme](/wealth/first-time-buyer-supports-ireland/).
- **Already have a mortgage:** if you are on a variable rate, or your fixed rate is ending soon, compare your rate with what lenders now offer. The jump in switching suggests many borrowers are doing this.
- **Trading up:** mover approvals were up 11.0% in number, with an average loan of about €377,000. If you are selling and buying, run both sides of the move through the [mortgage affordability guide](/wealth/mortgage-approval-affordability-ireland/).

## A note on what these figures are

A mortgage approval means a lender has issued a formal offer for a specific property. It is not a drawdown, and some approvals never complete. BPFI reports drawdowns separately. The numbers come from BPFI's member lenders. We have not adjusted them for seasonality.
