## What this calculator is for

Universal Social Charge is a separate tax on income. This calculator applies the standard 2026 USC bands to the income entered and shows the estimated annual charge.

## How to read the result

USC is progressive: each rate applies only to the slice of income within its band. If income exceeds the exemption threshold, standard USC is generally calculated on the full income rather than only the amount above €13,000.

## Worked example

Revenue's 2026 example for €50,000 applies 0.5% to the first €12,012, 2% to the next €16,688 and 3% to the remaining €21,300, giving €1,032.82.

## Irish context

The standard 2026 bands are 0.5% on the first €12,012, 2% on the next €16,688, 3% on the next €41,344 and 8% on the balance. The general exemption threshold is €13,000. Reduced rates can apply in specified circumstances, including some people aged 70+ or with a full medical card where income is within the relevant limit.

## What can change the answer

Medical-card status, age, income type and self-employed surcharge rules can affect USC. Some income is exempt and some special rates can apply.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not apply the highest USC rate to the whole salary. Do not assume earning €13,001 means USC applies only to €1. And do not use the standard schedule if you qualify for a reduced-rate treatment without checking Revenue.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is USC the same as PRSI?
No.

### Is there a €13,000 exemption?
Yes under the standard 2026 rules; once income exceeds it, USC generally applies to the full relevant income.

### Are reduced rates automatic?
Eligibility depends on Revenue conditions.

### Does this include the self-employed surcharge?
Not unless explicitly implemented in the tool.

## Method and limitations

The calculator applies the standard 2026 USC bands implemented in the site. It does not model every exemption, reduced-rate case or special income category.
