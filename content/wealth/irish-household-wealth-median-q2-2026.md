**Irish households held €1,429.5 billion in net wealth at the end of June 2026, a new record, according to the [Central Bank of Ireland's Household Wealth Report](https://www.centralbank.ie/statistics/data-and-analysis/household-wealth) published on Thursday 26 November. Spread evenly, that is roughly €724,000 per household. But very few households look like that average. The European Central Bank's distributional data for the same quarter puts the typical (median) Irish household at about €438,000.**

The headline total is useful for economists. For a household trying to work out where it stands, the median, the gap between owners and renters, and the cut-off points between groups say much more.

## The headline figures

From the Central Bank's [Q2 2026 report](https://www.centralbank.ie/docs/default-source/statistics/data-and-analysis/financial-accounts/household-wealth-data/household-releases/household-wealth-report---2026-q2f68e17144644629bacc1ff0000269695.pdf?sfvrsn=4fca751a_2):

- **Net wealth:** €1,429.5bn, up €42.8bn in the quarter.
- **Housing:** 66.9% of net wealth. Housing wealth rose €11.1bn in the quarter, mostly because prices went up.
- **Financial assets:** €635.9bn, including €225.2bn in cash and deposits and €295.7bn in pensions and insurance.
- **Debt:** €162.5bn in total liabilities, of which €157.1bn is loans. Household debt is 81.0% of annual disposable income and 9.9% of assets.
- **Concentration:** the wealthiest 10% hold 47.0% of net wealth, down from 53.9% ten years earlier.

The roughly €724,000 per household figure was reported by [The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/11/26/irish-housing-wealth-now-close-to-1-trillion/). It is the total divided by the number of households.

## Why the median matters more

An average gets pulled up by a small number of very wealthy households. The median is the household in the exact middle: half have more, half have less.

The ECB's [Distributional Wealth Accounts](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/data/datasets/DWA) for Ireland in Q2 2026 give these figures. They are experimental statistics and use a slightly different method from the Central Bank's quarterly accounts, so the totals do not match exactly (the ECB's total is about €1.46 trillion).

| Measure (Q2 2026) | Net wealth per household |
| --- | --- |
| Average (mean) | about €740,900 |
| **Median** | **about €438,000** |
| Gap between the two | about €302,900 |

The median is the better guide to a "typical" household. It is still a big number, because most of it is the value of a home.

**How fast the median has grown.** A year earlier, in Q2 2025, the median was about €395,200. That is a rise of about 10.8% (€438,000 ÷ €395,200). Ten years ago, in Q2 2016, it was about €164,900, so it has multiplied by about 2.7 in a decade. Most of that came from house prices, not from saving.

## Where the cut-off points are

The ECB data also gives the wealth needed to reach each group. These are net wealth figures: everything a household owns, including its home and pensions, minus everything it owes.

| To be in the... | You need net wealth of at least about |
| --- | --- |
| Top 80% (above the poorest fifth) | €44,300 |
| Top 60% | €326,000 |
| Top half | €438,400 |
| Top 40% | €557,300 |
| Top 20% | €986,800 |
| Top 10% | €1,533,500 |

There is a steep jump between the bottom fifth and the next group: from about €44,000 to about €326,000 over just two tenths of households. The ECB data does not say who sits on each side of that line, but the owner and renter figures below suggest that owning a home is the main dividing factor.

## Owners and renters live in different worlds

The ECB splits households by housing status. The gap is the clearest finding in the data:

- **Owners (outright or with a mortgage):** about **€1,050,100** average net wealth per household.
- **Tenants and people living rent-free:** about **€85,400** average net wealth per household.

That is a ratio of about 12 to 1. Average renter wealth has grown, from about €78,200 a year earlier (up about 9.1%), but owners have gained far more in euro terms because their homes have risen in value.

These are averages, not medians. Many renters have far less than €85,400, and some owners have far less than €1 million.

## Average wealth by group

Here is how average net wealth per household differs across the distribution, from the same ECB data:

| Group | Average net wealth per household |
| --- | --- |
| Bottom 20% | about €6,900 |
| Bottom half | about €147,000 |
| 6th tenth (just above the median) | about €496,700 |
| 7th tenth | about €636,100 |
| 8th tenth | about €840,100 |
| 9th tenth | about €1,227,800 |
| Top 10% | about €3,472,000 |

The Central Bank adds two points about what these groups own. Households in the bottom half hold their financial wealth mainly in deposits (18.5% of total assets) and are heavily borrowed: debt equals 28.1% of their assets. The top 10% hold more shares, funds and life assurance (19.4% of assets) and owe only 3.9% of what they own.

By working status, retired households average about €880,200, employed households about €561,800 and self-employed households about €1,400,100. Self-employed households hold much of their wealth in their own businesses.

## What it means for you

- **Work out your own figure.** Add your home's value, savings, investments and pension values, then subtract your mortgage and other debts. The [Net Worth Calculator](/net-worth-calculator/) does this. Compare the result with the median (about €438,000), not the €724,000 average.
- **If you rent:** your net wealth is mostly cash and pension. The gap with owners comes mainly from house prices, which you cannot control. What you can control is the rate you save and where it sits. The [Rent Tax Credit](/wealth/rent-tax-credit-ireland-who-can-claim/) is worth up to €1,000 a year for a single renter in 2026. A workplace pension with an employer match, or [MyFutureFund](/wealth/myfuturefund-vs-private-pension-ireland/), adds to wealth without needing a deposit.
- **If most of your savings are in deposits:** €225bn sits in Irish household cash and deposit accounts. [Inflation was 4.3% in October](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1111/1598813-cso-inflation-figures/), so money earning well under that is losing value. Keep an [emergency fund](/emergency-fund-calculator/) in cash, then look at what the rest earns. Our guide to the [Bank of Ireland 5% KickstartSaver](/news/bank-of-ireland-5-percent-savings-account/) shows how regular-saver accounts compare.
- **If you own your home:** a high net worth on paper does not pay bills. Housing wealth can only be spent by selling, moving or borrowing against it. If a large share of your wealth is your home, a pension and some cash give you options. See [How pensions work in Ireland](/wealth/how-pensions-work-ireland/).

## What these figures are, and are not

The Central Bank's figures come from the national financial accounts plus its own estimate of housing values. The distributional figures come from the ECB's experimental Distributional Wealth Accounts, which combine those accounts with survey data. Both measure wealth on paper at market values. They do not show income, and they do not show how easily wealth could be turned into cash.

All per-household and threshold figures in this article are as published in the ECB dataset for Ireland, Q2 2026, rounded to the nearest €100. The growth rates are our own calculations from the same series.
