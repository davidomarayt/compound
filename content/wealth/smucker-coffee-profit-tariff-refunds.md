**The J.M. Smucker Co., owner of Folgers, Dunkin' packaged coffee, Jif, Uncrustables and Milk-Bone, reported a 52% jump in adjusted earnings per share on falling sales. Nearly all of the extra profit came from one segment, coffee, helped by lower costs and tariff refunds that the company now says are essentially finished. The investment question is how much of this margin is durable.**

For the second quarter of fiscal 2027, ended 31 October 2026, Smucker reported **net sales of $2.283 billion, down 2%**. **Adjusted EPS was $3.20**, up from $2.10, including a **$0.22 benefit from tariff refunds**. GAAP diluted EPS rose only 3% to **$2.32**. Smucker raised its full-year adjusted EPS guidance to **$10.80 to $11.20**.

For the maintained quarterly record, see [Smucker's stock profile](/stocks/sjm/). This article focuses on where the profit came from and what the outlook implies.

## Coffee did almost all the work

Total segment profit rose from $479.0 million to **$630.9 million**, an increase of **$151.9 million**. U.S. Retail Coffee alone rose from $154.3 million to **$302.9 million**, up **$148.6 million**.

That means coffee supplied about **98% of the quarter's segment profit growth**, Compound's calculation. Its segment margin jumped to **37.5% from 18.2%**, even though coffee sales fell 5%: prices were down 3 percentage points and volume/mix down 2 points, with weaker Dunkin' and Folgers partly offset by Café Bustelo.

Smucker attributes the profit rise to "lower costs, including tariff refunds, and lower marketing spend", partly offset by lower prices. Put simply, coffee costs fell faster than Smucker cut its shelf prices. That is good for margins while it lasts. It also leaves room for retailers and competitors to push prices lower.

## The rest of the portfolio was mixed

- **Frozen handheld and spreads** was the clear volume story. Sales rose 7% on Uncrustables growth and segment profit rose 12% to $113.9 million.
- **Pet foods** profit fell 17% to $103.2 million on higher costs and lower dog-snack pricing.
- **Sweet baked snacks**, the former Hostess business, lost 11 points of volume. Profit still rose 54% to $33.5 million, helped by lower costs and lower marketing.
- **Away From Home**, mainly food-service, saw profit fall 18%.

Excluding coffee, segment profit was roughly flat, Compound's calculation.

## Tariff refunds are a one-off

Smucker received **$30.5 million** of refunds of tariffs paid under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act in the quarter, and **$146.8 million** in the first half, recorded as lower cost of goods. The 10-Q says that as of 31 October "substantially all anticipated" refunds have been received and it does not expect further refunds to have a material impact.

Using Smucker's 24.2% adjusted tax rate, the first-half refunds were worth roughly **$1.04 of adjusted EPS**, Compound's estimate. That is close to a tenth of the full-year guidance midpoint.

There is also a gap between GAAP and adjusted results. Adjusted figures exclude a **$58.7 million loss** on commodity and currency hedges not yet allocated to segments this quarter, compared with a **$103.0 million gain** a year earlier. That timing effect explains much of why GAAP EPS grew 3% while adjusted EPS grew 52%.

## The guidance implies a weaker second half

Smucker earned **$6.43 of adjusted EPS in the first half**. Full-year guidance of $10.80 to $11.20 implies **$4.37 to $4.77 in the second half**, Compound's calculation. That is 26% to 32% below the first half.

Even if the first-half refunds are stripped out, first-half adjusted EPS would be about $5.39, still above the implied second half. The company has not broken down the second-half bridge, but its guidance assumes net sales down 1% to 2% for the year, an adjusted gross margin of about 39% and SD&A expenses up about 8%.

Cash flow is steadier. First-half free cash flow was **$541 million**, against a reaffirmed full-year target of **$1.1 billion**. Total borrowings fell by about $259 million in the half to **$6.70 billion**, and net interest expense dropped 16% in the quarter.

## What would confirm or weaken the thesis

The thesis strengthens if coffee margins hold well above last year's level as prices fall, if Uncrustables keeps growing, and if sweet baked snacks volumes stop shrinking.

It weakens if coffee price cuts accelerate faster than costs, if pet foods margins keep slipping, or if second-half results land near the bottom of guidance without the refund support.

**Smucker's profit surge rests mainly on one segment and one-off refunds. Coffee margins have doubled while costs fell, but the refunds are over and the company's own guidance points to a weaker second half.** This analysis makes no price target.
