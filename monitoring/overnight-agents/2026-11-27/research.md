Reader question: how far apart are typical gross pay levels across Irish counties, and what does a county median actually measure?

CSO figures, median gross annual earnings per employment, 2025:

| Area | Median (EUR) | vs State |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| State | 46,305 | 100.0% |
| Dublin | 50,474 | 109.0% |
| Kildare | 50,043 | 108.1% |
| Cork | 48,328 | 104.4% |
| Kerry | 40,440 | 87.3% |
| Donegal | 38,739 | 83.7% |
| Monaghan | 38,517 | 83.2% |

The "vs State" column is ours (county ÷ 46,305). Also derived by us: Dublin minus Monaghan = EUR 11,957, Dublin is 31.0% above Monaghan (50,474 / 38,517 − 1). Gender gap by county as published: Meath males EUR 53,952 vs females EUR 41,502 (+30.0%, gap EUR 12,450); Sligo males EUR 46,154 vs females EUR 42,525 (+8.5%, gap EUR 3,629). Dublin residents held 29.5% of employments but 54.9% of top-1% earnings employments.

Limits (CSO notes): data are Revenue payroll records linked to DSP and the Business Register; units are **employments**, not people (a person with two jobs counts twice); restricted to employments active in October of the year; **gross** pay before tax, USC and PRSI, so not take-home pay; county is **county of residence**, not workplace; EAADS has no hours data, so gender and county gaps partly reflect hours and job mix and are not like-for-like pay-rate gaps. Medians cannot be added or averaged across counties. No inference about cost of living or house affordability is made from this alone.
