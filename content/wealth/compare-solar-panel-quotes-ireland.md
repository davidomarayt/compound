A solar installation is a major home purchase. In Ireland, the panels might last for decades, but the quote can hide short-term risks: a vague installer specification, assumptions about the grant, unpriced roof work or a battery that adds little financial value.

This guide is deliberately about **buying**, not a general introduction to solar. If you are still deciding whether to install panels at all, start with our [complete solar panels in Ireland guide](/wealth/solar-panels-ireland/). If you already have an offer, use the steps below.

## Solar panel prices in Ireland in 2026: a sensible starting point

There is **no official, audited national price list** for an Irish household solar installation. Public installer and quote-comparison sites publish different figures, often based on different roof, equipment and battery assumptions.

The bands below are **illustrative planning ranges drawn from publicly published 2026 Irish industry price guides**, not transaction-level averages or guaranteed offers. They assume a reasonably straightforward domestic roof, panels, inverter, standard installation and **no battery**. Your written quotation is the only reliable price for your own house.

Ask every company to provide the same information in writing. A good quotation makes it possible to work through the following without guessing.

## 2026 SEAI grants: do not compare the wrong prices

For qualifying homes, the standard Solar Electricity Grant currently provides **€700 per kWp for the first 2kWp**, then **€200 per additional kWp up to 4kWp**, capped at **€1,800**. A 3kWp system may receive €1,600; a qualifying 4kWp or larger installation can receive the maximum €1,800. The grant is not unlimited simply because you install more panels.

SEAI also now offers a separate **€600 battery grant for qualifying battery systems of 5kWh or larger**. A qualifying household installing both can apply for both grants. That can bring standard combined support to **€2,400**, subject to eligibility.

The property needs an MPRN, must have been built and occupied before 2025, and cannot already have received the corresponding grant at that MPRN. **Apply and receive approval before installation starts.** Check the full and latest [SEAI solar PV rules](https://www.seai.ie/grants/home-energy-grants/individual-grants/solar-electricity-grant) and [battery rules](https://www.seai.ie/grants/home-energy-grants/individual-grants/battery).

For eligible domestic supply-and-install work, the [Revenue rules](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/vat-on-property-and-construction/construction-fixtures-fittings-solar-panels/solar-panels.aspx) generally allow **0% VAT**. A stand-alone retail purchase of panels does not necessarily have the same VAT treatment. Ask the installer to confirm exactly which items and services are covered.

## Payback: the quote with the best financial return is not always the cheapest

Let us use the same theoretical 6kWp system for all three offers. Assume its roof-specific generation estimate is **5,100kWh a year**, 40% of that electricity is used directly in the home, imported electricity costs **30c/kWh** and exported electricity earns **15c/kWh**.

Using this deliberately simple scenario:

- **Quote A:** €7,500 ÷ €1,071 ≈ **7.0 years**;
- **Quote B:** €7,200 ÷ €1,071 ≈ **6.7 years**;
- **Quote C:** €8,400 ÷ €1,071 ≈ **7.8 years**.

These are **simple paybacks, not financial forecasts**. The model leaves out equipment deterioration, inverter replacement, maintenance, financing, changes in consumption, electricity and export rates, tax effects, and the time value of money. The generation, tariffs and self-consumption rates are illustrative assumptions—not claims about current offers from suppliers.

**Crucially, exported electricity is not equivalent to avoided imported electricity.** That is why a salesperson's “your panels will produce enough units to cover the annual bill” statement does not, by itself, demonstrate that your bills will disappear.

## Do you need the battery in the quote?

A battery can be useful, especially if your electricity demand is high after sunset or your tariff rewards carefully timed charging. But it does not create additional solar generation, and there are energy losses in storage.

Ask for two separate written figures: **solar-only** and **the same solar array plus battery**, with the battery's usable capacity and grant shown separately. If the battery adds €3,000 net and is expected to create €180 of extra annual value, its illustrative incremental simple payback is **16.7 years**—regardless of how attractive the overall panels-plus-battery payback may look.

A battery may still be chosen for resilience or convenience; just do not mistake those benefits for financial savings. ## The hidden costs and small print worth questioning

Common quote differences include:

- **Scaffolding and roof repairs.** A quote can be cheap because safe access or remedial work has not been priced.
- **Electrical work.** An older consumer unit, long cable run or necessary upgrade can change the total.
- **Panel and inverter mismatch.** Ask about DC panel capacity, inverter AC rating, clipping and potential connection requirements—not simply the number of panels.
- **Paperwork.** Confirm who handles the relevant ESB Networks process and whether export metering arrangements are included. Standard [microgeneration](https://www.esbnetworks.ie/services/get-connected/renewable-connection/micro-generation) generally covers inverter/export ratings up to approximately 6kVA single-phase or 11kVA three-phase.
- **Backup power claims.** A battery does **not** automatically keep sockets powered during a grid outage. Ask which circuits, changeover equipment and installed features provide backup.
- **Warranty costs.** A “25-year warranty” may apply to panel output while an inverter and workmanship carry shorter or different cover.
- **Price exclusions.** Clarify who pays for extras found after surveying, whether the deposit is refundable and exactly what qualifies as a scope change.

Any installer can offer an attractive-looking generation chart. What matters is whether it reflects the actual roof, shading and electrical design. See [East- and West-Facing Solar Panels in Ireland](/wealth/east-west-facing-solar-panels-ireland/) and [How Many Solar Panels Do You Need?](/wealth/how-many-solar-panels-do-i-need-ireland/) before upsizing solely on a sales projection.

## Choosing the installer: five red flags

**1. Pressure to pay a deposit today.** A genuine offer should survive a reasonable period to read the contract and compare like-for-like specifications.

**2. No itemised equipment list.** “Premium solar system” is not a panel brand, inverter model or warranty.

**3. Grant presented as automatic.** Ask for the official SEAI criteria and the payment route. A grant cannot be assumed for every property.

**4. Guaranteed payback without assumptions.** A payback claim requires annual generation, direct usage, import price, export rate and net cost.

**5. No clear legal counterparty or after-sales arrangements.** Know who installs, who warrants, and who fixes faults if a subcontractor or manufacturer is unavailable.

None of these alone proves a business is dishonest. They are reasons to request a clearer written answer before proceeding.

## Before signing: a four-quote process that takes the guesswork out

**Step 1 — Create one brief.** Use your annual kWh consumption, roof orientation, shading, target system size and battery preference. Send the same facts to all companies.

**Step 2 — Obtain at least four offers.** That follows [SEAI's own recommendation](https://www.seai.ie/grants/find-a-registered-professional/solar-pv-companies). Look at independent reviews, previous local projects and relevant registration, not only the price.

**Step 3 — Standardise the figures.** For each quote, record the total pre-grant cost, all excluded work, grant assumptions, final economic cost and equipment list.

**Step 4 — Run comparable payback scenarios.** Keep import and export rates, self-consumption and household electricity demand the same. Vary the installer’s site-specific production figure only when there is a reason.

**Step 5 — Verify the contract and grant approval.** Check payment milestones, workmanship and product warranties, completion documentation and the process if unforeseen roof or electrical work is needed.

## Solar quote questions: quick answers

### How many solar installation quotes should I get in Ireland?

**At least four**, according to SEAI's homeowner guidance. More may be useful where roof access, battery design or equipment differs substantially.

### Is €10,000 too expensive for a 6kWp solar system?

Not necessarily. A €10,000 all-in solar-only quote can be within broadly advertised 2026 planning ranges, but its value depends on roof access, panels, inverter, included electrical work, warranty and whether €10,000 is before or after the grant. Compare full specifications.

### Does the €1,800 SEAI grant apply to all solar quotes?

No. The maximum standard grant applies only when the applicant, property and installation qualify and the panel capacity reaches the cap. The home must meet SEAI conditions, and **approval must precede installation**.

### Is a 6kWp solar system the same as a 6kW inverter?

No. **kWp** is the sum of the panels' nominal rated DC output. An inverter's **kW/kVA** rating is a separate AC-side specification. The connection process is also relevant to export capability.

### Can I compare a solar quote with and without a battery?

Yes—and you should. Hold panel capacity and the other assumptions constant, compare the **incremental battery cost** with the additional expected savings, and separately consider backup or convenience benefits.

### Can I compare quotes from different counties?

Yes, but make every company price the **same site and scope**. Travel, scaffolding, roof type, site access and service support may legitimately affect the final price.
