<div class="pension-course-kicker">PENSIONS IN IRELAND 101</div>

<h2 class="pension-course-title">From your first contribution to retirement income.</h2>

Irish pensions look complicated because several systems overlap.

You can have a State Pension, a workplace scheme, a PRSA, an old pension from a previous employer and MyFutureFund—all at the same time.

The way through it is to separate the decisions.

**What pension do you have? What does your employer add? What tax relief applies? What is the money invested in? What does it cost? What happens when you change jobs? And how do you eventually turn the fund into retirement income?**

This eight-chapter course works through those questions in order.

## What changed in 2026

Ireland’s retirement landscape changed materially when **MyFutureFund launched on 1 January 2026**.

Eligible employees without a qualifying payroll pension are now automatically enrolled into a system where the employee, employer and State all contribute.

For 2026–2028, the scheduled contribution rates are:

<div class="pension-number-strip">
<div><span>Employee</span><strong>1.5%</strong></div>
<div><span>Employer</span><strong>1.5%</strong></div>
<div><span>State</span><strong>0.5%</strong></div>
<div><span>Total</span><strong>3.5%</strong></div>
</div>

That makes pension comparison more important, not less.

A worker may now need to compare:

- a workplace scheme;
- a PRSA;
- MyFutureFund;
- AVCs;
- and old pension benefits

rather than simply asking whether to “get a pension”.

Our dedicated [MyFutureFund vs Private Pension guide](/wealth/myfuturefund-vs-private-pension-ireland/) goes deeper into that comparison.

## The four pension numbers worth knowing

<div class="pension-stat-grid">
<div><span>€115,000</span><p>Maximum annual earnings taken into account for personal pension tax-relief calculations.</p></div>
<div><span>15%–40%</span><p>Age-related personal contribution limits, from under 30 through age 60+.</p></div>
<div><span>€200,000</span><p>Lifetime tax-free limit for relevant retirement lump sums from all sources.</p></div>

These are rules and limits—not retirement targets.

## A good pension plan answers these questions

By the end of the course, you should be able to answer:

- What type of pension do I have?
- How much is my employer contributing?
- Am I getting the full employer contribution available?
- What personal tax-relief headroom do I have?
- What is the pension actually invested in?
- What are the total charges?
- Is the risk appropriate for my retirement horizon?
- What old pensions do I have?
- What happens if I change jobs?
- At what age can each pension be accessed?
- What retirement lump sum may be available?
- How much of that lump sum is actually tax free?
- Will retirement income come from an annuity, ARF, DB pension, State Pension or a combination?
- What happens to the pension on death?

If several answers are unknown, that is where to focus before increasing complexity.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best pension in Ireland?

There is no universally best pension. Employer contributions, tax support, fees, investment choice, access rules and existing benefits all matter.

### What is a PRSA?

A Personal Retirement Savings Account is an individual pension contract with an authorised provider. It is portable between jobs and can receive personal and employer contributions subject to the relevant rules.

### How much pension tax relief can I get?

Revenue applies age-related percentage limits to qualifying personal pension contributions and uses a maximum earnings figure of €115,000. The exact relief also depends on your Income Tax position.

### Can I have several pensions?

Yes. People commonly build pension rights across several employments and personal arrangements. Some tax and retirement rules consider them together.

### When can I access a pension?

It depends on the pension type. PRSAs can normally be accessed from age 60. Occupational schemes have their own normal retirement ages, generally between 60 and 70, with some earlier-retirement provisions.

### Is 25% of my pension always tax free?

No. A personal pension can generally provide a retirement lump sum equal to 25% of the fund, but the amount actually tax free is subject to the separate €200,000 lifetime limit across relevant retirement lump sums.
