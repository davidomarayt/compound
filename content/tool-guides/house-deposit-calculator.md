## What this calculator is for

This calculator estimates the minimum deposit implied by standard Irish mortgage loan-to-value rules. It is useful for answering **how much cash must come from outside the mortgage** before considering income, affordability or buying costs.

## How to read the result

For a principal home, the standard LTV ceiling is generally 90%, implying a 10% deposit. For buy-to-let lending, the standard ceiling is generally 70%, implying a 30% deposit. The result also shows the maximum mortgage from LTV alone.

## Worked example

On a €400,000 principal home, 10% is €40,000 and the LTV-based mortgage is €360,000. On a €400,000 buy-to-let purchase, 30% is €120,000 and the LTV-based mortgage is €280,000.

## Irish context

The Central Bank's mortgage measures set the macroprudential framework, but lenders can require more equity and also apply income and affordability tests. A qualifying first-time buyer may also have access to separate supports such as Help to Buy or the First Home Scheme.

## What can change the answer

A larger deposit reduces the mortgage and can lower repayments and total interest. It can also move the borrower into a lower LTV band, which may affect available mortgage rates. But using every euro of savings as a deposit can leave the household financially fragile after completion.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not confuse a booking deposit with the total equity contribution needed at closing. Do not include stamp duty inside the mortgage deposit. And do not assume 10% is automatically enough merely because the LTV rule permits it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 10% always enough?
No. It is the standard LTV minimum for many principal-home purchases, but lenders can require more and income can constrain borrowing.

### Does the deposit include stamp duty?
No.

### Can a gift be used?
Sometimes, subject to lender evidence requirements and possible tax considerations.

### Does Help to Buy replace the deposit?
Not automatically. Eligibility, limits and lender rules still apply.

## Deposit percentage can influence the mortgage rate

The minimum deposit is not always the economically optimal deposit.

Mortgage products are often priced in loan-to-value bands. If adding several thousand euro to the deposit moves the mortgage into a lower LTV band, the borrower may qualify for a different rate. That rate difference then applies to the entire mortgage balance, not only the extra deposit.

This creates a useful calculation: compare the extra cash required to cross an LTV threshold with the interest saving from the lower rate.

But there is a limit. Putting €20,000 more into the house to save a small amount of interest can be unattractive if it leaves no emergency savings, especially just after moving when unexpected costs are common.

For first-time buyers, also separate **deposit source** from **deposit amount**. Savings, gifts, Help to Buy and shared-equity support can each have documentation or eligibility requirements. A lender may ask for evidence showing where funds came from.

The calculator answers the percentage arithmetic. Funding quality and lender acceptance are separate questions.

## Method and limitations

The tool applies 10% for a principal home and 30% for buy-to-let under the standard LTV framework. It does not determine lender approval, gifted-deposit acceptability, scheme eligibility or transaction costs.
