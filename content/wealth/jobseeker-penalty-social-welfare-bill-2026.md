In his [7 November Ard Fheis speech](https://www.finegael.ie/creating-an-ireland-alive-with-possibilities/), Tánaiste Simon Harris called for a new Pathways to Work plan and penalties for people who refuse to engage with employment services. For a household receiving Jobseeker’s Allowance, the figure to watch is in a Bill already before the Oireachtas. **Section 9 would raise the weekly deduction from €90 to €100 for certain claimants on the maximum personal rate.** A long spell out of work alone does not trigger the deduction.

## Who would the €100 apply to?

The [Bill as introduced](https://data.oireachtas.ie/ie/oireachtas/bill/2026/105/eng/initiated/b10526d.pdf) covers the maximum rates of Jobseeker’s Allowance, Jobseeker’s Benefit and Jobseeker’s Benefit for the Self-Employed where an activation penalty applies. Different amounts apply to reduced rates and to Supplementary Welfare Allowance. Someone on the separate [Jobseeker’s Pay-Related Benefit](/news/october-live-register-jobseekers-pay-related-benefit/) should not use the €100 figure to work out their payment. [Oireachtas explanatory memorandum](https://data.oireachtas.ie/ie/oireachtas/bill/2026/105/eng/memo/b10526d-memo.pdf)

The proposed start date in section 9 is **31 December 2026**, subject to enactment. For a person to whom the full deduction applied for three weeks, the amount withheld relative to their applicable full rate would be **3 × €100 = €300**, compared with **3 × €90 = €270** under the current deduction. The €30 difference compares the penalties alone. A person’s actual payment also depends on their underlying rate and circumstances.

## What to do if you receive an Intreo notice

Read what you have been asked to attend and when. If you have a good reason you cannot take part, contact the office on the notice, explain the circumstances and keep a copy of the message or supporting evidence. The Department’s [Jobseeker’s Allowance guidelines](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/publications/operational-guidelines-jobseekers-allowance/) distinguish failure to engage without good cause from a claimant who has a good reason. They also explain that continued refusal without good cause after at least 21 days on a reduced rate can lead to a separate disqualification.

If a decision reduces your payment, check which scheme, period and reason the letter identifies. The Department says an appeal should be made **within 60 days of the decision** and that you can request an oral hearing. The weekend speech itself gives nobody a new deduction or an individual payment date. [Official allowance and appeals guidance](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/jobseekers-allowance/)
