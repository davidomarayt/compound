**There are genuine medicines taken by mouth to help manage weight in Ireland—but the answer depends on the exact product, its authorised use and whether an Irish pharmacy can supply it.** Orlistat and Mysimba are established options. Wegovy tablets represent a newer GLP-1 route. Eli Lilly's orforglipron (Foundayo) has advanced through European regulatory review, while Viking Therapeutics' oral VK2735 is **still an experimental medicine in phase 3 trials**.

This guide focuses on **tablets and capsules**, not injections, slimming teas or unverified supplements. It distinguishes **authorisation**, **actual availability** and **HSE reimbursement**: none automatically guarantees the others.

## Which weight-loss pills are options in Ireland?

| --- | --- | --- |
| **Orlistat (Xenical, 120 mg)** | Reduces absorption of some dietary fat | Authorised; **prescription-only** for this product. Check pharmacy stock. |

**A product being authorised in the EU does not mean a particular strength is stocked in Ireland today.** Ask a pharmacist to confirm the exact presentation rather than relying on an online advertisement.

## Orlistat versus Mysimba: how are they different?

**Orlistat** acts mainly in the gut by blocking some of the enzymes that digest fat. A meal with more fat can lead to troublesome oily stools, urgency or other gastrointestinal effects. The [HSE's obesity-medicines guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/obesity/medicines/) describes prescription orlistat as an option under clinical eligibility criteria. The 120 mg Xenical product is prescription-only, while the HPRA separately lists some 60 mg Alli packs as pharmacy-only without a prescription. These are different products and supply categories; non-prescription does not mean appropriate for everyone.

**Mysimba** combines naltrexone and bupropion. It acts differently from orlistat and carries precautions relevant to blood pressure, seizures, opioid medicines and other conditions or interactions. The [EMA's Mysimba page](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/mysimba) specifies eligibility criteria and a treatment-response check; the medicine should be stopped after 16 weeks if the patient has not lost at least 5% of initial body weight. A prescriber must determine suitability.

Neither is a substitute for a clinical assessment, and weight-loss percentages from different trials should not be compared as if the studies were identical.

## What about Wegovy tablets and Rybelsus?

**Wegovy tablets and Rybelsus contain semaglutide, but they do not have the same approved purpose or dosing instructions.** Oral Wegovy is intended for weight management in eligible adults, while Rybelsus is authorised for type 2 diabetes. They are not interchangeable products.

The EMA describes a 64-week trial of Wegovy tablets in which mean weight change was **−13.61%** with the tablet versus **−2.18%** with placebo, alongside diet and physical-activity measures. Those are trial-group averages, **not a guaranteed result for a patient or an indication of Irish pharmacy supply**. Nausea, diarrhoea and vomiting are among recognised adverse effects. [EMA evidence summary](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/first-oral-glp-1-treatment-weight-management).

For a closer comparison of the formulations, see [Compound's oral GLP-1 medicines guide](/health/oral-glp-1-medicines-ireland/) and [semaglutide guide](/health/medicines/semaglutide/).

## New weight-loss pills coming through the pipeline

### Eli Lilly: orforglipron (Foundayo)

Orforglipron is an **oral, non-peptide GLP-1 receptor agonist** being developed by Eli Lilly. Unlike oral semaglutide, its chemical structure and absorption characteristics are different. The EMA's medicines committee **recommended** authorisation for Foundayo on **15 October 2026**, covering both weight management in eligible adults and type 2 diabetes. The committee recommendation is an important regulatory milestone, but is **not itself the European Commission's final authorisation**. Nor does it establish when Irish pharmacies will carry it or whether the HSE will fund it. [EMA Foundayo record](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/foundayo).

Potential advantages of an oral treatment include avoiding injections, but actual convenience depends on dosing, tolerability, cost and sustained outcomes. No specific Irish retail price or launch date was verified.

### Viking Therapeutics: oral VK2735

Viking is developing **VK2735 in both tablet and injectable formulations**. It targets two receptors—GLP-1 and GIP—whereas orforglipron is a GLP-1 receptor agonist. Viking announced the start of its **SUNRISE-1 and SUNRISE-2 oral phase 3 trials on 14 October 2026**. These evaluate daily oral VK2735 over 68 weeks in adults with obesity, including a population with type 2 diabetes. [Company phase 3 release](https://ir.vikingtherapeutics.com/2026-10-14-Viking-Therapeutics-Announces-Initiation-of-VK2735-Oral-Phase-3-Clinical-Program-in-Patients-with-Obesity).

An important caution: headlines about Viking's **22% placebo-adjusted weight loss at week 33** concern a study using **weekly injections**, not proof that its tablet achieves that result. The company's separate oral research must be judged by its own outcomes. Viking's [October development update](https://ir.vikingtherapeutics.com/2026-10-28-Viking-Therapeutics-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results-and-Provides-Corporate-Update) expects additional trial readouts in 2027; those are company plans, not guaranteed results or a launch date.

The tablet is **not approved for routine prescription in Ireland**. Its ultimate success depends on efficacy, long-term safety, trial completion and regulatory decisions.

## Which tablet is the most effective?

There is no responsible single ranking that treats all the figures above as directly comparable. Trials may enrol different populations, use different doses, last different periods and report different endpoints. For example, a placebo-adjusted percentage is **not** the same measurement as total mean weight change.

If you are comparing a medicine with your GP, ask about the treatment's authorised indication, expected benefits in people with similar characteristics, significant side effects, interactions, pregnancy considerations, continuation criteria and the total cost of treatment.

## Can you buy weight-loss pills without a prescription?

**Some lower-strength orlistat products are classified by the HPRA as pharmacy-only without a prescription; many other obesity medicines are prescription-only.** The legal classification applies to the precise medicine and strength, not the phrase “weight loss pills” in general. Even a pharmacy-only medicine can be unsuitable because of other illnesses, medicines or pregnancy.

The [HSE warns against buying obesity medicines online](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/obesity/medicines/) because products may be counterfeit or unsafe. Be sceptical of sellers advertising unapproved experimental tablets, “research peptides,” or guaranteed rapid weight loss.

## Am I eligible for a weight-management medicine in Ireland?

A medicine is usually considered in the context of a person's overall health, not simply whether they want to lose a few kilograms. Body mass index (BMI) is one screening measure; clinicians also consider weight-related conditions, previous treatment, other medicines, pregnancy plans, and whether the possible benefits outweigh the risks.

For **prescription orlistat**, the [HSE's guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/obesity/medicines/) describes use with a reduced-calorie diet when someone has a **BMI of at least 30**, or **at least 28 with a weight-related condition**. For **Mysimba**, the [EMA indication](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/mysimba) is a BMI of at least **30**, or **27–under 30 with a weight-related condition** such as type 2 diabetes, abnormal blood lipids or controlled high blood pressure. These are indication thresholds, not an automatic entitlement to a prescription, and the eligibility criteria of newer drugs are product-specific.

An appointment is more useful if you bring a list of current medicines (including supplements), your weight and relevant health history, and any previous experience with weight-management treatment. A pharmacist can help check medicine interactions and whether a named product is actually stocked locally.

## The safety differences that matter most

**Orlistat:** Because unabsorbed fat passes through the bowel, frequent oily stools, urgency and diarrhoea can occur. The [HSE notes](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/obesity/medicines/) that a lower-fat diet can reduce these effects. Orlistat may affect absorption of vitamins **A, D, E and K**; a pharmacist can advise whether a multivitamin is appropriate and when to take it. Interactions with other drugs matter, including anticoagulants, so disclose all medication before starting.

**Mysimba:** This medicine requires particular care with **blood pressure, seizure risk, opioid-containing painkillers and certain psychiatric medicines**. In its [2025 European safety review](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/referrals/mysimba), regulators concluded that benefits continue to outweigh risks within the authorised use, but **cardiovascular safety beyond 12 months remains uncertain**. They strengthened annual reassessment requirements. Patients should not discontinue prescribed medicines or change an opioid treatment plan on their own to start Mysimba.

**GLP-1 tablets:** Nausea, vomiting, constipation and diarrhoea may be troublesome; individual product leaflets include additional warnings and instructions. Symptoms that are severe or persistent require medical advice. The right product for a person with diabetes may differ from the one authorised specifically for obesity. Pregnancy and breastfeeding plans should be raised before any weight-management prescription. See the [GLP-1 side-effects guide](/health/glp1-side-effects-when-to-seek-help/) and [pregnancy guide](/health/glp1-pregnancy-ozempic-wegovy-mounjaro-ireland/).

## When should you know whether a pill is helping?

A useful prescription should have a **planned reassessment point**, not an indefinite repeat without checking results.

- For **orlistat**, the [HSE says](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/obesity/medicines/) a doctor may advise stopping if weight loss is below **5% after three months**.
- For **Mysimba**, the [EMA says](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/mysimba) treatment should stop after **16 weeks** if at least **5% of initial weight** has not been lost. Following the 2025 safety review, treatment should also be stopped after one year if that weight loss has not been maintained, and cardiovascular risk and benefit should be reassessed annually.
- For **newer GLP-1 tablets**, follow the specific approved leaflet and prescriber's monitoring plan. Do not transfer Mysimba's or orlistat's stopping thresholds to a different medicine.

For illustration, **5% of 100 kg is 5 kg**. The percentage is a response threshold for some treatment decisions, not a promise that every eligible patient will lose that amount.

## A practical Irish pharmacy cost checklist

Comparing two medicines by a headline pack price alone can give the wrong answer. Before starting, ask the pharmacy and prescriber these questions:

1. **Is the exact brand and strength stocked?** An EU marketing authorisation does not mean the Irish pharmacy can obtain it.
2. **How much is a full month at the prescribed maintenance dose?** Starter packs and dose-escalation packs may have different costs.
3. **Are consultations, dispensing or monitoring charged separately?** Include the complete expected expense.
4. **Does any HSE scheme actually cover this product for my indication?** The [HSE states that prescription orlistat and Mysimba are not covered by the medical card, Long-Term Illness Scheme or Drugs Payment Scheme](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/obesity/medicines/). Coverage for other products must be checked separately.
5. **What happens if treatment is ineffective or not tolerated?** Ask when follow-up will occur and whether stopping or switching would change your costs.

A genuinely useful comparison pairs **outcomes, risks and real total cost**. It does not invent a price for a medicine still in trials or assume that a newly approved tablet will reach Irish pharmacies immediately.

## What to do next

For a clinical decision, start with a GP or an appropriately qualified prescriber and a registered pharmacist. If weight management treatment is appropriate, discuss your medical history, other medicines, treatment preferences and monitoring plan. **Do not change between semaglutide tablets, other GLP-1 products or injections without prescribing advice.**
