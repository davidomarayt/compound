**Deere enters fiscal 2027 forecasting a return to growth in its large farm-equipment business. Yet its net income range of $4.50 billion to $5.25 billion is centred almost exactly on what it earned in fiscal 2026. The investment question is where that recovery leaks away, and how much cash is left once spending rises.**

Deere & Company reported **net income of $4.860 billion, or $17.95 per diluted share**, for the year ended 1 November 2026, down from $5.027 billion. Net sales and revenues rose 4% to $47.704 billion. Fourth-quarter net income was $1.051 billion, against $1.065 billion a year earlier.

The midpoint of the 2027 range is **$4.875 billion**, about 0.3% above 2026, a Compound calculation. That headline understates the operating forecast, but it also shows that a better large-ag year does not translate one-for-one into group profit.

For the maintained quarterly record, see [Deere’s stock profile](/stocks/de/). This article focuses on what the 2027 guidance implies.

## The recovery is concentrated in large ag

Deere’s Production & Precision Agriculture segment, which sells large tractors, combines and sprayers, had a hard year. Sales fell **9% to $15.682 billion** and operating profit fell 33% to $1.778 billion, an **11.3% margin**. The fourth quarter was weaker still: sales were down 15% and the margin fell to 10.1% from 12.7%.

For 2027 Deere forecasts US and Canadian large-ag industry demand **up 5% to 15%**, and segment sales up by the same range, including about 3% price realisation. It expects the margin to recover to **12.5% to 14.5%**.

Applied to 2026 sales, those ranges imply large-ag operating profit of roughly **$2.06 billion to $2.61 billion**, Compound’s calculation, against $1.778 billion this year. That is the recovery case: about $0.3 billion to $0.8 billion more segment profit.

## Two other segments give some of it back

The surprise is Small Agriculture & Turf. It was Deere’s standout in 2026, with sales up 15% to $11.739 billion and operating profit up 47% to $1.771 billion, a **15.1% margin**. For 2027 Deere guides sales roughly flat with a margin of **12.5% to 13.5%**. On flat sales, that is about $1.47 billion to $1.58 billion of profit, Compound’s calculation, so roughly $0.19 billion to $0.30 billion less.

Construction & Forestry earned $1.473 billion in 2026 on $13.871 billion of sales, a 10.6% margin. Deere guides sales flat to up 10% and a margin of 9.5% to 11.5%, so its contribution could fall or rise modestly.

Financial Services is guided to about **$0.90 billion** of net income, close to 2026’s $885 million. Its fourth-quarter profit fell 21% to $231 million on unfavourable financing spreads, a smaller average portfolio and a higher provision for credit losses.

## A one-off charge and a fading tariff benefit

The 2027 range also includes an estimated **one-time, non-cash pension settlement charge of $0.25 billion to $0.40 billion**, linked to a voluntary lump-sum offer to some US salaried retirees. Adding back the midpoint of that charge, $0.325 billion, gives an underlying midpoint of about **$5.20 billion**, roughly 7% above 2026. That is Compound’s calculation; Deere does not present an adjusted figure.

There is a second baseline issue. Deere recorded **$383 million of tariff recoveries** in 2026. Readers should not assume the same benefit repeats when comparing next year’s margins.

## Cash after capex is guided lower

Deere’s equipment operations, which exclude the finance arm, generated **$4.944 billion of operating cash flow** in 2026 and spent $1.141 billion on property and equipment. That leaves about **$3.80 billion** of simple free cash flow, Compound’s calculation. The operating figure includes $874 million of dividends received from Financial Services, down from $1.368 billion in 2025.

Deere paid **$1.753 billion in dividends**, repurchased **$796 million** of stock and spent $455 million on acquisitions, mainly the $439 million purchase of construction-technology business Tenna. Equipment-operations cash fell to $5.180 billion from $6.340 billion.

For 2027 Deere guides equipment operating cash flow of **$4.5 billion to $5.5 billion** and capital expenditure of about **$1.5 billion**. At the midpoints, free cash flow would be roughly $3.5 billion, Compound’s calculation and about 8% below 2026. Buybacks may therefore depend on where in the range the year lands.

## Dealer stock is a warning sign

Deere’s appendix shows US and Canadian dealer inventory of 100-plus horsepower two-wheel-drive tractors at **31% of trailing 12-month retail sales**, up from 23% a year earlier. Its own retail sales of those tractors were flat over the three months to October. Combine inventory was lower at 4%, against 8%.

Higher dealer stock can delay factory orders even when farmer demand improves. If the large-ag upturn is real, that ratio should fall through the first half of 2027.

## What would confirm or weaken the thesis

The case strengthens if large-ag orders and the dealer inventory ratio improve together, margins move into the 12.5% to 14.5% range without tariff help, and cash flow lands in the upper half of guidance.

It weakens if Small Ag & Turf margins slide below the guided range, credit losses keep rising in Financial Services, or high-horsepower tractor inventory stays elevated into the spring selling season.

**Deere’s 2027 guide does contain a real large-ag recovery. But a softer Small Ag & Turf margin, a pension charge and higher capital spending absorb much of it, so investors should judge 2027 by segment margins and free cash flow rather than the headline profit range.** This analysis makes no price target.
