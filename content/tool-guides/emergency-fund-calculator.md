## What this calculator is for

An emergency fund is accessible money set aside for unexpected costs or a temporary loss of income. This calculator turns your essential monthly expenses and chosen number of months into a target, then shows the gap from what you already have.

## How to read the result

The target is not a universal rule. Three, six or another number of months is a planning choice. The more variable your income, the more dependants you have and the harder it would be to replace income quickly, the more valuable a larger buffer can be.

## Worked example

If essential expenses are €2,500 a month and you choose six months, the target is €15,000. With €6,000 already saved, the gap is €9,000. Saving €500 a month would take about 18 months before allowing for interest.

## Irish context

An emergency fund is usually about liquidity and capital preservation rather than maximising investment return. Deposit interest in Ireland can be subject to DIRT, but the bigger design question is whether the money is accessible when needed.

## What can change the answer

The target changes with essential spending, job security, household income structure, insurance, dependants and access to other liquid savings. Review it after major life changes such as buying a home, having a child or moving from employment to contracting.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not count a credit-card limit as an emergency fund. Do not keep the entire buffer in volatile investments if you may need it during a market fall. And distinguish essential expenses from discretionary spending so the target reflects a realistic emergency budget.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is six months mandatory?
No.

### Should the fund be invested?
For money that may be needed quickly, accessibility and capital stability are usually more important than chasing return.

### Can two-income households hold less?
Potentially, but it depends on whether the incomes are genuinely independent and on household obligations.

### Should I include mortgage payments in essential expenses?
If they would still need to be paid during an emergency, yes.

## Method and limitations

The calculator multiplies monthly essential expenses by the selected number of months, subtracts current emergency savings and divides the remaining gap by the monthly saving amount to estimate time. It does not model interest, inflation or unexpected withdrawals.
