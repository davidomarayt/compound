**Workday, the HR and finance software company, is growing more slowly but earning more per share. Much of the gap comes from buying back stock, and since October part of that has been paid for with new debt. Investors need to know whether AI products can lift growth before the balance-sheet support runs out.**

For the third quarter of fiscal 2027, ended 31 October 2026, Workday reported **revenue of $2.705 billion, up 11.2%**, and **subscription revenue of $2.532 billion, up 12.8%**. It guided to fourth-quarter subscription revenue of **$2.600 billion, growth of 10%**, and raised full-year subscription guidance to **$9.958 billion, up 13%**.

For the maintained quarterly record, see [Workday's stock profile](/stocks/wday/). This article focuses on per-share growth, capital returns and the backlog.

## Per-share growth is running ahead of profit

Workday's non-GAAP operating margin rose to **31.4% from 28.5%**. That is a genuine improvement: total costs grew about 9%, more slowly than revenue. GAAP operating margin, which includes share-based pay and $51 million of restructuring costs, was **12.1%**.

The per-share figures tell a different story from the profit figures:

- **Non-GAAP net income** rose from $622 million to **$695 million, up about 12%**.
- **Non-GAAP diluted EPS** rose from $2.32 to **$2.82, up about 22%**.
- **Diluted weighted shares** fell from 268.6 million to **246.0 million, down about 8%**.

Those are Compound's calculations from the release. Roughly 9 to 10 points of the 22% EPS growth came from having fewer shares, not from higher profit. On a GAAP basis, net income actually fell to **$237 million from $252 million**, partly because interest and investment income dropped.

## The buyback is bigger than free cash flow

In the first nine months of the fiscal year, Workday repurchased **25.6 million shares for $3.66 billion**, at an average of **$142.66** a share. It also spent **$423 million** on taxes for shares withheld when employee awards vested. Together that is about **$4.1 billion**, against **free cash flow of $1.77 billion**, Compound's calculation.

The shortfall came from the balance sheet. Cash and marketable securities were broadly flat at **$5.64 billion**, while debt rose from **$2.99 billion to $5.46 billion** after Workday sold **$2.5 billion of senior notes** in October. Net cash, cash and securities minus debt, fell from about **$2.46 billion to about $0.18 billion**, Compound's calculation. That is before the roughly **$850 million C1.ai acquisition**, which completed in November.

The new notes carry coupons of **5.7% to 6.5%**. On the company's disclosed tranche sizes, that is roughly **$150 million a year** of interest, Compound's calculation. Workday still has **$3.3 billion** of buyback authorisation left.

Share-based compensation is also large. It was **$1.30 billion** in the nine months, equal to about three-quarters of free cash flow.

## Near-term backlog is growing, the long tail less so

Workday's **12-month subscription backlog** rose **12.3% to $9.216 billion**, which supports next year's revenue. Its **total subscription backlog** rose only **6.1% to $27.548 billion**.

Subtracting one from the other, the portion due after 12 months grew from about $17.76 billion to $18.33 billion, or about **3%**, Compound's calculation. Workday says backlog can move with renewal timing and contract length, so one quarter does not prove a trend. But slow growth in long-dated commitments is worth watching alongside a guided slowdown to 10% subscription growth.

## AI is growing from a small base

Workday says its AI products delivered nearly **$150 million of new annual contract value** in the quarter, more than three times a year earlier, and that more than **2,200 core customers** have bought at least one AI product. These are company metrics with no reconciliation to revenue. For scale, quarterly subscription revenue is $2.5 billion.

AI also brings costs. In October Workday signed a **$1.9 billion, five-year non-cancellable commitment** for a third-party hosted infrastructure platform. It also announced a September restructuring affecting about 2% of its workforce, with charges of about $65 million.

## What would confirm or weaken the thesis

The thesis strengthens if subscription growth stabilises near or above 10%, if long-dated backlog grows faster, and if AI contract value starts to show up in revenue while margins hold above 30%.

It weakens if growth keeps slowing, if Workday keeps borrowing to fund buybacks, or if the infrastructure commitment and acquisitions push costs up without accelerating sales.

**Workday is a profitable business with improving margins, but its adjusted EPS growth is flattered by a buyback that now exceeds its free cash flow and is partly debt-funded. The real test is whether AI sales lift subscription growth before that support fades.** This analysis makes no price target.
