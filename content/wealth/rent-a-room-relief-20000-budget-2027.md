*Updated 26 November 2026. This article originally examined the reported September proposal of up to €20,000. That figure has been superseded by the €16,000 Budget announcement.*

Ireland's planned increase in Rent-a-Room Relief faces a new question: should the extra €2,000 of exempt income be available only to people renting two or more bedrooms? Minister of State Robert Troy has asked the Tánaiste to consider that approach during finalisation of the Finance Bill, according to RTÉ on 25 November. The condition is a proposal, not an enacted requirement verified for this article.

## Three different numbers matter

| Figure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| €14,000 | Current annual gross-income exemption limit in Revenue guidance |
| €16,000 | Increased limit announced in Budget 2027 |
| €20,000 | Earlier reported pre-budget proposal, not the announced limit |

At the current threshold, the monthly equivalent is about €1,167. At €16,000 it would be €1,333, an increase of about €167 per month. These are mathematical equivalents rather than market rent estimates or recommendations.

## The possible effect on a one-room landlord

Assuming the current cliff-edge structure remains and the other qualifying conditions are met, here is how alternative designs would differ.

| Gross annual income | Rooms let | Current €14,000 rule | Blanket €16,000 scenario | €16,000 for multiple rooms only |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| €12,000 | 1 | Exempt | Exempt | Exempt |
| €15,000 | 1 | Above limit | Exempt | Above limit |
| €15,600 | 2 | Above limit | Exempt | Exempt |
| €16,200 | 2 | Above limit | Above limit | Above limit |

These are illustrative scenarios, not a prediction of the final legislation. A single room earning €1,250 a month generates €15,000 over 12 months. Two rooms earning €650 each generate €15,600. The proposed restriction would make the distinction between those arrangements important even though their annual incomes are similar.

## Why crossing the threshold matters

Under current rules the exemption is not a tax band. If qualifying gross income exceeds €14,000, the entire income falls outside the exemption, not simply the amount above the threshold. Normal rental-income rules, including eligible expenses, then apply. The eventual liability depends on the taxpayer's circumstances.

Gross income includes amounts received for incidental services associated with the accommodation, such as meals. Expenses do not reduce the gross amount for the purpose of testing the threshold.

## Who can qualify?

Revenue requires the accommodation to form part of an individual's main home in Ireland. Ownership is not itself a condition, although anyone renting rather than owning should check whether subletting is permitted. Typical holiday lettings do not qualify. The rules generally exclude short stays of 28 days or less, subject to specified exceptions such as student accommodation.

The income must still be declared in the appropriate tax return even when exempt. A jointly assessed couple should not assume each partner receives an independent €14,000 allowance for the same income.

## What to check before renting another room

A second lodger may increase household utility costs, insurance requirements and shared-space pressures. Compare the expected annual income with those additional costs, and consider whether the arrangement fits the household. Do not set a rent or commit to a second lodger solely on the assumption that the higher threshold will apply.
