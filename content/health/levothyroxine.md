## At a glance

Levothyroxine replaces the thyroid hormone thyroxine, also called T4. It is used for hypothyroidism and certain specialist indications. Treatment is adjusted with clinical and laboratory follow-up. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/levothyroxine/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### How TSH fits into the thyroid feedback loop

Tissues can convert T4 into T3, which binds nuclear receptors and alters gene expression. The pituitary responds to thyroid hormone levels by adjusting thyroid-stimulating hormone, or TSH. With common forms of primary hypothyroidism, TSH helps interpret replacement, although the approach differs in pregnancy and some pituitary conditions. [HPRA source](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/43278/Licence_PA0711-354-001_01052026114633.pdf)

## What the evidence measures

TSH and free T4 can take time to settle after a change. Symptoms such as fatigue have several possible causes. A stable way of taking the product and a record of formulation changes can help the clinician interpret the next test.

## Read the thyroid results in context

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| TSH | pituitary feedback signal, interpreted for the indication |
| Free T4 | available circulating T4, useful alongside TSH in some situations |


## Why should I report a new supplement before a thyroid test?

Some supplements affect absorption, and some can affect how a laboratory test is measured. The clinician needs your actual product and timing to interpret a result.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Iron, calcium and other medicines can affect absorption. Ask about spacing, pregnancy plans and what to do after a product switch. Contact the treating team for individual interpretation of abnormal results. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/levothyroxine/)
