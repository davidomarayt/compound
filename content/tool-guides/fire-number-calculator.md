## What this calculator is for

The FIRE 'number' is a planning shorthand: the portfolio size that might support a chosen annual spending level under an assumed withdrawal rate. It is useful for framing scale, but it is not a guarantee that a portfolio will last indefinitely.

## How to read the result

The core relationship is **annual spending ÷ withdrawal rate**. At 4%, €40,000 of annual spending implies €1 million. At 3.5%, the same spending implies about €1.14 million. Lower withdrawal assumptions produce larger target portfolios.

## Worked example

If you expect to need €30,000 a year from investments and model a 4% initial withdrawal rate, the simple target is €750,000. If part of your spending will later be covered by a pension or State Pension, the required bridge capital may differ.

## Irish context

Much of the popular 4% discussion comes from historical US-market research, including work inspired by the Trinity Study. Irish investors face different tax rules, product structures, pension access rules and State Pension arrangements. A US historical withdrawal rule should therefore not be treated as an Irish guarantee.

## What can change the answer

Retirement length, asset allocation, market returns, inflation, fees, tax and spending flexibility all affect sustainability. Sequence-of-returns risk matters: poor returns early in retirement can be more damaging than the same average return arriving in a different order.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not multiply current spending by 25 and call the problem solved. Allow for tax, healthcare, housing changes, children, one-off costs and the possibility of a retirement lasting much longer than expected. Keep pension assets and taxable assets distinct because access and tax differ.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 4% safe?
No withdrawal rate is universally safe.

### Should I include my home in the FIRE number?
Only if your plan actually converts home equity into spendable retirement resources.

### Does the State Pension reduce the number?
Potentially, because future guaranteed income can reduce the amount needed from investments after it starts.

### Does this calculator include tax?
Not unless explicitly modelled in the inputs.

## Method and limitations

The tool divides annual spending by the chosen withdrawal-rate assumption. It is a planning ratio, not a stochastic retirement simulation, and it does not model sequence risk, tax, asset allocation or future State Pension policy.
