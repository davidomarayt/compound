**Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) that reduces stomach acid.** It’s used for heartburn and acid reflux, and doctors also prescribe it for some stomach ulcers. If you’re taking it for reflux, you may need **two to three days** of treatment before symptoms start to improve. [HSE omeprazole guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/omeprazole/)

This guide covers adults in Ireland. Follow the leaflet for your exact product or the directions on your prescription. Sources checked 19 November 2026.

## The Science Behind the Drug

**Omeprazole targets the proton pump in the stomach’s acid-producing cells.** Those cells, called parietal cells, use an enzyme called **H⁺/K⁺-ATPase** to move hydrogen ions into the stomach in exchange for potassium ions. That is the final step in secreting stomach acid.

Omeprazole starts as an inactive **prodrug**. The very acidic environment next to an active pump converts it to a form that binds to the pump and disables it. The cell can make replacement pumps, so it can resume acid secretion. [HPRA prescribing information](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/27211/Licence_PA2315-129-001_02092024160756.pdf) · [Review of proton pump inhibition and recovery](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5942154/)

## How long does omeprazole take to work?

The Irish **Losec Control 20 mg** leaflet says improvement can take two to three days. If your symptoms clear completely, the leaflet says to stop the pharmacy course. If symptoms get worse or are still there after 14 days, speak to your GP. For recurring heartburn, ask a pharmacist or your GP about the cause and the best next step. [HPRA patient leaflet](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/28005/c7a9a762-08e1-4671-8512-1b5c52e33b81.pdf) · [HSE reflux guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/heartburn-and-acid-reflux/)

## Can you buy omeprazole without a prescription in Ireland?

**Yes. Irish pharmacies can supply certain products without a prescription**, including Losec Control 20 mg gastro-resistant tablets for short-term reflux treatment in adults. Ask the pharmacist to check whether the product suits you. [HPRA product listing](https://www.hpra.ie/find-a-medicine/for-human-use/authorised-medicines/details/28005)

- **Pharmacy course:** The usual dose for Losec Control is one 20 mg tablet daily for up to 14 days. The leaflet recommends taking it in the morning, preferably on an empty stomach. Swallow it whole. If swallowing is difficult, check the instructions for the exact product with your pharmacist. [Irish patient leaflet](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/28005/c7a9a762-08e1-4671-8512-1b5c52e33b81.pdf)
- **Prescription course:** Your dose and treatment length depend on why it was prescribed. A GP may prescribe a longer course for reflux or another condition. Follow the prescription and check with your GP before stopping long-term treatment. [HSE omeprazole guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/omeprazole/) · [HSE reflux guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/heartburn-and-acid-reflux/)

## What are the side effects of omeprazole?

Headache, nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, wind and stomach discomfort are possible. Ask a pharmacist or GP if side effects are persistent or troubling. [Irish patient leaflet](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/28005/c7a9a762-08e1-4671-8512-1b5c52e33b81.pdf)

Longer courses can lower magnesium or vitamin B12 levels in some people. The HSE advises discussing long-term treatment with your GP before stopping because symptoms can return when acid production rebounds. [HSE guidance on long-term use](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/omeprazole/)

## Who should speak to a pharmacist or GP first?

Tell them if you have liver problems, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or take other medicines. Make a point of mentioning **clopidogrel**, warfarin and any HIV medicines. The Losec Control leaflet specifically says to avoid it if you take **nelfinavir**. Keep taking prescribed medicines as directed and ask your pharmacist or prescriber to check the combination. [HSE interactions](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/omeprazole/) · [Irish patient leaflet](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/28005/c7a9a762-08e1-4671-8512-1b5c52e33b81.pdf)

If you also take [ibuprofen](/health/medicines/ibuprofen/), mention it to your pharmacist or GP. Ibuprofen can upset the stomach, and its suitability depends on your health and other medicines. [HSE omeprazole guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/omeprazole/) · [HSE NSAID guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/nsaids/)

## When should you get medical help?

- **Call 112 or 999** for sudden chest pain with tightness or heaviness, pain spreading to the arm, back, neck or jaw, or pain accompanied by breathlessness, sweating or nausea. These can be signs of a heart attack. Call emergency services for signs of a severe allergic reaction such as trouble breathing or swelling of the lips, tongue or throat. [HSE chest pain guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/chest-pain/) · [HSE omeprazole guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/omeprazole/)
- **Get urgent medical help** if you vomit blood or pass black, tarry stools. **Contact your GP** if food sticks when you swallow, you keep vomiting, or you are losing weight without trying. [Irish patient leaflet](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/28005/c7a9a762-08e1-4671-8512-1b5c52e33b81.pdf) · [HSE reflux guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/heartburn-and-acid-reflux/)
