Yes. If you work Tuesdays, Wednesdays and Thursdays in the Republic of Ireland, you can qualify for a Monday public holiday even though you never work Mondays. For a qualifying employee on a fixed roster and hourly rate, the cash value on a day they do not normally work is **one-fifth of normal weekly pay**. If your employer gives you paid leave instead, check your leave record as well as your payslip. [Workplace Relations Commission guidance](https://www.workplacerelations.ie/en/what_you_should_know/public-holidays/public_hols.html)

## First, check the five weeks before the holiday

Full-time employees qualify immediately. If you work part-time, count your hours **with the same employer** in the five weeks ending on the day before the public holiday. You need at least 40 hours in total. Five weeks at eight hours meets the threshold exactly. You can also qualify with uneven weeks, for example 12 + 0 + 10 + 10 + 8 = 40 hours. If you have two jobs, check each separately. Long absences can affect entitlement, so check the circumstances with payroll if you have been on extended leave. [Section 21 of the Organisation of Working Time Act](https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/1997/act/20/section/21/revised/en/html)

## What would a Monday public holiday be worth?

Take a shop employee working three six-hour shifts every Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday at **€16 an hour**, with no overtime or allowances. Their normal gross week is 18 × €16 = **€288**. They have worked this roster for five weeks and qualify. Here is how the same roster produces three different payslips, depending on the day and the benefit the employer selects.

| Public holiday | Calculation | Gross amount |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Monday, their usual day off | €288 ÷ 5 | €57.60 extra, bringing the week to €345.60 |
| Tuesday, shop closed for the holiday | 6 usual hours × €16 | €96 for Tuesday, already included in the normal €288 week |
| Tuesday, working the usual shift, with an additional day’s pay selected | 6 hours × €16 for work, plus €96 holiday benefit | €192 attributable to Tuesday, bringing the week to €384 |

Dividing €288 by **three** would give €96. That is the value of this worker’s usual shift. Dividing by **five** gives the €57.60 cash benefit for the Monday they normally have off. The Tuesday example assumes the employee’s last normal working day was also six hours. If your hours, commissions or pay vary, ask payroll which reference period and earnings it used. The [salary-to-hourly calculator](/salary-hourly-rate-calculator/) can help check your basic rate.

## What if payroll gives me time off?

The employer chooses the public holiday benefit. The options are the holiday itself off with pay, another paid day off within a month, an extra day of annual leave, or an additional day’s pay. If the holiday already falls on a paid day off, the employer must choose one of the other options. Someone who works the holiday receives the agreed pay for the hours worked as well as the holiday benefit. The €192 in the example depends on the employer choosing cash and the assumed rates. A contract may also provide a shift premium. [WRC public holiday rules](https://www.workplacerelations.ie/en/what_you_should_know/public-holidays/public_hols.html) · [How Sunday premium pay works](/wealth/sunday-premium-pay-ireland/)

**A weekend example:** St Stephen’s Day falls on Saturday 26 December in 2026. A qualifying Monday-to-Friday employee still earns a benefit. Monday 28 December is not automatically a paid day off. Ask which benefit has been allocated before arranging childcare or travel. Good Friday, by contrast, is an ordinary working day under these rules, although an employer can offer it as leave. [WRC calendar](https://www.workplacerelations.ie/en/what_you_should_know/public-holidays/public_hols.html)

## How to check a missing payment or day of leave

1. Mark the public holiday on your roster. Save the previous five weeks of timesheets, the last normal shift before the holiday and the payslip covering the date.
2. Ask payroll in writing: “Which public holiday benefit was allocated for [date], and where can I see the pay calculation, leave credit or paid day off?” Include your normal working days and hours. A salaried employee who got the holiday off on full pay may see their usual salary with no separate payment.
3. If you want to know **in advance**, section 21 lets you request the employer’s decision at least 21 days before the holiday and ask to be told at least 14 days before it. If the employer fails to comply with that request, the statutory default is the holiday itself off with pay, or an additional day’s pay if it was already a paid day off. Keep the dated request and reply. [Section 21(2)–(3)](https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/1997/act/20/section/21/revised/en/html)
