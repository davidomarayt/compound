**The Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 completed its Dáil stages on Wednesday 25 November. Two late Government amendments matter for anyone with a redundancy package or a large defined-benefit pension. The broad change that would have stopped some people getting both a bigger tax-free redundancy payment and a tax-free pension lump sum has been put on hold. A narrower rule aimed at one specific route through a PRSA was added in its place. Separately, the way big defined-benefit pensions are valued against the €2.2 million pension cap will change from 1 January 2027.**

The Bill still has to pass the Seanad and be signed into law before it becomes the Finance Act. The figures below come from the Bill text published by the Oireachtas and from the [official Dáil record](https://data.oireachtas.ie/akn/ie/debateRecord/dail/2026-11-24/debate/mul@/main.xml) of the Report Stage debate on 24 November. The [Oireachtas bill record](https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/bills/bill/2026/98/) shows Report and Final Stages completed on 25 November.

## The quick version

| Change | Who it affects | What the Dáil agreed | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wider redundancy lump-sum rule (old section 10) | People who get a tax-free termination payment and hold PRSAs or other modern pension products | Put on hold. It will only start when the Minister for Finance makes an order | No date set |
| New section 790AB | People who waived an occupational pension lump sum at redundancy, then moved that pension into a PRSA or similar product | The waived amount is taken off the tax-free lump sum they can later take from that product | The Bill gives it no separate start date, so the general 1 January 2027 date applies |
| New SFT valuation factors | People retiring with large defined-benefit pensions, mostly senior public servants and some private-sector executives | Lower factors, so the same pension counts as a smaller fund against the cap | From 1 January 2027 |

## Why the redundancy rule mattered

When you leave a job with an ex-gratia payment, part of it can be tax free. Revenue allows three ways to work out the tax-free amount: the basic exemption, the increased exemption and the **Standard Capital Superannuation Benefit (SCSB)**. Our [redundancy tax guide](/wealth/redundancy-ireland-statutory-ex-gratia-tax/) explains each one.

The SCSB and the increased exemption are cut by the value of any tax-free lump sum you can still get from that employer's pension. Tax law calls that value the "relevant capital sum". You can make the cut disappear by signing away (waiving) your right to the pension lump sum. People do this when a bigger tax-free redundancy payment now is worth more to them than a lump sum later.

The catch was that the relevant capital sum only counted traditional schemes, such as occupational pension schemes. PRSAs and other newer products were left out. [The Irish Times reported](https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/11/24/tax-free-lump-sum-loophole-set-to-close-in-finance-act/) that this let some people waive the lump sum at redundancy, move the pension into a PRSA, and still take a tax-free lump sum from the PRSA at retirement.

The Bill as published, in section 10, would have brought PRSAs and other "relevant pension arrangements" into the relevant capital sum. That is the change now on hold.

## What changed at Report Stage

On 24 November the Tánaiste and Minister for Finance, Simon Harris, told the Dáil that officials had found section 10, as worded, "could give rise to unintended consequences for modern pension products, particularly PRSAs". There was not enough time to fix every issue safely. So amendment No. 5, which the Dáil agreed, says section 10 will only come into operation "on such day as the Minister for Finance may by order appoint".

In its place, amendment No. 4 inserts a new **section 790AB** into the Taxes Consolidation Act. It applies where all three of these are true:

1. you got a termination payment (redundancy or otherwise, but not retirement) that qualified for relief under Schedule 3;
2. you irrevocably gave up your right to the occupational pension lump sum to get that relief; and
3. you then transferred that pension into a PRSA or another relevant pension arrangement.

If so, the amount you gave up is deducted from the tax-free lump sum you can take from the PRSA or other product when you retire. The Tánaiste said this deals with "a specific scenario that was identified by Revenue".

### A worked example

These figures are made up to show how the rule works. They are not a tax calculation for any real person.

- Aoife is made redundant after 20 years. Her employer pension would have paid a tax-free lump sum worth **€60,000** in today's money.
- Using the SCSB, her tax-free redundancy amount would be cut by that €60,000. She waives the pension lump sum, so the full SCSB applies to her ex-gratia payment.
- She then moves the pension into a PRSA.
- Under section 790AB, when she retires, **€60,000 is taken off** the tax-free lump sum the PRSA would otherwise allow. If the PRSA rules would have let her take €90,000 tax free, she could take €30,000 tax free (€90,000 − €60,000).

Two points are worth checking with a pension adviser or Revenue before you rely on this:

- **Start date.** Section 790AB has no start date of its own in the text. The Bill's general rule is that Part 1 comes into operation on 1 January 2027 unless it says otherwise.
- **Past redundancies.** The new section describes people who "received" a termination payment and "transferred" their entitlements. It does not name a cut-off date for those events. People who did this some years ago could be affected when they draw the lump sum. Revenue guidance, when it comes, should clarify this.

People who waived a lump sum and left their pension where it was are not affected by section 790AB. Neither is anyone who did not waive anything.

## Big defined-benefit pensions: new valuation factors from 2027

The second late change, amendment No. 6, was carried by 81 votes to 61. It changes how defined-benefit pensions are valued against the **standard fund threshold (SFT)**. The SFT is the most tax-relieved pension you can build up without an extra tax charge. Anything above it is taxed at 40% up front. The Tánaiste told the Dáil the SFT is currently €2.2 million. He said the Finance Act 2024 already set out steps up to €2.8 million by 2029.

A defined-benefit pension pays a yearly amount, so the law multiplies it by an age-related factor to turn it into a fund value. The new table cuts those factors for benefits built up since 1 January 2014:

| Age when the pension starts | Factor until 31 Dec 2026 | Factor from 1 Jan 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 or under | 37 | 25 |
| 55 | 33 | 23 |
| 60 | 30 | 21 |
| 65 | 26 | 19 |
| 66 | 25 | 18 |
| 70 and over | 22 | 16 |

A factor of 20 still applies to benefits built up before 1 January 2014.

**What it means in euro.** These examples ignore any lump sum and any pre-2014 service, both of which also count:

- A **€60,000** a year pension starting at 60 is valued at €1.8 million under the old factor (€60,000 × 30). From 2027 it is valued at €1.26 million (€60,000 × 21).
- A **€100,000** a year pension starting at 60 is valued at €3 million today, which is €800,000 over the €2.2 million SFT. At 40% that excess would cost €320,000 in tax. From 2027 the same pension is valued at €2.1 million (€100,000 × 21), below today's €2.2 million threshold.

Sinn Féin's Pearse Doherty opposed the change. He argued in the debate that it helps people whose pensions are far above what most workers earn. The Tánaiste said the change follows the de Buitléir review of the SFT. He said that review found the current rules were affecting recruitment and retention in senior posts.

The same amendment also changes how a public-service **occupational supplementary pension** is treated. One paid before State pension age will be counted as a once-off payment rather than a pension for life. The Tánaiste said this fixes an overvaluation.

**Who should pay attention:** anyone close to retirement with a large defined-benefit pension, especially if they were planning to retire in December 2026. The old factors apply to pensions that start on or before 31 December 2026, so for some people a retirement date in early 2027 will produce a lower valuation. Take advice before moving a retirement date for tax reasons alone.

## What has not changed

- The **€200,000 lifetime limit** on tax-free retirement lump sums is untouched by these amendments. Our [pension lump-sum tax guide](/wealth/pension-lump-sum-tax-ireland/) covers how it works.
- The basic exemption for redundancy payments is unchanged. You can test your own figures with the [Redundancy Calculator Ireland](/redundancy-calculator-ireland/).
- The headline Budget 2027 tax changes, such as the wider standard rate band and higher tax credits, are covered in our [Budget 2027 guide](/wealth/budget-2027-what-to-watch/).

## What to do now

1. **Facing redundancy and thinking of waiving a pension lump sum?** Ask your employer or pension trustees what the lump sum is worth. Compare that with the extra tax-free redundancy amount you would gain. If you might later move the pension to a PRSA, assume section 790AB will reduce the PRSA lump sum.
2. **Already waived and moved to a PRSA?** Keep your termination-payment paperwork and the waiver. You may need them when you retire.
3. **Retiring with a large defined-benefit pension?** Ask your scheme for an SFT valuation under both the 2026 and 2027 factors.

The Bill now goes to the Seanad. We will update this article if the Seanad stage changes any of these sections, or when the Finance Act is signed.
