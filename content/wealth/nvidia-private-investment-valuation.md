Nvidia’s growing investment portfolio needs a different test from its chip business: how much of the increase reflects new money committed, and how much reflects investments becoming more valuable? In the quarter ended 25 October 2026, its private-equity carrying value rose even though impairments and unrealised losses exceeded unrealised gains.

The distinction matters to shareholders who assign value to Nvidia’s investments alongside its operating business. A larger portfolio does not, by itself, demonstrate a successful investment return. Nor does an accounting loss establish that the entire strategy has failed.

## The portfolio grew because additions outweighed losses

The following bridge uses Nvidia’s [unaudited Q3 supplemental disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000088/supplementalfinancialinfor.htm), furnished with its 17 November earnings announcement. It is supplemental information accompanying an 8-K, not a Q3 Form 10-Q.

| Private-equity carrying-value movement | Q3 FY2027, US$bn |
|---|---:|
| Opening balance | 47.898 |
| Net additions | +15.431 |
| Unrealised gains | +0.804 |
| Reclassifications | −0.637 |
| Impairments and unrealised losses | −1.846 |
| Closing balance | 61.650 |

Compound calculates a **$13.752 billion increase**, or **28.7%**, in the carrying value. But gains less impairments and unrealised losses were **negative $1.042 billion**. Net additions exceeded the entire increase in the portfolio. That is the central investment finding: the balance expanded through additions despite adverse net valuation movements.

“Net additions” is the disclosure’s accounting label. It should not be relabelled as a precise cash-spending figure or treated as investment profit. Reclassifications also change the category being measured without necessarily representing a loss or a cash sale.

## The previous quarter provides a useful contrast

[Note 6 of Nvidia’s July Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm) reported $13.106 billion of net additions, $4.900 billion of unrealised gains and $121 million of impairments and unrealised losses in fiscal Q2. It also recorded $12.323 billion of reclassifications, primarily following public-market trading.

The July closing balance of $47.898 billion matches October’s opening balance. Subtracting losses from gains gives positive **$4.779 billion** in Q2, compared with negative $1.042 billion in Q3: a **$5.821 billion deterioration** in that particular accounting contribution.

That comparison is not a portfolio performance percentage. The holdings and their timing changed, and the disclosures do not supply the cash-flow timing needed to calculate a reliable investment return. It also excludes other investment categories. Applying the result to every Nvidia investment would overstate what this bridge establishes.

## Private valuations are not cash available to shareholders

Nvidia describes these holdings as primarily investments in privately held companies, measured using cost, impairments and observable price changes. The absence of a daily quoted share price makes the carrying value different from a readily executable sale price. A financing transaction can inform an accounting mark without giving Nvidia an immediate opportunity to sell its entire holding on the same terms.

This creates two questions for a valuation model. First, what value should be assigned to the investments after recognising uncertainty over eventual realisation? Second, what future funding will be required to pursue the investment strategy? A model that assumes both the full carrying value as immediately available cash and substantial future investment benefits risks overstating flexibility.

The broader business remains important. Nvidia’s [results release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/) reported $115.8 billion of quarterly revenue and 74.8% GAAP gross margin. Those are operating-scale measures; they do not validate the private portfolio’s eventual returns. Equally, the valuation setback described here is not evidence that customers stopped buying chips.

## What would strengthen the investment case?

Look for disclosure that separates additions, reclassifications, valuation changes and realised proceeds. Actual exits would provide evidence about realisation, while subsequent write-downs or repeated funding needs would change the risk assessment. Neither outcome can be inferred merely from a rising closing balance.

For now, the useful conclusion is narrower: Nvidia increased its exposure to private investments during a quarter when their net valuation contribution turned negative. Shareholders should model that exposure separately from the earnings power of the hardware and software business.
