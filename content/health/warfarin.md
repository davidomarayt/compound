## At a glance

Warfarin is an anticoagulant used when a clinician wants to reduce the risk of harmful clots. The appropriate INR target and test schedule depend on the indication and care plan. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/warfarin/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### What INR measures and why timing matters

Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase, the enzyme behind vitamin K recycling. The liver needs reduced vitamin K to activate several clotting factors, including II, VII, IX and X. The effect on clotting evolves as existing factors clear. INR standardises a clotting time measurement and helps assess whether anticoagulation is within the person’s agreed range. [DailyMed source](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=73b8e5ab-86f8-44f8-b290-346acb81c9ae)

## What the evidence measures

INR is a safety and treatment marker. Clinical outcomes such as prevention of clots and serious bleeding are separate. A single out-of-range result is interpreted alongside previous results, changes to medicines, illness and diet.

**Example record.** On Monday the clinic receives a reading outside the range it previously agreed with the patient. The person also reports starting a new prescription on Friday and being unwell over the weekend. Those details are useful because the change in the test could reflect several factors. The clinic can review the trend and advise when to repeat the test. The record documents a decision context. It gives no dose or dose change.

## What an INR record should show

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Result | date, reading and the target recorded by your clinic |
| Context | medicine changes, missed doses, illness or major dietary changes |


## What if an INR result is different from the last one?

Pass the reading, date, current schedule and recent changes to your clinic. A fictional example or generic target range cannot determine what you should take.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Head injury or serious bleeding needs urgent assessment. Check new prescriptions and non-prescription products with a pharmacist or anticoagulation clinic. Follow the clinic’s instructions rather than adjusting treatment from an online example. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/warfarin/)
