## What this calculator is for

Investment fees look small when quoted as annual percentages, but they reduce the amount of capital left to compound. This calculator compares otherwise identical growth scenarios with different fee assumptions so the long-term euro impact becomes visible.

## How to read the result

Focus on the **difference in ending wealth**, not only the annual fee percentage. A 1% fee is not simply 1% of the original investment once; it is deducted repeatedly from a changing balance and also removes the future growth that money could have earned.

## Worked example

If €100,000 grows for 25 years at 6% before fees, the difference between a 0.5% and 1.5% annual fee can become tens of thousands of euro because the fee drag compounds. The exact result depends on contributions and the return assumption.

## Irish context

Investment products can include management charges, platform fees, adviser fees, transaction costs and fund expenses. Comparing products requires understanding which charges are already reflected in quoted fund performance and which are charged separately.

## What can change the answer

The effect grows with time, portfolio size and the fee gap. Higher assumed gross returns can make the absolute euro difference larger, but they also increase the uncertainty of the projection.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not choose an investment solely because it is cheapest: diversification, suitability, tax, tracking quality, service and risk matter. But do not dismiss a recurring fee as 'only 1%' either. Also avoid double-counting fees already embedded in a fund's reported return.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 1% fee really important?
Over a long horizon it can be, because both the fee and lost compounding accumulate.

### Does this include tax?
No.

### Are all fees quoted the same way?
No. Read the product documentation.

### Is the cheapest fund always best?
No.

## Method and limitations

The calculator projects the same cash flows under the entered gross-return assumptions and deducts annualised fees in the model. It is an illustration and does not reproduce every charging convention or product tax regime.
