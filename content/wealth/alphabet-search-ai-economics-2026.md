Alphabet owns businesses with different growth paths and risk profiles. The investment question is not simply whether Google remains widely used. It is whether the company can keep converting that usage into cash while funding the technology and distribution needed to remain competitive.

This guide separates those questions. Use the [Alphabet company page](/stocks/googl/) for current reported results and the interactive share-price chart.

## How does Alphabet make money?

Alphabet's 2025 annual report separates Google Services, Google Cloud and Other Bets. Google Services includes advertising-led activities such as Search and YouTube, alongside subscriptions, platforms and devices. Google Cloud serves business customers. Other Bets contains businesses outside the core Google operations. The filing also distinguishes publicly traded Class A shares, GOOGL, from non-voting Class C shares, GOOG; Class B has enhanced voting rights. [Source: Alphabet annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000018/goog-20251231.htm).

| Activity | Core investor question | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Search advertising | Can valuable commercial queries remain profitable? | Treating all usage as equally monetisable |
| YouTube and other Services | Does engagement convert into attractive returns? | Ignoring content and distribution costs |
| Cloud | Can enterprise growth outpace the costs needed to serve it? | Treating revenue growth as free cash flow |
| Other Bets | What future value is justified by evidence? | Assigning mature-business certainty to experiments |

## Search: follow commercial value, not just query counts

A query that helps a customer choose a product can have different advertising economics from a query with little purchasing intent. More total usage does not necessarily produce proportionate revenue growth.

AI changes the analytical questions. If an answer satisfies the user directly, where does advertising fit? Does the user return more often? Does the additional engagement pay for the computational cost? These are questions to evaluate using disclosed evidence, not assumptions that every new interface is either a threat or a windfall.

Separate defence from expansion. Spending that protects existing cash flow may be necessary and valuable, but it should not automatically be counted as creating a second independent profit stream.

## Distribution has a cost

The ability to reach users is economically important. Investors should examine traffic-acquisition costs and relevant commercial arrangements alongside advertising revenue. A gross revenue number alone does not show how much remains after obtaining that distribution.

Legal and regulatory outcomes can change business practices or costs. Rather than guessing a single resolution, consider what happens if a distribution advantage becomes more expensive or less exclusive. Connect that scenario to margins and user acquisition, while keeping unresolved legal outcomes explicitly uncertain.

## Google Cloud: a different customer relationship

Cloud is driven by enterprise workloads and purchasing decisions rather than the same advertising model as Search. Compare revenue growth, operating profit and the resources required to support customers.

Backlog can indicate future demand, but its timing and profitability matter. A contract signed today may become revenue over several years. It should not be added to annual sales as if it were immediate incremental revenue.

Avoid valuing Cloud on optimistic margins while allocating all shared infrastructure costs to another part of the group. A sum-of-the-parts analysis still needs to reconcile to the company's overall cash generation.

## Other Bets: keep optionality separate

An emerging business can become valuable, but commercial opportunity, technical progress and distributable cash are different milestones. Use scenarios that reflect additional financing, execution time and the chance of disappointing outcomes.

A disciplined model can assign limited value to uncertain projects and show how successful outcomes would change the result. That is often easier to audit than treating the most exciting possible future as the base case.

## A cash-flow sensitivity exercise

The figures below are **hypothetical**, not Alphabet results. Suppose a group generates $100bn operating cash flow:

| Annual cash capex assumption | Operating cash flow less capex |
|---|---:|
| $30bn | $70bn |
| $50bn | $50bn |
| $70bn | $30bn |

This simple calculation excludes other financing and investment considerations. Its purpose is to show why infrastructure spending can materially change available cash even when the underlying operations remain profitable.

Do not assume all high spending will disappear after one year. Some equipment requires replacement, and continued growth may require continued expansion. Equally, do not assume every investment is unproductive merely because cash leaves before the return arrives.

## How to think about valuation

Estimate sustainable cash flow after the investment required to maintain competitive products. Then test growth, margins and an appropriate valuation range. Separate investment gains from recurring operating earnings when necessary.

The distinction between business quality and stock price matters. A company can retain strong competitive positions while shareholders earn disappointing returns if the purchase price already assumed more growth than the business delivers.

For GOOG versus GOOGL, consider voting rights and the actual prices and trading costs available through your broker. Neither ticker represents a separate Google business. Small price differences do not create a risk-free opportunity once rights, costs and execution are considered.

## Alphabet investor checklist

- Compare advertising growth with acquisition and delivery costs.
- Look for evidence about AI monetisation and service costs.
- Review Cloud growth and profitability together.
- Reconcile group operating cash flow with infrastructure spending.
- Treat Other Bets assumptions separately from established earnings.
- Check the share class, diluted share count and valuation used in your model.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Google and Alphabet different stocks?

Alphabet is the listed parent. GOOG and GOOGL are share classes in Alphabet, not separate investments in Search and the other businesses.

### Does more AI usage guarantee more profit?

No. Monetisation and the cost of serving that usage determine the result. Usage is one input, not the final economic outcome.

### Should Other Bets be valued at zero?

Not necessarily. The point is to make the uncertainty visible and avoid depending on speculative success to justify an otherwise expensive valuation.
