The Government plans to spend **€682 million less on accommodation for Ukrainian refugees and international protection applicants in 2027**, according to figures outlined by Justice Minister Jim O'Callaghan following Budget 2027.

The projected reduction, reported on 8 October, reflects changes to State-funded accommodation and efforts to process asylum applications more quickly.

## Where will the €682 million reduction come from?

| Accommodation programme | Planned reduction in 2027 |
|---|---:|
| Ukrainian temporary-protection accommodation | **€430 million** |
| International protection accommodation | **€252 million** |
| **Combined reduction** | **€682 million** |

The biggest change concerns accommodation for people who fled Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

O'Callaghan told the Dáil that Ukrainian accommodation cost approximately **€1.16 billion in 2024**. The allocation fell to **€606 million in 2026**, and the Government expects to require around **€177 million in 2027**.

If achieved, that would be roughly **85% below the 2024 level**.

## Why are accommodation costs expected to fall?

The Government plans to phase out commercially contracted State accommodation for Ukrainian beneficiaries of temporary protection by **March 2027**, with additional emphasis on people securing independent housing. It has also approved a reduction in the Accommodation Recognition Payment for hosts.

Separately, the Minister expects international protection accommodation spending to fall below **€1 billion in 2027**, citing faster application processing and changes to accommodation arrangements.

## What does it mean for taxpayers?

The €682 million is a **reduction in planned spending**, not money already saved or a separate fund earmarked for tax cuts.

Whether the figures are achieved depends on future accommodation demand, processing times and how successfully people move into other housing. Some costs could shift to other supports, including social welfare.

**The test in 2027 will be whether the Government delivers the budget reduction without adding substantial housing pressures or costs elsewhere.**
