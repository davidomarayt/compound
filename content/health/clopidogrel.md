## At a glance

Clopidogrel is an antiplatelet used in situations such as after certain vascular events or procedures. Its purpose and planned duration depend on the indication and the other antiplatelets prescribed. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/clopidogrel/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### P2Y12 signalling and how the liver activates the drug

A platelet releases ADP after activation. ADP acts on P2Y12 receptors and amplifies platelet recruitment. Clopidogrel is a prodrug. Liver enzymes including CYP2C19 help form the active metabolite, which blocks P2Y12 irreversibly for the life of affected platelets. [DailyMed source](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=55731d95-0c99-ff61-e063-6394a90a1ab5)

## What the evidence measures

Assessing benefit involves the outcomes relevant to the original indication, such as preventing heart attacks, strokes or stent thrombosis, alongside bleeding. A timed combination with aspirin can have a different plan from single-drug treatment.

## What should I bring before a planned procedure?

Bring the event or stent date if known, the medicine list and any written treatment schedule. The procedural team and prescriber can coordinate on bleeding and clotting risks.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Omeprazole and esomeprazole can affect clopidogrel activation. Ask the prescribing team to review the combination and to advise before surgery or any interruption. Serious bleeding needs urgent assessment. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/clopidogrel/)
