## At a glance

Beta blockers are used for several conditions, including some heart rhythm problems, angina and heart failure. Different products have different evidence and selectivity, so the individual prescription matters. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/beta-blockers/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Adrenaline signals, heart rate and differences between products

Adrenaline and noradrenaline bind beta receptors. Blocking beta-1 receptors in the heart can slow the heart rate and blunt its response to sympathetic stimulation. Blocking beta-1 receptors in the kidney can reduce renin release. Products that block beta-2 receptors can also affect airway smooth muscle, and selectivity can vary with the medicine and exposure. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/beta-blockers/)

## What the evidence measures

A change in resting pulse is a measurable effect. Outcomes such as angina frequency, rhythm control or fewer hospital admissions are separate and depend on the indication and product tested.

## Check the target of treatment

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Pulse or rhythm | ask what readings or symptoms the team wants recorded |
| Angina or heart failure | note changes in daily activity and symptoms |


## Are all beta blockers interchangeable?

They differ in receptor effects, formulations, duration and evidence for specific conditions. Bring the exact name and strength to a review rather than treating the class name as the prescription.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Tell the prescriber if you have asthma or COPD, low pulse symptoms, or take other medicines that slow heart rate. Discuss stopping with the prescriber because sudden withdrawal can cause problems. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/beta-blockers/)
