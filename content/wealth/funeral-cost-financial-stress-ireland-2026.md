A survey of 1,027 adults commissioned by MetLife and carried out with YouGov has highlighted the financial difficulties associated with arranging funerals in Ireland. RTÉ reported on 24 November that around one in five respondents experienced severe financial stress around funeral expenses, while 21% reported costs exceeding expectations.

The figures are survey responses, not a national estimate of the price of a funeral. They nevertheless point to a practical problem: a family can have very little time to make decisions involving thousands of euro.

## Build a budget around the complete bill

Ask the funeral director for an itemised written estimate before agreeing to optional services. Check what is included in the professional fee and what will be invoiced separately. Burial or cremation fees, a grave purchase, transport, notices, flowers and refreshments may have different suppliers. A headstone may be a separate later expense.

Use the [Compound funeral cost calculator](/funeral-cost-calculator/) to enter the quotes and requirements relevant to your family rather than treating any national average as a fixed price.

A contingency can help where the precise total remains uncertain. For example, a hypothetical €5,000 base budget plus 10% contingency is €5,500. That is a planning illustration, not a market quote.

## How much would a dedicated savings fund require?

The following scenarios assume no interest, no withdrawals and a starting balance of zero. The targets are illustrative rather than estimates of typical funeral prices.

| Savings goal | Over 3 years | Over 5 years | Over 10 years |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| €3,000 | €83.33/month | €50/month | €25/month |
| €5,000 | €138.89/month | €83.33/month | €41.67/month |
| €8,000 | €222.22/month | €133.33/month | €66.67/month |

An accessible reserve is different from an investment account whose value can fall. Check who can access the money in an emergency, especially when funds are held solely in the name of the deceased.

## Financial support when a family cannot afford the essentials

The Department of Social Protection says families who cannot meet essential funeral expenses can apply for an Additional Needs Payment through the Community Welfare Service. Eligibility and the amount are assessed individually, taking account of resources, income and expenses. There is no automatic entitlement to have the entire bill paid.

Check the [official Additional Needs Payment guidance](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/additional-needs-payment/) and contact the service promptly if you expect difficulty paying. Also ask an employer or insurer about any existing bereavement or death-related benefits; the policy terms determine whether anything is payable.

## Before an urgent decision arises

A conversation about burial or cremation preferences, the desired service and a realistic spending limit may reduce uncertainty. Keep contact details for relevant insurers and advisers accessible. A budget does not need to dictate how a family marks a death: it is a way to distinguish essential arrangements from optional spending.

The survey does not establish that any particular insurance or savings product is necessary. The appropriate approach depends on available cash, household commitments and existing cover.
