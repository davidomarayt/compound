<p><em>News analysis · 23 September 2026. The €20,000 figure discussed below is a reported Budget 2027 proposal. Revenue's current published Rent-a-Room Relief limit remains €14,000.</em></p>

Ireland's Rent-a-Room Relief could be expanded in Budget 2027, with a reported proposal allowing some homeowners who make **multiple rooms** available to earn up to **€20,000 a year tax-free**.

The proposal is understood to be aimed particularly at increasing student accommodation from bedrooms that already exist inside occupied homes.

The key point for homeowners today is simple:

> **Nothing has changed yet.**

Revenue's current published threshold remains **€14,000 a year in gross qualifying income**.

If qualifying gross Rent-a-Room income exceeds that limit, the current rules do not simply tax the excess. Revenue says the **entire amount becomes taxable**.

[Revenue: current Rent-a-Room qualifying conditions](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/land-and-property/rent-a-room-relief/qualifying-conditions.aspx).

Because this is a Budget proposal rather than a current rule, keep the existing €14,000 threshold separate from speculation. Compound's [Budget 2027 tracker](/wealth/budget-2027-what-to-watch/) follows the final decision, while our [tax credits guide](/wealth/tax-credits-ireland-explained/) explains how this relief differs from a tax credit.

## What is being proposed?

Reporting on 23 September says Higher Education Minister James Lawless is pushing for a change that could increase the tax-free ceiling to as much as **€20,000** for homeowners who rent more than one room.

The reported model would not necessarily be a blanket €20,000 allowance for every person renting a room.

Instead, the additional relief could be linked to the **number of rooms made available**.

That would make the proposal more directly targeted at homeowners who can provide accommodation to multiple students or lodgers.

[Classic Hits report on the €20,000 proposal](https://www.classichits.ie/news/national-news/rent-a-room-tax-break-could-rise-to-e20000-under-student-accommodation-plan/).

The Taoiseach had already confirmed that the Government was considering an expansion of the scheme, describing Rent-a-Room Relief as a measure that had helped bring more student accommodation onto the market.

[Irish Times: Rent-a-Room expansion under consideration](https://www.irishtimes.com/politics/2026/09/19/ministers-seek-more-help-with-third-level-fees-and-return-of-energy-credits-in-budget/).

RTÉ has also reported that increasing the relief available and/or the number of rooms covered is among the measures being discussed ahead of Budget 2027.

[RTÉ Budget 2027 analysis](https://www.rte.ie/news/analysis-and-comment/2026/0920/1592213-budget-2027-analysis/).

## What are the rules today?

Under Revenue's current rules, Rent-a-Room Relief can exempt up to **€14,000 of gross annual qualifying income**.

The €14,000 figure is a threshold, not a tax-free band.

Revenue also says qualifying Rent-a-Room income is exempt from:

- Income Tax;
- PRSI; and
- USC.

[Revenue: Rent-a-Room Relief](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/land-and-property/rent-a-room-relief/index.aspx).

## Why the €20,000 proposal could matter

A €20,000 annual threshold is equivalent to approximately **€1,667 a month in combined gross qualifying income**.

If two rooms were rented throughout the year, that would average about **€833 per room per month**.

If three rooms were rented throughout the year, it would average about **€556 per room per month**.

Those figures are illustrations, not suggested rents.

The important feature is the combined threshold.

Under today's €14,000 rule, renting more than one room can push a homeowner through the exemption ceiling relatively quickly.

A higher threshold specifically for multiple rooms could therefore change the economics of taking a second lodger.

## The current cliff edge is unusually important

Consider a homeowner receiving exactly €14,000 of qualifying gross Rent-a-Room income.

If all the conditions are satisfied, that income can be exempt.

Now increase the gross amount by €1.

At **€14,001**, Revenue's current rule means the person is no longer entitled to the relief for that income and the total becomes taxable, with the normal rental-income rules then becoming relevant.

That makes the threshold much more significant than an ordinary tax band.

Moving the threshold to €20,000 for qualifying multi-room lettings could therefore protect several thousand euro of additional income from crossing that cliff edge.

## Not every room rental qualifies

An increased threshold would not automatically make every type of short-term letting tax free.

Revenue's current conditions require the room or rooms to be in a **qualifying residence**.

The property must be in Ireland and must be the individual's sole or main residence during the relevant tax year.

The relief can apply to a self-contained unit attached to the home, such as a qualifying basement flat or converted attached garage.

A detached unit does not qualify merely because it is on the same property.

[Revenue: qualifying residence](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/land-and-property/rent-a-room-relief/what-type-of-residence-qualifies.aspx).

## Short-term tourist accommodation is excluded

Revenue specifically excludes income from short-term guests, including typical accommodation booked through online booking platforms.

The general rule also requires a minimum continuous letting period of more than 28 days.

There are exceptions for certain non-tourist residential arrangements, including:

- full-time or part-time students;
- language students;
- respite care;
- and four-day-a-week digs.

That makes student accommodation particularly relevant to the policy discussion.

## You still have to declare exempt income

Tax-free does not mean that the income disappears from the tax system.

Revenue says people claiming Rent-a-Room Relief must still enter the exempt amount on their Income Tax Return.

PAYE taxpayers generally do this through myAccount using Form 12.

Self-assessed taxpayers use Form 11 through ROS.

[Revenue: claiming Rent-a-Room Relief](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/land-and-property/rent-a-room-relief/how-is-relief-applied.aspx).

## Would €20,000 increase the supply of rooms?

That is the policy objective behind the reported proposal, but the eventual effect is uncertain.

Ireland already has bedrooms sitting unused in owner-occupied homes.

Bringing some of that capacity onto the market has one obvious advantage:

**it can create accommodation without waiting for a new apartment block or student residence to be built.**

But tax is only one reason a homeowner may decide not to rent a room.

Other considerations include:

- privacy;
- sharing kitchens and bathrooms;
- insurance;
- household bills;
- compatibility with a lodger;
- security;
- and the practical responsibilities involved.

A higher exemption may make the arrangement more attractive at the margin, particularly for someone considering renting a second room.

Whether it produces a large increase in supply will depend on how the final scheme is designed and how homeowners respond.

## What homeowners should do now

Do not plan on a €20,000 exemption yet.

The current Revenue rule remains **€14,000**.

Until Budget 2027 and any subsequent legislation confirm a change, homeowners should calculate their tax position using the existing threshold.

The proposal is nevertheless worth watching because a multi-room structure would be a meaningful change to how Rent-a-Room Relief currently operates.

If introduced as reported, it could shift the scheme from a general spare-room incentive toward a more explicit attempt to unlock **multiple bedrooms within existing homes**.
