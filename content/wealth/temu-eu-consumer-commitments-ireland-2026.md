**Temu has promised European consumer authorities that it will refund returns within 14 days, separate real discounts from flattering price comparisons, drop "almost sold out" pressure messages and stop forcing shoppers to play games like "spin the wheel" before they can browse. The commitments were accepted on Thursday 26 November, the day before Black Friday, after an action led by consumer authorities in Ireland, Belgium and Germany.**

For Irish shoppers the useful point is not that Temu has turned over a new leaf. It is that most of these promises describe rights you already had under EU law. Now there is a written list that the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC) and its counterparts can enforce against.

## What Temu has committed to

According to the [European Commission's announcement](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_2594), the commitments cover:

| Area | What Temu has promised |
| --- | --- |
| Refunds and returns | Refunds processed within 14 days; a withdrawal form in every official EU and EEA language |
| Prices | A clear difference between genuine discounts and comparisons with a "general reference price"; taxes and fees shown before checkout |
| Pressure selling | No more messages such as "almost sold out" on the main page and product listings |
| Games | An end to forced gamification, such as having to "spin the wheel" to get into the marketplace, across the website, app and ads |
| Reviews | A fake-review detector that checks reviews before they appear, a reporting tool, and an explanation of how star ratings are calculated |
| Checkout | A clear "order and pay" button, because the site had not made it clear that ordering meant paying |
| Customer support | More contact options, including phone support and a chatbot in all official EU and EEA languages |
| Green claims | Clearer, substantiated information on Temu's environmental donation scheme |

Temu also committed that future versions of its website and app will respect the same commitments. The Commission says this lets authorities act if the same problems come back in a new design.

## What was already the law

Most of the list matches rules that already apply to anyone selling online to Irish consumers.

- **The 14-day refund.** If you cancel an online order within the cooling-off period, the trader must refund you within 14 days of being told, the [CCPC says](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumer-advice/consumer-rights/buying-goods/buying-online). It can hold the refund until it gets the goods back or you show proof you sent them.
- **"Was" prices.** Under the [CCPC's price-reduction guidance](https://www.ccpc.ie/information-for-businesses/selling-goods-and-services/pricing-and-price-reductions/price-reductions), a claimed reduction must be measured against the lowest price the trader charged in at least the previous 30 days. A strike-through "reference price" that the product was never actually sold at is not a discount.
- **Pressure and games.** Fake scarcity messages and design tricks that push you into buying are unfair commercial practices under EU law. The Commission names the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, the Consumer Rights Directive and the Price Indication Directive as the rules Temu must follow.

What changes is enforceability. If Temu does not keep its word, national authorities can take enforcement measures, including fines of up to 4% or more of Temu's annual turnover in the countries concerned, the Commission says. National authorities, including the CCPC, will now monitor compliance.

## Proposed, accepted, or already done?

It helps to keep three things separate:

1. **Accepted today:** the commitments themselves, accepted by the EU Consumer Protection Cooperation (CPC) network on 26 November 2026.
2. **Already made:** the Commission says Temu made "numerous changes" to its EU and EEA website and app after the authorities first [notified it in November 2024](https://www.ccpc.ie/news-and-media/news/article/2024/11/08/ccpc-and-eu-consumer-authorities-inform-temu-that-they-are-under-scrutiny-for-potential-breaches-of-consumer-protection-law). The CCPC's Clara Thornton told [RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1126/1600757-temu-commits-to-changing-some-commercial-practices-in-eu/) that the platform has already made changes.
3. **Still open:** a separate Digital Services Act case on illegal and unsafe products. On 17 November the Commission preliminarily found Temu in breach of its duty to reduce that risk. That is a preliminary finding, not a final decision, and it is not covered by these commitments.

The announcement gives no deadline for any outstanding change. If you see something on the list still happening after today, it is worth reporting.

## What it means in euro

**A refund you are waiting for.** Say you cancel a €60 order on Tuesday 1 December and post it back the same day with proof of postage. Fourteen days from 1 December is 15 December. If the money has not come back by then, Temu is outside both the law and its own commitment. Keep the proof of postage and the date you told them.

**A "discount" that isn't one.** A listing shows €12.99 beside a struck-through €39.99 and "−67%". Under the commitment, Temu must make clear whether €39.99 is a price it actually charged before, or just a general reference price. If it is a reference price, the honest comparison is with what you would pay elsewhere, not with €39.99.

**The full cost at checkout.** Taxes and fees must now be shown before you pay. That matters because since 1 July 2026 an EU customs duty of €3 has applied to low-value parcels from outside the EU, charged per product type rather than per parcel. See our guide to [EU parcel charges on non-EU orders](/news/new-eu-parcel-fee-ireland-2026/) for how that adds up on a mixed order.

## What you can do

1. **Check the total on the final screen**, including any duty or fees, before tapping "order and pay".
2. **Ignore countdowns and "almost sold out" labels.** Temu has agreed to remove them from the main page and listings. If you still see them, screenshot them.
3. **Use the 14-day clock.** Note the date you cancel and post the item back. If the refund is late, quote the 14-day commitment.
4. **Pay by card**, which can give you a chargeback route through your bank if a refund never arrives.
5. **Report breaches** to the CCPC's consumer helpline on 01 402 5555, with screenshots and dates.
