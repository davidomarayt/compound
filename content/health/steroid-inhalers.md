## At a glance

Inhaled corticosteroids reduce inflammation in the airways. They are used in prescribed asthma and other respiratory regimens. The exact device and whether it contains other active ingredients matter. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-inhalers/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Airway inflammation, deposition and controller use

A steroid deposited in airway tissue binds the glucocorticoid receptor and changes expression of inflammation-related genes. This reduces inflammatory activity over time. A combined inhaler can also deliver a bronchodilator with different effects. Deposition in the mouth or throat helps explain local adverse effects. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-inhalers/)

## What the evidence measures

Trials assess symptom control and flare-ups over time. Immediate breathing sensations can depend on another ingredient, the condition and technique. Device technique should be checked before drawing conclusions from apparent lack of effect.

## Identify the medicine within the device

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Ingredients | inhaled steroid alone or a combination |
| Technique | ask for a demonstration and mouth-care advice for your device |


## What if rinsing advice differs between devices?

Ask about the actual inhaler and how to care for your mouth afterwards. Bring the device if you are unsure which active ingredients it contains or need a technique check.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Use the personalised respiratory action plan. Worsening severe breathlessness needs urgent help. Discuss recurrent hoarseness or suspected mouth thrush with the pharmacist or prescriber. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-inhalers/)
