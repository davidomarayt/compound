## At a glance

Escitalopram is an SSRI used for depression and some anxiety disorders. The clinic’s review interval will depend on the indication and how you are tolerating treatment. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/escitalopram/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### The serotonin transporter and the delay in symptom change

The serotonin transporter normally returns serotonin from the synapse to the releasing nerve cell. Escitalopram reduces that reuptake. This changes signalling early, while the clinical effect may involve slower adaptations in neural circuits. Serotonin signalling in the gut helps explain why bowel symptoms and nausea can occur. [DailyMed source](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c0401570-a3b0-bef2-7365-c23dc952b8b6)

## What the evidence measures

Look for change over weeks in a consistent symptom or daily task. Early changes in sleep or appetite can be useful to report, but they are only part of judging the overall response.

## What if I feel different in the first week?

Early changes do not establish the eventual clinical response. Record both useful changes and adverse effects. Contact the care team sooner if symptoms are severe or worsening.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Mention medicines that affect heart rhythm or serotonin before adding anything. Contact a clinician if symptoms worsen and get urgent support if you feel unable to stay safe. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/escitalopram/)
