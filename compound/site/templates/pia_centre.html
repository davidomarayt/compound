PIA Ireland: Personal Investment Account explained.

A PIA (Personal Investment Account) is Ireland's proposed investment account for individuals. The planned 2027 scheme would allow eligible shares, funds and ETFs to be held under a simpler tax framework, with a proposed annual charge of 1% on average account value above €50,000.

PIA EXPLAINED

What is a PIA in Ireland?

A Personal Investment Account (PIA) is a proposed Irish investment account for adults. You could contribute up to €12,000 each year and invest in eligible shares, bonds, funds and ETFs. Under the proposed rules, the provider would calculate an annual 1% charge only on the average account value above €50,000. The account is not available yet and investment values can fall.

The Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 as introduced calls it an Individual Investment Account (IIA). PIA is the familiar name used by some providers and publications; both names refer to the proposed investment account, not two separate products. This page is about investing, not the existing Personal Insolvency Arrangement also abbreviated PIA in Ireland.

The Government's targeted start date is 1 July 2027, but Revenue confirms the launch depends on legislation. The Bill as introduced is not final law, and individual providers may be ready on different dates.

How much would the PIA tax be?

On an illustrative €75,000 average account value, the proposed charge would be 1% × (€75,000 − €50,000) = €250 for the year. This uses the average account value, not simply the balance on 31 December. No charge arises where the relevant average is €50,000 or less.

QUICK ANSWERS

PIA Ireland: frequently asked questions

When can I open a PIA in Ireland?

The Government is targeting 1 July 2027, subject to legislation and provider readiness. No provider product is being described here as open for applications. See verified provider announcements.

What investments could a PIA hold?

The proposed framework allows qualifying listed shares and bonds, retail funds and ETFs. Actual availability will depend on the provider and final rules. Read the eligibility guide.

Is a PIA better than a pension?

Not automatically. Pensions have tax relief and different access rules; the PIA is designed for a different type of investing. Compare costs and constraints using the PIA versus pension calculator.

Is there a €50,000 limit on the account?

No. €50,000 is the proposed threshold below which there is no annual PIA charge, not a cap on account value. The proposed contribution limit is €12,000 per year. Read the full rules.

Does withdrawing money restore the contribution allowance?

Not under the Finance Bill as introduced: withdrawals do not restore annual contribution room. The final enacted rules may differ. See the contribution and withdrawal guidance.
