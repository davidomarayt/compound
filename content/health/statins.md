## At a glance

Statins lower LDL cholesterol and can reduce cardiovascular risk in appropriate people. The decision depends on baseline risk, indication and suitability of the individual medicine. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/statins/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### The liver cholesterol pathway and reading risk reduction

Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase in the liver, lowering cholesterol synthesis. Liver cells respond in part by increasing LDL receptors, which clear more LDL from the circulation. An LDL blood result is a measurable effect. Clinical trials also track events such as heart attack and stroke. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/statins/)

## What the evidence measures

A relative risk reduction can correspond to very different absolute benefits depending on starting risk and follow-up. Look for the same patient group, comparison and timeframe when reading benefit estimates.

The Cholesterol Treatment Trialists pooled 27 randomised trials. In participants whose estimated five-year risk of a major vascular event was below 10%, a reduction of 1 mmol/L in LDL cholesterol was associated with about **11 fewer major vascular events per 1,000 people over five years**. That estimate describes the study population and the amount of LDL lowering. Different starting risks or different LDL responses can change the expected absolute benefit. [Cholesterol Treatment Trialists analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22607822/)

## Read two outcomes separately

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| LDL | biomarker response to treatment |
| Vascular events | absolute difference in events over a stated period |


## What makes an absolute-risk figure useful?

It needs a starting risk, a timeframe and the outcome measured. Ask whether the people in the estimate are similar to you and whether it applies to first or repeat events.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Ask about new unexplained muscle symptoms or interactions with newly prescribed medicines. The relevant interactions differ with the particular statin. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/statins/)
