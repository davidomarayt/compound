A tax credit is a fixed sum taken off your income tax bill after the bill has been worked out. Revenue's own wording is that tax credits reduce the amount of tax you pay ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/calculating-your-income-tax/tax-credits.aspx)). For 2026 a single employee gets two of them almost automatically: a Personal Tax Credit of €2,000 and an Employee Tax Credit of €2,000, a total of €4,000 that comes off the tax rather than off the wages ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/tax-relief-charts/index.aspx)).

That is why a single worker earning €20,000 or less pays no income tax at all: 20% of that income is €4,000, and the credits cancel it out ([Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/money-and-tax/tax/income-tax-credits-and-reliefs/introduction-to-income-tax-credits-and-reliefs/)). You may still pay USC (the Universal Social Charge, a separate levy on gross income) and PRSI (Pay Related Social Insurance, the contribution that funds welfare payments and the State Pension), because income tax credits do not touch either. The "tax credits" figure on your payslip or Revenue letters is your yearly total, divided across your pay periods. The rest of this piece covers where that total comes from, what people commonly leave out of it, and how to check it.

## What is a tax credit, and how is it different from a rate band or a relief?

Three separate things shape the tax of a PAYE worker (Pay As You Earn, the system where your employer deducts income tax from your wages on Revenue's behalf), and they are constantly confused.

A rate band is the slice of income taxed at a given rate. Revenue defines it as "the amount of income which will be taxed at a particular percentage" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/calculating-your-income-tax/tax-rate-band.aspx)). Income up to your standard rate band is taxed at 20%, and anything above it at 40% ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/calculating-your-income-tax/tax-rate-band.aspx)). For 2026 that band is €44,000 for a single person and €53,000 for a married couple or civil partners with one income ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/tax-relief-charts/index.aspx)). The band decides how much tax the rates produce. It does nothing to the bill after that.

A tax credit is what gets deducted from that bill. Because it comes off the tax and not the income, a credit is worth exactly the same to someone on the 20% rate as to someone on the 40% rate.

A tax relief works earlier in the sum. Revenue says reliefs "can directly reduce the income on which you pay Income Tax" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/differences-between-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/tax-reliefs.aspx)), and the value "can be at the standard rate of tax (20%) or at the highest rate of tax (up to 40%)" depending on the relief ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/differences-between-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/tax-reliefs.aspx)). So a €100 relief claim is worth €40 to someone paying tax at 40% but only €20 to someone whose top rate is 20% ([Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/money-and-tax/tax/income-tax-credits-and-reliefs/introduction-to-income-tax-credits-and-reliefs/)). Revenue paperwork sometimes calls a relief an allowance or a deduction. Same thing.

The short version: bands decide how much tax is charged, reliefs shrink the income it is charged on, and credits shrink the final bill.

## How does a tax credit actually change what lands in my bank account?

Revenue tells your employer your total credits for the year, not the breakdown, and "under the Pay As You Earn (PAYE) system, tax credits are spread evenly throughout the year" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/calculating-your-income-tax/tax-credits.aspx)). In Revenue's worked examples, a single employee with the Personal and Employee credits has €4,000 for the year, which becomes a weekly tax credit of €76.93 if paid weekly or a monthly tax credit of €333.34 if paid monthly ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/calculating-your-income-tax/tax-credits.aspx)). Each pay day your employer works out the tax on that period's pay, knocks off that slice of credit, and deducts what is left.

Three rules follow from this, and each one catches people out:

- Credits only cancel tax you would otherwise pay. Revenue's line is that "you must have paid tax in your employment in order to use tax credits" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/calculating-your-income-tax/tax-credits.aspx)). If the tax on your pay is smaller than your credits, the spare credit goes unused.
- Spare credit rolls forward within the year, not beyond it. "Unused tax credits in a pay period are carried forward to a later pay period or periods in the same tax year" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/calculating-your-income-tax/tax-credits.aspx)), but you cannot get a refund of unused credits or carry them into the next year.
- Credits are set per year, so a change in your circumstances mid-year, such as marriage, a new tenancy or a child, changes nothing until you tell Revenue.

## What are the standard credits for a single person and for a married couple in 2026?

These are the main credits in Revenue's rates chart for 2026, and each is the same as it was in 2025 ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/tax-relief-charts/index.aspx)).

| Credit | 2026 amount | Who gets it |
|---|---|---|
| Personal Tax Credit, single person | €2,000 | Every single person |
| Personal Tax Credit, married or civil partners | €4,000 | A couple assessed together |
| Employee Tax Credit | €2,000 | Anyone with PAYE income |
| Earned Income Tax Credit | €2,000 | Self-employed people, instead of the Employee credit |
| Home Carer Tax Credit | €1,950 | A couple where one spouse cares for a child or dependant at home |

A single PAYE worker therefore starts from €4,000. A married couple with one income is Revenue's third worked example: the Married Person's Tax Credit of €4,000, the Employee Tax Credit of €2,000 and the Home Carer Tax Credit of €1,950 add up to €7,950 a year, or €152.89 a week ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/calculating-your-income-tax/tax-credits.aspx)). A couple with two incomes each keep an Employee Tax Credit of their own, because "you cannot transfer the credit to your spouse or civil partner" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/income-and-employment/employee-tax-credit/index.aspx)).

Two caveats on the Employee Tax Credit. "The maximum employee tax credit for 2026 is €2,000", but "if your PAYE income is below €10,000, then the amount of the credit is capped at 20% of this income" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/income-and-employment/employee-tax-credit/index.aspx)). And a proprietary director, meaning someone who controls more than 15% of a company's ordinary shares, cannot claim it on income from that directorship ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/income-and-employment/employee-tax-credit/index.aspx)).

The rate bands sit alongside the credits. For 2026 the 20% band is €44,000 for a single person and €53,000 for a one-income couple, and a two-income couple can add up to €35,000 more for the second earner ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/tax-relief-charts/index.aspx)).

## Which credits and reliefs do ordinary workers most often miss?

The credits above are usually on your certificate already. The ones below are not added unless you claim them, and Revenue's filing figures show how many people only get round to it after the year has ended. By 6 March 2026 Revenue had processed over 806,000 PAYE returns for 2025, almost 82% of them produced an overpayment, and refunds of over €637 million had been paid out ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/corporate/press-office/press-releases/2026/pr-031126-paye-filing.aspx)). Treat that percentage with care: people who expect money back file first, and almost 12% of the returns processed showed an underpayment instead ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/corporate/press-office/press-releases/2026/pr-031126-paye-filing.aspx)). Still, Rent Tax Credit claims had reached 216,839, Flat Rate Expenses 202,115, Remote Working Relief 85,247, and health expenses had been claimed by 260,995 taxpayers, with all four still rising months into the year ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/corporate/press-office/press-releases/2026/pr-031126-paye-filing.aspx)).

These are the credits worth checking first, with the 2026 amounts and the condition most likely to trip you up.

| Credit | 2026 amount | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| Rent Tax Credit, single person | €1,000 | Runs for the tax years 2022 to 2028; only €500 for 2022 and 2023 ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/land-and-property/rent-credit/index.aspx)) |
| Rent Tax Credit, jointly assessed couple | €2,000 | €1,000 for 2022 and 2023 ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/land-and-property/rent-credit/index.aspx)) |
| Home Carer Tax Credit | €1,950 | Full credit only if the carer earns less than €7,200; nothing at €11,100 or more ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/health-and-age/home-carer-credit/home-carer-tax-credit-rates.aspx)) |
| Single Person Child Carer Credit | €1,900 | Also brings an increased rate band of €4,000; only one parent or guardian can claim per year ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/children/single-person-child-carer-credit/index.aspx)) |

Then there are three reliefs. Remember these reduce the income that is taxed, not the bill itself, so their value depends on your top rate:

- **Health expenses.** Medical costs not covered by the State or by insurance get relief "at your standard rate of tax (20%)", while "nursing home expenses are given at your highest rate of tax (up to 40%)" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/health-and-age/health-expenses/index.aspx)).
- **Remote Working Relief.** "You can attribute 30% of the cost of electricity, heating and broadband to remote working", apportioned "based on the number of days worked from home over the year", and "the relief is given at your highest rate of tax" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/eworking/what-costs-can-you-claim-for.aspx)). Laptops, desks and chairs you bought yourself are not allowed ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/eworking/what-costs-can-you-claim-for.aspx)).
- **Flat Rate Expenses.** A fixed deduction for "certain employment related expenses. Examples may include tools, uniforms and statutory registration fees", with the amount "agreed between Revenue and representatives of groups or classes of employees" for each occupation ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/employing-people/employee-expenses/flat-rate-expense-allowances/index.aspx)). Check whether your job is on Revenue's list.

## How do I read my Tax Credit Certificate and fix it on myAccount?

Your Tax Credit Certificate (TCC) is the document that tells you, and your employer, what Revenue thinks you are entitled to. For each tax year it shows your "tax credits, reliefs and rate band", your "Universal Social Charge (USC) rates and rate band" and your Employment Identifier, "a unique reference set by your employer for your employment" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/calculating-your-income-tax/tax-credit-certificate.aspx)).

To find it, sign into myAccount (Revenue's online service for PAYE taxpayers), open the 'My Documents' folder in the top right of the home page, and pick the year ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/calculating-your-income-tax/tax-credit-certificate.aspx)). Then check four things against the tables above:

- Is the personal credit the right one for your status: single, married or in a civil partnership, or widowed?
- Is the Employee Tax Credit there, and is it the full amount if you earn above €10,000?
- Does the rate band match your situation, for example €44,000 single or €53,000 for a one-income couple?
- Are the extras you qualify for listed: Rent, Home Carer, Single Person Child Carer, Flat Rate Expenses?

Anything missing for the current year is added through 'Manage your tax for the current year' in myAccount ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/differences-between-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/tax-reliefs.aspx)). Revenue then issues a new certificate to your employer and the correction shows up in later payslips. If you have never had a certificate, or cannot find it, ask for one through MyEnquiries, Revenue's secure message service inside myAccount ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/calculating-your-income-tax/tax-credit-certificate.aspx)).

## How far back can I claim, and what is the deadline?

Revenue's four-year rule is short and has no discretion in it: "The limit is four years, meaning you can only request reviews or claim refunds for the last four years. For example, claims for 2022 must be made by 31 December 2026. Claims made after this time cannot be repaid." ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/four-year-rule/index.aspx)). In practice that means 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 are all open right now, and 2022 closes at the end of this year.

Back claims go through a PAYE Income Tax Return, which "must be made within four years after the end of the tax year to which the return relates" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/end-of-year-process/paye-income-tax-return.aspx)). In myAccount, click 'Review your tax for the previous 4 years' under 'PAYE Services', select 'Request' for the year, add your credits and reliefs, and your Statement of Liability (Revenue's final calculation for that year, showing a refund or an underpayment) arrives in approximately five working days ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/end-of-year-process/paye-income-tax-return.aspx)). Use the right year's amounts: the Rent Tax Credit was €500 for a single person and €1,000 for a couple in 2022 and 2023, before rising to €1,000 and €2,000 from 2024 ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/land-and-property/rent-credit/index.aspx)).

## What should I do this month?

Revenue's own advice is blunt: "I encourage all PAYE taxpayers to log on to Revenue's myAccount service to finalise their tax position as soon as they can" ([Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/corporate/press-office/press-releases/2026/pr-031126-paye-filing.aspx)). One evening covers it:

1. Log into myAccount, or register if you never have.
2. Open My Documents and read your 2026 Tax Credit Certificate line by line against the tables above.
3. Add anything missing for 2026 through 'Manage your tax for the current year'.
4. File a return for each of 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 through 'Review your tax for the previous 4 years', claiming rent, health expenses, remote working and flat rate expenses for every year they apply.
5. Put 31 December 2026 in your diary as the last day for 2022.

Expect a check rather than a windfall. The standard credits are usually applied already, and a return can show an underpayment as easily as a refund. But the amounts are large enough, and the deadline firm enough, that not looking is the one choice that guarantees you never find out.
