<section class="sol-article-grid"><article><h2>Why storing solar is not free</h2><p>Without storage, unused solar generation is exported and may earn a Clean Export Guarantee payment. When you store it, you lose that payment and some electricity in the charge/discharge process. A battery only adds financial value when the higher-priced imports you avoid outweigh these costs.</p><p>Model the import-versus-export trade-off in our <a href="/solar/export-rates/">tariff comparison</a>, or include an EV in the <a href="/solar-ev-battery-optimiser/">solar + EV + battery optimiser</a>.</p></article>
<article><h2>The €600 SEAI battery grant</h2><p>SEAI currently offers a separate flat <strong>€600</strong> for eligible batteries of at least <strong>5 kWh</strong>. Eligibility includes an MPRN, a home built and occupied before 2025, no previous battery funding at that MPRN, a registered PV contractor and an SEAI grant offer <strong>before installation begins</strong>. Installing solar at the same time is not required, but other scheme conditions apply.</p><p>Always verify the latest conditions with <a href="https://www.seai.ie/grants/home-energy-grants/individual-grants/battery">SEAI</a> and check the <a href="/solar/grants/">Compound grant checker</a>.</p></article></section>
<div class="sol-sourcebox"><h2>Questions before buying storage</h2><p>Confirm the <strong>usable</strong> (not nominal) battery kWh, continuous discharge kW, round-trip efficiency, cycle and capacity-retention warranties, inverter compatibility, installation position, insurance implications, servicing costs and who handles warranty repairs. Battery backup in a power cut requires a suitable isolation/changeover arrangement — it is <strong>not automatic</strong>.</p></div>
