## At a glance

Codeine is an opioid found in some pain treatments. Some packs also contain paracetamol or another ingredient, which changes what you need to check for duplication. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/codeine/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Opioid receptors, metabolism and why response varies

CYP2D6 converts some codeine into morphine. Morphine activates mu opioid receptors and changes how pain signals are processed. Differences in CYP2D6 activity can affect benefit and toxicity, and interactions can change the response. Opioid effects also help explain slowed bowel movement, drowsiness and respiratory depression. [DailyMed source](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5819bdf7-300e-45b8-8f3a-447b53656293)

## What the evidence measures

Review whether there was meaningful pain relief or improvement in activity, and how much drowsiness or constipation occurred. Short-term pain trials do not establish a benefit from prolonged use for every kind of pain.

## Read a combination pack in two passes

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Opioid ingredient | check for codeine and other sedating medicines |
| Other active ingredient | check the whole medicine list for duplication |


## How can a combination tablet hide a second medicine?

The front of a pack may emphasise the brand. Read the active ingredients and check other products for the same ingredients before asking a pharmacist about combining them.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Combining opioids with alcohol, benzodiazepines or other sedatives can increase breathing risk. Abnormally slow breathing or inability to wake someone needs emergency help. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/codeine/)
