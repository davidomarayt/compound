When Meta announced a new class of non-voting Class C shares on 21 October, the first reason it gave was money. The scale of investment needed for AI, its results release said, was significant, and the new class would give Meta "the ability to finance its growth". On 23 November Meta told the SEC that the shares will not be issued for now.

In a [Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026075541/meta-20261118.htm), Meta said shareholders had sued in the Delaware Court of Chancery to challenge the recapitalisation. While the pre-trial and trial proceedings run, Meta will not issue any Class C shares, including through the one-for-one stock dividend its board declared in October. The trial is scheduled to begin on 19 April 2027.

The pause matters less for the money Meta has already raised than for the funding tool it was meant to unlock.

## What Meta raised in four weeks

Between 26 October and 12 November, Meta sold four separate securities. All of them are tied to Class A shares or are plain debt, so none depends on Class C existing.

| Deal | Closed | Size | Cash to Meta |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 6.50% mandatory convertible preferred | 29 Oct | $12.5bn | Received, about $12.5bn net |
| Forward sale of 46m Class A shares at $625 | 29 Oct (option 4 Nov) | $28.75bn gross | On settlement, by 19 Nov 2027 |
| US dollar senior notes, five tranches | 5 Nov | $15.0bn | Received |
| Euro senior notes, six tranches | 12 Nov | €9.0bn | Received |

*Sources: Meta 8-Ks of 29 October, 5 November and 12 November 2026 and the two prospectus supplements of 26 October. The preferred size excludes an underwriters' option over up to 37.5 million more depositary shares, about $1.9bn at the $50 offer price.*

Counting only the dollar deals, that is about $56bn of capital arranged, plus €9bn. The forward sale is the largest piece, and Meta has not yet received that money. Under a forward sale, banks borrow shares and sell them now, and Meta delivers new shares and collects the price later. The [prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000119312526436410/d94886d424b5.htm) puts the initial forward price at $622.91875 a share. Across 46 million shares, Compound calculates about $28.65bn before expenses, subject to adjustments for interest rates and dividends. The $625 offer price was about 10% below the $694.25 closing price on 20 October, which the [information statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026068935/meta-20261102.htm) records.

## What the pause actually holds back

The 8-K names one direct casualty. Meta said it "will not launch its previously announced at-the-market program until the Class C common stock begins trading", and so does not expect to launch it until after the stock dividend is paid. An at-the-market programme lets a company sell shares gradually into the market, rather than in one large offering. The filings reviewed for this article do not give its size.

The other deals were written with a delay in mind:

- **Forward sale.** If Class C shares are issued, each forward agreement splits into a Class A and a Class C transaction. Without Class C, the agreements simply remain Class A contracts. The settlement deadline of 19 November 2027 does not depend on Class C.
- **Preferred shares.** These convert into Class A shares around 15 August 2029, unless Class C has been issued and listed by 15 April 2029, in which case they convert into Class C. Dividends can only be paid in Class C shares from the later of that event and the first anniversary of issue.
- **Founder Alignment Agreement.** This October agreement required the Class C dividend to be paid within five business days of the new charter taking effect. Mark Zuckerberg has waived that deadline. The waiver ends after a reasonable period once the case is resolved without blocking the dividend, on a final ruling that blocks it, or on 30 June 2027, whichever comes first.

On Compound's reading of these terms, the at-the-market programme is unlikely to start before the April 2027 trial is resolved, and the founder waiver has a 30 June 2027 backstop. Meta has not given a new date.

## Why the timing matters

The need for capital is not in doubt. Meta guided to 2026 capital expenditure, including finance lease principal, of $135bn to $145bn. The [Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026066773/meta-20260930.htm) shows $96.87bn of operating cash flow in the first nine months against $83.06bn of purchases of property and equipment, before finance lease payments. Third-quarter capex including finance lease principal was $34.91bn. Cash and marketable securities were $82.45bn at 30 September, and bond principal outstanding was $84.0bn.

Legal costs add to the demands. The same 10-Q records an August agreement with 52 attorneys general, with about $12.7bn payable in annual instalments over ten years. A New Mexico court imposed a $25bn civil penalty on 21 October with 15% post-judgment interest. Meta has accrued an undisclosed amount, intends to appeal and says it may need to post a bond or other collateral.

The new money is also more expensive than Meta's older debt. On Compound's arithmetic, the five dollar tranches carry annual coupons of about $990m, an average of 6.60% on $15bn. The euro notes carry about €479m a year, or 5.32%. The preferred shares, if dividends are paid in cash, cost $812.5m a year. Meta's notes outstanding at 30 September carried stated rates of 3.50% to 6.45%.

## The dilution so far

For Class A shareholders, the dilution from completed deals is measurable. The forward prospectus lists 2,214,455,318 Class A shares outstanding before the offering. The 46 million forward shares equal about 2.1% of that figure. The preferred will convert into between 17.0 million and 20.0 million Class A shares, depending on the share price in 2029. On that basis, the two deals together come to about 2.9% to 3.0% of the pre-offering Class A count. This excludes Class B shares and assumes the forward is settled by delivering shares, which Meta says it expects.

An at-the-market programme would add to that over time. The pause postpones that extra issuance, but it does not remove the reason for it.

## What to watch

Three dates matter. The Delaware trial starts on 19 April 2027. The founder waiver ends no later than 30 June 2027. The forward sale must settle by 19 November 2027. Meta's fourth-quarter 10-K should show how much of the forward it has drawn and whether the appeal bond in New Mexico has required cash or collateral.
