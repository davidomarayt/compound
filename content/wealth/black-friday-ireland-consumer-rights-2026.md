**Black Friday falls on 27 November, and Irish shoppers are more sceptical than ever. Only 23% of consumers trust Black Friday discounts, the lowest level since the Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC) began asking in 2022. The good news is that most of the protection does not depend on trust: a sale item comes with the same legal rights as a full-price one, and "was" prices have to follow a fixed rule.**

The [CCPC survey](https://www.ccpc.ie/news-and-media/news/article/2026/11/17/lowest-ever-levels-of-trust-in-black-friday-discounts), published on 17 November 2026, asked 1,028 Irish consumers about their plans. Some 73% said they do not trust that discounts are accurate, up from 61% in 2022, and 80% said they would check a discount before buying. Expected spending averaged €307, the lowest since the survey began and down from €431 in 2024.

## The rights in one table

| Situation | General rule in Ireland |
| --- | --- |
| The item is in a sale | Same rights as at full price |
| A shop shows a "was" price | It must be the lowest price in the previous 30 days |
| You bought online from an EU trader and changed your mind | Usually 14 days from delivery to cancel |
| You bought in a shop and changed your mind | No automatic right; it depends on the shop's policy |
| The item is faulty within 30 days | You can ask for a full refund |
| The item is faulty later | Repair or replacement first; refund if the fault is serious or can't be fixed |
| You knowingly bought a flawed item at a reduced price | You can't return it later for that specific flaw |

## How the "was" price rule works

Since 29 November 2022, a shop announcing a price reduction must show the prior price, and that prior price must be the lowest price the product was sold at in **at least the 30 days before the reduction**, according to the [CCPC's price-reduction guidance](https://www.ccpc.ie/information-for-businesses/selling-goods-and-services/pricing-and-price-reductions/price-reductions).

Two details matter during Black Friday week:

- **Successive cuts in one sale keep the same reference price.** If a TV drops from €700 to €600 and then to €500 in the same campaign, the "was" price stays at the lowest price before the first cut.
- **Separate campaigns are measured separately.** A Black Friday sale and a Cyber Monday sale each need their own prior price, based on the previous 30 days.

The rule does not cover everything that looks like a deal. Loyalty-card prices, personalised discounts and "buy two, get 10% off" offers are outside it.

The rule is enforced. In January, Brown Thomas Arnotts [pleaded guilty in Dublin District Court](https://www.ccpc.ie/news-and-media/news/article/2026/01/26/brown-thomas-arnotts-pleads-guilty-to-sales-pricing-breaches) to breaking sales pricing laws after CCPC checks that included the 2024 Black Friday period. And when European consumer authorities checked online shops during Black Friday sales, the [CCPC found](https://www.ccpc.ie/news-and-media/news/article/2026/03/26/investigation-by-ec--ccpc-and-european-consumer-authorities-finds-1-in-3-traders-incorrectly-display-discounts-online-during-black-friday-sales) that 30% of the 314 Irish online traders it screened referenced discounts incorrectly.

**What to do:** if a discount matters to your decision, note the price now and compare it on the day. The CCPC's own [tip](https://www.ccpc.ie/news-and-media/news/article/2026/11/10/top-ten-tips-for-navigating-black-friday-safely) is that "just because an item is 25% off, it doesn't mean it hasn't been that price before."

## Online orders: the 14-day cooling-off period

If you buy online from a trader in the EU, you usually have **14 days from the day you receive the goods** to cancel without giving a reason, according to the [CCPC's online shopping guidance](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumer-advice/consumer-rights/buying-goods/buying-online). After telling the trader, you have another 14 days to send the item back.

Three things catch people out:

- **You may pay the return postage.** For a change-of-mind return, you have to pay any return costs that apply, unless the trader offers free returns. That is different from a faulty item (see below).
- **Some goods are excluded.** Personalised or made-to-order items and goods that can't be returned for health or hygiene reasons don't carry the statutory right. Nor do bookings for a set date, such as flights, hotels or event tickets.
- **The refund clock.** The trader must refund you within 14 days of being told you are cancelling, though it can wait until it gets the goods back. The refund goes back through the same payment method unless you agree otherwise.

If the trader never told you about your right to cancel, the cancellation period is extended by up to 12 months.

**Buying from outside the EU is different.** The CCPC [warns](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumer-advice/consumer-rights/buying-goods/buying-outside-the-eu) that you have fewer protections when buying from businesses outside the EU, Iceland and Norway, and you can't use the Irish small claims procedure against them. Since 1 July 2026, EU customs duty also applies to packages from outside the EU, which can add to the price. See our guide to the [new EU parcel fee on non-EU orders](/news/new-eu-parcel-fee-ireland-2026/).

## In-store purchases: change of mind is not a right

Buying in a shop is different. You are [not automatically entitled](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumer-advice/consumer-rights/buying-goods/shopping-in-the-sales) to a refund or exchange because you changed your mind. Many shops offer returns anyway, but that is their policy, and they can change it during a sale. Check the policy, and ask for a gift receipt if you are buying presents.

## Faulty sale items: full rights still apply

Under the Consumer Rights Act 2022, the [CCPC says](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumer-advice/faulty-products-and-goods):

- **Within 30 days** of purchase or delivery, you can ask for a full refund for a fault. You don't have to accept a repair.
- **After 30 days**, the business must offer a repair or replacement. A refund is possible if the fault is serious or can't be fixed.
- **For 12 months**, a fault is assumed to have existed when you bought the item, unless the business shows otherwise.
- **For up to six years**, you can pursue a remedy for a faulty item.

The business must cover the cost of collecting a faulty item or reimburse your return costs. For a sale item, the refund is based on the price you paid, not the later sale price. The one exception is a fault you were told about when you bought it.

## Paying: card helps if things go wrong

The CCPC recommends paying by card because it gives you [chargeback protection](https://www.ccpc.ie/news-and-media/news/article/2026/11/10/top-ten-tips-for-navigating-black-friday-safely). If a trader doesn't deliver or won't resolve a problem, you can ask your bank or card provider to reverse the payment.

If you are thinking about spreading a Black Friday purchase with a "pay later" option, new Irish credit rules took effect on 20 November. See [what changed for buy now, pay later](/news/ireland-consumer-credit-bnpl-rules-november-2026/). And if a voucher is on your list, check [your gift voucher rights](/news/irish-gift-voucher-rights-ccpc-2026/) first.

## A two-minute checklist before you buy

1. Is the "was" price shown, and does it match what you have seen in the last 30 days?
2. Is the trader based in Ireland or the EU?
3. What will the returns cost if you change your mind?
4. Is the item personalised, or something that can't be returned for hygiene reasons?
5. Are you paying by card?
