## What to do first when you are arranging a funeral

If you need to make arrangements now, choose one family contact and ask the funeral director for two numbers in writing: the **total cost of the arrangements you have discussed** and **the amount you need to pay before the funeral**. Ask who will receive each payment. The director may handle charges for the cemetery or crematorium, while you may receive a separate bill from a venue or other supplier. Ask for the dates deposits and balances are due. If the quote is above what you can afford, say what you can pay and ask the director to price a simpler version before agreeing to the arrangements.

It is reasonable to ask a trusted person to take notes or read the written estimate with you. If a funeral plan, an existing grave or a policy may already cover something, tell the director at the start. Ask the provider or insurer to confirm what is covered and when it can pay. Keep the written estimate, invoice and any receipts together, especially if you will request payment from a bank or reimbursement from an estate.

## How much does a funeral cost in Ireland?

There is no single price that covers every funeral. A Red C survey commissioned by An Post Insurance and conducted in February 2024 among adults aged 50 and over reported an average of **€6,252**, published in its 2025 funeral guide. This is a dated survey benchmark. The calculator starts with an illustrative set of inputs and you can replace every amount with the quote for your own arrangements.

For a sense of how individual components add up, Dublin Cemeteries Trust’s Glasnevin price list, effective **16 March 2026**, lists **€810** for a private adult cremation plus **€140** for a standard 30-minute chapel service. On the same list, standard grave interment is **€1,270**. A one-burial grave is listed at **€1,875** in named sections subject to availability, so those two burial charges would total **€3,145**. These are examples from one location. They exclude the funeral director’s charges and any services or surcharges outside the listed items. Check your own cemetery or crematorium for current prices and availability.

## Check what the quote includes

Ask for an itemised estimate covering the funeral director’s services, coffin, preparation, use of a funeral home, hearse and transfers. Ask which charges the director pays to another provider on your behalf and which you will pay separately. These may include the plot, grave opening or cremation, chapel, celebrant, music or death notices. Ask whether weekend or bank holiday fees, additional journeys or longer chapel use would change the quote.

Before comparing two estimates, ask each provider to price the same arrangements. If one estimate includes the coffin and hearse, enter that package once. If the crematorium fee is already within the total you entered for the funeral director, leave the separate cemetery or crematorium field at zero. For burial, ask whether an existing family grave can be used, whether it has capacity and who must give permission. Even when a new plot is unnecessary, grave opening and work to a monument or kerbing can add costs. For cremation, ask what is included for chapel use and how ashes will be returned. A later interment, memorial or additional urn may have its own price.

If money is tight, ask the provider to identify the items that can change while keeping the parts of the ceremony that matter to you. For example, you might use the venue’s included service length, adjust the number of cars, or decide on a headstone separately when you have time to compare prices. The [CCPC’s funeral planning guide](https://www.ccpc.ie/manage-your-money/planning-for-the-future/planning-for-a-funeral) has a checklist of costs and payment questions.

## Worked example for a funeral with a payment due upfront

Suppose the funeral director’s package is **€2,500**, the crematorium and standard chapel are **€950**, and separate ceremony charges come to **€300**. The total is **€3,750**. If **€1,000** is due before the funeral and nobody has yet agreed to contribute, the upfront cash needed is **€1,000** and the later balance is **€2,750**. These are illustrative arrangements. The €950 line uses the published Glasnevin example.

If another person has confirmed **€400** towards the upfront bill, and a prepaid plan has separately confirmed **€500** towards the later bill, the amount you still need to cover is **€600 upfront** and **€2,250 later**. Confirm whose payment will arrive by each date. Support that arrives after the deposit is due helps with the balance but cannot cover the deposit on time.

## If you cannot cover the bill

**Talk to the funeral director before paying a deposit.** Ask which amounts are needed before the service, whether a simpler quote is possible, and what payment terms are available. If offered instalments, ask for the total repayable, any interest and the payment dates. Check who is agreeing to pay when the arrangements are booked and keep a record of any changes.

If the person who died had money in an account, **ask the bank’s bereavement team whether it can pay the funeral director directly before probate**. The bank’s process and available funds matter. For example, PTSB asks for an itemised funeral director’s invoice, a certified copy of the death certificate and a payment authorisation form. Ask your own bank which documents it needs, what expenses it can cover, whether it can reimburse a payment already made, and how long it expects the process to take. Build the budget around money confirmed for the date you need it. [PTSB’s funeral payment guidance](https://www.ptsb.ie/help-and-support/life-changes/bereavement-support/releasing-funeral-funds/).

If you or your family cannot meet essential funeral expenses, **contact the Community Welfare Service about an Additional Needs Payment**. The Department of Social Protection assesses your circumstances before deciding on the application. Ask what documents to provide and whether the service can consider your written estimate. For certain deaths connected to work or where qualifying Disablement Benefit applied, check separately whether the Occupational Injuries Benefit Scheme’s Funeral Grant may be relevant. If the bill leaves you struggling with repayments or other essentials, the CCPC points to free, confidential help from [MABS](https://www.mabs.ie/). [Additional Needs Payment](https://www.gov.ie/en/service/80b24c-exceptional-needs-payments-swa/) · [Occupational Injuries Death Benefit](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/death-benefit-scheme-under-the-occupational-injuries-scheme/).

## Planning ahead and protecting your budget

Write down your wishes and tell someone where to find them. If you are comparing prepaid options, ask who holds the money, which services are guaranteed, how increases in cemetery or crematorium fees are handled, what happens if you move, and the rules for cancellation or refunds. Share a copy of any written agreement with the person likely to arrange the funeral. For estate costs that arise separately, see Compound’s [Irish probate costs guide](/wealth/probate-costs-fees-ireland/).

## Method and limitations

The calculator adds your entered funeral director’s charges, cemetery or crematorium charges, separate ceremony costs and any optional expenses. The amount due before the funeral is capped at the total entered. Subtract confirmed sums by the deadline they are available, with each deducted only from that part of the bill. Enter any amount you can use at a different deadline in the corresponding field. The comparison uses the figures you enter in your browser. It covers the Republic of Ireland.

## Frequently asked questions

### What if I only have one all-in quote?

Enter it as a single figure and leave the fields it already covers at zero. Add only items you expect to pay separately. Enter the amount due before the funeral based on the actual payment terms.

### Is cremation always cheaper than burial?

Compare the full quotes for the arrangements you want. The cost of a new grave can make a significant difference to burial. An existing grave, a crematorium chapel service, or a later urn interment can change the comparison.

### Is the deposit an extra cost?

If the deposit is credited against the quote, it is part of the total. Enter it under the amount due before the funeral. If a provider describes any payment as an additional charge, ask for written confirmation of how it affects the bill.

### How do I compare a second quote?

Select **Save for comparison**, replace the first quote’s figures with the second, and check how both the total and the upfront payment change. If a quote is still missing prices for any items, ask the provider to confirm them before deciding.
