<p>Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker <strong>ANT</strong> on Thursday, 19 November. Its first trade was <strong>$111.50</strong>, a gain of <strong>$28.50 per share, or 34.3%</strong>, over the $83 IPO price. Barron’s reported that the stock opened at 11:48 a.m. New York time, or 4:48 p.m. in Ireland. <a href="https://www.barrons.com/livecoverage/ipo-anthropic-claude-stock-price-ticker/card/trading-begins--TRJxXbELHbbrMH3SBlW3">Barron’s</a> · <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/anthropics-historic-debut-put-ais-promise-peril-wall-st-test-2026-11-19/">Reuters</a></p>

<p>The next date for ANT investors is <strong>15 December 2026</strong>, when up to 303 million shares are scheduled to become eligible for resale. It is the second of 23 tranches in the final prospectus. The largest single release, up to <strong>3 billion shares</strong>, is tied to Anthropic’s first-quarter 2027 results or the 180-day deadline, whichever comes first. <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm">Final SEC prospectus, pp. 318–319</a></p>

## Anthropic’s first five lock-up releases

<p>The base IPO covered 980 million Class A shares at $83, a gross public offering of $81.34 billion before fees. Anthropic offered approximately 956.35 million new shares and existing holders offered 23.65 million. Separately, the lock-up schedule made up to 315 million Class A shares held by eligible non-founder directors, officers, employees and consultants available for potential resale from the first trading day. That first release is equivalent to about 32% of the base offering by share count. <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering">Anthropic’s pricing announcement</a> · <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm">Final SEC prospectus</a></p>

## When the biggest ANT share unlocks arrive

<p>The first large earnings-linked release comes on the second full Nasdaq trading day after Anthropic reports its 2026 annual results, when up to 1.7 billion shares can become eligible. Five subsequent releases of up to 480 million shares each are tied to the same results. The final scheduled tranche is up to 1.9 billion shares and is tied to third-quarter 2027 results or the 365-day deadline. <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm">Final SEC prospectus, p. 319</a></p>

## What the lock-up schedule means for ANT

<p>The 23 ceilings add up to roughly <strong>15.5 billion shares</strong>. That figure describes potential resale eligibility across the schedule, including shares underlying equity awards and convertible securities where applicable. The public float depends on which shares actually enter the market. Some holders can still face vesting requirements, insider-trading restrictions or Rule 144 limits. The underwriters can also waive lock-ups ahead of schedule. <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm">Final SEC prospectus, pp. 317–321 and 336</a></p>

<p>Only Class A trades on Nasdaq. Class N is generally non-voting and converts one-for-one into Class A on qualifying transfers, including open-market sales, subject to the charter’s terms. The schedule excludes both the IPO-offered shares and the separate $26 billion strategic placement of <strong>313,253,007 restricted Class N shares</strong>, which has its own resale conditions. A resale of existing shares transfers ownership. Share issuance through a related option exercise or other equity settlement can separately increase the outstanding share count. <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026074944/anthropicpbc-424b4.htm">Final SEC prospectus, pp. 317–321 and 347</a></p>

<p>The $111.50 opening trade measured demand at the debut. The release calendar shows when more holders may be able to sell. The effect on ANT will depend on how much stock they offer and how much investors are willing to buy at the prevailing price.</p>
