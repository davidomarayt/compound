## At a glance

Allopurinol lowers urate over time. It is used to prevent problems from urate accumulation, including recurrent gout, and has other specialist uses. An acute flare and the long-term urate burden are separate treatment problems. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/allopurinol/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### How urate and gout flares can move on different timelines

Xanthine oxidase converts hypoxanthine to xanthine and then to uric acid. Allopurinol and its active metabolite oxypurinol inhibit this enzyme. With sustained lowering of the urate concentration, the conditions that favour urate crystal growth change. Existing crystal deposits take longer to shrink than a blood test takes to improve. [DailyMed source](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5d330416-4289-762e-e063-6294a90a532e)

## What the evidence measures

Use serial serum urate measurements to understand whether treatment is moving towards the target agreed with the care team. Frequency of flares over months is a separate outcome. Flares can still happen early in treatment.

## Why can flares continue when urate is improving?

The blood level can change before old deposits have cleared. Judge long-term urate lowering and short-term flare treatment separately. Tell the team if flares are increasing or your plan for a flare is unclear.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

A new rash needs prompt assessment, especially if there is blistering, fever or mouth involvement. Mention azathioprine or mercaptopurine by name, since their metabolism can be affected. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/allopurinol/)
