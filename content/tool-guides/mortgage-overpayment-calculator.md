## What this calculator is for

A mortgage overpayment reduces principal earlier than the original repayment schedule. Because future interest is calculated on a lower outstanding balance, a sustained overpayment can reduce both the total interest paid and the time needed to clear the mortgage.

The basic calculator answers the common question: **what if I add the same extra amount to every monthly repayment from now on?**

Advanced mode goes further. It lets you delay the start of the regular overpayment and add a one-off lump sum in a chosen month. That makes it possible to model plans such as waiting until another loan is cleared, using an annual bonus, or applying a maturing savings balance to the mortgage later.

## How to read the result

There are three numbers worth reading together:

- **new mortgage term** — how long the model takes to clear the balance;
- **time saved** — the difference from the existing remaining term;
- **interest saved** — scheduled interest without the plan minus interest under the overpayment plan.

The calculator also shows the scheduled interest in each path. This is important because an overpayment is not merely a faster repayment schedule: it changes the amount of interest generated over the life of the loan.

The estimated monthly payment with overpayment is the normal scheduled repayment plus the regular extra amount once that extra payment begins. A one-off lump sum is separate from that monthly figure.

## Worked example

Suppose the current mortgage balance is **€250,000**, the interest rate is **3.5%**, and there are **25 years** remaining.

The normal repayment is about **€1,252 per month**.

If you add **€100 every month from now**, the balance falls faster. The exact term and interest saving are calculated month by month above.

Now switch to Advanced mode. Assume you cannot begin the €100 monthly overpayment for another 12 months, but you expect a **€10,000 lump sum in month 12**. The model keeps the normal repayment for the first year, applies the lump sum after the scheduled payment in month 12, and starts the €100 regular extra payment from month 13.

That advanced scenario will usually save more interest than delaying all extra payments, because the lump sum removes a meaningful amount of principal relatively early.

## Timing matters

Two overpayment plans with the same total cash paid can produce different interest savings.

Paying principal earlier normally saves more interest because the reduced balance affects more future interest calculations. A €10,000 lump sum today will therefore generally reduce more lifetime interest than the same €10,000 paid shortly before the mortgage ends, assuming the mortgage rate is otherwise unchanged.

The same logic applies to regular payments. If you intend to add €200 a month eventually, compare:

- starting immediately;
- starting after 12 months;
- starting after 24 months.

The calculator makes that timing trade-off visible.

## Irish fixed-rate mortgages: check the contract first

The mathematical benefit of an overpayment does not mean the lender must accept unlimited extra payments without consequence.

Irish fixed-rate mortgage contracts can restrict overpayments or apply an early-repayment charge or break fee in some circumstances. Variable-rate treatment can differ. The practical rule is simple: before sending a material lump sum or setting up a recurring overpayment, check the terms of the specific mortgage and ask the lender how the payment will be applied.

A lender may also give you choices after a lump-sum payment. Depending on the product and lender process, the effect may be to:

- keep the scheduled repayment broadly unchanged and shorten the term;
- recalculate the required repayment while keeping the original term;
- apply another treatment under the mortgage contract.

This calculator models the first style: principal falls faster while the original scheduled repayment continues.

## Overpaying versus switching the mortgage

If the current interest rate is uncompetitive, the highest-value move may be reducing the rate rather than simply paying extra into the existing mortgage.

For example, lowering the rate can reduce interest on the entire outstanding balance. An overpayment reduces the balance but leaves the rate unchanged. The best comparison is therefore not always "overpay or do nothing"; it can be "overpay the current mortgage, switch, or switch and then overpay."

Use the [Mortgage Switch & Rate Change Calculator](/mortgage-switch-calculator/) to test the rate side separately.

## Overpaying versus keeping cash

Mortgage overpayment gives a relatively predictable benefit under the entered rate: future mortgage interest is avoided on principal that no longer remains outstanding.

But liquidity has value too.

Before committing spare cash, consider whether you have:

- an adequate emergency reserve;
- expensive unsecured debt charging a higher rate;
- near-term costs such as renovations, childcare or a car replacement;
- pension contributions that attract tax relief;
- a need for cash flexibility that would be difficult to recover once money is placed into the home.

Money paid into the mortgage is not normally as accessible as money in a savings account. The fact that an overpayment improves the mortgage maths does not automatically make it the best use of every spare euro.

## Overpaying versus investing

A mortgage overpayment and an investment are not directly equivalent.

The mortgage saving is linked to the borrowing rate avoided and is relatively certain if the mortgage rate remains as entered. An investment return is uncertain, can be volatile, and may be subject to tax and fees.

A useful comparison is to ask what after-tax, after-fee investment return would be needed to compensate for the mortgage interest you can avoid, while also recognising that the investment carries risk and the mortgage overpayment reduces liquidity.

Use the [Compound Interest Calculator](/compound-interest-calculator/) for a separate investment scenario rather than inserting an assumed market return into the mortgage model.

## Rate changes alter the answer

This calculator holds the entered mortgage rate constant. That is necessary to make the calculation transparent, but many real mortgages will not stay at one rate for the entire remaining term.

If the mortgage is due to leave a fixed period, stress-test the plan at several rates. For example, run the same balance and overpayment at:

- the current rate;
- current rate + 0.5 percentage points;
- current rate + 1 percentage point.

At a higher rate, eliminating principal earlier generally produces a larger interest saving.

## What can change the answer

The main drivers are:

- current balance;
- interest rate;
- remaining term;
- monthly overpayment;
- when the monthly overpayment starts;
- size and timing of any lump sum.

Large balances, long terms and higher rates generally create more potential interest saving because more interest would otherwise be generated.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not assume every lender applies an extra payment exactly as this model does. Do not ignore a possible fixed-rate break charge. Do not compare the interest saved with an investment return without considering tax, fees and risk. And do not overpay so aggressively that the household is left without accessible emergency cash.

If the real plan involves multiple irregular lump sums, this calculator can approximate the strategy one lump sum at a time, but it is not a full lender account simulator.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does overpaying always reduce the mortgage term?
In this calculator, yes, because the original scheduled repayment continues while extra principal is paid. A lender may offer a different account treatment after a lump sum, so confirm how your mortgage works.

### Can I model a lump sum?
Yes. Advanced mode lets you enter one lump sum and the month in which it is applied.

### Can I delay the monthly overpayment?
Yes. In Advanced mode, enter the number of months to wait. A value of 12 means the first extra monthly payment is made in month 13.

### Does the calculator include break fees?
No. Any early-repayment or fixed-rate break charge must be checked with the lender and weighed against the projected interest saving.

### Is the interest saving guaranteed?
Only under the model assumptions. A different future mortgage rate, lender treatment, missed payments or fees will change the result.

## Method and limitations

The calculator first computes the standard annuity repayment for the balance, annual rate and remaining term entered.

It then creates two paths.

The **standard path** assumes that scheduled repayment continues for the full original term at a constant interest rate.

The **overpayment path** is amortised month by month. Each month the model:

1. calculates interest on the opening balance using the annual rate divided by 12;
2. applies the normal scheduled repayment;
3. adds the regular overpayment once the selected start delay has passed;
4. in Advanced mode, applies the one-off lump sum after the scheduled payment in the selected month;
5. stops when the balance reaches zero.

Interest saved is:

**standard scheduled interest − modelled interest under the overpayment plan**

Time saved is:

**original number of months − months required under the overpayment plan**

The model assumes a constant rate, monthly interest convention, no arrears, no payment holidays and no lender fee or break charge. It also models only one lump sum. Real lenders may calculate interest daily and can use different rounding or repayment-recalculation conventions, so the result should be treated as a high-quality planning estimate rather than an account statement.
