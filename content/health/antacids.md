## At a glance

Antacids are products used for short-term relief of symptoms such as heartburn and indigestion. The ingredient list matters because some products also contain an alginate or other components. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antacids/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Neutralising stomach acid and adding a reflux barrier

Acid-neutralising salts react with acid already in the stomach. Raising the pH can reduce irritation, but the effect fades as the product clears and the stomach continues to secrete acid. Alginates work differently by forming a barrier near the surface of the stomach contents. Proton pump inhibitors act on acid production, a third approach. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antacids/)

## What the evidence measures

A useful symptom record separates the speed of relief from how often the symptoms return. Frequent symptoms, trouble swallowing or persistent vomiting deserve a medical review even if an antacid gives temporary relief.

## Read the active-ingredient panel

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Neutraliser | look for a calcium, magnesium or aluminium salt |
| Alginate | look for an added ingredient intended to form a barrier |


## Does relief prove the symptoms were caused by excess acid?

Temporary relief can be useful information, but response to an antacid does not diagnose the cause. Note when symptoms happen, what the treatment changed and how often you have needed it.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Antacids can alter the absorption of other medicines. Check the particular pack and ask a pharmacist about spacing if you take regular prescriptions, especially thyroid medicine or iron. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antacids/)
