## What this calculator is for

This calculator takes recurring lifestyle costs and projects them across decades. Its purpose is not to frighten you with a giant number; it is to show how small recurring choices accumulate and how inflation changes nominal spending.

## How to read the result

Use both **nominal lifetime spending** and **today's-money spending**. A future €2 million total can look dramatic, but much of that difference may simply reflect decades of inflation.

## Worked example

A €100 monthly habit is €1,200 a year and €36,000 over 30 years before inflation or investment opportunity cost. The point is not that all recurring spending is bad; it is that recurring amounts deserve to be evaluated at the same scale as large one-off purchases.

## Irish context

CSO CPI data are the appropriate Irish reference for broad consumer inflation, but individual spending categories can move differently. Housing, childcare, transport and healthcare can each diverge from the headline index.

## What can change the answer

Inflation, age horizon, housing duration, childcare years and major-purchase frequency all drive the projection. The result is very sensitive to long time horizons, so scenario ranges are more useful than one precise number.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not interpret the nominal total as money you need today. Do not assume every spending category rises at the same rate. And do not treat the calculator as an argument to eliminate all discretionary spending; spending can create utility and quality of life.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is the nominal total so large?
Because both time and inflation compound.

### Is today's-money total more useful?
Often, because it expresses future spending in current purchasing-power terms.

### Does this include tax?
It models spending from cash available to the household, not tax liability.

### Is this a budget?
No. It is a long-horizon scenario.

## Method and limitations

The tool projects entered recurring and periodic costs year by year, inflating future cash spending at the chosen rate while also tracking a constant-price today's-money total. It does not forecast actual prices or life events.
