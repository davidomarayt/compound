## At a glance

Corticosteroid injections can be used in selected joints or soft tissues. The proposed target, expected duration of benefit and any other active ingredient should be explained before the procedure. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-injections/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Local anti-inflammatory action and short-term assessment

A corticosteroid binds the glucocorticoid receptor and reduces inflammation-related signalling. Injection delivers it near the target tissue. Some exposure can still reach the circulation. If a local anaesthetic is used, immediate numbness or pain relief and the later steroid effect arise through different mechanisms. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-injections/)

## What the evidence measures

Compare pain and function before and after the procedure. Benefit varies by diagnosis, injection site and time elapsed. A short-lived effect can have a different interpretation when an anaesthetic was also given.

## Track the result over time

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Immediately | record the initial change and whether numbing medicine was used |
| Later | record pain, movement and the activities you wanted to regain |


## Why keep a record of the injection site and date?

Benefits can be local and time-limited. Knowing the interval, site and response helps the clinician decide whether a repeat injection or a different approach is reasonable.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Seek urgent assessment for an increasingly hot, swollen and painful joint with fever or feeling very unwell. Ask about blood glucose checks if you have diabetes and about repeat injections. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-injections/)
