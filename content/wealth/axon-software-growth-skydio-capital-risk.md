**Axon is becoming more software-like, but it is also committing more money to hardware, inventory and drones. The investment question is whether those two directions strengthen each other or pull the economics apart.**

For the three months ended 30 September 2026, Axon reported **$909 million of revenue, up 28%**. Software & Services grew 43% to $437 million, while Connected Devices grew 16% to $472 million.

The mix is encouraging because recurring software can deepen customer relationships and carry attractive margins. Yet the quarter also showed what can happen when newer hardware becomes a larger part of the platform: total gross margin fell, cash generation weakened and revenue guidance absorbed delayed federal drone deployments.

For the maintained financial record, see [Axon’s stock profile](/stocks/axon/). This analysis asks whether software acceleration is enough to offset the capital and execution risk around devices and drones.

## Software is becoming a larger engine

Software & Services represented about **48% of quarterly revenue**, calculated from the reported segment figures. Its revenue rose 43%, annual recurring revenue increased 40% to $1.75 billion and net revenue retention was 126%.

Those operating measures are not interchangeable. Annual recurring revenue is a run-rate measure rather than recognised quarterly revenue. Net revenue retention is Axon’s software metric, excludes the hardware portion of subscriptions and is normalised for phased deployments. Still, together they indicate that existing customers are adopting more premium software while the customer base expands.

The margin was high but not immune to pressure. Software & Services gross margin was **71.4%, down from 73.8%**. Axon attributed the decline to scaling new products and a higher mix of professional services. It said software-only gross margin remained above 80%, which also means the broader segment carries implementation and service costs that a pure software comparison can miss.

Future contracted bookings reached $15.8 billion, up 39%. Axon expects to fulfil only 20% to 25% over the next year, with much of the remainder spread across roughly ten years. That is useful demand visibility, not near-term revenue and not an unconditional guarantee: the measure includes some contracts with termination or other clauses that are outside formal remaining performance obligations.

## Hardware growth came with a lower margin

Connected Devices revenue rose 16% to **$472 million**, but its gross margin fell to **43.0% from 49.9%**. Management cited a heavier mix of counter-drone equipment and higher memory costs. Total company gross margin declined to 56.7% from 60.1%.

This is the central economic tension. A connected camera, drone or TASER device can make Axon’s software more valuable and harder to replace. But faster growth in lower-margin equipment can dilute the benefit of software mix, at least while new categories scale.

Timing also became more visible. Axon removed about **$130 million of expected 2026 revenue** tied to three federal Dedrone deployments, including $30 million previously expected in Q3 and $100 million in Q4. Its Q4 revenue guidance of $990 million to $1.01 billion implies full-year revenue of about $3.61 billion to $3.63 billion.

Management described these as delayed deployments. Investors should treat that as guidance, not recognised revenue. Large public-sector hardware programmes can make quarterly results less predictable even when the long-term customer relationship remains intact.

## The Skydio investment raises the stakes

Axon separately announced an additional **$1.2 billion investment in Skydio**, comprising cash and Axon shares, subject to final transaction terms. The company said the deal would add about 20 percentage points to its fully diluted ownership at a $5.2 billion pre-money valuation and lift its stake to approximately 37%.

Axon also expects to become Skydio’s exclusive public-safety distributor in the United States and Canada. The transactions were expected to close across Q4 2026 and Q1 2027, subject to customary conditions.

The strategic logic is understandable. Drones can extend Axon’s network from evidence capture into first response, and distribution rights could connect hardware demand to software workflows. The financial risk is equally clear. A minority investment does not give Axon the same control as an owned business, while valuation changes could introduce volatility. Paying partly in shares also means the eventual ownership cost cannot be judged only from cash outflow.

## Cash flow shows the cost of preparation

Operating cash flow was **$20 million**, down from $60 million, and Axon’s non-GAAP free cash flow was a **$19 million outflow**, compared with a $33 million inflow a year earlier. Management said roughly $100 million of cash was used for strategic inventory investment.

Inventory can protect product availability, but cash tied up on the balance sheet must eventually convert into sales without write-downs or discounting. The test is not simply whether inventory rises; it is whether delivery timing, collections and margins justify the working-capital commitment.

Axon ended September with $1.7 billion of cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments and $2.9 billion of convertible and senior notes, a reported net debt position of $1.2 billion. The proposed Skydio commitment makes future cash conversion and share issuance more important than adjusted profit alone.

## What would confirm the thesis?

The strongest result would be software continuing to outgrow devices, recurring revenue retaining customers, and connected hardware margins recovering as new categories mature. Delayed federal deployments turning into recognised revenue without further slippage would improve confidence in execution. Inventory converting into cash and the Skydio partnership producing profitable distribution and software demand would connect the strategy to owner economics.

The counter-case is that hardware mix remains margin-dilutive, government programmes stay uneven, inventory absorbs cash and the Skydio investment adds valuation or dilution risk faster than it adds earnings.

**Axon’s software engine is strengthening, but the company is not becoming a simple software business.** Its appeal increasingly depends on whether a more capital-intensive hardware and drone ecosystem makes the recurring platform more valuable than the cash, margin and execution risk it introduces.
