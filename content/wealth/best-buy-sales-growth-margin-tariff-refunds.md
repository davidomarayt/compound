**Best Buy, the US electronics retailer, just had its strongest quarter of sales growth in five years. Profit rose faster still, but a one-off tariff refund did much of that work. Investors need to know whether higher sales are making the business more profitable, or only bigger.**

For the third quarter of fiscal 2027, the 13 weeks to 31 October 2026, Best Buy reported **revenue of $10.24 billion, up 5.8%**, and **comparable sales up 6.1%**, which the company says is its highest in five years. **Adjusted operating income rose 15% to $446 million**, and adjusted diluted EPS rose 16% to **$1.62**.

For the maintained quarterly record, see [Best Buy's stock profile](/stocks/bby/). This article focuses on what drove the margin.

## Take out the refund and the margin did not move

Best Buy's US gross profit rate rose to **24.1% from 23.3%**. The company says the main drivers were **$41 million of refunds of IEEPA tariffs** and growth in its Marketplace and Best Buy Ads businesses, partly offset by **lower product margin rates**. The refunds are included in adjusted results; the company does not exclude them.

Without the refunds, adjusted operating income would have been about **$405 million, up about 4%**, against sales up almost 6%. The adjusted operating margin would have been about **4.0%, the same as a year earlier**. These are Compound's calculations, which assume the refund flows straight to operating income, as gross profit does.

The US segment tells the same story. Its adjusted operating income rose 17% to **$422 million**. Without the refund it would have been about **$381 million**, up about 6%, and its margin would have been unchanged at about 4.0%.

On a per-share basis, the refund was worth roughly **$0.14** after tax, Compound's estimate using the quarter's 25.3% adjusted tax rate. Without it, adjusted EPS would have been about **$1.48, up about 6%** rather than 16%.

This is not the first refund. The second-quarter release reported about **$34 million** of IEEPA tariff refunds in US gross profit, so the two quarters together came to about **$75 million**. Neither release says how much more Best Buy expects to receive, or whether its guidance assumes any.

## Ads and Marketplace are helping, but costs rise too

Excluding the refund, the US gross profit rate still rose about **0.4 points**, Compound's calculation. Best Buy credits that to Marketplace, where other sellers list products on its site, and to Best Buy Ads, its advertising business. Both tend to carry higher margins than selling a television or a laptop. The company does not disclose their revenue or profit separately.

The gain was largely absorbed by costs. US adjusted selling, general and administrative expenses rose to **19.6% of revenue from 19.2%**, which the company attributes to higher incentive pay and investment in Marketplace and Ads. International adjusted operating income fell to **$24 million from $28 million** on lower product margins.

## Where the sales came from

US comparable sales rose 6.2%, and online sales rose 10.8%. Computing and mobile phones, about half of US revenue, grew **7.1%**. Entertainment grew **27.5%**, from a small base of 7% of revenue. Consumer electronics grew only 1.3% and appliances 2.9%. The company named computing, mobile phones and "emerging categories such as AI glasses and trading cards" as the biggest drivers.

A product cycle in computers and phones can lift sales for a while. It does not by itself lift margins, especially when product margin rates are falling.

## Guidance points to a better holiday quarter

Best Buy raised its full-year outlook: **comparable sales growth of 3.4% to 4.0%**, an adjusted operating income rate of **about 4.5%**, and **adjusted EPS of $6.90 to $7.05**, up from $6.70 to $6.90. For the fourth quarter it expects comparable sales growth of **2.0% to 4.0%** and an adjusted operating income rate of **4.9% to 5.1%**. That implies the holiday quarter will be the most profitable of the year.

The balance sheet looks ready for the season. Cash was **$1.99 billion**, against $923 million a year earlier. Inventory rose 11% to $8.90 billion, but accounts payable rose 17% to $8.56 billion, equal to about 96% of inventory, so suppliers are funding almost all of the holiday stock. Nine-month free cash flow, operating cash flow less capital spending, was about **$1.03 billion**, up from about $155 million, Compound's calculation. Best Buy paid $606 million in dividends in that period and plans about **$300 million** of buybacks for the year.

## What would confirm or weaken the thesis

The thesis strengthens if the fourth quarter reaches the guided margin of about 5% with little help from refunds, if Marketplace and Ads keep lifting the gross profit rate, and if product margin rates stop falling.

It weakens if sales growth fades once the computer and phone upgrade cycle passes, if SG&A keeps rising faster than sales, or if refunds turn out to have carried a large share of the raised EPS guidance.

**Best Buy's sales are genuinely stronger. But in the third quarter, almost all of the margin improvement came from a tariff refund. The test now is whether ads and Marketplace can lift profit on their own once the refunds stop.** This analysis makes no price target.
