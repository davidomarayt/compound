## At a glance

Venlafaxine is an SNRI used for depression and certain anxiety disorders. Immediate-release and prolonged-release products have different instructions. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/venlafaxine/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Serotonin and noradrenaline transporters and formulation differences

Venlafaxine and an active metabolite affect serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake. The relative contribution can vary with exposure. The effect on signalling can interact with other serotonergic treatments. Changes in noradrenaline signalling also help explain why clinicians may monitor blood pressure. [DailyMed source](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5fecf4b2-67fe-a7e9-e063-6394a90a793e)

## What the evidence measures

Assess benefit against the condition being treated, including function as well as symptoms. Side effects, tolerability and what happens around missed doses can be useful information for the prescriber.

## Why check whether it says prolonged release?

The release pattern changes how the product is taken. If a refill looks different or you missed a dose, refer to the exact leaflet and pharmacist rather than following another formulation’s directions.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Discuss missed doses and any taper with the prescriber. Seek urgent help for serious mental health deterioration or symptoms such as severe agitation with fever or confusion. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/venlafaxine/)
