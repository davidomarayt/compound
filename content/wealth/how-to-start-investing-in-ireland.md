# A complete beginner course for Irish investors

You do not need to become a stock picker to start investing.

You need a system.

For an Irish investor, that system begins with four questions:

1. **What is the money for?**
2. **When might you need it?**
3. **Which wrapper should hold it?**
4. **What tax regime applies to what you buy?**

Only after those questions are answered does the product choice become useful.

This course takes you through that process in order. It is designed for someone starting from zero, but each chapter also works as a standalone reference.

## Why investing in Ireland needs its own guide

A lot of investing advice online is written for Americans or people in the UK.

The broad principles can still be useful. The tax wrappers and reporting rules often are not.

An Irish resident can face very different tax treatment depending on whether money is held in:

- a pension or PRSA;
- direct company shares;
- an ETF or investment fund;
- a deposit account; or
- another investment structure.

That means “buy the cheapest fund” or “just buy the S&P 500” is not a complete Irish investing plan.

The course is built around **after-tax, after-fee, real-world decision making**, not around finding the most exciting product.

## What you will be able to do by the end

After working through all nine chapters, you should be able to:

- separate short-term cash from genuine long-term investment money;
- compare pension investing with investing through a normal brokerage account;
- explain the practical difference between direct shares, ETFs and funds;
- identify the main Irish tax questions to ask before buying;
- recognise when a portfolio is concentrated despite owning several products;
- compare brokers using regulation, custody, fees, FX and record keeping;
- calculate the long-term impact of recurring fees;
- choose a sustainable monthly contribution; and
- maintain a portfolio without reacting to every market headline.

The objective is not to make you dependent on this course.

It is to give you a framework you can reuse.

## What this course does not do

It does not tell you which stock to buy.

It does not promise a return.

It does not assume the highest-risk portfolio is the best one.

And it does not pretend that one investment product is right for every Irish household.

The aim is to make the decisions around investing easier to understand so that you can make them deliberately.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start investing in Ireland?

Start by defining the goal and time horizon, separating emergency cash, reviewing expensive debt and deciding whether the money belongs in a pension or a taxable account. Then understand the tax treatment, choose a regulated provider and select investments that match the risk you can maintain.

### Do I need a lot of money to start?

No. The important questions are whether the amount is sustainable, whether transaction and platform fees are proportionate, and whether the money can remain invested for the intended period.

### Should I use a pension or a normal investment account?

They solve different problems. A pension is designed for retirement and can have valuable tax treatment, but access is restricted. A taxable investment account is usually more flexible but can create tax obligations as gains or income arise. [Chapter 2 compares them in detail.](/wealth/pension-vs-investing-ireland/)

### Are ETFs taxed differently in Ireland?

Many funds and ETFs can fall under Ireland's investment-fund tax rules rather than ordinary CGT rules, but the exact treatment depends on the legal structure and domicile. [Chapter 4 explains the Irish investment-tax map.](/wealth/investment-tax-ireland/)

### What is the best investment platform in Ireland?

There is no single best platform for everyone. Compare the legal entity, regulator, custody arrangements, dealing and FX costs, recurring fees, tax records and transfer options. [Chapter 6 gives a neutral comparison framework.](/wealth/choose-investment-broker-ireland/)
