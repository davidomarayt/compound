## What this calculator is for

This calculator estimates the monthly repayment, total interest and total amount repaid on a standard fixed-payment loan using the amount borrowed, APR-style annual rate and term entered.

## How to read the result

A lower monthly payment is not automatically cheaper. Extending the term spreads the balance over more months and usually increases total interest.

## Worked example

A €10,000 loan at 8% over three years costs more per month than the same loan over five years, but the five-year loan usually produces more total interest because the balance remains outstanding longer.

## Irish context

When comparing Irish loans, look at APR, fees, term and total cost of credit. CCPC emphasises APR as a comparison measure, but the actual euro cost and contract terms still matter.

## What can change the answer

Rate, term, amount borrowed and fees determine cost. Early-repayment terms can also matter if you expect to clear the loan ahead of schedule.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not compare only the headline monthly payment. Do not add optional insurance or fees to one quote but not another. And avoid borrowing over a term longer than the useful life of the item where possible.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a longer term reduce interest?
Usually the opposite: it lowers the monthly payment but increases total interest.

### Is APR the same as interest rate?
APR is designed to reflect the annual cost of credit and can incorporate charges; check the lender disclosure.

### Does the tool include fees?
Only if the tool has an explicit fee input; otherwise no.

### Can I model 0% finance?
Yes, if the product genuinely has no interest or hidden charges relevant to the calculation.

## Method and limitations

The calculator uses the standard amortising-loan payment formula with a constant monthly rate over the selected term. It does not model changing rates, late fees or lender-specific early-repayment rules.
