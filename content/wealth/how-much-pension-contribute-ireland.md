# How much should you contribute to a pension?

There is no universal Irish pension percentage.

The useful answer comes from four layers:

1. **employer contributions;**
2. **the retirement income you are trying to build;**
3. **the Revenue tax-relief ceiling;**
4. **the amount your household can sustain.**

The Revenue limit is not a recommendation.

It is simply the maximum amount of qualifying personal contributions on which Income Tax relief may be available.

## Start with employer matching

If your employer matches contributions, understand that rule first.

Example:

Salary: €60,000  
Employee contribution required for full match: 5%  
Employee contribution: €3,000  
Employer contribution: €3,000

That gives €6,000 a year before investment returns.

If the employee instead contributes 2%, and the employer only matches 2%, the total falls to €2,400.

For many workers, reaching the full employer match is the most important first pension contribution decision.

## Then understand your Revenue ceiling

For personal pension contributions, Revenue uses age-related percentage limits.

<div class="pension-rate-grid">
<div><span>Under 30</span><strong>15%</strong></div>
<div><span>30–39</span><strong>20%</strong></div>
<div><span>40–49</span><strong>25%</strong></div>
<div><span>50–54</span><strong>30%</strong></div>
<div><span>55–59</span><strong>35%</strong></div>
<div><span>60+</span><strong>40%</strong></div>
</div>

Revenue also applies an annual earnings ceiling of **€115,000** when calculating relief.

These percentages apply to your own qualifying contributions.

Employer contributions are a separate part of the pension funding picture.

Use the [Pension Tax Relief Calculator](/pension-tax-relief-calculator/) to calculate the relevant personal relief ceiling.

## The tax ceiling is not the savings target

A 32-year-old earning €50,000 has an age-related limit of 20%.

That does not mean they must contribute €10,000.

It means up to €10,000 of qualifying personal contributions could fall within the percentage ceiling, subject to the other Revenue rules.

A sustainable contribution below that amount can still build substantial retirement assets.

The target should come from the desired retirement outcome.

## Work backwards from retirement

A useful pension plan asks:

> **What annual income do I want in retirement, and what other income will already exist?**

Suppose a household expects:

- State Pension income;
- an existing occupational pension;
- and retirement income from a PRSA.

The PRSA does not need to fund the entire retirement budget by itself.

Estimate:

**desired spending – expected secure income = gap to be funded**

Then model the fund required to support that gap.

Use the [Pension Projection Calculator](/pension-projection-calculator/) to test:

- current pension value;
- monthly contribution;
- employer contribution;
- years to retirement;
- assumed investment return;
- and fees.

## Contribution rate matters more than chasing return

Consider two levers:

- increase expected investment return from 5% to 7%;
- increase monthly pension saving from €300 to €450.

The second lever is under your control.

The first is not.

A pension plan should therefore focus first on:

- contribution level;
- fees;
- time;
- diversification;
- and behaviour.

Return assumptions should be treated as scenarios, not promises.

## Starting age changes the burden

Starting earlier gives contributions more time to compound.

Someone beginning at 25 can reach the same target with a lower annual contribution than someone beginning at 45, all else equal.

That is not a reason for a late starter to give up.

It is a reason to use a realistic projection and, where affordable, increase contributions.

The Pensions Authority specifically notes that people may need to contribute more if:

- they started saving later;
- projected benefits look insufficient;
- or they want additional retirement benefits.

## AVCs

An **Additional Voluntary Contribution** is an extra contribution paid by a member of an occupational pension scheme to improve retirement benefits.

AVCs can qualify for Income Tax relief within Revenue limits.

If an occupational scheme does not permit AVCs, the Pensions Authority states that an employer must provide access to a Standard PRSA for AVC purposes.

AVCs are not separate from the age-related personal contribution ceiling.

They count when assessing the overall qualifying personal contributions.

## A practical contribution ladder

<div><span>LEVEL 2</span><strong>Build an adequate retirement projection</strong><p>Use your actual pension balance, retirement age and expected retirement income sources.</p></div>

This is not a rule that everyone must climb to the top.

It is a decision sequence.

## Percentage of salary or fixed euro amount?

A salary percentage is convenient because it automatically rises with pay.

A fixed euro amount can be easier for household budgeting.

Either can work.

What matters is that:

- the contribution reaches the pension consistently;
- the employer contribution is captured;
- fees do not absorb an unreasonable share;
- and the amount remains affordable.

## When should contributions increase?

Useful triggers include:

- salary increase;
- promotion;
- mortgage rate reduction;
- childcare costs falling;
- consumer debt being cleared;
- annual bonus;
- approaching retirement with a shortfall.

You can decide the rule in advance.

Example:

> Half of every net pay rise goes to lifestyle, half to long-term saving.

That prevents every increase in income from disappearing into higher spending.

## What about very high earners?

The Revenue earnings ceiling means the age-related relief percentage is applied to a maximum of €115,000 of earnings for this purpose.

A high earner may still make pension contributions above the personal tax-relievable amount in some circumstances, but the tax treatment needs to be understood before doing so.

Large pension funds also interact with the **Standard Fund Threshold**.

For 2026, Revenue states that the Standard Fund Threshold is **€2.2 million**.

That is a specialist issue, but it becomes relevant for very large retirement funds.

## Do not sacrifice financial resilience

Pension money is restricted.

Maximising a tax deduction while leaving no emergency savings can create a household liquidity problem.

A pension contribution is appropriate only if the money can genuinely remain retirement money.

Retirement planning and short-term resilience have to coexist.

## A simple annual pension review

Once a year, record:

- current fund value;
- employee contribution;
- employer contribution;
- total annual charges;
- investment allocation;
- projected retirement value;
- projected retirement income;
- Revenue contribution headroom;
- and whether your retirement goal changed.

Then decide whether the contribution needs to change.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 10% of salary enough for a pension in Ireland?

It may be enough for one person and inadequate for another. Age, current fund, employer contributions, retirement age and desired income all matter.

### Should I max out my pension tax relief?

Not automatically. The relief ceiling is not a savings recommendation. Consider liquidity, debt, short-term goals and retirement needs.

### Do employer contributions count toward my age-related personal relief limit?

The age-related percentage limits apply to qualifying personal contributions. Employer contributions are considered separately, subject to the pension product rules.

### Can I increase my pension with AVCs?

Yes, where the scheme permits them. AVCs are subject to Revenue’s tax-relief limits for your qualifying personal contributions.
