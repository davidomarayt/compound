If you were unable to work for six months, how would your mortgage, rent, groceries, childcare and other bills be paid?

That is the question **income protection insurance** is designed to answer. In Ireland, an eligible policy can pay part of your earnings if illness or injury prevents you from working. It can be particularly relevant if your household depends on one income, your employer offers limited paid sick leave, or you are self-employed.

The detail matters. The advertised cover percentage is **not automatically the amount you receive into your bank account**. There can be a waiting period, deductions for State benefits or employer sick pay, medical evidence requirements and tax on the payment.

## What is income protection insurance?

Income protection, sometimes called **income insurance**, **salary protection**, **income continuance** or **permanent health insurance (PHI)**, is intended to help replace earnings when you are medically unable to work due to a covered illness or injury.

It is not simply insurance against losing your job. **Ordinary redundancy, dismissal or unemployment is not what standard long-term income protection is designed to cover.** If you are concerned about a job loss instead, see our guide to [redundancy payments and tax in Ireland](/wealth/redundancy-ireland-statutory-ex-gratia-tax/).

The point of income protection is to help you meet ongoing commitments while you recover. For many households, these include:

- housing payments, rent and utilities;
- food, transport and childcare;
- insurance premiums and essential debt repayments;
- household expenses that continue when employment income stops.

Unlike a lump-sum insurance payout, standard income protection is typically an ongoing benefit paid while the qualifying incapacity continues, until the policy's stopping point.

<h2 id="how-it-works">How does income protection work? Five steps</h2>

**1. Select a cover amount.** Choose an insured income within the provider's earnings limit, based on what you actually need and what you can insure.

**2. Choose a deferred period.** This is the period of incapacity you must complete before the claim starts paying. A longer period can reduce premiums, but requires more financial reserves or employer sick pay.

**3. Apply and complete underwriting.** The insurer considers your occupation, earnings, health history and other information. It may offer normal terms, load the premium, exclude conditions or decline cover.

**4. Keep the policy in force.** Pay premiums and comply with policy conditions. If you need to claim, the insurer examines medical evidence and whether you meet the policy's incapacity definition.

**5. Receive qualifying payments.** After the deferred period, the insurer calculates the eligible benefit and deducts any relevant offsets. Payments normally stop if you recover, no longer satisfy the claims definition, reach the policy end age or another stopping condition applies.

A doctor's certificate on its own does **not** guarantee a successful insurance claim. The medical evidence must also satisfy your specific policy wording.

<h2 id="how-much-cover">How much income protection can you get in Ireland?</h2>

Many Irish income-protection products advertise cover of up to **75% of earnings**. Some policies use a lower maximum such as 66%, and individual products can have an annual euro cap. For example, Irish Life advertises cover up to 75% of salary, less specified other benefits, subject to its product's maximum.

That percentage applies to **eligible earnings and contractual calculations**, not automatically to your take-home pay.

### Worked example: earning €60,000

Suppose you earn €60,000 a year and obtain a policy with a 75% maximum:

| Step | Illustrative amount |
|---|---:|
| Gross annual earnings | €60,000 |
| 75% maximum cover before offsets | €45,000 per year |
| Equivalent monthly gross ceiling | €3,750 |
| Hypothetical monthly employer sick pay | €1,000 |
| Hypothetical monthly State payment counted as an offset | €1,000 |
| Potential insurer payment under this simplified scenario | **€1,750 gross per month** |

The €1,000 amounts are **fictional numbers used to show how offsets work**; they are not statements of actual State entitlements. The insurer's own calculation may use different definitions or limits.

If the other payments cease, the insurer's payment could change under the policy. If your pre-claim earnings fall, the benefit you can claim may also be lower than the cover originally chosen.

Most importantly, **gross insured benefit is not net income after tax**. Revenue requires PAYE and USC on approved PHI benefits.

### A practical way to choose cover

Start with essential monthly expenses, then subtract realistic income that would continue during incapacity. Include what a partner could sustainably contribute without assuming they will work indefinitely at the same level.

Compare the remaining gap with the policy's maximum. More cover is not always better if it cannot be claimed because of offsets or income restrictions.

## What is a deferred period, and which one should you choose?

The deferred period is the waiting time after you become unable to work before eligible insurance payments begin.

The CCPC lists typical periods of four, 13, 26 and 52 weeks, although the options vary by insurer and product.

| Deferred period | Who might consider it? | Key trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| 4 weeks | Someone with very limited paid sick leave or savings | Generally higher premium; not available on every policy |
| 13 weeks | Someone able to cover about three months without insurer payments | Balances shorter exposure with price |
| 26 weeks | Someone with six months of employer sick pay or substantial cash reserves | Usually cheaper than shorter waiting periods |
| 52 weeks | Someone able to self-fund a year of illness | Potentially cheaper, but much longer financial gap |

**Do not select a 26-week deferred period just because it produces a cheaper quote.** Check whether you can genuinely finance all essential bills for six months if your income disappears.

If your employer pays three months on full salary followed by three months at half salary, a deferred period should be evaluated against that exact schedule—not the words "six months sick pay" alone.

<h2 id="cost">How much does income protection insurance cost in Ireland in 2026?</h2>

There is **no single reliable nationwide monthly premium** that applies to every buyer. The CCPC explains that cost depends on age, occupation risk, insured amount, deferral period, term, health and lifestyle.

A 30-year-old office worker and a 50-year-old tradesperson can receive very different terms, even at the same salary.

### What pushes premiums higher?

- **Age:** An older new applicant generally pays more.
- **Occupation:** Physically demanding or hazardous work can be more expensive or ineligible.
- **Short waiting period:** An early benefit start normally raises premiums.
- **Higher cover:** Insuring more income generally costs more.
- **Longer policy term:** Cover extending towards retirement can cost more.
- **Medical history and exclusions:** Underwriting can change both price and availability.
- **Inflation-linked benefits:** Indexation may increase the policy's premium or benefit over time.

### What does the cost look like after tax relief?

Rather than pretend to know your insurer's quote, the table below shows **hypothetical premium amounts** and what they would cost after full qualifying income-tax relief.

| Example quoted premium | After 20% income-tax relief | After 40% income-tax relief |
|---|---:|---:|
| €50 per month | €40 per month | €30 per month |
| €75 per month | €60 per month | €45 per month |
| €100 per month | €80 per month | €60 per month |
| €150 per month | €120 per month | €90 per month |

These are arithmetic illustrations, **not available market quotes or promised savings**. Full relief assumes a qualifying Revenue-approved scheme, enough tax liability, the relevant marginal rate and premiums within the 10% cap. No additional PRSI or USC relief is assumed.

A slightly cheaper policy may be poor value if it has a restrictive disability definition, an unsuitable deferred period or exclusions that matter to you.

<h2 id="tax-relief">Income protection tax relief Ireland: how does it work?</h2>

This is one of the biggest differences between income protection and ordinary private health insurance.

Revenue calls qualifying arrangements **Permanent Health Benefit schemes**. The policy or scheme must be approved by Revenue to qualify for premium relief.

### The 10% of income rule

Income-tax relief applies to eligible premiums up to **10% of your total income for the tax year**. The ceiling is on the **premiums eligible for relief**. It is *not* a rule saying your insurer will only cover 10% of your salary.

For example:

- Total annual income: **€60,000**
- Maximum annual premiums within the 10% rule: **€6,000**
- Hypothetical annual premium: **€1,080** (€90 monthly)
- Assuming all €1,080 qualifies and is relieved at 40%: **€432 annual income-tax reduction**
- Equivalent effective cost: **€648 per year**, or **€54 per month**

A person relieved at 20% would instead pay an effective **€72 per month** on the same €90 premium, assuming full eligibility.

This is **income-tax relief**, not a blanket 40% cash discount for everyone. Your own tax band, approvals and eligibility matter. Revenue explicitly states that PHI premiums do not receive PRSI or USC relief.

### How to claim income protection tax relief

If your premiums are deducted through an approved employer scheme under the **net-pay arrangement**, the income-tax relief is usually applied through payroll. Do not claim it a second time.

If you pay for an individual policy yourself, Revenue's guidance for PAYE taxpayers is to:

1. Sign in to [Revenue myAccount](https://www.revenue.ie/en/online-services/services/paye-services/myaccount.aspx).
2. Choose **Manage your tax for the current year** and claim tax credits.
3. Find the **Health / Income Continuance** category.
4. Supply your insurer and premium details, and keep supporting records.

For earlier tax years, Revenue explains how to use **Review your Tax** and the annual Income Tax Return. Self-assessed taxpayers generally address qualifying premiums through their Form 11.

Check whether the insurer confirms the policy is **Revenue-approved**. A similar-sounding insurance policy is not sufficient evidence.

### Are income protection payouts tax-free?

**No.** Revenue states that payments from approved PHI schemes are subject to **PAYE and USC**, with the scheme administrator deducting tax. Do not budget on receiving the full advertised gross benefit as spendable cash.

Self-employed claimants have particular tax treatment choices; Revenue refers to notifying it on Form PH (5) within six months of taking out the policy if they want benefits treated as trading income. Get individual tax guidance where relevant.

<h2 id="sick-pay">Income protection vs employer sick pay and State benefits</h2>

Before buying a policy, list **every payment you could receive while unable to work**. The CCPC specifically recommends checking employer sick pay, social welfare, and ill-health retirement or pension arrangements.

### Statutory sick pay in Ireland in 2026

Despite earlier plans for a gradual increase to ten days, the Government **retained the legal entitlement at five days** in 2026.

Eligible employees with at least 13 weeks' service can receive up to five medically certified sick days per calendar year, paid at **70% of usual earnings, capped at €110 per day**.

Five days is a very short period compared with a long-term illness. Your employer may provide a more generous contractual or occupational scheme.

The Department of Social Protection has published detailed [2026 guidance on how statutory sick leave interacts with Illness Benefit](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/publications/illness-benefit-injury-benefit-and-statutory-sick-leave-in-2025/).

### What about Illness Benefit?

Illness Benefit is a **separate State scheme** for eligible people who cannot work because of medically certified illness and meet PRSI conditions. It is not necessarily the same amount as your salary, and it is not automatic for everyone.

The Department of Social Protection's guidance lists qualifying PRSI classes and contribution tests. It also explains waiting days and the interaction with sick leave. Always check [current Illness Benefit entitlement](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/illness-benefit/) before estimating your private insurance gap.

Private insurers may deduct some State benefits from their maximum income-protection payout. Read the offset clause carefully; you cannot assume every payment will stack in full.

### Group income protection at work

Your employer, union or professional association might already offer income continuance. Group schemes can be more affordable than individual cover, but may have:

- a set cover percentage or maximum;
- a fixed deferred period;
- eligibility or service requirements;
- limited portability when you leave employment;
- different underwriting and exclusions;
- benefits that interact with the employer's sick-pay arrangements.

Request the **full scheme booklet**, not just the benefit line on an HR portal. Ask HR what happens if you change jobs, take unpaid leave or leave the company while claiming.

<h2 id="self-employed">Income protection if you are self-employed in Ireland</h2>

Self-employed people can face a particularly severe gap: if they cannot work, they may have no employer-funded sick pay and no replacement for business earnings.

Standard **Class S PRSI does not provide ordinary Illness Benefit**; the Department of Social Protection's Class S benefit list does include certain other supports, such as Invalidity Pension, where qualifying conditions are met. Some people have entitlements based on other contribution classes or particular occupations, so check your individual PRSI record.

For a sole trader or director, an insurer will usually want evidence of **earnings that can actually be insured**. Business turnover is not the same as personal income. Company dividends, retained profits and irregular trading earnings may receive different treatment under policy definitions.

If self-employed, compare:

- what counts as insured earnings;
- the accounts or tax returns required;
- whether income is averaged over several years;
- whether the cover continues if trading stops or employment status changes;
- the insurer's definition of incapacity;
- which costs must be met during the deferred period;
- how tax relief and taxable benefit payments work for your specific arrangement.

A personal policy is not the same as **business overheads insurance** or key-person cover; those products protect different exposures.

<h2 id="compare">Which income protection insurance policy is best in Ireland?</h2>

There is no credible universal winner. Price depends on individual underwriting, and the most important contract terms can vary.

Irish providers that offer income protection include [Aviva](https://www.aviva.ie/protection/income-protection/), [Irish Life](https://www.irishlife.ie/life-insurance/income-protection/), [New Ireland Assurance](https://www.newireland.ie/insurance/income-protection/) and [Zurich](https://www.zurich.ie/life-insurance/income-protection/). Other distribution channels and group arrangements may also be relevant. This list is not a product ranking.

Use the same age, occupation, earnings, cover amount, deferred period and end age when requesting comparable quotes.

### What to compare beyond the monthly premium

| Policy feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Own-occupation wording** | Does the insurer assess your inability to do your *own* job, or any suitable work? Check changes in the definition over time. |
| **Deferred period** | Can you pay the bills until the policy begins? |
| **Maximum benefit and offsets** | What will really be paid after sick pay, State benefits and other sources? |
| **Cease age** | Does cover end at 55, 60, 65, 67 or another specified age? |
| **Guaranteed vs reviewable premium** | Could the premium be changed during the term? |
| **Inflation protection** | Can benefits rise in line with inflation, and does this increase premiums? |
| **Rehabilitation or partial return to work** | What happens if you can work reduced hours but not full-time? |
| **Exclusions and disclosure** | Which health conditions, occupations or circumstances are excluded? |
| **Claims requirements** | What medical, earnings and ongoing evidence is necessary? |
| **Leaving work or switching jobs** | Will an employer scheme remain available, and can you replace it? |

### Disability definition: a detail worth reading twice

The CCPC warns that some policies only pay if you are incapable of **any** paid work rather than your normal job. That can be materially more restrictive for a specialist worker whose illness prevents their occupation but not every possible job.

Ask for the full policy wording and written examples of how the incapacity test applies to your occupation. Marketing phrases such as "protect your salary" are no substitute for the actual definition.

### Should you use a broker?

A broker who can compare relevant insurers may help you assess the occupation classes, exclusions and differences between policies. Ask which insurers are being compared, whether the adviser is independent or restricted, what commissions or fees apply, and what ongoing service is provided.

Check advisers and firms through the [Central Bank of Ireland's financial service provider register](https://registers.centralbank.ie/FirmSearchPage.aspx/TCSPSearchPage.aspx).

## What does income protection not cover?

Standard policies will not cover every loss of income. Common issues include:

- redundancy, business failure or voluntary unemployment;
- a condition specifically excluded at underwriting;
- a claim that does not meet the insurer's disability definition;
- incapacity during a deferred period;
- periods when the policy has lapsed;
- earnings above what the insurer considers eligible;
- circumstances covered by an exclusion or other limitation in your contract.

Specific medical conditions are not automatically excluded from *every* policy. Outcomes depend on the provider, your disclosures, the policy type and the wording.

**Declare your health and occupational information accurately** when applying. If something changes between application and acceptance, ask the insurer what must be disclosed. Never assume a past diagnosis is irrelevant without checking the questions asked.

## Income protection vs serious illness, life insurance and health insurance

These products solve different problems.

| Type of insurance | Main purpose | Typical payment |
|---|---|---|
| **Income protection** | Replace part of earnings if a covered illness or injury prevents work | Regular taxable income benefit after waiting period |
| **Specified serious illness cover** | Pay if you meet the precise definition of a specified condition | Lump sum |
| **Life insurance** | Financial support after an insured person's death | Usually lump sum |
| **Mortgage protection** | Repay a covered mortgage balance on death, subject to terms | Debt-repaying lump sum |
| **Private health insurance** | Cover specified medical treatment and health expenses | Payments/reimbursements for eligible healthcare |

A person can have excellent private hospital cover but no income if they cannot return to work. Similarly, a life policy does not normally replace monthly earnings during a long illness.

Read [Health Insurance in Ireland](/wealth/health-insurance-ireland/) and [Mortgage Protection and Drawdown](/wealth/mortgage-protection-drawdown-ireland/) for the separate questions those products address.

<h2 id="claims">How do you claim on income protection insurance?</h2>

The process depends on the insurer, but a careful starting sequence is:

1. **Tell your insurer or broker promptly** if you become unfit to work, even when the deferred period has not ended. Ask about notification deadlines.
2. **Ask for the claim form and the exact incapacity test.** Confirm which doctor or specialist reports are needed.
3. **Gather proof of income and employment**, such as payslips, employer confirmation, tax returns or accounts where relevant.
4. **Disclose other payments** such as sick pay, State benefits or other income that might affect the calculation.
5. **Keep records** of medical appointments, correspondence, forms submitted and decisions.
6. **Respond to reviews**: an ongoing claim may require updated clinical evidence or assessments.
7. **Ask how part-time return-to-work affects benefits** before agreeing to a phased return.

If a claim is refused, request the exact reasons and policy clauses in writing, plus information about the insurer's internal appeals or complaints process.

The [Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman](https://www.fspo.ie/make-a-complaint/how-to-make-a-complaint-to-your-provider/financial-services-provider/) advises consumers to complain to the provider first. Providers can take up to **40 working days** to deal with a financial-services complaint; the FSPO can advise on next steps if the issue is unresolved or the final response is not received.

## How to decide whether income protection is worth it

Consider four questions:

**1. What happens to household cash flow after two weeks, three months and a year without your earnings?** A short-term cash buffer is not always protection against long-term incapacity.

**2. Exactly what sick pay and State support would you receive?** Ask HR for the policy in writing and check PRSI eligibility rather than estimating from memory.

**3. What contract would you actually be buying?** Compare incapacity definitions, exclusions, deferred periods and end ages before judging price.

**4. Can you continue paying the premium comfortably?** There is little value in choosing unaffordable cover that later lapses.

<h2 id="faq">Income protection insurance Ireland: frequently asked questions</h2>

### Is income protection insurance worth it in Ireland?

It can be valuable if your household would struggle with a long period without your earnings, especially if you have dependants, debt or limited paid sick leave. It may be less urgent if you already have an adequate employer group scheme or enough assets and dependable replacement income. Read the policy before deciding.

### Can income protection cover 75% of my salary?

Many products advertise a maximum of 75% of eligible earnings, subject to euro caps, underwriting, policy terms and deductions for other benefits. That does not mean 75% automatically arrives tax-free in your account.

### How much is income protection per month in Ireland?

There is no valid flat national premium. Quotes depend on your age, occupation, health, amount of cover, policy end age and deferred period. Obtain comparable written quotes; use the hypothetical after-tax examples above only to understand the calculation.

### Is income protection tax deductible in Ireland?

Qualifying premiums on a **Revenue-approved** Permanent Health Benefit scheme can receive income-tax relief, subject to the 10% of annual income premium limit and your personal tax circumstances. Revenue explains how to claim through myAccount or payroll.

### Do I get 40% tax relief on income protection?

You may obtain relief at 40% if that is the relevant marginal income-tax rate applied to the qualifying premium. Others may receive 20% or a mix, depending on their circumstances. PRSI and USC relief are not included.

### Is income protection tax-free when claimed?

No. Revenue says approved-scheme PHI payments are taxable under PAYE and USC.

### Does income protection cover mental health problems?

Whether a mental health condition qualifies depends on the contract's definitions, exclusions, medical evidence and underwriting. Do not assume all conditions are automatically included or excluded. Get the insurer's written terms and seek appropriate clinical advice.

### Will income protection pay for back pain?

It may pay for a back condition if you meet the disability definition and the condition is not excluded, but back-related claims and underwriting are policy-specific. A diagnosis does not guarantee a claim.

### Does income protection cover pregnancy or maternity leave?

Normal maternity leave is not the same as an insured incapacity. Certain medical complications might be assessed under a particular policy's definitions and exclusions. Obtain written clarification before relying on cover.

### Can self-employed people buy income protection?

Yes, subject to eligibility and underwriting. Providers may require accounts and proof of insurable personal earnings, and Class S self-employed workers generally do not qualify for ordinary Illness Benefit simply through that PRSI class.

### Can I claim income protection and Illness Benefit together?

Possibly, if you independently qualify for each, but many policies **offset** qualifying State payments when calculating the insurer benefit. You should not assume both pay their full advertised amounts.

### Is income protection the same as permanent health insurance?

In this context, permanent health insurance (PHI) generally refers to income-continuance cover for incapacity, not the private medical insurance used to pay for hospital or healthcare treatment.

### What happens if I change jobs?

For individual cover, check whether your occupation, pay or employment status affects the terms. For employer or union schemes, cover may end when you leave. Ask about continuation rights and new underwriting before resigning.

### Can I cancel or switch income protection?

Usually you can seek cancellation, subject to the contract and notice rules, but replacing an existing policy may mean fresh medical underwriting, different exclusions or higher premiums. **Do not cancel the old policy until replacement cover is accepted and in force.**

### How long does income protection last?

An accepted claim generally continues while policy conditions are met and until an applicable stopping point such as return to work, death or the policy's benefit end age. Some policies have different review conditions or limits. Check the wording.

## The bottom line

The right income protection policy is not necessarily the cheapest. It is the one that fills a genuine earnings gap **at the time you need it**, uses a disability definition appropriate to your work, lasts long enough, and remains affordable after correctly calculating tax relief.

Before buying, read the [CCPC's independent income-protection advice](https://www.ccpc.ie/manage-your-money/day-to-day-finances/insurance/income-protection-insurance) and the [Revenue rules for Permanent Health Benefit schemes](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/health-and-age/permanent-health-benefit-contributions/index.aspx). Compare actual contracts, not just prices or headlines.
