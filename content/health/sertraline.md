## At a glance

Sertraline is an SSRI used for depression and selected anxiety-related conditions. Improvement is assessed against the original goal of treatment and the side effects that matter to the person. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/sertraline/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Why symptom changes take longer than transporter effects

The serotonin transporter recycles serotonin from the space between nerve cells. Sertraline inhibits this transporter. The immediate change in signalling and the longer adaptations relevant to symptoms occur on different timescales. Serotonin signalling elsewhere, including the gut, contributes to possible adverse effects. [DailyMed source](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e0e1f35e-114d-bb39-34f1-70208b94ac9e)

## What the evidence measures

When reviewing the evidence, check which condition was studied and which scale or daily activity changed. Early adverse effects and later changes in mood, anxiety or compulsions are worth tracking separately.

## Build a simple timeline

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Start | baseline symptoms and what interferes with daily life |
| Review | changes over time, side effects and any barriers to taking it |


## What should a first follow-up cover besides mood?

Bring sleep, activity, adherence and any adverse effects. For anxiety or compulsions, describe a concrete change in a situation you used to avoid or a task that used to be difficult.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Talk to the prescriber about other serotonergic medicines and any plan to stop. Seek urgent help for thoughts of self-harm or if you cannot keep yourself safe. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/sertraline/)
