## At a glance

Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs are used for pain and inflammation. Oral and topical products have different exposure, and medicines within the class have different safety profiles. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/nsaids/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Cyclo-oxygenase, inflammation and where risk comes from

COX enzymes help make prostaglandins. Reducing prostaglandin production can reduce pain sensitisation and inflammation. Prostaglandins also help protect the stomach and maintain blood flow to the kidneys in some circumstances. Those roles help explain why blocking the pathway can cause harm as well as relief. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/nsaids/)

## What the evidence measures

Look for a meaningful change in movement or the activity that prompted treatment. Evidence varies by condition, duration and formulation. A short-term pain score does not measure the chance of gastrointestinal or kidney complications.

## Check your list for duplicate NSAIDs

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Prescriptions | list any tablets, capsules and gels by ingredient |
| Over-the-counter items | check combination cold and pain products |


## Why include gels at a medicines review?

A gel and a tablet can contain medicines in the same class. The amount reaching circulation generally differs, but the prescriber needs to know both products to judge duplication and overall risk.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Use a pharmacist to check interactions, especially if you take a blood thinner or a combination of a diuretic and some blood pressure medicines. Signs of gastrointestinal bleeding need urgent assessment. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/nsaids/)
