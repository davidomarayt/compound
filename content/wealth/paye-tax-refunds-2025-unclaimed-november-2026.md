Fresh reporting on PAYE overpayments has renewed a familiar question for Irish workers: **does Revenue owe me a tax refund?** Newspaper coverage on 9–10 November cites a parliamentary answer indicating that many taxpayer units have not yet finalised their 2025 tax positions. But the key distinction is between **a preliminary indication of overpayment and a confirmed refund**. [Revenue's explanation of preliminary results](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/end-of-year-process/preliminary-end-year-statement.aspx)

One press report, citing figures provided in response to a parliamentary question, puts the number of 2025 taxpayer units potentially in an overpayment position at roughly **376,000**, with an indicative average of **€591**. Those are **reported, provisional figures**, not 376,000 approved payments or a personal entitlement of €591. The original parliamentary response was not independently available for this article; readers should treat the reported aggregate as provisional rather than audited primary-source data. [Contemporaneous report](https://www.joe.ie/business/more-than-375000-paye-workers-could-be-due-tax-rebate-ahead-of-christmas-888898)

What *is* directly confirmed by Revenue is how to check your individual position and an approaching deadline that could permanently remove older claims.

## The 2022 deadline matters even if you have not checked 2025

[Revenue's four-year rule](https://www.revenue.ie/en/personal-tax-credits-reliefs-and-exemptions/four-year-rule/index.aspx) says that **refund claims for the 2022 tax year must be made no later than 31 December 2026**. Claims made after the relevant four-year window cannot be repaid. It is worth reviewing 2022 now rather than assuming that a later 2025 refund application also preserves older years.

This rule is about the tax year **2022**, not a universal deadline for every 2025 claim. The 2023–2025 years have later statutory cutoffs. Do not rely on receiving a general reminder for each year.

## How to see whether Revenue owes you money

1. Go directly to **[revenue.ie](https://www.revenue.ie/)** and sign in to **myAccount**. Avoid links in unsolicited text messages or emails claiming an automatic rebate.
2. Under PAYE Services, select **Review your tax for the previous 4 years** and choose the tax year.
3. Request a **Statement of Liability** to see the preliminary result. It may show overpayment, underpayment or a balanced position.
4. Complete the **PAYE Income Tax Return** for that year. Declare relevant additional income and claim credits or reliefs to which you are actually entitled.
5. Read the **final Statement of Liability**. The preliminary figure can rise, fall, disappear or become tax owed once your complete circumstances are assessed.

Revenue explicitly states that an income tax return is required to receive a repayment arising from an overpayment. A preliminary notice alone is not a payment instruction. [Revenue's step-by-step guidance](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/end-of-year-process/preliminary-end-year-statement.aspx)

## What might change the final calculation?

Review legitimate **medical expenses**, **qualifying remote-working costs**, **rent relief** and any other applicable credits against official eligibility rules. Also disclose non-PAYE income when required. For a hypothetical worker with a provisional €300 overpayment and an additional valid €100 tax credit, the final result *might* increase, but only if the credit applies, the liability permits it and no further tax is due. This is an illustration, not a guarantee of €400.

For people **jointly assessed** as a couple, Revenue says refunds are paid to each individual in proportion to the tax each actually paid. An aggregated household number may therefore not correspond to a single incoming transfer. [Revenue refund and joint-assessment guidance](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/end-of-year-process/refund-if-due.aspx)

## When would a confirmed refund arrive?

Revenue says that when a **Statement of Liability confirms an overpayment**, the repayment normally reaches the bank account held on your record in **three to five working days**. Check the IBAN shown in your Revenue profile. Where a transfer is not possible, Revenue says it can issue a cheque. This is the timing **after confirmation**, not an assurance that a new claim will be processed the moment it is submitted.

Revenue also warns that some completed tax reviews reveal an **underpayment** rather than a refund. If that happens, read the calculation and contact Revenue through authenticated myAccount channels before agreeing to payment requests from a third party.

**Practical next step:** If you have not reviewed your PAYE years, start with **2022 before 31 December 2026**, then check 2023, 2024 and 2025 separately. Save the final liability statements for your records.
