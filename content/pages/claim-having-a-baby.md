Start with the dates you expect to take leave. Then check your PRSI record, ask payroll what it will pay, and find out when each State payment starts. That gives you a monthly budget from the weeks before birth to the point when you start paying for childcare.

## Before the birth {#before-birth}

- **Maternity Benefit.** The Department of Social Protection pays eligible workers for 26 weeks at the standard 2026 rate of €299 per week. Employees should apply at least six weeks before leave and get the MB2 certificate from their employer. Self-employed applicants should apply at least 12 weeks before leave and use the MB3 medical certificate. [Check eligibility and apply for Maternity Benefit](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/maternity-benefit/).
- **Paternity Benefit.** Eligible parents can claim two consecutive weeks at the standard 2026 rate of €299 per week. Start the leave within 26 weeks of birth or adoption. Check the employer certification and application steps before choosing dates. [Check eligibility and apply for Paternity Benefit](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/paternity-benefit/).
- **Employer pay.** Ask payroll how much your employer pays during each type of leave, whether the State benefit goes to you or your employer, and what happens when the top-up ends. Use the combined amount you expect to keep when calculating your budget.

## After the birth {#after-birth}

**Register the birth and check Child Benefit.** For a baby born in Ireland, the Department sends a claim form after registration if this is your first Child Benefit claim. If you already receive it, the Department usually adds the baby when you register the birth. Different application steps apply for births abroad or late registration. The standard monthly payment for one eligible child is €140, with a €280 Newborn Baby Grant added to the first payment for an eligible newborn. [Check Child Benefit and the application routes](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/child-benefit/).

## Before your child turns two {#parents-benefit}

**Plan Parent’s Leave and claim Parent’s Benefit.** Each eligible parent can claim up to nine weeks, together or in separate weeks, within two years of birth or adoption placement. At the standard 2026 rate, nine weeks comes to €2,691 before tax. You need your child’s PPS number, and the Department says to claim within six months of taking Parent’s Leave. [Check the PRSI rules and apply for Parent’s Benefit](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/parents-benefit/).

## Before paying for childcare {#childcare}

1. Ask the provider for the weekly fee, the number of hours it charges for and whether it participates in the National Childcare Scheme (NCS).
2. Check the **universal and income-assessed** NCS routes using the [official National Childcare Scheme calculator](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-children-disability-and-equality/campaigns/national-childcare-scheme/). Use the award and hours confirmed for your child when setting your budget.
3. When your child reaches the eligible age, check [ECCE preschool](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-children-disability-and-equality/publications/early-childhood-care-and-education-programme-ecce/). Ask for the bill after the preschool hours are accounted for and confirm what the NCS can cover separately. The provider’s fee for extra hours, meals or other services may still apply.
