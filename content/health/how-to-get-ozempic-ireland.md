If you search **“how to get Ozempic in Ireland”**, the shortest accurate answer is this:

> **Ozempic is available in Ireland, but you need a prescription. The HSE describes Ozempic as a treatment for adults with type 2 diabetes. If the purpose is weight management, Wegovy is the semaglutide product specifically used for obesity in HSE guidance.**

That distinction matters because the words *Ozempic*, *semaglutide* and *weight-loss injection* are often used as if they mean the same thing. They do not.

This guide explains the legitimate route to treatment in Ireland, what a doctor may assess, the current HSE funding position, what it can cost privately and how Wegovy and Mounjaro fit into the picture.

## Can you get Ozempic in Ireland?

Yes. Ozempic is authorised in the European Union and is available in Ireland as a **prescription-only medicine**.

The Irish medicines database lists Ozempic as a product subject to medical prescription, while the EMA's authorised indication is the treatment of adults with insufficiently controlled **type 2 diabetes**, alongside diet and exercise.

In practice, the regulated route is:

1. **Speak to a doctor** about the health problem you are trying to treat.
2. The clinician assesses whether a prescription medicine is appropriate.
3. If Ozempic is prescribed, you receive a prescription.
4. A **pharmacy** dispenses the medicine, subject to availability and the prescription.

There is no legitimate route in which a social-media seller, messaging-app account or unverified website replaces the prescription and pharmacy process.

## Do you need a prescription for Ozempic in Ireland?

Yes.

Medicines.ie lists Ozempic as **prescription only**, and the HSE says obesity medicines should be prescribed by a doctor or, for some obesity treatments, certain specialist nurses.

A website questionnaire or a payment page does not by itself make a medicine legitimate. What matters is whether there is a genuine clinical assessment, a lawful prescription and regulated pharmacy supply.

The HSE specifically warns people not to buy obesity medicines online because they cannot be sure the product is genuine or meets safety standards.

## Can you get Ozempic for weight loss in Ireland?

This is where the terminology becomes important.

The HSE describes:

| Product | Active ingredient | HSE-described role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ozempic** | Semaglutide | Treatment of type 2 diabetes |
| **Wegovy** | Semaglutide | Treatment of obesity |
| **Mounjaro** | Tirzepatide | Type 2 diabetes and adult weight management under relevant criteria |

Ozempic and Wegovy contain the same active ingredient, **semaglutide**, but they are different medicinal products with different authorised uses and product information.

If your main reason for seeking treatment is obesity or weight management, it is more useful to tell the doctor **what you are trying to treat** than to arrive assuming that Ozempic must be the answer.

A clinician can then consider the appropriate options.

## Who can get Wegovy for weight loss in Ireland?

According to current HSE patient guidance, Wegovy can be prescribed if an adult has:

- a **BMI of 30 or more**; or
- a **BMI of 27 or more** and at least one weight-related health problem, such as high blood pressure, pre-diabetes or high cholesterol.

These thresholds are **not an automatic prescription**.

The HSE says a doctor may also discuss health and weight history, BMI, obesity complications, other health conditions, medicines and possible side effects before starting obesity medicine.

## What happens when you ask your GP about Ozempic or weight-loss medication?

A useful consultation is broader than asking for a brand name.

The HSE says an obesity assessment may include:

- BMI;
- waist-to-height ratio where appropriate;
- weight-related health conditions;
- how weight affects health and daily life;
- changes in weight over time;
- sleep, diet and physical activity;
- medicines;
- family history; and
- other relevant health issues.

For Ozempic specifically, the key question is whether **type 2 diabetes** is being treated and whether semaglutide is appropriate in the context of the person's wider diabetes care.

For weight management, the conversation may instead include Wegovy, Mounjaro, other medicines, specialist services and non-medicine approaches.

## Can my GP prescribe Ozempic in Ireland?

A doctor can prescribe Ozempic when clinically appropriate. HSE patient guidance specifically says Ozempic can be prescribed by your doctor if you have **type 2 diabetes**.

That should not be read as meaning that every person with type 2 diabetes will receive Ozempic. Choice of treatment depends on the individual's clinical situation.

If the reason for the appointment is weight management rather than diabetes, HSE guidance distinguishes Ozempic from **Wegovy**, the semaglutide product for obesity.

## Is Ozempic covered by the Medical Card in Ireland?

The HSE currently states that Ozempic is available on the **Medical Card and Long-Term Illness Scheme if you have type 2 diabetes**.

That reimbursement position is different from the position for obesity medicines.

The HSE currently lists **Wegovy as not covered** by the Medical Card, Long-Term Illness Scheme or Drugs Payment Scheme. It also lists **Mounjaro as not covered** by those schemes for the obesity-treatment context described on its patient page.

Funding rules can change, so check the current HSE position rather than relying on an old social-media post or price article.

## Ozempic versus Wegovy: what is the difference?

The simplest distinction is **indication**.

Both contain semaglutide. The EMA describes Ozempic as a diabetes medicine for adults with insufficiently controlled type 2 diabetes.

HSE guidance describes Wegovy as semaglutide for obesity.

That means “I want semaglutide for weight management” and “I need treatment for type 2 diabetes” are different clinical questions even though the same active ingredient appears in both brands.

Do not interchange pens, doses or instructions based on something you have read online. Use the prescription and patient information for the exact product supplied.

## What about Mounjaro?

Mounjaro contains **tirzepatide**, not semaglutide.

Current HSE guidance says it can be prescribed to adults for weight management if they have a BMI of at least 30, or at least 27 with a weight-related health problem.

That does not make Mounjaro automatically preferable to Wegovy or appropriate for a particular person. Effectiveness, side effects, medical history, access, cost and follow-up all matter.

## Can you buy Ozempic online in Ireland?

Treat this as a medicine-safety question, not simply an ecommerce question.

The HSE warns people **not to buy obesity medicines online** because the product may be fake or may not meet safety standards.

The HPRA reported detaining **48,752 units of illegal GLP-1-type products in 2025**, up from 1,582 in 2024. The agency said many products were marketed as drops or microneedle patches, and tested samples of some detained patches did not contain the semaglutide claimed on the packaging.

A legitimate clinical service may use remote consultations in appropriate circumstances, but that does not make an unverified medicine seller legitimate. The important safeguards are a real prescriber, a lawful prescription and regulated pharmacy supply.

## What side effects should you know about?

The HSE lists common semaglutide side effects including:

- nausea;
- constipation;
- diarrhoea;
- heartburn and indigestion;
- stomach cramps;
- vomiting;
- headaches;
- fatigue; and
- low blood glucose when used with some other glucose-lowering medicines.

This is not a complete safety list, and suitability depends on the individual and the exact product.

If you are taking a GLP-1 medicine, use the patient leaflet supplied with it and speak to your prescriber or pharmacist about symptoms or concerns. Our [GLP-1 side-effects guide](/health/glp1-side-effects-when-to-seek-help/) separates common effects from symptoms that deserve urgent medical attention.

## Pregnancy, breastfeeding and other medicines matter

The HSE advises telling your doctor before taking an obesity medicine if you are trying to become pregnant, breastfeeding, have had an allergic reaction to medicines or take other medicines.

Prescription weight-management treatment is not something to start based only on BMI or an online calculator.

That is particularly important because GLP-1 and related medicines can interact with wider clinical circumstances that a website cannot assess.

## Should you calculate your BMI before the appointment?

You can, but treat it as preparation rather than self-diagnosis.

The HSE says BMI is one way to screen for weight-related risk but also states that **BMI alone does not measure health**. It cannot distinguish muscle from fat, and ethnicity can affect the BMI level at which health risks increase.

## A practical checklist before you book

If you are considering a consultation about weight or diabetes treatment, it can help to know:

- your current medicines and supplements;
- relevant diagnoses;
- recent weight history;
- what outcome you actually want to improve;
- whether cost and long-term affordability matter;
- whether you are pregnant, breastfeeding or planning pregnancy; and
- what follow-up the provider offers after the first prescription.

The aim is not to pass a test to obtain a particular brand. It is to give the clinician enough information to discuss an appropriate and sustainable treatment plan.

### Can I get Ozempic for weight loss?

Ozempic's authorised role is type 2 diabetes. HSE guidance identifies Wegovy, another semaglutide product, for obesity treatment. Discuss the health problem and treatment options with a prescriber rather than assuming the products are interchangeable.

HSE guidance includes tirzepatide, sold as Mounjaro, as a prescription option for adults meeting the relevant weight-management criteria.
