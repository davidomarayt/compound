Ireland is aiming to allow households to use plug-in solar by **the end of June 2027**, provided the necessary electrical safety and regulatory measures are in place. The *Irish Independent* reported on 22 November that the rollout is expected next summer and that kits are already being marketed to Irish households. [Selfbuild’s coverage of Energy Minister Darragh O’Brien’s timeline](https://selfbuild.ie/news/plug-in-solar-panels-could-be-available-in-ireland-by-next-summer/) gives the end of June as the target.

The appeal is the lower upfront cost and the possibility of using solar in a flat or rented home with a suitable outdoor space. In Compound’s worked example, a **€550 kit saves €63 to €126 a year** depending on how much electricity the household uses as it is generated. At those savings, simple payback ranges from about **4.4 to 8.7 years**.

## Can you use plug-in solar in Ireland today?

As of 22 November 2026, Ireland’s proposed route for connecting a solar kit to an ordinary household socket is still in development. The existing route for domestic grid-connected microgeneration involves a registered electrical contractor and an NC6 notification to [ESB Networks](https://www.esbnetworks.ie/services/get-connected/renewable-connection/micro-generation). Wait for the Irish connection rules before buying a kit for use in a domestic socket.

At a July [Oireachtas committee hearing](https://voterconnect.ie/proceedings/joint_committee_on_climate_environment_and_energy/2026-07-15/dbsect_2), officials discussed an **800 W output limit**, product standards and a simplified registration process. The final rules will determine which products qualify and what households will have to do before connecting one. An inverter’s output in watts measures power at a moment in time. Annual generation in kilowatt-hours measures how much energy the kit produces over a year.

## How much could plug-in solar save on a bill?

A small kit typically combines solar panels and a microinverter. The electricity it produces can offset demand from appliances already running during daylight hours. Each kilowatt-hour you use as it is generated saves the price you would have paid for that unit. The standing charge stays on the bill.

The table assumes **€550 upfront, 450 kWh generated a year and an avoided electricity rate of 35c/kWh**. Those are modelling inputs. Irish retail prices and generation will vary by kit and location. The example assigns zero value to surplus electricity and includes no grant.

| Share of generation used at home | Grid electricity avoided each year | Annual bill saving | Simple payback |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 40% | 180 kWh | €63 | 8.7 years |
| 60% | 270 kWh | €94.50 | 5.8 years |
| 80% | 360 kWh | €126 | 4.4 years |

Simple payback divides the upfront cost by the annual saving. It omits tariff changes, repairs and financing. At 60% self-consumption, using a rate of 28c/kWh instead of 35c/kWh lowers the annual saving to **€75.60** and stretches payback to about **7.3 years**. A shaded site can lengthen payback further by reducing annual generation.

If you have a smart meter, look at electricity use during daylight hours on a typical working day. An apartment that uses little electricity until evening could capture a smaller share of the output than a home with daytime demand. For a quote, ask for an annual generation estimate that reflects your balcony’s direction, tilt and shade.

## Will a plug-in kit qualify for export payments or a grant?

The [Clean Export Guarantee](https://www.cru.ie/consumer-information/microgeneration/) pays eligible microgenerators for electricity they export. How those arrangements will apply to plug-in kits has yet to be confirmed. The [SEAI domestic solar grant](https://www.seai.ie/grants/home-energy-grants/individual-grants/solar-electricity-grant) has its own application and installation requirements. Base any payback estimate on bill savings until the authorities confirm how these kits will be treated.

## What should renters and apartment owners check?

Check your tenancy or building rules for permission to attach panels to a balcony, railing or exterior wall. Ask the landlord or owners’ management company where applicable. A sunny outdoor space and a safe mounting location matter just as much as the kit price. Once Irish rules are published, check whether the specific kit and proposed connection meet them.
