## What this calculator is for

Switching a mortgage can reduce the interest rate, but the decision should be based on the full economics rather than the headline rate alone. This tool compares monthly repayment, switching costs, break fees, cashback, term and estimated lifetime cost.

## How to read the result

Start by comparing the current and alternative mortgages over the **same remaining term**. If you then lengthen the new term, recognise that a lower monthly payment may partly come from paying the mortgage for longer.

## Worked example

If switching costs net of cashback are €1,200 and the monthly repayment falls by €100, simple cash-flow break-even is about 12 months. But a longer term can still increase total interest, so break-even should not be the only metric.

## Irish context

CCPC recommends comparing rates, costs and conditions when switching. A borrower leaving a fixed rate should ask the existing lender for the actual break-fee quote. A current property valuation can also matter because lower LTV can unlock different pricing.

## What can change the answer

The outcome changes with the new rate, new term, break fee, legal/valuation costs and cashback. Future rate changes matter too, especially when a new fixed period expires or a variable rate is selected.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not let cashback dominate the decision. Do not compare a 20-year current mortgage with a 30-year alternative and conclude the lower payment alone means the switch is cheaper. And do not cancel mortgage protection before replacement arrangements are confirmed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a lower monthly payment always better?
No.

### Does the tool include future rate changes?
No.

### Can I switch while fixed?
Possibly, but a break fee may apply.

### Should I ask my current lender for another rate first?
Yes. A product switch with the same lender can be worth comparing with a full lender move.

## Compare like with like before changing the term

The cleanest switching comparison keeps the **remaining mortgage term the same** and changes only the rate and costs. That reveals the value of the rate reduction without hiding anything inside a longer repayment period.

After that, you can deliberately test a longer term if cash-flow relief is one of your goals. The calculator then helps quantify what you are buying: lower monthly payments in exchange for potentially more years of interest.

APRC can also be useful when comparing lender offers because it is designed to reflect the overall annual cost of credit under specified assumptions. It is not a perfect forecast, but it can expose products where a low headline fixed rate sits alongside fees or later pricing.

If the switch is mainly driven by cashback, rerun the model with cashback set to zero. If the new mortgage still looks attractive, the underlying economics are stronger rather than depending on a one-off incentive.

## Method and limitations

The tool calculates scheduled repayments under both rate/term sets, adds switching costs and break fee, subtracts cashback, and estimates break-even from the monthly saving. It assumes each entered rate remains constant.
