**Investing in Ireland 101 · Chapter 9 of 9**

[← How much to invest each month](/wealth/how-much-to-invest-per-month-ireland/) · [Course hub](/wealth/how-to-start-investing-in-ireland/)

Building the portfolio is the beginning.

Maintaining it should be boring.

A good long-term system does not require a new opinion every morning. It requires enough maintenance to keep the portfolio aligned with the goal while avoiding unnecessary tax, fees and emotional decisions.

## What actually needs to be reviewed?

Five things:

1. the goal;
2. the contribution;
3. the asset allocation;
4. the cost;
5. the records.

Market headlines are not on the list.

## 1. Has the goal changed?

The same portfolio can become inappropriate without any investment changing.

Examples:

- retirement moves ten years closer;
- a house purchase becomes likely;
- a child will need education funding sooner;
- you start a business;
- your income becomes less secure.

The portfolio exists to serve the goal.

When the goal changes, revisit the risk.

## 2. Has your ability to contribute changed?

A pay rise, job loss, new mortgage or childcare cost can change the sustainable monthly contribution.

Do not judge the plan only by portfolio performance.

Contribution rate is one of the few variables you directly control.

Use the [Regular Savings Calculator](/regular-savings-calculator/) or [Compound Interest Calculator](/compound-interest-calculator/) to see what a contribution change means over the remaining horizon.

## 3. Has the portfolio drifted?

Suppose your target was:

- 70% equities;
- 30% bonds.

After equities rise strongly, the portfolio becomes:

- 82% equities;
- 18% bonds.

You now have a riskier portfolio than you deliberately selected.

That is **portfolio drift**.

## Rebalancing

Rebalancing means moving the portfolio back toward its intended allocation.

There are several methods.

### Calendar rebalancing

Review on a schedule, such as annually.

Advantage: simple.

Disadvantage: you may trade even when the allocation has barely moved.

### Threshold rebalancing

Act only if an allocation moves beyond a defined tolerance.

Advantage: responds to meaningful drift.

Disadvantage: requires monitoring.

### Contribution rebalancing

Direct new money toward the underweight part of the portfolio.

Advantage: can reduce the need to sell in a taxable account.

Disadvantage: may be slow for large portfolios.

The best method is the one you can implement consistently while accounting for tax and transaction costs.

## Rebalancing is not market timing

Market timing asks:

> “What will outperform next?”

Rebalancing asks:

> “Has my portfolio moved away from the risk I originally chose?”

They are different.

Rebalancing can require selling some of what has risen and adding to what has lagged. That can feel uncomfortable because recent winners are usually the investments receiving the most positive attention.

## Tax-aware rebalancing in Ireland

Selling in a taxable account can create a CGT event for direct shares or a chargeable event under another tax regime.

Before selling purely to rebalance, consider whether you can:

- redirect new contributions;
- rebalance inside a pension first;
- use cash flows;
- or make smaller changes.

Tax should not prevent necessary risk management, but it should be included in the cost.

## 4. Review fees every year

Fees change.

Providers introduce new pricing.

Portfolio size grows.

A fee that was acceptable at €20,000 may look very different at €300,000.

Once a year, record:

- fund expense ratios;
- platform fees;
- adviser fees;
- pension charges;
- FX costs;
- trading costs;
- transfer charges.

Use the [Investment Fee Calculator](/investment-fee-calculator/) if you are considering a lower-cost alternative.

## 5. Maintain tax records continuously

Do not make “reconstruct eight years of trades” a future project.

For each transaction, record:

- date;
- security;
- quantity;
- euro cost/proceeds;
- fees;
- exchange rate or euro conversion;
- dividends/distributions;
- withholding tax;
- tax category;
- and supporting statement.

For investments subject to an eight-year deemed-disposal regime, tracking acquisition dates is particularly important.

## A simple annual portfolio review

Set one date each year.

Then answer:

| Question | Yes/No |
|---|---|
| Is the goal unchanged? |  |
| Is the target date unchanged? |  |
| Is emergency cash still adequate? |  |
| Is expensive debt under control? |  |
| Are pension/employer benefits being used as intended? |  |
| Is the asset allocation still close to target? |  |
| Has any company/sector/country become too large? |  |
| Have total fees changed? |  |
| Have Irish tax rules changed? |  |
| Are all records complete? |  |
| Is the contribution still sustainable? |  |

Then make only the changes the answers require.

## When should you check the portfolio?

You can check prices as often as you like.

But information is not the same as action.

Frequent checking can create a false sense that good investing requires frequent decisions.

For a long-term investor, a periodic structured review is usually more useful than reacting to every price move.

## What should trigger an unscheduled review?

A genuine life change:

- job loss;
- new job;
- marriage;
- child;
- house purchase;
- inheritance;
- business launch;
- serious change in income;
- major pension change;
- approaching the goal date.

Those can change your risk capacity.

A television headline usually does not.

## What if one investment rises dramatically?

Imagine one share grows from 5% to 25% of the portfolio.

The company may still be excellent.

But the portfolio is now far more dependent on one outcome.

Ask:

> If I had cash today, would I deliberately build a portfolio with 25% in this company?

If the answer is no, concentration deserves attention.

Tax can influence how quickly the position is reduced, but “it went up” is not itself a diversification strategy.

## What if the portfolio falls 30%?

Return to the plan.

Check:

- Did the investment thesis change?
- Did the goal date change?
- Did your cash needs change?
- Is the portfolio more concentrated than you thought?
- Are you taking more risk than you can tolerate?

If the only change is the market price, remember that volatility was part of the risk assumed when the portfolio was built.

That does not mean “never sell”. It means separate **price movement** from **plan failure**.

## Review tax rules, not just investments

Irish tax rules can change.

The investment-fund individual rate changed from 41% to 38% from 1 January 2026.

That is a reminder that a long-term portfolio needs an occasional tax review even if no investment changes.

Record the source and date when you make tax assumptions.

## Review the pension alongside the brokerage account

Your portfolio is not only the account you enjoy looking at.

Include:

- occupational pension;
- PRSA;
- MyFutureFund where relevant;
- taxable investments;
- cash;
- significant company shares;
- investment property;
- and concentrated business interests.

Household-level diversification matters more than making one small brokerage account look perfectly balanced.

## The “do nothing” decision

A review does not need to produce a trade.

Sometimes the correct outcome is:

- goal unchanged;
- allocation acceptable;
- fees reasonable;
- records complete;
- contributions continuing.

Result:

**do nothing.**

That is a legitimate portfolio-management decision.

## The Investing in Ireland 101 system

The full process is:

**Prepare → choose wrapper → understand investments → understand tax → diversify → choose provider → control fees → automate contributions → maintain.**

There is no secret tenth chapter containing the winning stock.

The edge is a system you can continue using when markets are boring, euphoric and frightening.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I rebalance my portfolio?

There is no universal schedule. Periodic or threshold-based reviews can both work. Tax and transaction costs should be considered before trading.

### Should I check my investments every day?

Daily checking is not required for long-term investing. A structured review of goals, risk, allocation, fees and records is usually more useful.

### What records should an Irish investor keep?

Keep transaction dates, costs, proceeds, fees, currency conversions, dividends, withholding tax, product details and tax-relevant dates such as fund acquisition lots.

### When should I change my investment strategy?

A meaningful change in goals, time horizon, finances or risk capacity is a stronger reason to review than short-term market news.
