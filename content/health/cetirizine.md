**Cetirizine is an antihistamine that can ease sneezing, a runny nose and itchy or watery eyes caused by allergies.** It can also relieve symptoms of chronic hives. Zirtek is one brand listed in Ireland. Cetirizine can cause drowsiness in some people. [HPRA Irish product information](https://www.hpra.ie/find-a-medicine/for-human-use/authorised-medicines/details/22531) · [HSE antihistamine guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antihistamines/)

This guide covers adults in Ireland. Use the leaflet for your exact product. Sources checked 19 November 2026.

## The Science Behind the Drug

**Cetirizine blocks the action of histamine at the H1 receptor.** When the immune system reacts to something like pollen, cells release histamine. Histamine signals through H1 receptors on cells in tissues such as the nose, eyes and skin. That signalling contributes to itching, sneezing, watery eyes and raised patches of skin in hives. [HSE allergic rhinitis guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/allergic-rhinitis/allergic-rhinitis-treatment/) · [HSE hives guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/hives/)

Cetirizine binds to H1 receptors and reduces their response to histamine. Its principal target is **peripheral H1 receptors**, meaning receptors outside the brain and spinal cord. This helps explain why it can relieve several allergy symptoms at once. Drowsiness is still possible, so it’s worth seeing how the medicine affects you before you drive. [HPRA prescribing information, sections 4.7–4.8 and 5.1](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/22531/Licence_PA0891-008-002_02102023122152.pdf)

## Can you buy cetirizine without a prescription in Ireland?

For **Zirtek 10 mg tablets**, the HPRA lists pharmacy sale without a prescription in packs of up to seven tablets. Larger packs of that product require a prescription. A pharmacist can check the available options and whether cetirizine suits you. [HPRA Irish supply conditions](https://www.hpra.ie/find-a-medicine/for-human-use/authorised-medicines/details/22531)

The cited 10 mg tablet leaflet gives one tablet once daily as the usual adult dose. Check the instructions for your exact product. **Ask your pharmacist or GP first if you have kidney disease**, as it can affect which dose or medicine is suitable. [HPRA patient leaflet](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/22531/9e1e2fe3-b2cd-4538-ac45-bb326fa7398e.pdf)

## Can cetirizine make you drowsy?

**Yes.** The tablet leaflet lists sleepiness as a common side effect. Avoid driving or operating machinery if you feel sleepy or less alert. Check with your pharmacist if you also take medicine that can make you sleepy, and avoid alcohol while taking cetirizine. [HPRA patient leaflet](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/22531/9e1e2fe3-b2cd-4538-ac45-bb326fa7398e.pdf) · [HSE antihistamine guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antihistamines/)

Headache, dizziness, tiredness and dry mouth are also listed. Check with a pharmacist or GP if side effects persist or if you’re pregnant, breastfeeding or taking other medicines. [HPRA patient leaflet](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/22531/9e1e2fe3-b2cd-4538-ac45-bb326fa7398e.pdf)

## When should you get medical help?

**Call 112 or 999 if you suspect a severe allergic reaction**, for example if there’s sudden throat or tongue swelling, trouble breathing or fainting after contact with an allergen. Use an adrenaline auto-injector immediately if one is available. Cetirizine should never delay adrenaline or an emergency call. [HSE anaphylaxis guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/anaphylaxis/)

## Why it helps some allergy symptoms more than others

Histamine signalling is prominent in itch, sneezing and hives. Cetirizine reduces the effect of histamine at H1 receptors, so these symptoms may respond even if you still have a blocked nose. Nasal blockage can reflect a broader inflammatory response. If you need advice on the next step, separate the symptoms and report which ones actually changed. A pharmacist can compare the target of cetirizine with a [nasal steroid](/health/medicines/steroid-nasal-sprays/) or another option. [HSE allergic rhinitis guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/allergic-rhinitis/)

## Check alertness and the exact pack

Even when a medicine is described as less sedating, the first several times you take it can reveal how you personally respond. Check the leaflet for advice on driving and alcohol. Report sleepiness separately from relief of itching or sneezing, especially if you use sleep aids or other products for colds and allergy. The pharmacist can look for duplicate antihistamines on the ingredient lists. [HSE antihistamine guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antihistamines/)

If you have kidney disease, ask whether it changes the suitability or instructions for the specific product. Bring the pack if you are using a liquid or a different strength than you used before. Hives with breathing difficulty, throat swelling or collapse needs emergency help. [HSE antihistamine guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/antihistamines/)
