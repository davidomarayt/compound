## At a glance

Prednisolone is a corticosteroid used to control inflammation or immune activity in many conditions. The dose, duration and whether it needs tapering are specific to the prescription. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/prednisolone/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Glucocorticoid receptors and why treatment length matters

Once inside a cell, prednisolone binds the glucocorticoid receptor. The resulting complex changes gene regulation and reduces inflammatory signalling. This broad action can aid treatment while also changing blood glucose, immune responses and the body’s own cortisol production. The risks accumulate differently with different treatment exposures. [DailyMed source](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=35f0b197-c3ae-445a-8da7-9ffcd4472e05)

## What the evidence measures

Track the symptoms or clinical measures relevant to the condition. Ask about a stopping plan for treatment that has lasted long enough to affect adrenal function. For repeated courses, the combined exposure may be important.

Glucocorticoid effects on glucose can matter even when the inflammation is improving. A clinician can explain whether glucose monitoring is relevant and what pattern to report. Keep any glucose records with the course dates so changes can be interpreted in context.

## Put the course in context

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Original problem | symptom or test the course aims to improve |
| Exposure | note each recent course and other steroid products |


## Why should I list inhalers and creams too?

Each product contributes differently to steroid exposure. The prescriber can use the complete list when assessing repeated courses, interactions and whether additional precautions apply.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Follow the prescriber’s stopping instructions and ask whether you need a steroid card or emergency advice for illness. Seek advice about severe infection symptoms or major mood changes. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/prednisolone/)
