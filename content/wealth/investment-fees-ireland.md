**Investing in Ireland 101 · Chapter 7 of 9**

[← Choosing a broker](/wealth/choose-investment-broker-ireland/) · [Course hub](/wealth/how-to-start-investing-in-ireland/) · [Next: How much to invest →](/wealth/how-much-to-invest-per-month-ireland/)

A 1% annual fee sounds small.

Over thirty years, it can represent six figures.

That is because fees compound too—only in the wrong direction.

Every euro removed from the portfolio is a euro that cannot earn future returns.

## The main investment costs

The CCPC identifies several common categories of investment charges, including entry, exit, ongoing management and performance charges.

For a modern Irish investor, the practical fee stack can include:

| Fee | Where it appears |
|---|---|
| Fund ongoing charge | Inside ETF/fund |
| Platform fee | Broker, adviser or investment platform |
| Adviser fee | Initial and/or ongoing advice |
| Dealing commission | Each purchase/sale |
| FX conversion | Buying foreign-currency assets |
| Bid–ask spread | Market trading friction |
| Custody fee | Holding assets |
| Performance fee | Some active funds |
| Transfer/exit fee | Leaving provider |
| Pension policy charge | Some pension structures |

The headline fund fee is only one layer.

## The 30-year fee example

Assume:

- €500 invested at the end of every month;
- 30 years;
- 7% gross annual return;
- the stated fee reduces the annual return;
- no tax;
- no inflation;
- smooth returns.

The results are illustrative, not forecasts.

| Annual fee | Illustrative ending value |
|---|---:|
| 0% | €609,985 |
| 0.25% | €580,733 |
| 1.00% | €502,258 |
| 2.00% | €416,129 |

The difference between 0% and 1% is about **€107,727**.

The difference between 0.25% and 1% is about **€78,475**.

Small percentages can represent large amounts when applied to a growing portfolio every year.

Use the [Investment Fee Calculator](/investment-fee-calculator/) for your own assumptions.

## Why the fee gap gets larger with time

In year one, a 1% fee on a small portfolio may not look important.

But as the portfolio grows, the euro fee grows.

You also lose the future growth that the deducted fee would have earned.

That is the second-order cost of fees.

## Cheap does not automatically mean good

The objective is not to minimise fees at all costs.

You may reasonably pay for:

- regulated financial advice;
- tax planning;
- financial planning;
- behavioural support;
- a pension wrapper;
- a specialised service;
- or administration you genuinely value.

The question is:

> **What am I getting for this recurring charge?**

A 1% advice fee that materially improves planning and behaviour is a different proposition from a hidden 1% platform charge that adds no value.

## Fund fee versus platform fee

Suppose a fund costs 0.20% a year.

The platform charges 0.50%.

The total visible recurring cost is already 0.70% before:

- adviser fees;
- trading;
- FX;
- spreads;
- or tax.

Always add the layers.

## Percentage fees become more expensive as you succeed

A 1% fee on €10,000 is €100 a year.

A 1% fee on €500,000 is €5,000 a year.

The service may not be fifty times more complex just because the portfolio is fifty times larger.

That does not make percentage fees inherently wrong. It means the euro amount should be reviewed as the portfolio grows.

## FX fees: the recurring leak

Assume you invest €500 monthly into a dollar-priced asset.

At a 1% currency conversion cost:

- €5 is lost each month;
- €60 is lost each year;
- before considering the return those euros could have earned.

If dividends are later converted back to euro with another FX charge, the friction can occur repeatedly.

## Trading commissions and small contributions

Fixed commissions matter most when contribution sizes are small.

A €2 commission on a €50 purchase is 4%.

A €2 commission on a €2,000 purchase is 0.1%.

That can influence whether it makes sense to invest weekly, monthly or less frequently.

Do not optimise the schedule around fees alone, but do the arithmetic.

## Bid–ask spread

When an ETF has:

- buy price: €100.10
- sell price: €99.90

the €0.20 difference is part of the trading cost.

Highly liquid securities often have tighter spreads, while niche products can be wider.

The spread is not usually shown as a separate “fee” on the statement, but it affects the effective price.

## Active management fees

An active fund charging more than an index fund must overcome a larger cost hurdle before the investor receives the same net return.

The relevant question is not whether an active manager had a strong recent year.

It is whether the higher expected cost is justified by the service and strategy you are buying.

## Advice fees

Ask advisers to express fees in both:

- percentage terms;
- and euro terms.

For example:

0.75% of €400,000 = €3,000 a year.

Then ask:

- What is included?
- Does the fee fall as assets grow?
- Are fund/platform fees additional?
- Is there an initial planning fee?
- Is there a minimum fee?
- What happens if you stop the service?

Clarity is the goal.

## Pension fees

A pension can have strong tax advantages and still be expensive.

Check:

- contribution allocation;
- annual management charge;
- policy fee;
- adviser remuneration;
- fund fee;
- switching cost;
- transfer cost.

Compare the pension's after-fee value rather than assuming the tax wrapper makes costs irrelevant.

## The fee hierarchy

When comparing two otherwise similar options, look in this order:

**1. Recurring percentage fees**  
They compound for as long as the portfolio exists.

**2. FX costs**  
They can repeat with every contribution and dividend.

**3. Platform/custody charges**  
Often recurring.

**4. Trading costs and spreads**  
Depend on frequency.

**5. One-off fees**  
Can still matter but do not compound every year.

## When switching for lower fees can backfire

Do not switch solely because another platform looks cheaper.

Consider:

- exit charges;
- tax consequences of selling;
- transfer fees;
- time out of the market;
- loss of pension guarantees;
- different custody arrangements;
- and administrative burden.

A lower annual fee can be worth pursuing, but calculate the transition cost.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 1% a high investment fee?

It depends on what the 1% includes. As a recurring percentage of assets, it can have a very large long-term effect. Compare the euro cost and the service received.

### What is an ETF expense ratio?

It is an annual fund-level cost expressed as a percentage of assets. It is only one part of the investor's total cost.

### Are zero-commission trades free?

Not necessarily. FX, spreads, platform fees and fund charges can still apply.

### Should I always choose the cheapest fund?

No. Cost is important, but the investment must also match the goal, risk, diversification and tax requirements.
