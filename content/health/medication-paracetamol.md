**Paracetamol is a medicine used to relieve mild to moderate pain and reduce fever.** It is the active ingredient in many products, including some Panadol formulations and combination cold-and-flu medicines.

## What is paracetamol used for?

Paracetamol can help with headaches, aches and pains, and fever. It is not generally used for the anti-inflammatory effect associated with NSAIDs such as ibuprofen.

## Why does the dose matter?

Taking too much paracetamol can cause severe liver damage. A major risk is unknowingly taking two products containing paracetamol at the same time, such as a painkiller and a cold remedy. **If you may have taken more than directed, seek urgent medical advice even if you have no symptoms.**

## Who should check with a pharmacist?

People with liver or kidney problems, those who regularly drink substantial amounts of alcohol, and anyone taking other medicines should check suitability and dosing with a professional. Children's doses are product- and age-dependent: never infer them from an adult tablet dose.

## Possible side effects

Paracetamol is generally well tolerated when taken correctly, but allergic reactions and other serious adverse effects are possible. Seek urgent help for difficulty breathing, swelling of the face or throat, or a severe skin reaction.

## How much should I take?

Follow the instructions on your exact pack or the advice of your pharmacist or prescriber. Formulations, strengths, age limits and dosing instructions differ.

## Paracetamol vs ibuprofen

Paracetamol treats pain and fever; ibuprofen also reduces inflammation but has different stomach, kidney and cardiovascular precautions. See [ibuprofen vs paracetamol](/health/medications/ibuprofen-vs-paracetamol/).

## Official Irish medicine information

See the [HPRA paracetamol 500 mg product record](https://www.hpra.ie/find-a-medicine/for-human-use/authorised-medicines/details/27543) for an approved patient leaflet. The leaflet applies to that particular product.
