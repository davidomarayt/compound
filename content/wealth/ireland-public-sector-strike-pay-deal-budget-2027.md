Ireland has set aside **€1.2 billion** in Budget 2027 to support a new public service pay agreement. A teacher or nurse trying to budget for January still has two unanswered questions. How much will their salary rise, and from which payslip?

Those questions took on fresh urgency on Monday. Public service unions are preparing a **24-hour stoppage on Thursday, 12 November**, the day European leaders are due in Dublin for the European Political Community meeting. A deal could still avert the strike. Fórsa said unions would meet the Government-appointed group on Tuesday, 3 November, while preparations for rallies in Dublin and elsewhere continue.

## What a percentage means on a payslip

Union negotiators say workers have lost purchasing power since 2021. That distinction matters at the kitchen table. A pay rise matching next year’s inflation would help a salary keep pace from here. It would leave any earlier gap in place.

Take a worker earning **€45,000**. An illustrative 2% rise adds €900 to annual basic pay, or **€75 a month before deductions**. Start it in January and the worker earns the full €900 extra that calendar year. Start it in July and they earn €450 extra that year, even though their salary reaches the same €45,900 annual rate. These figures illustrate the timing effect. No 2% settlement has been agreed.

How the increase is distributed matters too. A flat €900 adds 3% to a €30,000 salary and 1.5% to a €60,000 salary. A straight 2% rise gives those same workers €600 and €1,200 respectively. Two deals can therefore cost the same across those two jobs and produce quite different outcomes for each household.

## Why the Department of Finance will watch the start date

The €1.2 billion is a provision for 2027. The eventual agreement will determine how much reaches payroll during that year and what the State owes the following January. In the July example, the employer pays €450 extra in year one and €900 in the first full year, even without another increase. A one-off €450 payment would look identical in the first year’s accounts but add nothing to the next year’s basic salary.

The strike itself poses a different financial question. If a school closes for a day, a parent may have to pay for childcare or give up a shift. A postponed clinic appointment may mean taking a second morning off work. The cost turns on which services operate and what arrangements families can make.

When a proposal emerges, the figures worth looking for are the increase at each salary level, its effective date and the annual cost once every increase is in place. Together they will show what a worker can put in the household budget and what the State must carry into the next one.
