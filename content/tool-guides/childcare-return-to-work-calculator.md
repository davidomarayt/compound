## What this calculator is for

This calculator compares the household cash-flow effect of returning to paid work with the childcare and work-related costs that come with it. It is not a statement about the value of work or caregiving; it isolates the financial side of the decision.

## How to read the result

The headline result should be read as **incremental household cash flow**. Start with the additional take-home pay, subtract childcare after any modelled National Childcare Scheme support, then subtract commuting and other work costs.

## Worked example

If returning to work adds €2,200 a month of take-home pay, childcare after support costs €1,200 and commuting/other work costs €300, the immediate cash-flow gain is about €700 a month before pension value, career progression or other long-term effects.

## Irish context

Ireland's National Childcare Scheme provides universal and income-assessed subsidies subject to eligibility and hourly limits. The exact subsidy can depend on child age, hours, provider participation and household circumstances, so the calculator's entered rate is a scenario rather than an entitlement decision.

## What can change the answer

Tax, pension contributions, NCS subsidy, number of children, childcare hours, commuting and part-time versus full-time work can change the result dramatically. Long-term career earnings and pension accumulation are not fully captured by a one-year cash-flow comparison.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not compare gross salary with childcare cost. Use take-home pay. Do not ignore commuting, lunches, work clothing or reduced eligibility for means-tested supports. But also do not ignore employer pension contributions or long-term career effects simply because they do not appear in this month's bank balance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a negative first-year cash flow proof I should not work?
No. Financial cash flow is only one dimension.

### Does the calculator decide NCS eligibility?
No.

### Should employer pension contributions count?
They are economically valuable even though they are not immediate cash.

### What about career progression?
That is difficult to quantify and is outside the simple monthly comparison.

## Method and limitations

The tool estimates take-home income under its tax assumptions, subtracts entered childcare and work costs, and compares the result with the alternative scenario. It does not value career progression, unpaid care, employment rights or every social-welfare interaction.
