**Moving abroad would not close your PIA.** Under the rules the Dáil passed on 25 November, you keep the account and your investments. Three things change while you live outside Ireland: you cannot pay any new money in, you cannot switch provider, and the Irish 1% yearly charge on value above €50,000 keeps running. When you move back, you tell your provider and can start contributing again.

PIA is the common name. The Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 calls it an *Individual Investment Account* (IIA). The rules for moving abroad are in a new section 747M, which the Bill calls a **change of residence**.

**Status, checked 28 November 2026:** these are proposed rules. The Dáil passed the Bill on 25 November and the official "as passed by Dáil Éireann" text is now published. The Seanad stages and signing into law are still to come, and the Seanad could still change it. No provider is taking applications. The 1 July 2027 opening target depends on enactment and on providers being ready. Follow the [PIA launch tracker](/pia/#pia-launch-tracker) for each confirmed step.

## You must be Irish tax resident to open one

A **qualifying individual** is someone who, *at the time of opening* the account, is 18 or older, is resident in Ireland for tax purposes and has a PPS number (747H). When you open the account you sign a declaration saying you are tax resident. It includes an undertaking to tell the provider "without undue delay" if anything in it stops being correct (747O(1)(d)).

So you cannot open a PIA from abroad. But residence is tested at opening. Nothing in the Bill closes an existing account because you later leave.

## When your "period of non-residence" starts

Section 747M sets the start date in one of two ways. It depends on whether you will still count as Irish resident for the tax year you leave in.

- **You leave and will not be Irish resident for that tax year.** The period starts on the day you first leave with that intention.
- **You leave but will still be Irish resident for that tax year.** This can happen when you have already spent enough days here. The period starts on **1 January of the next tax year**.

The period ends on the day you come back to live here, if you intend to be resident for the tax year after the one you return in.

You must notify your provider in writing, in a form Revenue regulations will set. In the first case you do this soon after the period begins. In the second, you do it soon after you leave. Providers then report the start and end dates to Revenue in their yearly return (747T).

## What stops while you are abroad

- **No new contributions** (747M(5)(a)). The provider must stop accepting them **within 30 days** of the "relevant date". That is the earlier of the date you gave as the start of your non-residence and the date the provider found out (747M(6)).
- **Money paid in during that 30-day window still counts as valid** (747M(8)). This covers a standing order that goes out just after you leave.
- **Later payments are treated as never made.** The provider must pay them back to you. If the money has already been invested, you get the market value on the day of repayment, minus reasonable selling costs (747M(9)). A provider that accepts them without reasonable excuse faces a **€3,000 penalty** (747M(10)).
- **No switching provider** (747M(5)(b)). The only exception is if your provider is leaving the market.

## What does not stop

**Withdrawals.** Section 747M does not restrict taking money out.

**The Irish investment account tax.** Section 747Q charges the tax on a qualifying individual who holds an account on 31 December. The definition looks at your residence when you opened it, not later. The charge is 1% of the account's **average daily value above €50,000**, and the provider still works it out and collects it. Nothing in the Bill text switches that charge off while you live abroad.

**Your new country's tax rules.** The Bill only sets Irish tax. The country you move to may tax the account's income or gains its own way. It may not recognise the PIA's special treatment, and a tax treaty may or may not help. This article does not cover any other country's rules. Get advice there before you go.

## Worked examples

These are hypothetical. They use full tax years after the planned 2027 launch. They assume the Dáil-passed text becomes law unchanged.

### Example 1: a standing order after you leave

Aoife pays €500 into her PIA on the 5th of every month. She moves to Australia on 1 September 2029 and does not expect to be Irish resident for 2029. Her period of non-residence starts on **1 September**. She writes to her provider on 10 September giving that date, so the relevant date is **1 September** and the 30-day window runs to **1 October**.

| Payment | Within 30 days of 1 September? | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 5 September, €500 | Yes | Valid contribution, stays invested |
| 5 October, €500 (if the provider lets it through) | No | Treated as never made; repaid at market value less selling costs |

The lesson: cancel the standing order before you go, and notify the provider promptly.

### Example 2: the charge keeps running

Aoife's account has an average daily value of **€80,000** in 2030, while she lives in Australia. The Irish charge is 1% × (€80,000 − €50,000) = **€300**. Her provider collects it, as it would if she lived in Cork. If her average value were **€45,000**, the Irish charge would be **€0**. Either way, Australia's own tax treatment is a separate question.

### Example 3: leaving late in the year

Ciarán leaves for Canada on 20 October 2029. He has been in Ireland long enough to be Irish resident for 2029, but he does not expect to be resident for 2030. His period of non-residence starts on **1 January 2030**. Under this rule, he can keep contributing until the end of 2029, up to the usual €12,000 for the year. He should still notify his provider soon after leaving.

### Coming home

Ciarán returns in 2033, intending to be resident for 2034. He notifies his provider. The provider can accept contributions **from the date of that notice** (747M(7)), up to €12,000 a year as normal. The Bill does not let him carry forward the years he missed.

## What could still change

The Seanad has not yet debated the Bill. Revenue regulations under section 747AA will set the form of the notices. Revenue's own guidance on how residence intentions are judged has not been published. Until the Act is signed and commenced, treat every rule here as proposed.
