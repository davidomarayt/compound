## At a glance

Nasal corticosteroids reduce inflammation in conditions such as allergic rhinitis. Correct use involves both the individual product and delivery to the lining of the nose. [HSE guide](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-nasal-sprays/)

## The Science Behind the Drug

### Inflammation at the nasal lining and technique

The spray deposits corticosteroid onto the nasal lining, where it activates glucocorticoid receptors. Changes in inflammatory gene signalling reduce swelling and other symptoms over time. This is a different mechanism from fast-acting decongestants that narrow blood vessels. [HSE source](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-nasal-sprays/)

## What the evidence measures

Track congestion, sneezing, sleep and nosebleeds over time. Check the prescribed technique and use schedule before interpreting an incomplete response.

## Two points for a spray technique check

| What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Direction | demonstrate where you aim the nozzle |
| Pattern | record how regularly you use it and which symptoms persist |


## Why discuss technique before changing products?

The way a spray is aimed affects delivery and irritation. A technique check and a record of symptoms can help distinguish delivery issues from limited treatment response.

## When to ask for help or a medication review

Ask a pharmacist to check technique if nosebleeds or irritation recur. Refer to product-specific guidance for children, pregnancy and use alongside other steroid products. [HSE patient guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/medicines/steroid-nasal-sprays/)
