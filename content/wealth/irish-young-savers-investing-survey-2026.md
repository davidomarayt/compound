**Almost three-quarters of young people surveyed say they regularly put money aside, but fewer than one in three feel confident that they understand investment products. That 43-point gap is the most useful finding in new Irish research: a saving habit does not automatically produce informed investing.**

Bank of Ireland published its second [Youth Mind and Money survey](https://www.bankofireland.com/about-bank-of-ireland/press-releases/2026/strong-savings-culture-among-young-people/) on 20 November 2026. Ipsos B&A surveyed a stated nationally representative sample of 526 people aged 13 to 30 between 31 July and 19 August.

The research was commissioned and published by a bank, and the release does not provide every questionnaire or cross-tabulation. It is useful evidence about the surveyed group, not proof that every teenager or young adult behaves the same way.

## Saving rose, while investment confidence stayed low

| Survey finding | 2026 result | Comparison given in the release |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Regularly put money aside | 73% | 64% in 2025 |
| Say saving is very important | 84% | — |
| Interested in investing | 56% | — |
| Confident they understand investment products | 30% | — |
| Say fear of losing money discourages investing | 69% | — |
| Say financial control supports mental health | 78% | 70% in 2025 |

Regular saving increased by **9 percentage points**, equivalent to a relative rise of about **14%** from the 2025 result:

> (73 − 64) ÷ 64 = **14.1%**

But the gap between investment interest and confidence was **26 percentage points**: 56% were interested, while 30% felt confident they understood the products.

## The crypto result needs careful reading

The release says reported crypto participation fell from **36% in 2025 to 16% in 2026**, a 20-point fall. It also says the share associated with property investment fell from 23% to 8%, while stocks rose from 47% to 59%.

The published summary does not fully explain the denominator for every asset question or whether the people answering in both years had the same characteristics. These are two survey samples, not the same individuals tracked over time, so the figures should not be described as measured money moving directly from crypto or property into shares.

The risk signal is clearer: 82% agreed it is possible to lose a substantial amount or all of the money invested in crypto. The survey also reports that males were more likely than females to have sought investment advice online or through social media, at 47% versus 28%.

## A saving habit is not an investment plan

Saving and investing do different jobs:

- **Cash savings** protect money needed soon and absorb emergencies without forcing an asset sale.
- **Investments** can be appropriate for longer goals but can fall sharply and may carry fees and tax.
- **Debt repayment** may provide a more certain benefit than taking market risk, depending on the interest rate.

For example, someone whose essential spending is €1,500 a month would need **€4,500** to hold three months of expenses. That is an illustrative target, not a universal rule:

> €1,500 × 3 = **€4,500**

Compound's [emergency-fund calculator](/emergency-fund-calculator/) can replace that assumption with actual household costs. The [regular-savings calculator](/regular-savings-calculator/) shows how a monthly contribution accumulates without pretending a return is guaranteed.

## Four checks before moving from saving to investing

1. **Set the time horizon.** Money for rent, college or a deposit in the next few years should not depend on a volatile asset recovering on schedule.
2. **Understand the product.** Check what is owned, the fees, access restrictions, tax treatment and worst plausible loss.
3. **Verify the provider.** Do not treat a social-media following as authorisation or protection.
4. **Separate interest from confidence.** If the product cannot be explained in plain language, pause before funding it.

Eligible deposits at an authorised institution may be protected by the [Deposit Guarantee Scheme](https://www.centralbank.ie/consumer-hub/deposit-guarantee-scheme), generally up to €100,000 per person per institution. Investments do not receive the same protection against market losses.

People looking at long-term investing can start with Compound's [Investing in Ireland guide](/wealth/investing-in-ireland/) and [investment-fee guide](/wealth/investment-fees-ireland/). The proposed Personal Investment Account is also covered in the [PIA guide](/wealth/personal-investment-account-ireland/), which distinguishes the planned rules from an account that is already open.

The new survey is encouraging about the habit of saving. Its warning is equally important: high interest in investing alongside low confidence creates fertile ground for unsuitable products, exaggerated returns and online advice that is not tailored to the person taking the risk.
