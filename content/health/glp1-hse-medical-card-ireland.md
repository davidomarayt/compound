<p class="glp-note">Republic of Ireland · Adult treatment · Funding information checked 14 September 2026. Individual eligibility must be confirmed by your prescriber and pharmacist.</p>

You can leave a consultation with a prescription and still have no clear answer to the next question: who pays for it?

That uncertainty is particularly common around GLP-1 medicines. Someone you know may receive a diabetes medicine through a State scheme, while another person is quoted a private price for treatment described online as much the same thing. Both accounts can be accurate. The medicine, the condition being treated and the rules of the scheme all matter.

For adults seeking weight-management treatment, the current position is specific: **Wegovy and Mounjaro are not covered by the medical card, Long-Term Illness Scheme or Drugs Payment Scheme, according to the HSE. Saxenda has a restricted funding route.** A medical card by itself does not remove those restrictions. [HSE funding guidance](https://www2.hse.ie/conditions/obesity/medicines/)

Ozempic's authorised indication is type 2 diabetes. Sharing an active ingredient with Wegovy does not make their prescriptions or funding interchangeable. ## Why the €80 Drugs Payment Scheme limit may not help

The Drugs Payment Scheme limits a family's spending to **€80 in a calendar month on approved prescribed medicines and certain other approved items**. There is no means test. The decisive word here is approved: the scheme is not a universal ceiling on anything a doctor prescribes. [HSE: Drugs Payment Scheme](https://www2.hse.ie/services/schemes-allowances/drugs-payment-scheme/card/)

Consider an illustrative household paying €80 for eligible medicines and €300 for a medicine excluded from the scheme. Its combined bill would be €380, not €80. The €300 is an example to explain the rule, not a price quote for a particular injection.

Before collecting a prescription, ask the pharmacist to separate the bill into eligible scheme items and private items. That is more useful than simply asking whether the pharmacy accepts a DPS card.

The Long-Term Illness Scheme answers a different question. It covers certain medicines and appliances for specified conditions, without a means test. Diabetes mellitus is on the list; gestational diabetes is excluded. Having a qualifying condition does not turn the scheme into blanket cover for unrelated treatment. [HSE: Long-Term Illness Scheme](https://www2.hse.ie/services/schemes-allowances/lti/about/)

## Saxenda: the exception with detailed conditions

The adult Saxenda protocol is narrower than general eligibility for obesity medication. Applications are considered for people aged **18–74**, with **BMI of at least 35**, **prediabetes** and **high cardiovascular risk**, alongside participation in dietary and physical-activity interventions. Clinical exclusions also apply.

The protocol requires recent evidence: fasting glucose and HbA1c results for prediabetes, and cardiovascular-risk information. These readings must be within 30 days of application. It also allows consideration of treatment already being used to manage cardiovascular risk. This is therefore not something a reader can reliably settle with a BMI calculator and an old blood-test result.

The initiating clinician applies through the HSE's reimbursement system; approval should be in place before a scheme-funded prescription is issued. [HSE adult Saxenda protocol, sections 2–3](https://healthservice.hse.ie/documents/7341/Managed_Access_Protocol_-_Liraglutide_Saxenda_for_weight_management_in_adults.pdf)

The practical question for a consultation is: “Could you check whether I meet the current managed access criteria, and tell me which measurements or records you need?” Bring existing results if available, but let the clinician decide whether updated testing is necessary.

## Initial approval is not the whole funding journey

Saxenda's first approval covers six months, described by the HSE as 24 weeks. Continuation requires a second application assessing the response after **12 weeks at 3 mg daily**, including whether at least **5% of initial body weight** has been lost. This is not simply a six-month weight-loss target measured from the first injection.

The combined approval period is two years from the initial application. [HSE: adult managed access process](https://healthservice.hse.ie/staff/information-healthcare-workers/medicines-management-programme-mmp/managed-access-protocols/)

The full protocol says continued treatment beyond that period requires a new application. It does not promise automatic renewal. [HSE protocol, section 4](https://healthservice.hse.ie/documents/7341/Managed_Access_Protocol_-_Liraglutide_Saxenda_for_weight_management_in_adults.pdf)

Ask who will organise the continuation review and when it needs to happen. Put the review date beside the prescription-renewal date in your diary. An approval deadline is worth discussing before the last funded pack, especially if you would struggle to pay privately.

There is a separate adolescent Saxenda pathway, introduced in February 2026 and involving approved specialist consultants. The adult criteria above should not be applied to a child. [HSE: adolescent managed access](https://healthservice.hse.ie/staff/information-healthcare-workers/medicines-management-programme-mmp/managed-access-protocols/)

## Does a positive assessment mean Mounjaro will be funded soon?

Not necessarily. Ireland's NCPE assesses clinical and economic evidence to advise the HSE. The HSE makes the reimbursement decision. A regulatory licence, a value-for-money assessment and individual patient approval are different decisions. [NCPE: reimbursement process](https://www.ncpe.ie/submission-process/overview-of-the-drug-reimbursement-process/)

For tirzepatide's weight-management application, the NCPE completed its assessment on 3 December 2025. Its recommendation was conditional: consider reimbursement if cost-effectiveness improves relative to existing treatments. That is not an announcement that prescriptions are now covered, nor does it provide a reliable start date. [NCPE: tirzepatide assessment](https://www.ncpe.ie/tirzepatide-mounjaro-hta-id-24024/)

The Wegovy record also needs careful reading. The NCPE records withdrawal in March 2025 of the application for the four-pack pre-filled-pen presentation. That entry should not be stretched into a prediction about every future presentation or funding decision. [NCPE: Wegovy](https://www.ncpe.ie/semaglutide-wegovy/)

For a household budget, count funding when eligibility and availability are confirmed. A promising headline is not a payment arrangement.

## What to establish before you spend money

Try to leave the consultation or pharmacy with answers to four questions:

1. **What is the exact medicine and treatment indication?** Record the product name, rather than just “a GLP-1”.
2. **Which scheme, if any, applies to me?** Ask whether individual approval is required and whether it has been granted.
3. **What remains payable?** Separate medicine costs, consultation charges and follow-up care. For private examples, see our [Irish price comparison](/health/wegovy-mounjaro-ozempic-cost-ireland/).
4. **What happens at review or if funding ends?** Agree who to contact before treatment becomes unaffordable. Our [guide to stopping treatment](/health/stopping-glp1-weight-regain/) explains why a follow-up plan matters.

If access is declined, ask for the precise reason: a medicine outside the scheme, an unmet clinical criterion, missing information or an application still being processed. Those are different problems and need different next steps. A refusal of funding is not, by itself, a verdict on whether someone's health needs deserve care.
