The PIA, also called an Individual Investment Account (IIA) in the Finance Bill, has a **target launch of 1 July 2027**, subject to final legislation and provider readiness.

## Should I invest now or wait for the PIA in Ireland?

**Planning to buy a home?** This calculator deals with investing before the scheme opens, not mortgage underwriting. Our separate [PIA and mortgage-deposit guide](/wealth/pia-mortgage-deposit-ireland/) explains what lenders might ask if your deposit is invested in an ETF or shares, including evidence of source of funds.

**Short answer:** There is no general rule that you should stop investing until the proposed **Personal Investment Account (PIA)** launches. If your money is for a long-term goal, waiting in cash means missing potential market gains, but investing now exposes you to potential losses and existing Irish investment taxes. **Selling today's investments to fund a future PIA can itself create a tax bill.** If the money might be needed within the next few years, keeping it safe and accessible may be more important than any hypothetical market return.

The right comparison is not simply "tax-free PIA versus taxed shares". It is **investing now in an ordinary account and potentially selling on 1 July 2027** versus **holding cash until then**, after allowing for investment costs, CGT or fund exit tax on a sale, deposit interest tax (DIRT), and the proposed PIA contribution cap. That is what our independent calculator models.

The Government has announced a **€12,000 annual contribution limit**, a **€50,000 average account-value threshold**, and a proposed **1% annual PIA charge on the portion above that threshold**. These are not currently live account terms. The short-window tool stops at the planned opening date; for a longer projection, use our [full PIA calculator](/pia-calculator/).

## What would €5,000 look like at the planned PIA opening?

The worked scenarios below use **17 November 2026** as the purchase date and **1 July 2027** as the target disposal date (**226 calendar days**). Each route starts with €5,000. Cash earns a hypothetical **2% gross AER**, with **33% DIRT**; investments have a **0.25% annual fee** and **€10 of total buy/sell dealing costs**. The share model assumes **no dividends**, 31% CGT at disposal and the full unused €1,270 CGT exemption. The accumulating fund/ETF model uses a **proposed 35% exit tax** on positive gains when sold. No other gains, losses or taxable income are assumed.

The larger direct-share value at +6% is driven in part by the *assumed available CGT exemption*, not because direct shares are automatically more tax-efficient or safer than diversified funds. At −15%, both investment routes lose money while the hypothetical deposit earns interest; a 6% average is not something you can rely on over just 226 days.

## Can I move investments I already own into a PIA?

**The Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 as introduced provides for contributions in money, not a simple in-kind transfer of ordinary brokerage holdings into the proposed PIA.** Transfers between PIA providers have separate rules. If you buy shares or an ETF now and want to use the proceeds to fund a PIA, you may have to sell that holding first, potentially crystallising CGT or fund exit tax, and then contribute cash within your remaining annual allowance. This is not equivalent to relabelling an existing brokerage account as a PIA.

An investor with €20,000 of shares today should **not** expect to move all €20,000 into a PIA in 2027 if the €12,000 annual cap remains. Likewise, a holding worth €12,500 after investment growth does not automatically create €12,500 of PIA contribution room. Money above the cap can remain outside, but the model does not project that excess balance beyond launch.

Crucially, **you do not have to sell your existing investments**. Another option is to keep a taxable portfolio invested for the long term and use *new money* for a PIA once an eligible provider opens. The calculator models a sale at launch so the two paths have a common comparison date, not because switching is always the right choice.

## What taxes matter if I invest before July 2027?

### Direct shares: Capital Gains Tax on a sale

For a direct share sale that falls under the standard Irish CGT regime, the rate has been **31% for applicable disposals from 7 October 2026**. An individual may have a **€1,270 annual personal exemption** available against chargeable gains, but it is shared across the tax year's eligible disposals, not granted anew for each share. Losses, sale costs and other circumstances can change the liability. Dividend income is another tax consideration **not** represented in this short-window share model.

### Accumulating ETFs and funds: check the actual tax regime

Many Irish-domiciled and equivalent offshore funds, including relevant accumulating ETFs, are subject to the investment-undertaking/offshore-fund exit-tax regime instead of ordinary share CGT. The rate for relevant individual funds in **2026 is 38%**. The Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026 proposes **35% from 1 January 2027**, subject to enactment. This tool models a positive gain realised on a July 2027 sale at the selected tax rate. **Not every ETF has the same Irish tax classification**, so check the exact fund and domicile. Eight-year deemed disposal is irrelevant to a newly purchased holding sold after seven months, but can matter if you keep an ETF outside the PIA long term.

### Savings: DIRT on interest

Revenue's general **DIRT rate is 33%** on relevant deposit interest. The calculator compounds the assumed advertised annual interest rate over the waiting period and deducts DIRT from that interest once at the target date. Real accounts may credit interest monthly or annually, change rates, have terms or limits, or involve PRSI in some cases. A savings account is not equivalent to shares in market risk, but can protect the money needed for a near-term purpose if the account and deposit protection conditions are suitable.

## How does the invest-now-or-wait calculator work? {#pia-wait-method}

This is deliberately a **launch-window calculator, not a 10-year PIA-return forecast**. It isolates what your *starting amount* might become by 1 July 2027 so you can weigh the consequence of delaying the initial investment.

**Time:** The browser counts calendar days from the current date in the Europe/Dublin timezone to 1 July 2027. The dated examples above were calculated on 17 November 2026. The model uses a 365.2425-day year to annualise the fraction of time.

**Invest-now route:** It subtracts half the entered fixed dealing costs at purchase, compounds the remaining amount by the hypothetical annual total return and an annual percentage fee, then subtracts the other half of the fixed dealing costs on sale. For direct shares, it applies 31% CGT to any remaining positive gain **after** the entered unused CGT exemption. For the relevant accumulating fund/ETF example, it applies the selected exit-tax rate to the modelled positive fund gain on sale. It assumes no distributions, foreign withholding, intermediate sales or tax-loss offsets, and does not include a full statutory tax computation.

**Wait route:** The same starting amount earns the entered *gross* savings AER over the same period; 33% DIRT is applied to the accrued positive interest. The calculator assumes the interest is available at launch and does not model PRSI.

**Break-even return:** The tool finds the hypothetical **annualised investment return before fees** that would make the investment-sale proceeds equal the net savings balance, using the selected asset tax, fees and cash assumptions. This is not a forecast or a required investment return.

**PIA contribution limit:** It shows the lower of the estimated cash available and **€12,000** as a potential launch contribution in each route. This is not a tax-free gain threshold; it is an annual limit on money paid in, which may be shared with other contributions made in the same calendar year. The tool does **not** model the future PIA's 1% annual average-value charge, provider platform fees, future holdings, 2027 part-year valuation, or any potential delay to account availability.

Real investments can be volatile, suffer sudden losses, attract additional fund, broker or FX charges, and carry different risk from bank deposits. No smooth annual return is promised.

## Is it worth waiting? Five questions to ask

1. **When will you need the money?** An emergency fund, deposit or imminent large expense is not normally appropriate capital for speculative short-term market exposure.
2. **Which investment would you buy?** Diversified accumulation funds and individual company shares differ substantially in risk and Irish tax treatment.
3. **Would you actually sell at launch?** Keeping current holdings and using new PIA contributions could avoid an otherwise unnecessary disposal.
4. **What contribution room will you have?** The proposed €12,000 limit is per calendar year, so sale proceeds in excess of your unused allowance cannot simply all move in.
5. **What are the actual costs?** Broker charges, spreads, fund expense ratios, cash AER and the eventual PIA provider's terms can all change the choice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I stop investing and wait for the PIA?

Not automatically. Waiting avoids market losses over the waiting period but gives up potential returns. Investing earlier can help long-term compounding, yet any gain may be taxed if you sell to enter the PIA. Compare the investment time horizon, liquidity needs, tax treatment and actual fees rather than treating the launch date as an instruction to buy or sell.

### When can I open a PIA in Ireland?

The Government target is **1 July 2027**, not an account available today. Timing depends on the final legislation, Revenue systems and individual providers. Follow our [source-checked PIA launch tracker](/pia/#pia-launch-tracker) and [provider announcements](/pia/providers/).

### Is the €50,000 threshold the amount I can contribute?

No. The announced **€50,000 is an account-value threshold for the proposed 1% annual charge**, while the **€12,000 is the annual contribution cap**. The proposed charge uses average daily closing values, not just gains or the 31 December balance. See the [PIA daily-valuation explanation](/pia/daily-valuation/).

### Can I sell an ETF now and buy it again inside the PIA?

Under the draft design, funding the new account is a **cash contribution**, and selling an outside ETF can be taxable. Whether a provider offers the identical ETF, and what the final tax and fee treatment will be, must be confirmed once the scheme and products are finalised. Read our [PIA investment-eligibility guide](/pia/investment-eligibility/).

### Is a PIA better than a pension or an ordinary brokerage account?

Each serves different needs. Pensions may provide valuable upfront income-tax relief and employer contributions but normally restrict access. The proposed PIA is intended to make eligible investing easier with a different tax model and no analogous upfront income-tax deduction. Use our [PIA vs pension calculator](/pia/vs-pension/) or the [PIA vs ordinary investing after-tax calculator](/pia/vs-ordinary-investing/) for those separate decisions.
