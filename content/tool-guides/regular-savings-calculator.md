## What this calculator is for

This tool projects what a starting balance plus regular monthly contributions could grow to over a chosen period. It is useful for deposits, investment plans and long-term savings scenarios, provided the return assumption is interpreted carefully.

## How to read the result

The final balance contains two different things: money you contributed and modelled growth. The larger the growth share, the more sensitive the result is to return, fees, tax and sequence of returns.

## Worked example

Starting with €5,000 and adding €300 a month for 10 years means €41,000 of total contributions before growth. A 5% modelled return can lift the projected balance above that, but the projection is not a promise that any investment will earn 5% every year.

## Irish context

Irish deposit interest is generally subject to 33% DIRT for Irish-resident individuals. Investments can face other tax regimes, including CGT for some direct assets and different rules for many funds. Product-specific tax is outside this simple projection.

## What can change the answer

Time, contribution rate and return all matter. Increasing the monthly contribution has a direct effect; extending the horizon gives both contributions and returns more time. Fees can compound negatively over long periods.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not compare a gross investment return with a net deposit return without adjusting for fees and tax. Do not use historical equity returns as a guaranteed future rate. And remember that inflation can make a large future number less impressive in real terms.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are contributions added at the start or end of the month?
The calculator's standard projection uses regular monthly additions under its stated method.

### Does it include DIRT?
No.

### Can I use it for a pension?
For a rough accumulation illustration, yes, but pension tax relief, employer contributions and access rules require separate modelling.

### Is the return guaranteed?
No.

## Method and limitations

The annual return is treated as an **effective annual return**. Compound converts it to the equivalent monthly rate using **(1 + annual return)^(1/12) − 1**, then applies that rate monthly and adds the contribution at the end of each month.

This convention means a 6% annual assumption compounds to 6% over a full year rather than using 0.5% each month (which would produce slightly more than 6% annually). The model still assumes smooth returns and ignores tax, fees and volatility unless separately reflected in your assumptions.

## Saving rate versus investment return

For shorter savings horizons, the amount you contribute usually matters far more than a small difference in annual return.

For example, increasing monthly saving by €100 adds €6,000 of direct contributions over five years before any growth. By contrast, trying to earn an extra 1 percentage point of return on a relatively small balance may add much less while exposing you to more risk.

That changes over very long horizons because compounding has more time to work. The calculator is therefore most useful when you test both levers separately: first ask what happens if you save more, then ask what happens if returns differ.

For money needed within a few years—such as a house deposit—the certainty and accessibility of the capital may matter more than maximising expected return.
