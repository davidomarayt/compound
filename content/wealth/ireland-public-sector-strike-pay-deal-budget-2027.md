**Updated Monday 9 November.** The Government has put a revised offer to public service unions ahead of Thursday’s planned stoppage. Tánaiste Simon Harris confirmed the change on Sunday. Union preparations for 12 November continued. With no settlement announced by this morning’s cutoff, a teacher, nurse or civil servant cannot yet count a new payment in their November budget. [Tánaiste’s remarks, audio](https://highlandradio.com/2026/11/08/significantly-improved-pay-offer-made-to-public-sector-workers/) · [Fórsa strike guidance](https://www.forsa.ie/faqs-strike-rallies-2026/)

## How would the reported back payment work?

[RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/news/ireland/2026/1109/1598461-pay-talks/) and [The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/politics/2026/11/08/public-sector-workers-offered-pay-increases-worth-105-ahead-of-planned-strikes/) report that the offer includes a 2026 back payment of **€1,200 or 2%, whichever is greater**. They report headline increases of 10.5% over a proposed agreement extending into early 2029. These are reported negotiating terms. The Tánaiste confirmed that the Government had improved its offer, without publishing a salary schedule in those remarks.

The crossover is **€60,000** because 2% of €60,000 is €1,200. On a €45,000 salary, 2% is €900, so the reported €1,200 minimum would be higher. On €70,000, 2% is €1,400. Those are gross illustrations assuming the full salary is eligible. The final text would need to establish eligibility, treatment of part-time service and the payment date. The headline percentage spans several years and cannot be applied to this month’s payslip.

## Will schools and appointments operate on Thursday?

Fórsa was still advising members to prepare for the 24-hour stoppage. [ASTI’s instructions](https://www.asti.ie/industrial-action-2026/) direct members to rallies and say there will be no pickets at schools. An empty gate is therefore a poor guide to whether a child has class. Check the school’s own message before paying for cover. If you have a clinic booking, follow the provider’s instructions about your particular appointment.

## How the earlier offer differed

In its [account of the rejected offer](https://www.forsa.ie/faqs-strike-rallies-2026/), Fórsa put the general increases at 6.75%, with a further 1% for local bargaining. The first proposed increase of 1.75% was worth €787.50 over a full year on €45,000 basic pay. That earlier calculation describes a rejected proposal.
